# ORG wave 2 notes

## Method
17 web searches plus 2 page fetches, no idea bank, wave 1 names file read only to avoid duplicates. Produced 26 records: 14 new capabilities (ORG-C38 to ORG-C51) and 12 extensions of wave 1 ids (same id reused, `extends` set).

## Sources by type
- Job descriptions / day-in-the-life: Sprout Social, Indeed, Hootsuite, Coursera, CareerFoundry, Qureos, seojobsearch.com, for social media manager, content strategist, community manager, SEO manager.
- Agency service menus and pricing: Fractl and Green Flag Digital (digital PR), Respona and RankYak (link building), Single Grain and Touchstorm (YouTube channel management), Content Allies and Podsqueeze (podcast).
- SaaS tool feature lists: Sprout Social and Buffer (scheduling), Profound and Peec AI (AEO/AI visibility), BrightLocal (local SEO/GBP), Surfer vs Clearscope (content scoring), OpusClip and Repurpose.io (video repurposing), GRIN/Modash/Aspire (creator gifting and seeding).
- Freelance marketplaces: Fiverr and Upwork gig listings for SEO copywriting and social content.
- AI agent products: Blotato and o-mega.ai roundups of browser agents for social posting and reply automation, noting the shift toward official APIs over browser clicking.
- India-specific: WhatsApp Business API marketing guides (Hawknest, Trilliant Digital) for D2C brands.

## What wave 1 missed most
Three whole surfaces had no dedicated wave 1 record despite being named in the brief: YouTube channel operations (titles, thumbnails, playlists, chapters, Shorts, community tab), a brand podcast as a production/distribution pipeline, and a brand newsletter as an owned content channel distinct from lifecycle CRM email. Also missing: an owned community space (WhatsApp community, Discord, Circle, private group) as its own capability, separate from replying to public comments; social/PR crisis and reputation incident response, separate from the pre-publish claims gate; social listening for trend and sentiment tracking, separate from AI-answer-engine visibility; Pinterest-specific search and board strategy; LinkedIn founder and employer-brand thought leadership; and employer-branding/recruiting content. On the extension side, the biggest gaps were operational automation that gifting platforms already sell (inventory-aware shipment generation, usage-rights and whitelisting capture, fraud vetting) sitting underneath the wave 1 seeding capability, and revenue attribution (UTM, promo codes, CAC/LTV) sitting underneath the wave 1 performance-reporting capability, which today only covers engagement.

## Possible but not yet common
Four records marked `possible`, concrete rather than speculative: an autonomous FAQ-tier AI-fact-correction loop that re-checks engines on a schedule after a source fix and drafts publisher outreach; an always-on creator discovery agent that scans public posts for category conversations from creators not yet connected to the brand and drafts personalized outreach for batch approval; a weekly auto-drafted brief that merges competitor social activity, press coverage and AI-answer changes into one page without a person opening each source; and closing the loop from community feedback mining into an actually tracked, resolved ticket rather than a report that ends at hand-off. None of these exist as a single sold product today; each stitches together mechanics that already exist separately (gifting platforms, AI visibility trackers, social listening tools, issue trackers) into one agent-run pipeline.
