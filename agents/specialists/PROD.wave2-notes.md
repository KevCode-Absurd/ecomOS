## What wave 1 missed most

Quick commerce (Blinkit, Zepto, Instamart) was entirely absent despite being named in the brief: onboarding, the MRP-based ROAS distortion that inflates reported returns 30-50%, ad-credit management, dark-store fill rate, and the 90-day unit-economics validation window. Biggest single gap: 4 records (C48-C51).

Second: domestic operations mechanics below the strategic calls wave 1 covers. Wave 1 has 3PL/freight selection (C21) and reorder-point math (C22), not the daily layer underneath: WMS slotting and labor scheduling, returns grading, multi-courier rate-shop and failover, NDR outreach, COD remittance reconciliation, courier scorecarding. Daily jobs of a fulfilment manager and shipping executive that no wave-1 record touches.

Third: India compliance beyond BIS/hallmarking (C45): HSN/SAC validation against e-invoice rejection, Legal Metrology and FSSAI licence-renewal tracking, EPR plastic-packaging filing. Concrete and recurring, done today by nobody or by the founder ad hoc.

Fourth: catalogue as a data-management discipline, not just an ads feed (C47) or one marketplace's listing (C34/C44): a real PIM layer with completeness scoring, per-channel formatting, cross-channel price/stock audits, image/video spec validation.

## Possible, not yet commonly sold

An agent that runs the actual multi-round supplier negotiation over email/WhatsApp within founder-set guardrails (procurement agents today mostly prep the human, per Lyzr/Keelvar/Pactum, rather than negotiate themselves for small D2C import orders). A background watch on IndiaMART/Alibaba for a better-than-incumbent supplier against a held spec, alerting only, never contacting. A per-order NDR stop/retry decision driven by the brand's own margin and RTO cost instead of one blanket aggregator rule. A COD-to-prepaid conversion test run as a designed, read experiment rather than a policy nobody tests. An EPR compliance tracker, since brands currently either ignore it or outsource it blind to a consultant.
