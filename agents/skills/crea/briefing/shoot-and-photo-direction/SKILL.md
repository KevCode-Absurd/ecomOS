invoke_when: a real shoot is running, or any AI-touched image needs direction
  so the product shown matches what a customer actually receives
inputs: concept or founder brief, real product facts (dimensions, material,
  color, fit)
outputs: image-accuracy brief, shot list, QA'd final frame set
---
Purpose: keep every photographed or AI-touched product image honest to what
ships, since a mismatch here becomes a return, a bad review or a platform
complaint, not just a design nitpick.

Steps
1. Set direction naming the exact product variant or SKU being shot; a
   generic "the product" instruction invites the wrong color or size on set.
2. Build a numbered shot list tied to a specific ad or page use, with the
   subject's gaze pointed toward the CTA whenever a person is in frame.
3. Book the studio, location and any model within the approved production
   budget from the current monthly plan.
4. Collect signed model and location releases before the shoot starts, never
   retroactively.
5. Check every retouched or AI-touched frame against the real product facts:
   color must match, scale must read correctly next to a known object,
   material texture must still look like the real material, not a stylized
   redraw.
6. File and version the checked frames under the naming standard before
   they are usable downstream.

Checks: release on file before use; no fact-bearing detail altered by
retouching or AI.

Output: the image-accuracy brief, the shot list, and the QA'd final frames.

Example: a rug brand's AI background swap is allowed to change the room
scene, never the rug's actual weave pattern or true color under warm light.
