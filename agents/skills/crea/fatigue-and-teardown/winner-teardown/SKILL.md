---
name: fatigue-and-teardown/winner-teardown
owner: crea-paid-creative-copy
version: 1
invoke_when: the week's top and bottom ads need breaking into their parts
  with a written, numbers-backed hypothesis
inputs: ad-level metrics, account averages, ad creative and copy, competitor
  data (signal only)
outputs: part-by-part breakdown, hypothesis per ad, funnel-role tag
---
Purpose: explain why an ad worked or did not from its actual numbers and its
actual parts, so the next angle or hook decision builds on a real reason,
not a guess dressed as analysis.

Steps
1. Pull the ad's own metrics next to the account average for the same
   period, keeping totals separate from any breakdown.
2. Break the ad into its parts, the hook, the structural sequence, the
   proof used, the offer stated, and the CTA, naming each one explicitly.
3. Write a specific hypothesis for each part's contribution, backed by the
   metric that supports it, for example "the hook drove an above-average
   hold rate but the proof point did not convert it."
4. From the real numbers, assign a funnel role, cold hook winner, retention
   proof, bottom-funnel closer, matching the metric pattern that earned it.
5. Note where a winning structure echoes a competitor pattern already
   tagged as signal, explicitly marked as signal only, never as the reason
   it won.

Checks: hypothesis cites a number; competitor note marked signal only.

Output: the breakdown, the hypothesis, and the funnel-role tag per ad.

Example: a losing ad's hook_rate is above average but CVR is far below it;
the hypothesis names the offer as the likely failure point, not the hook.
