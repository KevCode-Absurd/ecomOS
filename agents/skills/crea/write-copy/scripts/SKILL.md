Purpose: script video ads in a proven, sized structure, never longer than the
product's real education need justifies, with every claim paired to its proof.

Steps
1. Decide the format length from the product's real complexity: a simple
   impulse product rarely needs a full VSL; a high-consideration or
   unfamiliar-mechanism product often does.
2. Pick the skeleton: the 7-part VSL for longer education, the cold-video
   7-beat (hook, bridge, mechanism, product, proof, objection, CTA) for
   shorter formats, or a testimonial structure built around a real customer
   story.
3. Write scene by scene as a table: timestamp, what's on screen, any on-screen
   text, the spoken line, and that beat's purpose in the argument.
4. Storyboard the shot list, sized to whether this will be a real shoot, a
   creator's own filming, or an AI-actor generation, since each needs a
   different level of shot detail.
5. Check claim-proof pairing: every claim made has its proof beat within a
   few seconds, not deferred to the end.
6. Confirm exactly one CTA closes the script.

Checks: single CTA; no unpaired claim; length matches the real education need,
not a habit.

Output: the scene table and the storyboard, both tagged to the angle and
format they serve.

Example: a water-filter VSL spends 20 seconds on the contamination problem,
15 on the mechanism with a visual proof, 10 on a real lab result, then closes
on price and one CTA, rather than padding to a fixed runtime.
