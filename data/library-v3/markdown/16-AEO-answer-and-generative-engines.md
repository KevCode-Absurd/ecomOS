## The AI Filter: deciding when a keyword is a trap vs an unlinked-mention play
Use when: Doing keyword research for a store and deciding where to chase clicks versus where to aim for brand mention inside AI answers.

## Write content capsules so AI engines can extract clean answers
Use when: Writing buying guides, blog posts and FAQ sections on product or category pages aimed at AI citations.
- Structure each section as a question heading (H2) followed immediately by a direct answer. The capsule must make sense on its own, without the surrounding article.
- Answer the buyer question immediately. Suggested store application: under a fit question, state verified size and compatibility facts before a longer explanation.
- Keep the capsule around 20–25 words. Going over 50 words is acceptable if the answer genuinely requires detail, but brevity is the default.
- Target roughly 60–70% of the piece written in capsule form. The remaining 30–40% can be freer prose (process explanations, narrative sections) so the article still reads well to humans.
- Do not put links inside the capsule itself — keep it clean for extraction. Put citations in the surrounding text instead.
- Cite sources for factual statements, especially any numbers, by linking a contextual keyword to the origin. The source argues that citing sources increases trust and is important for AI engines, and that fear of losing traffic to citations signals a content problem, not a linking problem.

## LLM Mechanics: RAG vs. Model Training
Use when: Deciding where to invest effort when trying to influence what an AI assistant says about your products.
- Understand the two layers: the core model, trained on large web corpora, performs next-word prediction (e.g. the capital of California).
- Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) performs a live search and then summarizes the results — this is the layer that produces citations.
- Almost everything actionable in AEO lives in RAG; influencing the core model is extremely hard and may take a year or more to show.

- Practical implication for a store: prioritize being present in the retrievable set (review sites, comparisons, video, communities, your own indexed pages) over trying to influence training data.
- Skip the obscure tactic of mass-producing pages that assert a brand is best for a keyword; Smith says few would want to spend time on it.

## Expect AI-generated content to collapse citation diversity (model collapse risk)
Use when: Evaluating the long-term risk of an AI-content-heavy content strategy for your store's category pages and buying guides.
- Understand the two places derivatives enter a system: the core model training and the retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) layer that supplies citations.
- A published paper on 'model collapse' showed that training a core model on its own derivatives quickly breaks it — producing hallucinations and failures.
- The source ran a RAG-side study: generate 10 derivative articles, put them into rankings, summarize, then generate 10 more and summarize the summaries in an infinite loop.
- The result: answers converge on a single opinion. The wisdom-of-the-crowd effect (averaging many diverse opinions beats the best single individual) shrinks to one answer.
- The analogous incentive argument: if scraped or generated content worked, everyone would do it, and engines would eventually have to suppress derivatives — as Google did to shopping comparison sites in 2007.
- For an ecommerce operator, the practical takeaway is to keep original, first-hand content (real product testing, real customer questions, real usage photos/data) that cannot be produced by re-summarizing others.
