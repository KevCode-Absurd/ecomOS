Scores are out of 50 (5 criteria, 1 to 10). Judge 2 called it a near tie and gave the playbook arm the better test plan.

## What decided it

Four things cost the playbook arm the test. None of them is a copywriting failure.

1. A data window slip. Arm B's 90-day and 30-day windows included 24 Sep, a partial capture day. That day holds 1 order of ₹7,73,520 against ₹39 of spend on a paused bracelet showcase ad. It moved bracelets from 1.11 to 4.21 ROAS and product showcase from 1.12 to 4.55 in its tables, and lifted its 30-day ROAS to 5.64. Arm A stopped at 23 Sep. All 3 judges found this.
2. One false price claim. Arm B's ad NB-03 headline said "Gold Gifts Under ₹25,000". The landing page it points to is titled "Finer Jewellery Under 30,000 INR" and holds pieces up to ₹29,990. Arm A had no wrong price, offer or policy claim in its 12 ads.
3. Budget arithmetic. Arm B's site-visitor ad sets got ₹621 a day, ₹17,375 over 4 weeks, below its own ₹20,000 read spend, so 4 of its 12 ad sets could never be judged by its own rule. Its scale rule contradicted itself, and 2 of its controls were paused or 8 days old. Arm A's budgets reached every read point and its controls were live ads.
4. Two blank date placeholders left in client copy.

Arm A was not clean either. Judges found "natural pearls" on a page that lists cultured or synthetic pearls, an "Exchange Any Time" headline on a limited exchange policy, a reworded customer quote, and "most repairs free" with no page behind it. It carried 21 to 23 confirm-before-launch tags against 11 to 15 for Arm B.

## Where the playbook arm was better

- The audit: the finding that warm "MOF + BOF" ad sets sit inside prospecting campaigns (₹20.9 lakh, 16% of spend), the attribution audit (4 settings in use), and a claim-by-claim scan of live copy that surfaced buyback, IGI, "India's largest" and 5 different customer counts. Judges verified these.
- The plan mechanics: 7-day click on every campaign, a frequency-based cap on past-customer spend computed from CPM, 3 visuals per concept, a keep, yellow, kill and scale table at a 3x CPA read point, and copy scored in code for length and reading grade.
- Fewer confirm tags and per-ad claim-check lines, which 2 of 3 judges read as the mark of a specialist process.

## What this says about the playbook

Across 2 runs the playbook arm is 1 judge to 4. In run 1 (site speed) it split 2 judges. Here it lost all 3, and it lost on data hygiene, claim verification and budget arithmetic, not on the words. The 11-ADC playbook is a writing playbook. It did not stop a partial-day window, a wrong price cap or an unreadable ad set budget, because it has no rule that says: end the window on the last full day; quote the page title and price you read, with the date; show that every ad set's budget reaches its read spend.

The brief did a lot of the work. Both arms got the same detailed brief (3 objectives, 12 ads, claim check per ad, baselines from the account, owner decisions), and judge 3 noted both reports share one skeleton. With a strong brief and the same model in both arms, the playbook's marginal value was procedure, and the procedure leaked in 4 places that a checklist could have caught.

Kevin's own pick is the deciding signal. The judges' scores are close on 2 of 3 cards, and the ads themselves are what he would run or edit.

## Changes made to the playbooks after this run

- 11-ADC Group O, R132 and R133: a claim check line must quote the page title, the price or number seen, and the date read; a price-cap headline must match the landing page's own title or filter; no placeholders in client copy, use `[confirm before launch: what]`.
- 24-DEP Group G R116 and 06-MEA Group A R103: end every analysis window on the last full captured day and state it; an ad set's 4-week budget must be at or above its read spend or the plan says the ad set is unreadable; a control must be a live ad with spend in the last 7 days.
- 11-ADC section 11b: test learnings log for this run.

## Changes for the next run

- Give both arms a thin brief, not a full one, so the playbook has room to show what it adds.
- Add a fourth judge: Kevin, with the same 5 criteria.
- Have judges verify the same 3 pre-chosen facts per report plus 3 of their own, so scores are comparable across judges.
- Consider a stage 3: 1 agent applies the 11-ADC checklist to both reports' ads and counts what it catches. That measures the checklist on its own.
