# Gehna on Meta: what the copy is doing, and 12 ads to do better

Prepared 24 Sep 2026 for the owner of Gehna (gehnaindia.com). It covers the last 12 months of the Meta ad account (23 Sep 2025 to 24 Sep 2026), what the website promises, and a 4-week plan with 12 new ads for 3 audiences.

How to read the numbers. Currency is INR. ROAS is total purchase value divided by total spend for the period. CPA is spend divided by purchases. "12 months" is the whole window; "90 days" is 26 Jun to 23 Sep 2026. Where Meta gave no figure it is unavailable, never 0. Attribution is the ad set default. Age, gender and platform figures come from a separate breakdown and are not added to the totals.

## Part 4. What is missing or weak

Audience. Cold and warm people see the same words because every prospecting campaign stacks all audiences together. Men take 28% of spend at 1.53 and are never addressed as gift-givers. Past customers get nothing, although the average order is ₹30,000 to ₹35,000 and the site's reviews are full of repeat buyers.

Funnel and offer. Retargeting returns 4.5 to 6.5 on one text that still says 5,000+ customers. No cart copy answers why people leave: size, delivery time, money, trust. Sale copy returns 5 to 9 and runs 2 weeks a year; the rest of the year the ads carry no reason to buy now, while free shipping, COD, instalments, a free sizer, 7-day refunds and insured delivery sit in the FAQ.

Proof and product. 6 different customer and order numbers are in use. The home page has 30 reviews that say what a doubtful first-time buyer needs to hear, and none is in an ad. Rings and chains have the most designs (156 and 148) and the fewest sales, and the ring copy never mentions the free sizer or resize. The emerald, ruby, pearl and navratna edits that lead the home page are not in any ad. Descriptions are empty on every ad and headlines repeat "Gehna" or "Redesigning beauty" on ₹48 lakh.

## Part 5. The brief

### Who buys

An Indian woman, 25 to 54, best returns 35 to 54, buying for herself first and as a gift second. She wants real gold and real stones at a weight she can wear every day, and she is not sure it is safe to buy fine jewellery from a website. With a ₹35,000 average order, one doubt is enough to stop her. The second buyer is a gift-giver, often a man, who has never been spoken to.

### The question and the idea, per audience

| Audience | The question in her head | The one idea |
|---|---|---|
| New buyers | "Is this real gold and real stones, and what if it is wrong?" | Real gold, real stones, a real way out. Name the material, the hallmark and the 7-day refund, then show a product to start with. |
| Visitors and cart abandoners | "I liked it. Why did I not buy?" | Answer the one reason she stopped: delivery, size, money or trust, in the first line. |
| Past customers | "What next, and is it worth going back?" | Your next piece is easier than your first: complete the set, new arrivals in your size, gift the box you know, exchange the old piece. |

### Voice rules and claims

Warm, confident, short. Talk to one woman, one idea per line. Name the material. Open with a plain question, a real customer quote or a short rhythmic line, never the brand name. Stack proof at the end. Digits for numbers. No inflated words. 1 emoji at the end of a line, at most 4 in a caption, 1 in a headline. Concrete before-to-after: "wear it for 7 days, then decide", "pay 20% now", "order when it fits".

Claims used freely: the approved list. Claims used with `[confirm before launch]` because they are prices, counts or policy details to check on launch day: full refund within 7 days; free gift packing; insured transit; dispatch times; COD; instalments; free sizer and resize; lifetime exchange terms; lifetime repair; every design count and price. Never used: discounts, sale dates, "largest", "astrologically powerful", "100% handcrafted", "buyback", "IGI", the old customer numbers, and diamond or lab-grown claims on pieces that do not have them.

### Structure

3 new campaigns, Sales objective, optimised for purchases, with ad set budgets so every ad gets an even spend. One ad per ad set, so inside each campaign the words are the only variable. Nothing existing is edited, paused or rebudgeted.

### Controls

New-buyer ads are read beside the account's best live prospecting ads, in the earrings campaign of 25 June: Gifting (₹1,91,542, 43 purchases, ROAS 9.14 in 90 days), Trust overload (12.60) and Social proof (₹2,93,100, 30 purchases, 3.09); the campaign returned 5.16 at CPA ₹6,946. Visitor and cart ads are read beside the live cart catalogue ad "Did something catch your eye?" (₹2,69,597, 40 purchases, 4.46, CPA ₹6,740) and its engager copy (5.11). Past customers have no control; they are judged against the retargeting baseline and on whether they buy at all.

### Baselines and rules

From the last 90 days: account ROAS 3.77, CPA ₹9,326, CTR 4.03%; prospecting ROAS 3.63, CPA ₹9,983, CTR 4.09%; retargeting (cart, catalogue and engager campaigns together) ₹4,93,785 spend, 73 purchases, ₹22,58,272 value, ROAS 4.57, CPA ₹6,764. Among the 27 ads above the ₹30,000 floor, ROAS quartiles are 1.49, 3.09 and 5.49. Kill lines are 60% of the matching baseline, close to the bottom quarter of live ads. Scale lines are 120% and need at least 3 purchases, so a scale needs real orders, not one lucky ₹1 lakh sale.

| Audience | Learning spend before any ROAS call | Kill | Hold | Scale |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| New buyers | ₹30,000 per ad | ROAS under 2.18, or CTR under 2.45% | 2.18 to 4.36 | Over 4.36 with 3+ purchases and CPA under ₹7,986 |
| Visitors and carts | ₹20,000 per ad | ROAS under 2.74 | 2.74 to 5.49 | Over 5.49 with 3+ purchases and CPA under ₹5,411 |
| Past customers | ₹15,000 per ad | 0 purchases at ₹15,000, or ROAS under 2.26 at ₹20,000 | 2.26 to 4.52 | Over 4.52 with 3+ purchases |

Early stop for any ad: CTR under 2.0% after ₹10,000, half the account average; no top ad of the year sits below it. Scaling means raising that ad set by 20% every 3 days while it stays above the line, funded first from killed ads in the same campaign, never from the existing campaigns. If a campaign's ads are all killed, its budget goes back to the existing campaigns.

The 4 weeks run into the festive season (Navratri from mid October, Diwali on 8 Nov 2026), which lifts everything. So the final call is made on the gap between each new ad and its control over the same weeks. The baseline rules are the floor; the control comparison is the verdict.

### What is measured, and when

Every ad, every day, read on Mondays: spend, impressions, CTR, CPM, frequency, add-to-cart count and cost, purchases, purchase value, ROAS and CPA, on the same attribution as the controls. For past customers, orders matched to the customer list in the store back end, which is the only honest read for that campaign.

Week 1: audiences built and the customer list uploaded on the Friday before; every flagged item confirmed; the 3 campaigns launched on Monday. No ROAS calls; only the ₹10,000 CTR stop. Week 2: first kill and hold calls on ads past their learning spend; past customers switch to round 2 at the end of the week. Week 3: kill and scale calls; freed budget moves to the leader in the same campaign; one variant of each leading first line is written, ready but not launched. Week 4: final read, each audience's 4 ads ranked on ROAS with CPA and purchases, the control's same-week numbers in the same table, and a recommendation on which ads become permanent from week 5, which get a second round with a new picture, and which are retired.

### What the owner receives each week

A one-page sheet every Monday: the table for all 12 ads and the 5 control ads; the calls made, with the number that triggered each; spend against the ₹3,24,532 plan; any claim that needed changing; and 3 lines on what the first lines are teaching. At week 4, a short memo with the ranking, the ads to keep, the next 4 first lines, and the budget the winners get in month 2.

## Part 8. Decisions for the owner

1. Customer list. Can order emails and phone numbers for the last 365 days go into Meta as a custom audience? Without it, the past-customer campaign cannot run.
2. Policy claims. Is it acceptable to advertise COD, instalments, the free ring sizer, the free resize, insured transit and the lifetime exchange terms? All are on the site; none is on the approved list. RT-3 and PC-4 depend on this.
3. Named reviews. May the ads quote Google reviewers by name, or should they be initials or "verified buyer"?
4. Counts and prices. The design counts, the "under ₹20,000" and "under ₹30,000" limits and the ₹24,850 ring price are true on 24 Sep 2026. Who confirms them on launch day, and should the counts become "hundreds" to avoid weekly edits?
5. One customer number. The ads use 5,000+, 8,500, 10K+, 25,000+ orders and 30,000+ orders. This plan uses 10,000+ customers; the home page says 30,000+ orders. Pick one and use it everywhere.
6. Men. Should NB-2 run to men? They are 28% of spend at 1.53 with no gift copy. This ad is the first honest read on whether they pay back.
7. Age. Start new buyers at 25 or 18? Ages 18 to 24 returned 1.06 in the last 3 months. The plan says 25.
8. Pictures. The plan reuses existing videos, images and the catalogue. New stills for the ring and chain ads should be real product photos.
9. Pixel events. Does the site send a product category with page views, so RT-2 can reach ring viewers only?
10. Festive overlap. Start now, or after Diwali? A sale during the 4 weeks would swamp the read. Is a Diwali sale planned, and when?
11. Budget source. The 30% comes out of today's spend. If the owner prefers to add it on top, the total rises to about ₹14 lakh.
12. Exchange wording. Is "Exchange Any Time" acceptable with the policy linked, or should PC-4 say "limited lifetime exchange"?
13. Language. Should a first line be tried in Tamil or Hindi? Nothing in the account says either way.
