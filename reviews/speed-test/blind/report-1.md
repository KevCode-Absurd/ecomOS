# Gehna website speed report

Date: 24 September 2026. Site: www.gehnaindia.com. Prepared for the Gehna owner, to hand to the developer.

## 1. Verdict

Your site is slow on the phones most of your shoppers use, and the reason is not your server or your product photos. Both of those are good. The problem is how the pages are built. Two things cause most of the delay. First, every page downloads 2 huge font files, 897 KB and 956 KB, before anything else. On a slow 4G phone those 2 files alone take 13 to 19 seconds and everything else waits behind them. Second, product pages and category pages are drawn by JavaScript in the phone, not sent ready-made from the server, and that JavaScript only starts running once the fonts have finished. So on a slow 4G phone a shopper sees the home page banner after 7.9 seconds, the rings grid after about 15 seconds, and the main product image after 18.3 seconds. Google's "good" line is 2.5 seconds. The fix for the fonts is about 1 developer day and should cut the home page to about 3 seconds and the product page to about 9 seconds. Sending the product image, name and price from the server is a bigger job, 3 to 10 developer days, and it is the only way to get product pages to about 2 to 2.5 seconds. All 10 fixes together are 12 to 25 developer days.

## 2. What was measured, how, and the numbers

How: a test browser set up like a mid-range Android phone (Pixel 5 size, 393 x 851) on a slow 4G connection (1.6 Mbps download, 150 ms delay) with a 4x slower processor, starting with an empty cache each time. This is close to what Google's own mobile test does. Each page was loaded 3 times and the middle value is reported. Google's PageSpeed test itself could not be run on the day (its free daily limit was used up), so there is no score out of 100 yet. One extra run of the home page was made on a desktop with a fast connection. The test machine was in the USA; Indian shoppers will get a slightly faster first response from the server, but everything after that (file sizes, order of loading, JavaScript work) is the same for them.

Terms used below, each in one line:
- LCP (largest contentful paint): when the biggest thing on the screen appears. Google's good line is 2.5 s.
- CLS (layout shift): how much the page jumps around while loading. Good is 0.1 or less.
- TTFB (server response): how long the server takes to start answering. Good is under 0.8 s.
- Blocking time: how long the phone's processor is busy running JavaScript and cannot react to taps. Lower is better; over about 0.6 s is flagged.

| Page | Server response | Biggest element appears (LCP) | What that element was | Layout shift (CLS) | Blocking time | Page fully loaded | Files | Data downloaded |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | 0.43 s | 7.9 s | Banner image (PNG, 279 KB) | 0.000 | 4.0 s | 19.3 s | 113 | 4.04 MB |
| Rings category | 0.13 s | 6.6 s (the loading spinner); the grid itself at about 15 s | Spinner, then the grid of 156 rings | 0.110 | 2.6 s | 13.4 s | 93 | 2.86 MB |
| Product (emerald ring) | 0.35 s | 18.3 s | Main product image (AVIF, 25 KB) | 0.129 | 3.0 s | 14.3 s | 90 | 2.88 MB |

Against Google's lines: LCP is "poor" (over 4 s) on all 3 pages. Layout shift is over the line on the category and product pages. Server response is good on all 3. The 3 runs of each page were within 0.3 s of each other on LCP, so these are not one-off results.

Desktop, fast connection, home page: LCP 5.3 s, layout shift 0.004, 3.28 MB. Even on a fast line the banner takes over 5 s, because the 2 big fonts are fetched first and the banner image had to be resized by the server on that visit.

What the slow phone shows in words: on the home page, a white screen for about 1 s, then the header and search box, then 7 empty grey circles for the categories that fill in over several seconds, then the banner at about 8 s. Nothing jumps. On the rings page, the header in under 1 s, then a spinning loader for about 14 s, then all 156 rings appear at once and push the page down. On the product page, the header in under 1 s, then a spinner, then the image, name, price and Add to Cart all appear together at about 17 s. A shopper who taps an ad and lands on a product page sees nothing to buy for 17 s on slow 4G.

## 3. How the site is built and where the time goes

The site is a custom-built web app (Next.js and React with Material UI), not Shopify. Pages are served through Cloudflare and Amazon CloudFront, which cache them close to the shopper. Product data comes from your own API. Search and filters come from Tagalys. Payments are Razorpay. Marketing tags are Google Tag Manager, Google Analytics, the Meta pixel, Klaviyo, Microsoft Clarity, CustomerLabs and Google Sign-In.

Where the 19 seconds go on the home page, on the slow 4G phone:

| Time | What is happening |
|---|---|
| 0 to 0.5 s | Server answers. Fast. |
| 0.6 s to 19.2 s | 4 font files download. 2 of them are 897 KB and 956 KB. They are marked "download first", so they hog the connection the whole time. |
| 0.6 s to 8.0 s | The banner image (279 KB) downloads slowly because it shares the connection with the fonts. It appears at 8.0 s. |
| 1.2 s to 16.3 s | The 7 small category circles download. They are 60 pixels wide on screen but the files are 320 pixels wide and 123 to 234 KB each, about 805 KB in total. |
| 1.9 s to 10.1 s | 30 JavaScript files, 443 KB, download. |
| 19.2 s | The fonts finish, the page counts as "loaded", and only now does the JavaScript start running. 25 bursts of heavy work follow, the longest 0.9 s. |
| 20.8 s | The page asks the API for product carousel data. |
| 20.7 s onwards | Marketing tags (Meta, Tag Manager, Klaviyo, Clarity) run. This part is fine: they wait until after load, as they should. |

The product page follows the same shape. Fonts until 14.2 s. JavaScript files arrive by 9.0 s but do not run until the page is "loaded" at 14.3 s. Then 2.3 s of heavy processing, then 6 requests to the API, then the product image is requested at 16.4 s and shows at 18.3 s. The image itself is tiny (25 KB) and arrives 1.5 s after it is asked for. The image is not slow. The page simply does not ask for it until very late, because the product content is not in the HTML the server sends.

What is already good and should not be touched: server response under 0.5 s; static files cached for a year; product photos in the modern AVIF format at sensible sizes (10 to 30 KB each); marketing tags held until after load; no pop-ups; text shows in a fallback font while the real font loads; the layout fits the phone screen.

Data split on the home page: fonts 48%, images 34%, JavaScript 15%, everything else 3%.

## 4. The problems found, in depth

**Problem 1. Two 900 KB font files on every page.** The headline font (Nanum Myeongjo, the serif face used for lines like "Good Things, No Waiting") is shipped as the full font in an old format, including thousands of Korean characters the site never uses. 2 weights, 897 KB and 956 KB. The body font (Lato) is fine at 35 KB per weight. All 4 are told to download before anything else. Fonts are 1.9 MB of a 4.0 MB home page. On the slow 4G phone they take the connection from 0.3 s to between 13 s and 19 s depending on the page. Trimmed to the letters, digits and symbols the site uses (including the rupee sign) and converted to the modern WOFF2 format, the same 4 files should be under 150 KB in total, a cut of about 1.77 MB on every page view.

**Problem 2. Product and category content is drawn in the phone, not sent by the server.** The category page HTML contains only the logo. The product page HTML contains no product name, no price and no product image; the data is there in a hidden block but the phone has to run all the JavaScript to turn it into a page. On slow phones that is what pushes the product image to 18 s. It also means Google's crawler first sees an empty page.

**Problem 3. The JavaScript waits for the fonts.** Measured on all 3 pages: the JavaScript files finish downloading 4 to 9 s before they start running, and they start within a tenth of a second of the fonts finishing. Cloudflare's "Rocket Loader" setting is on, and it is the usual cause of scripts being held until the page is "loaded". Because the fonts hold the page open, everything the shopper wants waits for the fonts twice: once for the connection and once for this. Fixing the fonts fixes most of it; turning Rocket Loader off is a 1-hour test.

**Problem 4. Home page images.** The mobile banner is served as a 279 KB PNG while the desktop banner of the same section is served as a 78 KB AVIF; the image resizer seems to skip files that are narrower than the size it is asked for (the developer should confirm). The 7 category circles are 320 pixel files for 60 pixel circles, 805 KB in total, and they are marked "load lazily" even though they are on screen. And 41 images on the page are marked "high priority", of which only 1 (the banner) is on screen; the other 40 are product tiles 1,000 to 2,800 pixels down the page, competing with the banner for the connection.

**Problem 5. The page jumps on category and product pages.** On the rings page, when the grid replaces the spinner at about 15 s, the page jumps (0.059 of the 0.110 total). On the product page, the "Two Decades of Expertise, Glowing Reviews" band appears late above the product and pushes the image, price and Add to Cart down (0.080 of the 0.129 total). Same values in all 3 runs, so it is a structural issue, not random.

**Problem 6. Too much JavaScript work.** The phone's processor is busy for 3 to 4 s per page on the slow profile, in bursts of up to 1.3 s during which taps do nothing. The home page has 3,128 elements and 152 image tags, mostly in carousels that are all built at once. The shared code file loaded by every page is 145 KB.

**Problem 7. Cache settings.** The server-resized images (banners, icons, GIFs) are cached for only 30 minutes at the CDN, and even product photos with a 1-day setting were often not in the CDN cache during the test. When a resized image is not cached, the server has to resize it again: the desktop banner took 4.2 s to arrive for that reason. Separately, product and category pages are marked "private, do not cache" by the app, but Cloudflare is caching them anyway for up to an hour. That needs a decision either way.

**Problem 8. Small leftovers.** A Universal Analytics tag still loads on every page (Google stopped processing that data in 2023 and 2024). The CustomerLabs script returned "403 forbidden" in the test. Google's tag loads more than once. The loading spinner is a 26 KB animated GIF on every category and product page, and a 44 to 53 KB GIF logo loads on product pages. The product page has a video set to autoplay and preload. Unknown web addresses get a "temporary redirect" to a 404 page instead of a proper "not found" answer, which also skips the cache. And /cart goes to the 404 page.

Third-party tags are not the problem here: 8 to 9 outside services per page, about 110 KB, all after load. Do not spend time on them first.

## 5. The top 10 fixes, ranked

Estimated gains are from arithmetic on the measurements (bytes divided by the measured connection speed of about 185 KB per second on the test profile, plus the measured processing and API waits). They are estimates for the same slow 4G test, not promises. Every fix must be proven by running the same 3-load test before and after.

**Fix 1. Trim and convert the fonts.** Gain: home page LCP from 7.9 s to about 3.2 s; product page from 18.3 s to about 9.4 s; category grid from about 15 s to about 9 s; 1.77 MB less on every page. Basis: 1,771 KB saved divided by 185 KB per second frees 9.6 s of download on every page; the banner then only shares the connection with about 200 KB of other files. Effort: 4 to 8 hours. Owner: developer. Steps: (1) find where the fonts are declared in the code (the `next/font` setup with the .ttf files); (2) switch to Google's font loader with the `latin` subset for both Lato and Nanum Myeongjo, weights 400 and 700, or, if the build server has no internet, make Latin-only WOFF2 files with the `fonttools` subsetter and point the local font setup at them; make sure the rupee sign is in the subset; (3) check whether the bold serif is used anywhere; if not, drop that file; (4) deploy and check that the page's font links are all .woff2 and total under 150 KB; (5) run the 3-load test; pass if home LCP is under 4 s and product LCP under 11 s; (6) check on a real phone that headlines and prices still show in the serif face with the rupee sign in the same face. Risk: a missing character shows in the fallback font; add it to the subset. Rollback: revert the font setup.

**Fix 2. Send the product image, name, price and Add to Cart from the server.** Gain: product LCP from about 9.4 s (after fix 1) to about 2.0 to 2.5 s; from today, about 16 s. Basis: with the image in the HTML, its request starts right after the page arrives; HTML 25 KB + fonts 150 KB + styles 80 KB + image 25 KB = 280 KB at 185 KB per second is 1.5 s, plus 0.35 s server response and a little drawing time. Effort: 24 to 80 hours depending on the code. Owner: developer. Steps: (1) confirm the gap: fetching the product page source today shows no `<h1>`; (2) find why the server skips the content: look for components loaded with "no server rendering", data queries with server rendering turned off, or a "wait until mounted" flag that shows the spinner; (3) the product data is already fetched on the server (it is in the page's hidden data block), so pass it as a prop and render the buy box from it on both server and phone; (4) render the first product image as a plain image with `priority` (this adds a preload and high priority), outside the client-only carousel; other slides stay lazy; (5) render the name, price and Add to Cart on the server too, with a fixed price format so server and phone produce the same text; (6) keep reviews, related products and widgets client-side and below the buy box, with fixed-height containers (fix 5); (7) put it behind a feature flag; (8) verify: the page source now contains the main image with high priority, the `<h1>` and the price; 3-load test on 3 different products, pass if the LCP element is the product image and under 3 s with layout shift under 0.05; test variant switch, quantity, Add to Cart, cart drawer and checkout start on a real phone; check that view item, add to cart and a test purchase each fire once in analytics, with cookie consent accepted and refused. Risk: server and phone drawing different HTML causes warnings and a flash; fix by keeping the two identical. Rollback: turn the flag off. Depends on: fix 1 and fix 8 (the image must come from the CDN cache, or a cold resize adds 1.5 to 4 s).

**Fix 3. Send the first category tiles from the server.** Gain: rings page LCP from a spinner at 6.6 s (real grid at 15 s) to about 1.6 to 2.5 s with a real product tile as the biggest element. Basis: 4 tiles of 13 KB plus HTML, fonts and styles is about 247 KB at 185 KB per second, 1.3 s, plus server response. Effort: 16 to 40 hours. Owner: developer. Steps: (1) in the category page's server function, fetch page 1 of the listing (12 to 16 products, default sort, no filters) from Tagalys or your API; (2) render the tiles on the server with proper sizes, high priority on the first 4 only, lazy on the rest; (3) hand the page-1 data to the client list so it does not fetch it again; filters, sort and paging stay as they are; (4) skeleton cards (fix 5) for positions not yet loaded; (5) decide the caching rule for category pages with fix 8, since prices are now in the HTML; (6) verify: page source shows 12 or more product links with images; 3-load test on 3 categories, pass if LCP is a product tile under 3 s and layout shift under 0.05; filters and the "(156 found)" count still match. Risk: page 1 order can differ from Tagalys after a re-index; use Tagalys on the server if that matters. Rollback: flag. Depends on: fixes 1 and 5.

**Fix 4. Home page images: banner as AVIF, small icons, one high-priority image.** Gain: home LCP from about 3.2 s (after fix 1) to about 2.2 s; about 0.9 MB less above the fold. Basis: icons 805 KB to about 70 KB saves 735 KB, which is 4.0 s of download that today overlaps the banner; banner 279 KB to about 100 KB saves 1.0 s. Effort: 4 to 6 hours. Owner: developer, with the designer for the banner export. Steps: (1) re-export the mobile banner as a JPEG 1,200 pixels wide at quality 75 to 80 (wide enough that the resizer converts it to AVIF like the desktop one) and check the served file is `image/avif` and under 100 KB; (2) give the banner `priority` and `sizes="100vw"` and confirm the page head now has a preload link for it; (3) for the 7 category circles set `sizes="60px"` so the resizer picks a 128 or 256 pixel version (AVIF, under 10 KB each) and mark them `eager` since they are on screen; (4) in the product tile component remove `priority` unless the tile is above the fold (none on home; the first 4 on category pages); (5) verify: the page source has exactly 1 `fetchpriority="high"` image; carousel tiles do not download until you scroll; 3-load test, pass if home LCP under 3 s and above-fold images under 250 KB in total. Risk: icons could look soft on tablets; check that breakpoint. Rollback: revert the props and files.

**Fix 5. Reserve space for late content.** Gain: layout shift on the rings page from 0.110 to about 0.05 and on the product page from 0.129 to about 0.05; near 0 once fixes 2 and 3 land. Basis: the two body-level jumps measured are 0.059 and 0.080; removing them leaves 0.051 and 0.049. Effort: 3 to 4 hours. Owner: developer. Steps: (1) measure the height of the reviews band on the product page and the filter and sort bar on the category page at 393, 360 and 414 pixel widths; (2) give the reviews band a minimum height equal to its rendered height so its space exists before it appears (better: render its fixed text on the server in fix 2); (3) replace the centred spinner on category pages with a skeleton grid of 8 grey cards at the real tile size (167 pixels wide, rows 277 pixels apart) plus a skeleton bar for filters; (4) give the product gallery box a fixed aspect ratio so the spinner and the image occupy the same space; (5) verify: 3-load test, pass if layout shift is under 0.05 and no shift is at body level; also check desktop. Risk: a reserved height leaves a gap if the band is sometimes shorter. Rollback: remove the rule.

**Fix 6. Turn off Cloudflare Rocket Loader and stop data fetches waiting for "page loaded".** Gain: on its own, product LCP from 18.3 s to about 14 s (JavaScript would run when it arrives at 9 s instead of at 14.3 s); after fix 1 the extra gain is small, but it removes a setting that reorders your app's own scripts and can break page hydration. This is a test, not a promise. Effort: 1 to 3 hours. Owner: whoever holds the Cloudflare login, plus the developer. Steps: (1) Cloudflare dashboard, Speed, Optimization, Content Optimization, Rocket Loader: off; (2) wait 2 minutes, run the 3-load test on the product and home pages and compare when the first heavy JavaScript work starts against the "loaded" time; (3) if scripts still wait for "loaded", search the code for load-event listeners or "ready" flags that gate the product and listing fetches and move them to run on mount; keep the marketing tags after load as they are; (4) click through the whole site and check each analytics event fires once. Rollback: turn it back on.

**Fix 7. JavaScript diet.** Gain: blocking time from 2.6 to 4.0 s toward under 1 s; no measured seconds figure yet, the target comes from Google's lines. Effort: 24 to 40 hours over 2 to 3 weeks, in small measured steps. Owner: developer. Steps: (1) run the bundle analyser and list what is in the 145 KB shared file (expect Material UI, Apollo, Swiper, tracking wrappers, a video player); (2) import Material UI components by path so unused ones are dropped; (3) move Swiper and the video player out of the shared file into the pages that use them; replace the video player library with a plain video tag; (4) on the home page, build each below-fold carousel only when it scrolls into view, with 6 to 8 tiles on mobile, aiming for under 1,500 elements and under 40 image tags at load; (5) turn off link prefetching inside grids and carousels (keep it on the main menu); (6) on the product page merge the 6 API calls into 1 or 2 and ask only for the fields shown; (7) after each step: 3-load test, record blocking time and the longest task; test carousels, variants, filters, Add to Cart, cart and checkout. Risk: moving content out of the server HTML by mistake; keep server rendering on for anything with product content. Rollback: one commit at a time.

**Fix 8. Cache rules.** Gain: removes 1.5 to 4.2 s waits when an image is not in the CDN cache (measured on misses); required for fix 2's estimate; a decision on page caching that is currently accidental. Effort: 3 to 4 hours. Owner: developer and the Cloudflare or AWS admin. Steps: (1) in the Next.js config set the minimum image cache time to 30 days; the image addresses already include the file and width, so this is safe; (2) in CloudFront, make sure the image behaviour caches on the full query string and the Accept header, with a 30-day default; (3) confirm in Cloudflare that a second request for the same image is a cache hit; (4) decide the page rule: either let the app mark product and category pages cacheable for 5 minutes at the edge with an automatic purge when a price or stock changes, or keep them private and remove the Cloudflare rule that caches them anyway; do not leave the current mix; (5) make /cart go to the cart or a proper page, and make unknown addresses return a real 404; (6) verify: second request for a product image shows a CloudFront hit; a price change appears on the product and category page within 5 minutes; test cart and checkout on a fresh device. Risk: a stale price if the purge is not wired; test the purge first. Rollback: revert the config.

**Fix 9. Clean-ups.** Gain: 100 to 190 KB and 4 to 6 requests less per page view; correct 404s; no GIFs in the load path. Effort: 6 to 8 hours. Owner: developer; owner for the analytics and CustomerLabs decisions. Steps: (1) confirm in GA4 that view item, add to cart, begin checkout and purchase are all tracked, then remove the Universal Analytics tag and its old scripts; (2) keep 1 Google tag loader (Tag Manager) and check each event fires once with consent accepted and refused; (3) decide whether CustomerLabs is still in use; if not, remove it; (4) replace the GIF spinner with the skeleton or a CSS spinner; (5) replace the GIF logo on product pages with an SVG or a small AVIF; (6) set the product video to not preload, not autoplay on mobile, with a poster image and a play button; (7) return HTTP 404 for unknown addresses and add links to the top 2 categories on the 404 page; (8) remove the unused icon font declaration. Do one item per release and re-test. Risk: losing a conversion event if the old tag was its only carrier; step 1 checks that. Rollback: Tag Manager version history.

**Fix 10. Monitoring and a speed budget.** Gain: protects everything above. Effort: 6 to 8 hours to set up, then about 1 hour a month. Owner: developer sets up the check; the audit team runs the monthly test. Steps: (1) add Lighthouse CI to the deploy pipeline on the 3 reference pages with limits: fonts under 150 KB, total under 1.5 MB, LCP warn at 2.5 s and fail at 4 s, layout shift under 0.1, at most 10 outside services; a pull request that adds a 900 KB font must fail; (2) run the same 3-load phone test monthly and before any sale, and keep the results; (3) run Google's PageSpeed test weekly once its quota allows, and read its real-user data 28 days after each release; (4) house rule: any new tag, font, banner or carousel gets a before-and-after test before it goes live; no site changes in the 2 weeks before a sale.

Budget summary (per page, mobile, first visit): total under 1.5 MB; fonts under 150 KB; the biggest image under 100 KB; 1 high-priority image; LCP under 2.5 s; layout shift under 0.05; blocking time under 0.6 s; no task over 0.2 s; JavaScript under 350 KB; at most 10 outside services; under 1,500 elements; no GIFs; server response under 0.8 s; main image, name, price and the first 12 tiles present in the server HTML.

## 6. Sequencing and the expected result after each week

One change per release, with the 3-load test between releases, so each gain can be credited to its change.

**Week 1: weight and priority.** Day 1: record Google's PageSpeed baseline when its quota allows, and run the Rocket Loader test (fix 6). Days 1 to 2: fonts (fix 1). Day 3: image cache setting (fix 8, image part). Days 3 to 4: banner, icons and priority clean-up (fix 4). Day 5: reserved space and skeletons (fix 5). Product and category pages are still far from 2.5 s; that is weeks 2 and 3.

**Week 2: the product page.** Fix 2 (server-rendered buy box) behind a flag, plus the non-tracking clean-ups from fix 9 as separate commits, plus the CI speed check from fix 10. ## 7. The business case

Your own numbers, last 30 days (25 August to 23 September 2026, website orders): sales ₹96,83,409, 285 orders, average order ₹33,977, 6,34,288 visits, 12,053 add-to-carts. That is ₹3.23 lakh a day, or ₹11.78 crore a year at this rate with no seasonality. Conversion is 285 orders per 6,34,288 visits, 0.045%, although the "visits" figure needs confirming (sessions, users or page views changes this a lot).

What a faster site could be worth, as scenarios, not forecasts. The published studies say a 1 second gain is worth about 5 to 7% more conversions; your product page is losing about 15 seconds, and those per-second figures cannot simply be multiplied by 15, so flat scenarios are used:

| If conversion rises by | Extra sales per 30 days | Extra sales per year |
|---|---|---|
| 3% (cautious) | ₹2.9 lakh | ₹35.3 lakh |
| 5.6% (one industry figure per second saved) | ₹5.4 lakh | ₹66.0 lakh |
| 7% (another industry figure per second) | ₹6.8 lakh | ₹82.5 lakh |
| 10% (an assumption for a multi-second gain) | ₹9.7 lakh | ₹1.18 crore |

Cost: 91 to 201 developer hours across the 10 fixes, about 12 to 25 developer days. Put your developer's day rate in; even the cautious 3% scenario (₹2.9 lakh per month) covers 25 developer days within a few months at typical rates.

Two outside reference points, both third-party: one study found a site that loads in 1 second converts about 2.5 times as well as one that loads in 5 seconds; Google reports that 53% of mobile visitors leave a page that takes over 3 seconds. Your product page shows nothing to buy for 17 seconds on slow 4G.

Ad spend: every paid click that lands on a product page and leaves before the image appears is money spent on a product nobody saw. To size this you need your monthly ad spend, the share of paid sessions landing on product pages and their bounce rate. Those were not available for this report.

The only number that will count is your own before and after: the same pages, the same test, and conversion by device for the 28 days after each release.

## 8. What could not be verified

- Google's PageSpeed score and its real-user data (LCP, layout shift, interaction delay) for the 3 pages: the free quota was used up on the day. Record them before the first change ships.
- The phone measurements come from one test browser on one profile. A second tool and a real mid-range Android on mobile data should confirm them.
- Interaction delay (how fast Add to Cart and filters respond for real users) needs real-user data; the lab stand-in is blocking time.
- Why the mobile banner is served as PNG while the desktop one becomes AVIF: the working theory is that the resizer skips files narrower than the requested size; the developer should confirm in the code.
- Whether the JavaScript waits for "page loaded" because of Rocket Loader or because of the app's own code: fix 6 is the 1-hour test.
- Whether the Universal Analytics tag and CustomerLabs are still wanted, and what is inside the Tag Manager container.
- Whether Cloudflare caching pages marked "private" is intended, and how CloudFront caches resized images by query string.
- Cart, checkout, variant switching and Add to Cart speed: not tested, because the audit was read-only and made no test purchase.
- Whether /cart is meant to land on the 404 page.
- Mobile share of visits and revenue, conversion by device, bounce rate, top landing pages, ad spend, developer day rate and profit margin: needed for a sharper business case.
- Cache hit rates in the Cloudflare and CloudFront dashboards.
- The product page video: seen in the test data below the fold with autoplay and preload set; check it on a real phone.
- All gains in section 5 are model estimates from the measured file sizes and timings. Each must be proven with the same 3-load test before and after, and then with Google's real-user data 28 days later.
