# Founder interview: 45 minutes to fill the brand brain

Drafted 29 Sep 2026 for the first brand onboarding. Any market, any category. The questions only ask for what the founder alone knows. Anything on the site, in the ads or in the connected accounts is pulled before the call and never asked.

## Before the call

We do this, the founder does not.

- Crawl the site: products, prices, offers, policies, claims, voice, visuals, stack.
- Pull the ads: Meta Ad Library by page id, live angles, formats, hooks.
- Read the connected accounts: revenue by country, channel and product; AOV; repeat rate; returns; ad spend and results; email flows; top search terms.
- Rank competitors from the site, the ads and public threads.

Two days before the call the founder fills a written intake, 5 minutes:

| Field | Why |
|---|---|
| Brand name, site, year started | identity |
| Industry and category, in their words | market research pulls category dynamics from this |
| Countries sold in today, which one is home | brain splits every market-specific field by country |
| Currencies, marketplaces, wholesale or offline, yes or no | tells us which revenue we cannot see |
| Accounts connected: store, ads, analytics, email, support | tells us which questions are already answered |
| Who will be on the call, and their role | owners |

## Rules for the call

- Record with consent, transcribe, extract to founder records (tier approved). Anything unanswered becomes an open question in the brain.
- Numbers as ranges are fine. "Roughly 40 to 45 percent" beats "I would have to check".
- Never ask what the site, the ads or the data already say. If the founder starts answering one of those, thank them and move on.
- 25 core questions plus two closing questions in 45 minutes. 3 reserve questions if time remains or the category needs them. 10 written items after the call.
- One follow-up per question at most. The follow-up is written under each question.

## The 25 core questions, and two to close

### A. Where you want to go (5 minutes)

**1. In 12 months, what has to be true for you to say this worked?** One number and one thing that is not a number.
Feeds: decisions, diagnosis. Why ask: intent is not in any account.
Follow-up: which of those two matters more if you had to pick?

**2. What breaks first if sales doubled next month?** Cash, stock, people, delivery, support, or something else.
Feeds: operations, economics, diagnosis. Why ask: the constraint decides which growth ideas are allowed.
Follow-up: what is the one thing you would need in place before you would want that growth?

**3. What is off the table, even if it would sell?** Discount depth, channels, claims, marketplaces, influencer types, product categories. These become the red lines every proposal is checked against.
Feeds: policies, decisions, red lines. Why ask: the site shows what you do, not what you refuse.
Follow-up: has any of those ever been tested and regretted?

### B. The numbers nobody can pull (8 minutes)

**4. For your top 3 products, what does one unit cost you landed?** Product, freight, duty, packaging. Rough is fine.
Feeds: economics, products. Why ask: the store has the price, not the cost. Every margin number in the system depends on this.
Follow-up: which product carries the best margin, and which the worst?

**5. What is your floor, and what is your cap?** The lowest contribution per order you can live with, the most you would pay for a new customer, and the most you would spend per month per channel without a call to you. If you have none of these, what do you look at today to decide whether ads are working?
Feeds: economics, decisions. Why ask: ad results without a floor cannot be judged.
Follow-up: is that floor different for a first order and a repeat order?

**6. What revenue can we not see?** Wholesale, offline, marketplaces, WhatsApp orders, cash on delivery, distributors. Share of total, and where the records live.
Feeds: channels, marketplaces, economics, assumptions. Why ask: the store data is only part of the business; the share tells us how much.
Follow-up: does any of it show up inside the store data as bulk or test orders?

**7. What does a return or a failed delivery really cost you?** Shipping both ways, restocking, write-offs. Which products or regions are worst.
Feeds: economics, operations, objections. Why ask: the data counts returns, not their cost or cause.
Follow-up: what share of returns are the customer's size or expectation, and what share are your fault?

### C. Markets and category (4 minutes)

**8. Countries: where do you want to grow next, and where would you refuse to sell?** We already have where revenue comes from.
Feeds: identity (markets), decisions. Why ask: the data shows the past; this is the plan.
Follow-up: what stopped you from growing there so far?

**9. What is the one thing about your category an outsider gets wrong?** Seasonality, regulation, how often people buy, gifting, who pays and who uses.
Feeds: industry record, market research inputs. Why ask: category dynamics drive the market research screen; the founder's version is the seed.
Follow-up: what rule of thumb do you use that a new hire always doubts at first?

### D. Competitors and where you lose (4 minutes)

**10. Name the 3 to 5 brands you actually lose sales to.** Not the ones you admire. For each, why does the customer pick them?
Feeds: competitors, objections. Why ask: public lists show who advertises, not who wins the sale.
Follow-up: which one worries you most 12 months out?

**11. What do they do that you refuse to copy, and where exactly do you win against them?** The moment a customer chooses you over them, in your words.
Feeds: competitors, positioning, red lines. Why ask: only the founder can say which competitor moves are forbidden here, and the site says what you claim, not what actually closes.
Follow-up: what do they do that you wish you had started earlier?

### E. What customers tell you that never gets written down (7 minutes)

**12. Describe your best customer as a person you have talked to.** Who they are, why they buy, what they say when they reorder.
Feeds: avatars. Why ask: reviews are public and skew to extremes; conversations are private and specific.
Follow-up: what does that person read, watch or follow?

**13. The three doubts you hear most on calls, WhatsApp, DMs and support, in the customer's words.** Which one costs the most sales?
Feeds: objections, objection ladders, language bank. Why ask: support tickets may be connected later; the founder's ranking is the priority order now.
Follow-up: what do you say back, and does it work?

**14. Why do customers leave or not reorder?** What have they told you directly?
Feeds: objections, learnings, retention. Why ask: the data shows who did not return, not why.
Follow-up: which of those reasons could you fix in 30 days?

**15. Words: what do customers call the product and the problem, and which of your own words do they never use?**
Feeds: language bank, voice, glossary. Why ask: the site uses the brand's words; ads need the customer's.
Follow-up: is there a word you are tired of, or one you are proud of?

### F. Proof, rules and voice (6 minutes)

**16. What can you prove?** Certifications, lab tests, awards, patents, founder credentials, factory facts. Where is the evidence file?
Feeds: claims (approved tier), proofs. Why ask: the site may claim it; the brain needs the evidence to let agents use it.
Follow-up: is there a proof you have and have never used?

**17. What must never be said?** Legal or regulatory limits, promises you cannot keep, claims you know are shaky, words and visuals that are off-brand.
Feeds: red lines, legal, claims (forbidden). Why ask: no source shows a rule that has never been broken.
Follow-up: has a regulator, platform or lawyer ever pushed back on a claim?

**18. The making of the product: which choices cost you money and you kept anyway?** Materials, supplier, process, price point. Why?
Feeds: story bank, positioning, claims. Why ask: this is the story behind the specs, and it is nowhere public unless the founder tells it.
Follow-up: which of those choices would a customer pay more for if they knew?

**19. How should we sound, and how should we never sound?** Three words for the voice, one brand you would hate to be mistaken for, and whether you want to be the face of the brand and for how many hours a week.
Feeds: voice, founder on camera, creative rules. Why ask: the site shows one register; ads, email and support each need the founder's line on tone, and nobody else can say whether the founder appears.
Follow-up: name one piece of your own marketing you are proud of and one you regret.
### G. History: what you tried (5 minutes)

**20. Top 3 things that worked and then stopped, or could not scale.** Why did they stop?
Feeds: learnings, experiments memory. Why ask: the ad account shows spend, not why a winner was turned off.
Follow-up: what would it take to bring the best one back?

**21. Top 3 things that failed.** Offers, channels, agencies, launches, creatives. What did you learn, and what must never be repeated?
Feeds: learnings, decisions, red lines. Why ask: failures are the cheapest lessons in the brain, and they live only in the founder's memory.
Follow-up: was the failure the idea or the execution?

**22. Which of your numbers do you not trust, and why?** Tracking gaps, attribution, bulk orders mixed in, test orders, offline sales, a broken pixel.
Feeds: assumptions, measurement caveats. Why ask: this decides which parts of the connected data the system may lean on.
Follow-up: when did each problem start?

### H. Operating reality (5 minutes)

**23. Stock and supply: reorder lead time, minimum order, which products cannot scale, which are going away, what launches in the next 90 days.**
Feeds: operations, products, calendar. Why ask: inventory levels are in the store; lead times, MOQs and plans are not.
Follow-up: which product would you push hardest if stock were no issue?

**24. Who does what, and what may an agent do without asking?** Team, agencies, freelancers. Who approves creative, offers and spend. How fast can you ship a landing page or a new ad. Which of those jobs an agent may do alone, which it drafts for your approval, which stay with a person.
Feeds: team, owners, approvals. Why ask: every action the system proposes needs an owner and a realistic speed.
Follow-up: which of those would you hand to an agent tomorrow if it did the work well?

**25. Your calendar: the peaks and dead zones that matter for you.** Diwali, Rakhi, wedding season, monsoon, back to school, Black Friday, Ramadan, Lunar New Year, or whatever fits the markets. When do you start preparing for each?
Feeds: calendar, per-country peaks. Why ask: last year's sales show when you sold, not when you plan or when you missed a peak.
Follow-up: which peak did you under-prepare for last year?

### Close (1 minute)

**26. If we could only fix one thing in the next 30 days, what would you pick?**
Feeds: diagnosis, first action. Why ask: this is the founder's own ranking, before the system ranks anything.

**27. What should I have asked?**
Feeds: open questions.

## Reserve questions, if time remains

**R1. What is the substitute?** When someone buys neither you nor a competitor, what do they do instead? Feeds: category frames, positioning.

**R2. What do customers use the product for that you did not design it for?** Any surprising segment? Feeds: avatars, product use cases.

**R3. What changes from one market to the next?** Price, sizes, product mix, language, delivery expectations, festivals and peaks. Feeds: market fields per country, calendar.

## Written after the call, 30 minutes at the founder's desk

Better written than spoken. Sent the same day, due within a week. Each fills a shelf directly.

| # | What we ask for | Fills |
|---|---|---|
| W1 | Discount rules: deepest discount allowed, products never discounted, when discounts are allowed, per market. The logic behind the free shipping threshold. | offers |
| W2 | Up to 20 pieces of your own marketing you would ship again as is, and up to 20 you rejected, each with one line on why. Ads, emails, pages, posts, support replies. | voice |
| W3 | The proof file for every claim you make: certificates, lab tests, awards, patents, founder credentials, factory facts. | claims |
| W4 | Rights: which customer photos, videos and quotes you have permission to use, and where that permission is recorded. | creators, assets |
| W5 | Every agency, creator, affiliate and freelancer paid in the last 2 years: what it cost, what they made, use again or not. | creators, history |
| W6 | Supplier payment terms, marketplace and gateway payout days, and the month you are always tight. | finance |
| W7 | Support macros, the FAQ document, and the return and refund process as it really runs. | operations |
| W8 | Brand kit: logos, colours, fonts, templates, photo style, and examples of what is off-brand. | content and brand kit |
| W9 | Rank the value propositions your site makes today, best to weakest, and strike any you no longer believe. | messaging |
| W10 | Access: the accounts to connect and who administers each. | trust and access |

## Do not ask (already pulled)

- What you sell, prices, pack sizes, offers, shipping and return policy, sizes, materials as listed. Site.
- Your current ad angles, formats, hooks, how many ads are live, how long they run. Ad Library.
- Revenue by country, channel and product; AOV; repeat rate; return rate; ad spend and results; email flow performance; top search terms; traffic sources. Connected accounts.
- Your stack and apps. Site and stack detector.
- Press coverage, funding, open roles. Public search.

## After the call

- Transcript to founder records within 24 hours, each with the question number as its source and tier approved.
- Every "I would have to check" becomes an open question with an owner and a date.
- The founder gets a one-page readback to confirm before any screen uses the answers.
- The 25 core questions are the maximum. When a category needs a question that is not here, it replaces one; it does not get added.
