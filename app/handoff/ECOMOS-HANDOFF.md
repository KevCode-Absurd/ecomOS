## 1. What we are trying to do

We are building **ecomOS: an operating system for an e-commerce brand.** It does the work a founder today buys from an agency, a freelancer, a spreadsheet or their own late nights: research, offers and pricing, creative, copy, landing pages, paid ads, email, customer service, stock, cash, measurement and reporting.

The promise, in one line: **connect your accounts, answer one 45-minute interview, and every Monday get a short, ranked list of what to do next, in dollars, with the work drafted and ready for you to approve.**

It is general purpose. It must work for any store, in any market (India and the US first). It is not built for one client. Non-subscription stores come first; a subscription version comes later.

Who uses it:
- **The founder** of a small or mid-size online brand, usually on Shopify, often spending on Meta and Google ads, who cannot afford or does not trust a full agency.
- **An agency owner** running several such brands, who needs the same work done faster and with proof.

Where the edge comes from:
1. Expert e-commerce knowledge, held as a bank of about 4,650 tested tactics ("ideas") and about 7,600 short knowledge cards from 256 source documents.
2. The brand's own data, voice, references and test history, held in a **brand brain**.
3. A loop that remembers: every test result is written down, wins become the standard, losses are recorded so they are not repeated.

## 2. What went wrong the first time, and the rules that follow

We built 28 screens on a sample brand with simulated data. The output became jargon. A founder could not use it. So we stopped, and we are rebuilding from what a founder actually needs. The lessons are now rules:

- **Plain words.** Short sentences. Digits. Below tenth-grade reading level. No em dashes. No jargon without a plain word next to it.
- **Money first.** Every job ties to a metric, and every metric ties up to revenue or gross profit (gross profit feeds revenue, so either counts as the top).
- **Numbers are computed in code, not by a model.** Missing data shows as "unavailable", never as zero. Simulated data is labelled simulated on every page. A ratio for a period is total over total (period ROAS = total value / total spend), never an average of daily ratios.
- **Nothing ships without the founder.** Agents draft; the founder or a named person approves. Ad accounts are read-only to the system: it drafts the change, a person makes it.
- **No invented claims.** Every claim in an ad or page traces to proof in the brain.
- **Competitor ads are signals, not proven winners.** Use them for principles, layout and mood. Never copy text, logos, products or faces. An ad running a long time is a hint, not proof.
- **Customer posts on Reddit and similar sites are research only.** Never quote them as testimonials, and never store usernames.
- **Brand first.** Build the brand brain before choosing creatives, pages or offers.

**e. The loop.** Audit the brand, rank the top 10 moves by money, have the founder approve, then deploy (drafted by agents, shipped by a person), measure, and write the result to memory. A win is promoted to the standard, called T0. A loss writes a fail note with the reason and a date to recheck. Agents are small and domain-specific, with one orchestrator.

## 4. How we think about jobs

We have made three lists so far.

1. **First list: what the owner wants done.** Jobs per screen, ranked by money, customer satisfaction and ease, each with a metric chain up to revenue or gross profit.
2. **Second list: jobs as in *Competing Against Luck* (Christensen).** A job is the progress a person wants in a specific moment. It has functional, emotional and social sides. Each job names:
   - what the owner hires today: an agency, a spreadsheet, their gut, or nothing
   - what has to be fired
   - the four forces: push of the situation, pull of the new, habit of the present, and anxiety of the new
   - who should not hire it

   It is written as: "When [moment], help me [progress], so I [outcome]." Jobs that nobody does today rank higher.
3. **Third list: tactical and executable.** This is the one to emulate.

### The third list's shape: three levels

- **Top jobs** per screen, 3 to 5. They state the owner's progress in the "When ..., help me ..., so I ..." form.
- **Tactical jobs** under the top jobs, 20 per screen. Each is a task a person or agent can start this week. Verb first, concrete nouns.
- **Steps** inside each tactical job, 3 to 7. Each step is one action with a visible result, and includes the human check where a model could be wrong.

Each tactical job also names:
- **Enablers.** The engine behind the job. One enabler powers many jobs across screens. For example, the job is "publish more ads for people who do not know us yet", and the enabler is an awareness heat map.
- **Inputs.** What must exist before the job can run, each with a status:

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `connected` | From a day-one account. |
| `brain` | From a brand brain shelf. |
| `founder` | Only the founder can give it. |
| `have` | Already in the system: the idea bank or the knowledge cards. |
| `setup` | Install or connect in under a day. |
| `buy` | A paid tool or person, such as an AI video tool. |
| `enabler` | Needs an enabler built first. |

- **Metric chain.** Three levels:
  1. The first metric that moves, with its window and source.
  2. The metric one level up.
  3. Revenue, gross profit, contribution margin, cash, customer satisfaction or risk.
- **Score.** Each part is scored 1 to 5:
  - money: revenue or gross profit impact
  - satisfaction: for the customer or the founder
  - speed: how fast the first metric moves
  - ease: effort and cost to act

  Total = 4 x money + 2 x satisfaction + 2 x speed + 2 x ease. The maximum is 50.
- **Ready.** 1 to 5, for how much of the inputs exist on onboarding day. A **start now** job has ready 4 or 5 and a first output in hours or days.

## 5. Two worked examples: the depth we want

**Example 1: levels of awareness.**
- **The question.** Are we running only bottom-of-funnel ads, for people who are product aware or most aware? Are we skipping top-of-funnel ads, such as native articles, for people who are unaware or problem aware? How are competitors spread across the levels?
- **The method.** Classify our whole ad library, and competitors' ads, by awareness level and by angle: product, solution or problem. Weight each level by spend. Where we are thin, filter the inspiration library by that level and suggest what to make.
- **Google too.** Split search terms and campaigns into top, middle and bottom of funnel.
- **The job:** publish more top-of-funnel creative, so we reach new buyers instead of circling the same audience.
- **The enabler:** an awareness heat map. It powers many insights at the back end: our spend mix, the gap to rivals, which level buys the cheapest new customers, and what to brief next.

**Example 2: AI video ads.**
- **Inputs:**
  - access to an AI video tool, such as Higgsfield
  - past winning ads and why they won
  - the awareness gap
  - the brain's voice and allowed claims
  - principles taken from competitors
  - real product footage
- **Steps:**
  1. Write the script.
  2. Write 3 to 5 variations of the script, each with a new hook or angle.
  3. Cut each variation to 15, 30 and 30 to 45 seconds.
  4. Render them.
  5. Lay out the grid and pick one cut per stack.
  6. Launch them as one test.
  7. Record which length and which variation won.

## 8. Plain glossary

- **Awareness levels** (Eugene Schwartz):
  - unaware: does not know they have the problem
  - problem aware
  - solution aware: knows solutions exist
  - product aware: knows our product
  - most aware: ready to buy and needs a reason now
- **Top, middle and bottom of funnel:** new people, people weighing options, and people about to buy or buying again.
- **Gross profit per order:** sales, minus product cost, shipping, payment fees, discounts and returns.
- **Contribution margin:** gross profit minus ad spend and other variable costs.
- **Congruence:** the ad's promise, offer, look and awareness level match the page it lands on.
- **T0:** the current standard that new work starts from. A test win is promoted to T0.
- **Partnership ads:** ads run through a creator's handle with their permission.
- **Answer engines:** AI assistants such as ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity that buyers ask what to buy.
