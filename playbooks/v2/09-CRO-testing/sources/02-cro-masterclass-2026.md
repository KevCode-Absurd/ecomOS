## 1. What this source is for

It tells an operator what number to optimise (revenue or contribution per visitor, not conversion rate), how to diagnose a conversion-rate change before redesigning, the order to work in (clarity, copy, offer, proof, friction), how to build a mobile PDP hero, how to keep ads and landing pages in message match, how to structure offers, proof and checkout, when A/B tests are valid, and when to use an advertorial instead of a PDP. It is most useful for a Shopify DTC brand buying cold paid social traffic.

## 2. Principles

1. **Conversion rate is an output, not a lever.** Source 2 says it is the output of "about 50 different inputs, most of which are not on the page": product category, price point, AOV, traffic source, how much the creative educates, purchase intent, cold-traffic volume, product quality, return rates. Use it as a diagnosis, not a north star. Do not compare it across brands. [953e03390b0d] [b9f3fede007a] [372439a05cbd]
2. **Revenue per visitor (RPV) is the better metric.** RPV = conversion rate x AOV. Contribution per visitor = RPV x gross margin %. The presenter would not subtract CAC or ad spend; keep it simple (gross profit per visitor). The agency tracks RPV over about 2.5 years to see if conversion is improving. Closer to "base reality" because it is a revenue number. [b2dc4e814c63] [b9f3fede007a] [372439a05cbd]
3. **Blended conversion rate falls as cold traffic grows.** Cold traffic converts below warm and returning traffic, so scaling cold spend pulls the blend toward the cold rate. "Really big brands have lower conversion rates." Returning customers also raise conversion rate and AOV (returning AOV is usually higher). [953e03390b0d] [372439a05cbd]
4. **Every single metric can be gamed.** Turn off Facebook ads and conversion rate rises overnight (only warm traffic left) while you lose millions. Run traffic campaigns and sessions rise with no revenue. Push high-AOV products and AOV rises while CAC may make them unprofitable. Raise spend 4x and sales rise at a loss. So judge the business on LTGP:CAC, contribution profit and finance KPIs. [91d639e7e2e2]
5. **Conversion rate has a ceiling set by category, price point and traffic quality, not page quality.** Once clarity, offer and proof are done, the next lever is first-order contribution margin, product launches, product quality and repeat purchase. Brands that keep redesigning the same page hunt a ceiling the page does not control. [953e03390b0d]
6. **But CRO is still high leverage, because it lowers CAC.** CAC = ad spend / new-customer orders ~= ad spend / (sessions x conversion rate). Doubling conversion halves CAC, which lets you raise spend back to the old CAC ceiling and "double or triple volume". The presenter has "almost never seen" anyone double conversion; small lifts still cut CAC in proportion. It is the cheapest lever because more spend and better in-platform efficiency both need money (creative production). [82fbec40a149]
7. **Conversion rate is a small second-order input to CPM.** Meta's expected action rate = expected CTR x expected conversion rate, both based on history. Higher conversion lowers CPM slightly. Do not do CRO for this reason alone. [82fbec40a149]
8. **Work in this order: clarity and hierarchy, copy, offer, proof, friction.** Ranked by ICE overall (impact, confidence, ease), not by 1 factor. The offer has the biggest impact ("a bad offer keeps conversion at 0%; a good one can reach 80%", the free $1M car thought experiment) but is the hardest, so clarity comes first. The same order is also a good top-to-bottom page structure. [9e7d2fb74d83] [3965231e76dc] [347f8b289bd6]
9. **Clarity fixes do not need a split test.** Fixing an unclear page has a quick impact and "you're not going to really need to do any split testing". [6503bc821ea6] [e02ace8044e5]
10. **A PDP must answer 4 questions above the fold on mobile.** What is it? Why this one (unique mechanism)? Why now? Why trust you? Check on your phone, not your computer. (The recap says "core five questions"; the body lists 4.) [bb761687ad68] [36f1d8130259]
11. **Order above-the-fold elements by persuasive weight.** Value proposition, then proof, then price, then CTA. Leave out SKU details, size and quantity pickers above the fold; handle them in cart, or default to the larger size or a bundle. [e76096210c1d] [e10616e94452]
12. **Continuity (message match) "might be the biggest hack" in the video.** When the ad changes persona, offer or angle, the landing page must match. One person, one problem, one mechanism per page. Broad benefit lists make a shopper doubt the product fits their problem. Applies to ads too: do not agitate many problems in one ad. [a5cf6ee34972] [19d7eed88979] [322887ac21c3]
13. **Write about the person, not the product. Every line passes the "so what" test.** Lead with the outcome, justify with the feature: "Firmer skin in 4 weeks using a triple peptide complex", not "Triple peptide complex". [790f6b3372c2] [55e7424e6d18] [79bdf741dfc4]
14. **Your product is probably not unique; position it as "one of one".** 3 ways: mine reviews for unique feedback; combine 2 real benefits into a new claim; reposition into white space for an unserved audience (Blue Ocean Strategy, "purple ocean"). Free shipping, great service and fast delivery are givens, not differentiators. [cf0bc0f31721] [79bdf741dfc4]
15. **End the copy by handling every other solution.** Otherwise shoppers default to a cheaper option or one they already have. [9f610afef497] [55e7424e6d18]
16. **FAQs answer real objections, not brand trivia.** No "What is the shipping policy?" or "How did the brand start?". Use the top reasons people do not buy, from customer research. [499b4e67252a] [55e7424e6d18]
17. **The offer is positioning, not discounting.** Discounting is "lazy". The same product reframed as a "New Year's reset" bundle at full price converted better than it ever did on sale, with no margin loss. A good offer raises perceived value while cost of goods stays low; margin reflects offer quality. [0ca0ef2a3d5d] [1e7c5456a957]
18. **Blanket discounts erode margin and train customers to wait.** 20% off everything lifts revenue but cuts profit and becomes a habit the brand cannot exit. [1e7c5456a957]
19. **Free shipping is not always a win.** It teaches customers to value delivery separately. Shipping collected is "almost pure profit". Give free shipping only at a threshold that pulls AOV up; most brands set the threshold too low. [23f840cd83b0] [8b2d9df04dfb]
20. **Reviews: going from 0 to about 100 can roughly double conversion.** The jump is mostly from 0 to 80 reviews; beyond that (150, 300, 400) it is "immeasurable". Personal experience, 2019 to 2020 own brands. [a507cc86ec03]
21. **Image and video reviews are worth far more than text reviews.** Auto-sort them first. [0a1f9ee893a3] [1d5e67c73cdd]
22. **Proof must match the claim.** 5 hyper-relevant reviews beat 400 generic "great product" reviews. [0a1f9ee893a3] [a507cc86ec03] [322887ac21c3]
23. **Generic trust badges do nothing.** Footer security seals "do absolutely nothing to close anyone". Guarantees and returns policies are table stakes, but still useful as a risk reversal after the proof. [cdd5df001439] [1d5e67c73cdd]
24. **A 4.8 rating reads as more genuine than 5.0.** Opinion. [36f1d8130259] [a507cc86ec03]
25. **Most "CRO wins" are noise.** Detecting a 10% to 20% lift needs about 1,000 conversions per variant. Peeking daily and stopping at significance inflates false positives. "CRO really ends up kind of just being star signs for ecom." Not an argument against testing: valid for 8 to 9-figure brands with enough volume, clean ad structure and short time to purchase. [699a7cd5ac4c] [e02ace8044e5]
26. **Micro-tweaks rarely move RPV** unless the store does about $40M+ online, where squeezing an extra ~0.1% becomes worth it. [e02ace8044e5]
27. **Cold VSL and unaware traffic needs a landing page, not a PDP.** Unless the PDP is rebuilt into almost a landing page. Landing pages are "substantially underleveraged". Good landing pages, VSLs and ads are all just good copywriting in the same order. [8bfe80b22edc] [aa0e72b56778] [23bbae178981]
28. **Mobile first.** 60% to 95% of traffic is mobile depending on brand; put about 90% of CRO effort on mobile, 10% on desktop. [a56c27356031] [bb761687ad68]
29. **Over-optimising conversion can destroy LTV.** Dark patterns and over-claiming lift front-end conversion and lower CAC but raise returns and chargebacks and cut repeat rate and lifetime gross profit. Watch LTV by angle. [dad9c85ea261]
30. **Load time is "one of the easiest leverage points".** Mockup example: 4.8 s versus 0.9 s. Source 2 gives no further how (see folder 08-SPD). [36f1d8130259]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build correct conversion and RPV numbers in Shopify
1. Revenue: do not use Shopify "Total sales" raw, it includes tax. Use Net sales + Shipping collected (the agency's method), or Total sales minus tax. `[verify UI]` [91d639e7e2e2]
2. AOV: Shopify's AOV excludes shipping collected. Add it back before RPV = conversion rate x AOV; RPV comes out higher. `[verify UI]`
3. Sessions: Shopify sessions include more bot traffic. Default to GA4 sessions. `[verify UI]` `[needs brand data: GA4 vs Shopify session gap]`
4. Conversion rate: compute from corrected orders and GA4 sessions.
5. Contribution per visitor = RPV x gross margin %. Do not add CAC. [b9f3fede007a]
6. Track RPV as a long trend (agency uses about 2.5 years).
7. Read every metric next to LTGP:CAC, contribution profit and returning-customer rate. [91d639e7e2e2]

### 3.2 Diagnose a conversion-rate change before touching the page
1. Split conversion by traffic temperature: cold Meta versus warm and returning. [953e03390b0d]
2. Check whether cold share rose. If yes, the blend falls even with no page change (example: blended 2.8%, cold 1%, warm 4%).
3. Check returning-customer rate; high returning share inflates conversion rate and AOV.
4. Check category, price point, AOV and creative education changes.
5. If the page already has clear clarity, offer and proof, look at product quality, first-order contribution, launches and repeat purchase before a redesign.
6. Judge the outcome on RPV or contribution per visitor. [372439a05cbd]

### 3.3 CRO work order (the 5 levels)
1. Clarity and hierarchy: can a shopper understand it instantly and is it above the fold? [9e7d2fb74d83]
2. Copy: does it remove doubt and speak to the real desire?
3. Offer: is the thing worth converting on?
4. Proof: do they believe the claims and the unique mechanism?
5. Friction: is anything physically in the way of purchase?
6. Implement obvious clarity fixes directly; test later bigger swings. [6503bc821ea6]

### 3.4 Mobile PDP hero (above the fold)
1. Open the PDP on a phone. [36f1d8130259]
2. What is it: replace a generic title ("Face serum 30 ml") with a mechanism-bearing one ("The only overnight retinol buffered to work while you sleep so you wake up firmer, not red"; title "Firmer skin by morning", subtitle "Overnight repair serum").
3. Why this one: state the differentiator; example sub-line "Retinol loses strength on the shelf, so we bottle in small batches. This batch was filled this month."
4. Why now: a real reason to buy today (freshness line; "4 payments of $12" instalments pull purchase forward).
5. Why trust: rating and reviews in the hero (4.8 stars).
6. Order: value proposition, proof, price, CTA. [e76096210c1d]
7. Remove SKU detail, size options and quantity pickers from the hero, or default to the bigger size or a bundle to raise first-purchase gross profit. [e10616e94452]
8. Add a scroll cue ("more below", fade out) so the page does not look finished.
9. Check message match with the ad that sends traffic. [a5cf6ee34972]
10. Check load time. [36f1d8130259]

### 3.5 Message match from ad to page
1. Classify each ad concept by what changes: persona, offer, or angle (unique mechanism). [a5cf6ee34972]
2. If persona changes (creatine for women in menopause vs creatine for 19-year-old men building muscle), build separate PDPs or landing pages.
3. If the offer changes (single vs bundle vs different product), use a separate page.
4. If the angle changes (creatine for cognition vs recovery; collagen for skin vs joints), keep the page on that 1 angle. Stacking all angles as a list of benefits lowers conversion (collagen arthritis example).
5. Match hero, mechanism, objections, testimonials and CTA to the ad's promise and wording. [19d7eed88979] [322887ac21c3]
6. Keep the ad itself specific: 1 person, 1 problem.
7. Card addition: prioritise concepts with meaningful spend, since each page adds build and QA work. [a5cf6ee34972]

### 3.6 Copy rewrite
1. List every feature on the page. Ask "so what?" for each. Write the outcome first, then the feature as proof. [790f6b3372c2]
2. Find a one-of-one angle: export all reviews, run them through an LLM (Claude, ChatGPT) and ask for unique feedback that differentiates the product; read the source reviews to confirm. [cf0bc0f31721] [79bdf741dfc4]
3. Or combine 2 real benefits (pillowcase that helps acne and is comfortable to sleep on). Do not string givens together (free shipping + great service + fast delivery is not unique).
4. Or find white space: same product, underserved audience (Bloom: greens powder for women against AG1; the presenter says he "might be getting this wrong").
5. Put the one-of-one angle high on the page, pulled from real customer words ("The first retinol that didn't make me peel", "the line customers keep writing"). Only claim it if competitors cannot honestly say it.
6. Shrink table stakes (vegan, 30 ml, made in Australia, apply nightly) into small dot points low on the page. [cf0bc0f31721]
7. Near the end of the copy, handle every alternative solution. [9f610afef497]
8. Build the FAQ from customer research. Example top 3 for a retinol: works on sensitive skin (answer with mechanism plus a matching quote)? how long until results? can I use it with my other actives? [499b4e67252a] [55e7424e6d18]
9. Pain-led or benefit-led vs feature-led headline is one of the few things "worth split testing": big swing, above the fold, seen by everyone. [55e7424e6d18]

### 3.7 Offer build (quick version; the presenter points to a separate 90-minute offer video)
1. Reframe the product around the customer's moment (New Year's reset) before discounting. [0ca0ef2a3d5d]
2. Build a routine bundle whose parts are steps to the outcome (gentle cleanser, overnight repair serum, recovery night cream). [1e7c5456a957]
3. Price the bundle discount from fulfilment savings: example value $136, sold $122, $14 off, equal to the shipping and fulfilment cost of 3 separate orders, so margin is unchanged. [d5a74f7edadd]
4. Add a gift with purchase with low COGS and high perceived value at a threshold (travel ritual set "worth $30", COGS $4 to $5, on orders over $60). Gift improves breakeven ROAS.
5. Set a free-shipping threshold above typical carts (example $66, "would need to be really thoughtful"; test over time). [23f840cd83b0]
6. Keep driving traffic to the single product page too. In the cart drawer, offer a 1-click upgrade from the single item to the routine that unlocks the gift, free shipping and savings. [6baa7dce36c7]
7. Add a post-purchase 1-click upsell right after checkout, relevant to the order, no card re-entry (ritual bag $19, framed as usually $35 to $40, 50% off for 5 minutes). [0ca0ef2a3d5d]
8. Judge on RPV and contribution, not AOV alone. [0ca0ef2a3d5d]

### 3.8 Proof section
1. If reviews are 0, get reviews first; nothing else in this chapter matters until you have them. [a507cc86ec03]
2. Auto-sort reviews with images and video first. `[verify UI]` [0a1f9ee893a3]
3. Incentivise photo and video reviews legally: a $10 gift card on the next order, or $10 cash back on the order for a review with an image. You cannot pay for reviews in most countries; you can reward submitting one. `[web check: incentivised-review rules in the brand's markets]` [1cdf5f4a3630]
4. Choose reviews and before-and-after testimonials that prove the page's claim (firmer skin in 4 weeks). More demographics shown (for example 4 before-and-afters) is better. Add a specific stat if you can validate it. [1d5e67c73cdd]
5. Remove trust badges. [cdd5df001439]
6. Close with risk reversal: money-back guarantee or returns promise stated plainly. [1d5e67c73cdd]

### 3.9 Checkout audit (Baymard reasons, as cited)
1. Extra costs: sum shipping, tax, fees added at checkout; keep under 10% of AOV (on a $100 order, under $10). [27026398ed8a] [8b2d9df04dfb]
2. Slow delivery: get faster delivery, or offer fast options at checkout, or split-test hiding delivery times for 7 to 10 days (hiding adds ambiguity and lowers conversion; test whether it beats showing slow dates). [27026398ed8a]
3. Card trust: more proof and brand presence; do not look like a scam (mostly a startup problem).
4. Forced account: turn off forced accounts; guest checkout as default. The presenter "believes" forced accounts may be a Shopify default; check. `[verify UI]` [94a63f9f740b]
5. Long checkout: cut fields, make fields optional, shorten the address block to essentials. [c6984ab0f0cc]
6. Enable Shop Pay, Apple Pay, Google Pay and any fast checkout; put express options at the top. `[verify UI]` [a5c46083a30f]
7. Returns policy: state it clearly at the decision point ("30-day free returns"), but do not rush to free returns, above all in fashion; test and accept some loss if more profitable. [1e401776f34b]
8. Show total cost up front; avoid drip pricing (IM8 shows a monthly price but sells quarterly only, so the cart jumps from about $100 to $300 to $400). [8b2d9df04dfb]
9. Show a free-shipping progress message ("spend $12 more for free shipping"). [c6984ab0f0cc]
10. If tax is included but shown as a line, label it clearly so it does not look like a new charge. [8b2d9df04dfb]
11. One-page vs multi-step: split test; their data shows one-page "a little" better. [c6984ab0f0cc]
12. Add email and SMS abandonment flows. [3163cc34fa79]

### 3.10 Decide whether an A/B test is valid
1. Estimate required sample: about 1,000 conversions per variant for a 10% to 20% lift; at 2.5% conversion, about 40,000 sessions per variant, 80,000 for a simple A/B. Card adds: calculate the real number for your baseline and effect. [699a7cd5ac4c]
2. Check the page can reach that in a sensible time; most 7-figure brands cannot on 1 PDP in a month.
3. Do not peek daily and stop at first significance.
4. Remove contamination: Klaviyo sends spike conversion; an on-page split tool will fork email traffic too. Use custom landing pages that get only cold Meta traffic. [699a7cd5ac4c]
5. Account for multi-click journeys: on big brands about 1.5 Meta clicks per conversion on average, so about 50% of buyers click more than once and may see both variants.
6. A Meta ad-set or campaign-level split can fork traffic instead. `[verify UI]`
7. Test only big swings (headline framing, offer, message-matched page). [e02ace8044e5]
8. Judge on RPV or contribution per visitor.

### 3.11 Prioritise with ICE
1. List all possible changes. [a56c27356031]
2. Score Impact, Confidence, Ease each out of 10.
3. Multiply the 3 scores.
4. Sort high to low and work down.
5. Install Microsoft Clarity for heatmaps and recordings to feed the list. `[verify UI]`

### 3.12 Long-form landing page for cold VSL traffic
1. Hook in the ad's language, about the customer ("If you fall asleep fine but wake up at 3 a.m., the problem probably is not your willpower. It's a mineral most adults quietly run low on"). [23bbae178981] [aa0e72b56778]
2. Problem: agitate and explain.
3. Mechanism: how the product solves it.
4. Objections: about 3 common FAQs.
5. Proof: through a mechanism lens (a specific stat, before/after sleep quality study) and 5-star reviews; ideally real people verifying.
6. Risk reversal (60-night money-back guarantee).
7. CTA ("Start sleeping deeper") into an add-to-cart slider, upsell, or a subscription funnel.
8. If the offer is too low ticket for cold traffic, bundle it. [aa0e72b56778]
9. Other formats: listicles, advertorials, quiz funnels. [8bfe80b22edc]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is the conversion drop a page problem?** Split by cold vs warm vs returning, check cold share, AOV, price, category. Good: segment rates steady; blend moved by mix. Bad: cold-segment rate fell with steady creative. Next: audit the page only if the segment rate fell. [953e03390b0d]
- **Metric hygiene.** Revenue excludes tax, includes shipping collected; sessions from GA4. Bad: using Shopify total sales and sessions raw. [91d639e7e2e2]
- **4-question hero check on a phone.** Good: all 4 answered, ordered value, proof, price, CTA. Bad: default Shopify PDP with a generic title, no reason to choose, no urgency, no proof, no scroll cue. [bb761687ad68] [36f1d8130259]
- **Message-match check.** Open each active ad and its landing page side by side. Good: same persona, promise and words. Bad: specific ad into a generic PDP listing 9 benefits. [a5cf6ee34972] [19d7eed88979]
- **So-what check on every line.** Bad: "30 ml, vegan, made in Australia, triple peptide complex, apply nightly" as headline features. [790f6b3372c2]
- **Table-stakes check.** Are "free shipping, fast dispatch, great service" given hero space? Next: shrink them. [cf0bc0f31721]
- **FAQ check.** Bad: shipping policy and brand story questions. Good: the 3 top non-purchase reasons from research. [499b4e67252a]
- **Offer check.** Is the main offer a blanket % discount? Next: routine bundle, gift threshold, cart upgrade, post-purchase upsell. [1e7c5456a957]
- **Proof check.** Reviews present? Photo and video first? Proof matches the claim? Trust badges instead of proof? Risk reversal present? [1d5e67c73cdd] [cdd5df001439]
- **Checkout check.** Extras under 10% of AOV; guest default; express pay on top; lean fields; returns visible; total shown early; tax line not misread; abandonment flows live. [3163cc34fa79] [c6984ab0f0cc]
- **Test validity check.** Enough conversions per variant? Peeking? Klaviyo or retention traffic in the test? Multi-click exposure? Primary metric RPV? [699a7cd5ac4c]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Using Shopify total sales (with tax), AOV without shipping, and bot-heavy sessions. [91d639e7e2e2]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Internal tension on offers.** The offer has the biggest impact, yet ranks 3rd because it is hard. Cards flag not to let offer problems wait. [9e7d2fb74d83]
- **Internal tension on testing.** "Most CRO wins are noise" and "don't split test clarity fixes", yet he recommends testing shipping thresholds, delivery-time display (7 to 10 days, which is short against his own sample math), one-page checkout and hero framing. [e02ace8044e5] [699a7cd5ac4c]
- **4 vs 5 questions.** The body lists 4 above-the-fold questions; the recap says "core five". [bb761687ad68]
- **Shipping threshold stated 2 ways.** "Up to about 10% of the average order value" and "sub 10% of total revenue". [23f840cd83b0] [8b2d9df04dfb]
- **Trust badges conflict.** Source 2 says badges do nothing. Source 3 in this folder puts trust seals and a "trust policy bar" in winning tests. Source 1 uses secure-checkout logos as credibility. Not tested here. [cdd5df001439]
- **Sample-size conflict.** Source 2: ~40,000 sessions per variant. Source 3: 50,000 to 100,000 sessions per test, 2 to 4 weeks. Thresholds for serious testing: Source 2 about $40M+ online revenue; Source 3 about 300,000 monthly sessions. [699a7cd5ac4c] [e02ace8044e5]
- **Conversion rate as vanity vs Source 1.** Source 1 calls conversion rate "the only metric that matters". Source 2 rejects that. [b9f3fede007a]
- **Baymard numbers are cited from memory of the study.** Survey reasons, not a store diagnosis; the 14% "total cost" item is ambiguous. `[web check: current Baymard figures]` [3163cc34fa79] [8b2d9df04dfb]
- **Claims examples.** "Firmer skin in 4 weeks" and skin-firmness percentages are illustrative; the presenter admits he does not know how firmness % would be validated. Health and beauty claims need substantiation. [790f6b3372c2] [1d5e67c73cdd]
- **Review incentives.** Legal rules vary; cards add disclosure caveats. [1cdf5f4a3630]
- **Hiding delivery times** to avoid slow-delivery abandonment may conflict with consumer rules and card advice on transparency. `[web check: delivery disclosure rules]` [27026398ed8a]
- **Missing how.** No sample-size formula, no contribution-margin definition beyond gross margin, no bundle calculation formula, no decision matrix for PDP vs advertorial vs listicle vs quiz, no copywriting detail (deferred to a later video), no offer detail (deferred to a 90-minute video). [b2dc4e814c63] [8bfe80b22edc] [d5a74f7edadd]
