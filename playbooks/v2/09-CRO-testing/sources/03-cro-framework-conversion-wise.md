## 1. What this source is for

It tells an operator which CRO model fits the store's traffic (big swings below about 300,000 monthly sessions, a full testing programme above), how to gather the quantitative "what" and the qualitative "why", how to write and rank a hypothesis, how to run a valid A/B test, and which specific changes won for real Shopify brands (mega menu, PDP, cart, collection page, listicle, cart gamification, bundles, hero rewrites). It is the most concrete source in this batch on research inputs and test hygiene.

## 2. Principles

1. **Optimise revenue per visitor (RPV) or revenue per session, not conversion rate, "9 times out of 10".** RPV mixes conversion rate and AOV. "I could improve all of your conversion rates in about 2 minutes. All I need to do is slash your prices", and you would make less per visitor. [a9ed03163804] [83db79dbdb65]
2. **CRO fixes the "leaky bucket".** Paid traffic into an unoptimised store leaks cash. Source 3 claims CRO moves profit per visitor more than paid media, email or retention. Presenter opinion; he is a CRO seller. [a9ed03163804]
3. **Pick the CRO model by monthly sessions.** Under about 300,000 sessions a month: take big swings. Over about 300,000 (with a good enough conversion rate and enough orders): comprehensive CRO with audits and isolated A/B tests. The line is a 2025 internal update, not universal. [91c7b6d064ec] [3d52bd4930fe] [e7dfd865fcfd]
4. **Under 300,000 sessions, do not test button colours, a single hero image or a small text snippet.** Make large, research-led changes. [365c9ff30a24]
5. **Put a landing page between the ad and the store.** Sending paid traffic straight to PDPs, homepages or collection pages "is leaving money on the table". The landing page pre-sells, then sends people to the "money pages". [2d977920a505] [6695665021f2]
6. **Match landing-page messaging to the ad creative.** If the creative does not speak to the page, you lose money. [6695665021f2]
7. **Data is "single-handedly the most important thing" in CRO.** Quantitative data shows what is happening; qualitative data shows why. Combine them to get hypotheses. [25979af2691c]
8. **The post-purchase survey is "probably single-handedly the most effective way to optimise your store".** Ask at least 2 questions: "What made you purchase today?" and "What made you almost not purchase today?" The second is "gold". [b4505f6c4061] [91410aaad2c8]
9. **More people swipe through the whole image carousel than scroll the whole page.** Treat the PDP gallery as a mini landing page. Presenter observation, no data shown. [116d1016fa3b]
10. **Important selling points are often hidden below the fold.** In 1 case the key point sat above the footer and about 6% of visitors saw it. [9568c40e1d88]
11. **False clicks and rage clicks cost money.** People click things styled like buttons or links that are not. Removing them gave "massive" wins in high-volume stores. [9568c40e1d88] [a2d5c573076a]
12. **Every hypothesis has 3 parts: the change, the expected outcome, the rationale.** The rationale must come from data. "Nothing to do with CRO is complicated... putting the pieces of data together and using common sense." [a6c0f42b99a3]
13. **Statistical power matters more than significance.** Significance is like 3 coin flips giving 2 heads; power means enough data that the result will repeat. [c54652571336] [4d0785cdcf2b]
14. **Most tests lose.** A 30% to 35% win rate is "doing very, very well". Winners pay for the losers many times over ("times 100", rhetorical) because retained wins compound. Learn why tests lose and feed that into the next test. [bbab5b1b0329] [f09cfbc3ef44]
15. **Protect winners.** Ezra says new leadership at 1 brand overwrote winning tests; they are now putting them back. "A win will usually stick into perpetuity." [f09cfbc3ef44] [bbab5b1b0329]
16. **80% of the agency's tests are bigger swings above the fold**, "because that's really what's going to drive the revenue increase". This is their bias, not a rule (card caveat). [4d0785cdcf2b]
17. **Herd behaviour.** New visitors follow what others buy; bestseller cues in menus and on collection grids nudge them. [1823c8a7c180] [6f635b9d003d]
18. **Match the design to the demographic.** For an older (pro-age) audience: larger fonts, target-age images, scannable text, a prominent money-back guarantee. [116d1016fa3b] [6695665021f2]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Choose the CRO model
1. Pull monthly sessions and orders. [91c7b6d064ec]
2. If under about 300,000 sessions a month, run the "big swings" list (3.2). [365c9ff30a24]
3. If over about 300,000 and conversion rate and order count are "good enough" (not defined), run comprehensive CRO: deep audits (qual and quant), isolated A/B tests, 4 to 8 tests a month, full data stack (GA4 analysis, heatmaps, surveys, session recordings), roadmaps and frameworks, learning loops on wins and losses, cross-functional team (designers, developers, copywriters, CRO strategists). [3d52bd4930fe]
4. Card addition: check test power for the specific test anyway; the traffic line alone does not prove a test is viable. [e7dfd865fcfd]

### 3.2 Big swings for stores under about 300,000 sessions (the "6 to 8 things" slide; the spoken list)
1. Landing page design concepts: listicles ("top 5 reasons people are loving this new supplement"), sales pages, advertorials (first or third person content reviewing a product). Test different angles. [2d977920a505] [6695665021f2]
2. PDP redesigns based on data. [365c9ff30a24]
3. Offer testing every month: buy 1 get 1 free, "3 for the price of 2" (transcribed "three for the power of two"), build-a-bundle pages, different price points; price testing with a tool. [d12e614c0b4e] [e3ba0fb7d6f6]
4. Free-shipping and price tests in cart and checkout; gamify the cart to raise AOV. [e3ba0fb7d6f6]
5. Post-purchase upsells and surveys (Zipify named; Ezra's company). `[verify UI]`
6. Ad creative alignment: landing page message matches the creative. [6695665021f2]

### 3.3 Quantitative research (the "what"). Bold items in the talk are "non-negotiables" for every store
1. **Funnel drop-off analysis (non-negotiable).** Map ad to PDP, cart, checkout, thank-you page, upsell (OCU/Zipify). Find where people drop. Full GA analysis is not required under 300,000 sessions. [bbe716a39c74] [9568c40e1d88]
2. **Heatmap on the PDP (non-negotiable at 10,000 or 10 million sessions).** Microsoft Clarity is free ("not the best, quite laggy"). heatmap.com tracks by revenue, not only clicks (partner disclosed). Check clicks, scroll depth and where key points sit. `[verify UI]` [9568c40e1d88]
3. **False and rage clicks.** Find elements styled as buttons or links that do nothing; fix them. [9568c40e1d88]
4. **Price and offer testing.** Find the offer that sells profitably through cold media. [d12e614c0b4e]
5. Allow about 1 to 2 weeks to gather the non-negotiable data under 300,000 sessions. [bbe716a39c74]

### 3.4 Qualitative research (the "why")
1. **Usability testing and user interviews.** Get people on the site and watch; call your customers: why did you buy, was the purchase easy, was anything unclear? [c0690a74d2de] [fc9b05411697]
2. **Post-purchase survey (non-negotiable).** Set it up now with a survey app (KnoCommerce named). Ask "What made you purchase today?" and "What made you almost not purchase today?" `[verify UI]` [b4505f6c4061]
3. **One-time survey if no post-purchase survey yet.** Use Typeform or similar; email VIPs and customers; offer a chance to win a $250 Amazon gift card. [b3b265cf22f6]
4. **Buy from your own store (exercise).** Has the team bought from its own store in the last 4 weeks? Buy on mobile, desktop and tablet. Hand your phone to a 70-year-old grandmother and watch her buy. Look for exit popups and cookie banners covering the PDP CTA, cookie bars over the cart button. Do full UX/UI audits. [a2d5c573076a]
5. **Review mining (non-negotiable if you have 500 to 1,000+ reviews).** Export reviews to CSV, upload to GPT or another AI, prompt for commonalities, tone of voice, buzzwords, key phrases, friction points and objections. Answer them on the front end. [fc9b05411697]
6. For comprehensive clients the agency spends 2 to 3 weeks and produces about a 100-page audit (audience, wants, objections, friction, needs), used on site, in ads and in retention.

### 3.5 Turn research into a hypothesis
1. Change: what will you alter? [a6c0f42b99a3]
2. Expected outcome: what result do you expect (only possible after enough research)?
3. Rationale: which data supports it? Example: post-purchase surveys said buyers did not know how long supplement results take, so add a section just below the fold: "see noticeable results in the first 6 weeks". [109929fb0656] [91410aaad2c8]
4. Write a predefined, measurable hypothesis ("this listicle will convert better because X, Y, Z"). [4d0785cdcf2b]
5. Card additions: write page, audience and primary metric; cross-check survey themes with funnel and heatmap data; re-survey to see if the objection declines. [25979af2691c] [b4505f6c4061]

### 3.6 Prioritise with PXL (as described)
1. Put all test ideas from research in a "leaderboard" spreadsheet. [4a2e3d77d68e]
2. Score each 1 to 10 on the factors the presenter names: is it above the fold? is it noticeable in 5 seconds? how easy is it to implement? (The real PXL framework is not the agency's; the presenter credits it to others and does not give its full criteria.) `[web check: PXL framework criteria]`
3. Rank by highest score and start at the top: easiest, biggest impact, above the fold. [a6c0f42b99a3]

### 3.7 Run an A/B test
1. Control (current) vs 1 or more variations (A/B, A/B/C/D). Multivariate only at high volume. [4d0785cdcf2b]
2. Use a testing tool that reports significance, but make sure the test has power. [c54652571336]
3. Sample: the agency runs 50,000 to 100,000 sessions through a test, depending on where the test is. [4d0785cdcf2b]
4. Duration: 2 to 4 weeks; for comprehensive (over 300,000) at least 2 weeks. Cover 2 Mondays, 2 Tuesdays, 2 Wednesdays, 2 Thursdays so a one-day shock (a competitor's Monday sale) does not trigger a wrong kill. [7d481c590b57]
5. Randomise traffic. [4d0785cdcf2b]
6. Avoid conflicting tests: not 2 tests on 1 PDP area at once. A homepage test and a cart test can run together; an above-the-fold and a below-the-fold test can too. [7d481c590b57] [4d0785cdcf2b]
7. QA each test 2 or 3 times before launch; a bug makes a winner look like a loser. [4d0785cdcf2b]
8. Judge on revenue per session or visitor. [83db79dbdb65]
9. Keep a test log; implement winners; learn from losers. [f09cfbc3ef44] [bbab5b1b0329]

### 3.8 Mega menu redesign (Boom winning test 1)
1. Check session recordings: new visitors on homepage or store pages open the mega menu to see what is on offer. [033531683bba]
2. Add a bestsellers carousel at the top of the menu. [1823c8a7c180]
3. Add icons and some social proof.
4. Move "non-money links" (contact, reviews, FAQs) to the bottom.
5. Measure product-page visits, add to cart, purchase and RPV (card addition). [033531683bba]

### 3.9 PDP above-the-fold changes (Boom winning test 2, informed by customer surveys)
1. Move reviews and social proof (stars) higher. [116d1016fa3b]
2. Show visible savings.
3. Add a sticky add-to-cart.
4. Replace carousel dots with thumbnails so people see there are more images.
5. Make the gallery a mini landing page: benefits, testimonials, UGC, video, guarantee seals.
6. Make the money-back guarantee visible next to the CTA (it was in a collapsed box; they un-collapsed it) for the older audience.

### 3.10 Cart changes (Boom winning test 3)
1. If no discount is active, remove the discount-code box from cart (and coupon page). Reason: shoppers leave to search "[brand] discount code", hit bad coupon sites and popups, and do not come back. Worse for older shoppers. [19e8c5c27387] [84ebf548938d]
2. Add a "trust policy bar" in the cart: easy returns, support, satisfaction guarantee, money-back guarantee. [84ebf548938d]
3. If a promotion is active, keep the field working (card addition). [19e8c5c27387]

### 3.11 Collection page (Boom winning test 4)
1. Show products above the fold on mobile: a 2 x 2 grid, 4 products. If a hero banner or text block hides them, "start again". [6f635b9d003d]
2. Add labels (bestseller, new, low in stock, trending) to a few products only, never all. Use the same idea wherever products sit side by side (homepage, "often bought with"). [6f635b9d003d]
3. For consumable repeat-buy products, put the price on the collection card button and allow add to cart from the collection, cutting the steps from collection, view details, PDP, add to cart. "Doesn't work with every single product or niche." [6f635b9d003d]

### 3.12 Listicle landing page for cold traffic (Boom winning test 5)
1. Native, organic listicle: "5 makeup tips for older women". [6695665021f2]
2. Replace young models with the target demographic.
3. Increase font sizes.
4. Make text scannable: clear value-driven headline, numbered points 1 to 5, bolded subheads, no long paragraphs. "As if they were 5 years old."
5. Redesign the buy box so the offer is value- and benefit-driven and easy to understand.

### 3.13 Cart gamification (clothing client)
1. Add free-shipping threshold prompts. [e3ba0fb7d6f6]
2. Add free-gift thresholds that unlock as spend rises.
3. Add in-cart upsells (the client used 2 sticker sets, gift cards, a box-and-socks bundle).

### 3.14 Bundle offer below add to cart (sunglasses client)
1. Below add to cart, add "buy 3 packs, save 20%". [e3ba0fb7d6f6]
2. Open a custom modal to pick a colour per pair.
3. Works for low-price items people own several of ($30 to $40 sunglasses), not luxury.

### 3.15 PDP copy redesign (client originally over 300,000 sessions)
1. Use post-purchase surveys, surveys and customer calls to find pain points and desires. [116d1016fa3b]
2. Rewrite above the fold and the description to match them.
3. Turn blocks of description text into benefit-driven bullets (paste into GPT and ask it to convert); bold key words; talk about outcomes, not "shiny objects or features". [116d1016fa3b]

### 3.16 Homepage or sales-page hero (2 clients)
1. Clear headline on what the product is, who it is for and why use it. [4653645c47ac]
2. Push social proof up.
3. Benefit-driven bullets.
4. Product image.
5. One clear CTA; explain what happens on click and where they go; "shop bestsellers" "probably typically works the best" if unsure.
6. Trust seals by the CTA (money-back guarantee, "over 10,000 happy customers", vegan-friendly, made in the US) and "featured in" logos, only if true. [4653645c47ac]
7. Trust policy bar on the sales page.
8. Answer the top objection from post-purchase data in the hero (sandal brand: leather quality, so hero copy on craftsmanship, sourcing, handcrafting). [d8e9c11154e5]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Traffic model check.** Monthly sessions vs 300,000; orders; test power. Bad: running button-colour tests on 40,000 sessions. Next: switch to big swings. [91c7b6d064ec] [c54652571336]
- **Funnel drop-off.** Which step loses the most (PDP, cart, checkout, thank-you, upsell)? A drop shows where, not why. [bbe716a39c74]
- **Scroll depth.** What share of visitors reach your key selling point, guarantee, reviews? Bad: 6% reach it. Next: move it up. [9568c40e1d88]
- **False and rage clicks.** Elements that look clickable but are not. Next: make them work or restyle. [9568c40e1d88]
- **Self-purchase and grandmother test.** Popups or cookie bars covering CTAs? Bugs on any device? [a2d5c573076a]
- **Post-purchase "almost not" answers.** Group into objections (quality, time to results, fit, shipping, guarantee, how to use). Is each answered near the decision? [91410aaad2c8] [109929fb0656]
- **Carousel check.** Dots or thumbnails? Is the gallery selling (benefits, UGC, video, guarantee)? [116d1016fa3b]
- **Guarantee visibility.** Collapsed or hidden? For risk-sensitive audiences, show it by the CTA. [116d1016fa3b]
- **Cart coupon field.** Shown with no live promo? Remove. [19e8c5c27387]
- **Cart trust.** Is there a returns, support and guarantee bar? [84ebf548938d]
- **Collection page on mobile.** Are 4 products visible above the fold? Labels on a few, not all? Can repeat buyers add to cart from the grid? [6f635b9d003d]
- **Mega menu.** Do new visitors open it? Does it push bestsellers or list plain links? Are non-money links at the bottom? [033531683bba]
- **Paid path.** Do ads go straight to PDP or homepage? Is there a pre-sell page that matches the ad? [2d977920a505]
- **Demographic fit.** Images, font size and readability match the buyer? [6695665021f2]
- **Test QA.** Tested 2 to 3 times on devices before launch? Tracking firing? [4d0785cdcf2b]
- **Test result.** Judged on RPV, run 2+ weeks, each weekday twice, no conflicting test on the same area? [7d481c590b57] [83db79dbdb65]
- **Winner protection.** Are past winners still live after theme or team changes? [f09cfbc3ef44]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Chasing conversion rate instead of RPV (price cuts raise CR and cut revenue). [83db79dbdb65]
- Running micro-tests (button colour, 1 image, a text snippet) with under 300,000 sessions. [365c9ff30a24]
- Sending paid traffic straight to PDP, homepage or collection pages. [2d977920a505]
- Ad creative that does not match the landing page. [6695665021f2]
- Hiding the top selling points near the footer. [9568c40e1d88]
- Elements styled like buttons that are not clickable. [9568c40e1d88]
- Never buying from your own store; missing popups and cookie bars that cover CTAs. [a2d5c573076a]
- No post-purchase survey. [b4505f6c4061]
- Carousel dots instead of thumbnails; a gallery that does not sell. [116d1016fa3b]
- A collapsed or hidden guarantee for a risk-averse audience. [116d1016fa3b]
- A discount box in cart with no active discount. [19e8c5c27387]
- Collection pages with big hero banners that push products below the fold; labelling every product. [6f635b9d003d]
- Default-theme mega menus with plain links. [033531683bba]
- Killing a test after 1 bad day; running under 2 weeks. [7d481c590b57]
- Running conflicting tests on the same PDP area. [4d0785cdcf2b]
