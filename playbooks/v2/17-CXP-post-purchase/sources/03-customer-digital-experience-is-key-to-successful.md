## 3. Procedures

**A. Deciding build vs. buy (from guest's argument, no numbered process in source):**
1. List what actually differentiates your brand: assortment, layout, supplier choices, brand persona. Keep these in-house.
2. List what is commodity technical capability: ranking algorithms, query understanding, data normalization across a large catalog. Treat these as buy/partner candidates.
3. Honestly estimate the ongoing cost of owning the infrastructure (headcount, opportunity cost of engineering time, distraction from brand strategy) against a vendor's track record across many clients.
4. If choosing a vendor, require them to state the business objectives the tool is optimized for (margin, relevance, private-label visibility) rather than treating it as a black box.
5. Confirm current integration, data and reporting requirements directly with the vendor before committing; the source does not give specifics and product capabilities change. [ee1d80f4de9d]

**B. Scoping an AI/ML search or merchandising project (from guest's argument):**
1. Write the business objective in plain business terms first (e.g. "increase margin per visitor," not "add AI").
2. Decide which signals the model may use (purchase history, browsing, query terms, seasonality, margin/COGS data) and which it must not.
3. Define the outcome metric you will judge the project by in advance (margin per visitor, conversion, average order value).
4. Match the technology configuration to that objective — a margin-tuned engine will rank differently than a pure-relevance or pure-popularity engine.
5. Reassign merchandiser time from manual term/layout monitoring to setting strategy, personas and assortment direction.
6. Monitor live results and correct the model's ranking rather than assuming it is right from day one. [800f306a36a5]

**C. Setting up Margin Per Visitor as a metric (from card, extending the guest's description):**
1. Define MPV as selected basket-margin total divided by unique visitors in the period, including visitors who did not buy. Use a separate "margin per session" label if measuring sessions instead of visitors.
2. Decide and state which costs are deducted from revenue to reach "margin" — product cost alone will not capture heavy support, returns or fulfillment cost; include these where you are trying to measure contribution.
3. Apply the same margin lens to customer segments (lifetime margin value, not lifetime revenue).
4. Feed the resulting margin signal into search/recommendation ranking so relevant, comparable-relevance items favor higher-margin or private-label placement.
5. Track MPV over comparable periods for your own store; do not use the source's historical industry-margin numbers as your benchmark. [0272f48ebf68]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check landing position of key searches.** How: test whether important queries return the target item on page 1 vs. page 2/3. Good: item surfaces immediately. Bad: item buried past page 1. Next step if bad: expect increased phone/service contacts and abandoned sessions; treat it as a lost-sale signal, not a minor UX flaw. [668dd6245995], [6d5eea41344a]
- **Check for ambiguous-query handling.** How: test short/ambiguous terms (the source's example: "BO") within different browsing contexts. Good: results shift based on section/context (boating vs. footwear vs. hunting). Bad: same generic result regardless of context. [86cba9e8253e]
- **Check per-SKU cost-of-goods data quality before trusting MPV.** How: audit whether COGS is populated and current across the catalog. Good: clean, current per-SKU cost data. Bad: missing or stale COGS. Next step if bad: treat MPV as directionally useful only until data is cleaned up; do not make major spend decisions on a dirty MPV figure. [0272f48ebf68]
- **Check whether merchandisers spend time on manual monitoring vs. strategy.** How: audit merchandiser time allocation (dead-term checking, layout policing) vs. strategic work (assortment, supplier, persona decisions). Good: strategic work dominates. Bad: manual monitoring dominates — a sign the store is under-investing in automation or over-invested in a self-built system that needs constant tending. [ee1d80f4de9d], [800f306a36a5]
- **Check personalization for assortment narrowing.** How: monitor whether history-based recommendations are excluding new or discovery items from visibility over time. Good: personalization still surfaces new/seasonal items. Bad: shoppers only ever see what they already bought. [84c660fc619e]
- **Check checkout/account flow for repeated logins or excessive page-throughs.** How: walk the flow as a returning and a new user. Good: minimal, non-repeated authentication and few screens to purchase. Bad: forced re-login or many sequential pages — a source-cited abandonment driver. [668dd6245995]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Building proprietary search/AI/personalization technology in-house instead of partnering, because retailers cannot reliably out-compete major tech firms for engineering talent, and the ongoing infrastructure burden drains focus from brand strategy. [ee1d80f4de9d]
- Ranking search and recommendation results purely by popularity, which ignores individual fit (size, need, context) and ignores the business's own margin and promotional goals. [86cba9e8253e]
- Deploying "AI for AI's sake" — technology added for its own sake or to satisfy investor narratives, without a defined business objective or outcome metric. [800f306a36a5]
- Measuring only revenue per visitor / top-line growth without a margin lens, especially as cost of goods rises and margins compress industry-wide. [0272f48ebf68], [31f92baa2ab2]
- Forcing repeated logins or long page-through sequences in cart, checkout or account flows, which the source links directly to shoppers not returning. [668dd6245995]
- Ignoring browsing/behavioral context when resolving ambiguous search queries, producing generic results in a case where the section or history should have disambiguated. [86cba9e8253e]
- Leaving merchandisers stuck on manual, reactive monitoring (checking dead search terms, policing layouts after the fact — the guest's "it rained yesterday, I should have brought an umbrella" analogy) instead of predictive tooling plus strategic work. [ee1d80f4de9d], [800f306a36a5]
- Growth-at-all-costs selling below cost to defend market share without treating it as a deliberate, costed strategic choice. [31f92baa2ab2]
