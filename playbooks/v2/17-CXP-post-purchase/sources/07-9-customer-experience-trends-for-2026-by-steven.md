## 1. What this source is for

Gives a CX leader a forecast of how AI and LLMs will change three things: how customers search and buy, what a go-to-market funnel looks like, and how post-purchase support and service are run. It is a strategy and prioritization input for post-purchase and AI-roadmap planning, not an implementation guide. Explicitly out of scope per the source's own header: paid ad campaign strategy and ROAS tracking (routed to 06-MEA) and organic social content planning (routed to 13-ORG). Use this source to decide *what to invest in next*, not *how to build it*; it gives almost no build-level detail.

## 3. Procedures

The talk itself gives no numbered how-to steps; the following sequences come from the cards built on this source and are included here as the closest thing to procedures. Cite the card id when using them.

**A. Audit algorithmic visibility with unbranded LLM prompts** [9d2f6feec1cd]
1. List unbranded questions an ideal customer would ask an AI assistant, covering main categories, price tiers and use cases.
2. Run each prompt repeatedly across the LLMs that matter to the brand (source names ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini); record which brands are named, in what order, and whether the brand appears at all.
3. Repeat runs on different days; treat any single run as a noisy signal, not a fixed ranking, since the presenter reports inconsistent results across runs and industries.
4. Note which third-party sources the models appear to lean on (review sites, forums, retailer listings, press) to know which surfaces to influence.
5. Re-run the same prompt set periodically as a visibility tracker and tie changes back to PR, review-generation or content work.
6. Set an internal decision rule: if a meaningful minority of the audience starts searches on AI platforms, treat invisibility there as a revenue risk worth budget.

**B. Collect genuine product-experience reviews to feed AI discovery** [291fd5b847c5]
1. Inventory where the brand is already discussed by third parties: review platforms, forums, comparison articles, press, creator content.
2. Collect honest reviews without filtering requests to only satisfied buyers; treat the source's claimed AI-influence link as a hypothesis to test, not a fact.
3. Build review collection into post-purchase flows (thank-you pages, email, packaging inserts) to keep volume and recency growing.
4. Encourage customers to describe specific use cases and outcomes, since detailed mentions give models more to retrieve and cite.
5. Support presence in the communities and comparison sites identified in procedure A, step 4, without fabricating reviews.
6. Measure impact by re-running the procedure-A prompt audit over time.

**C. Redesign the funnel for AI-platform commerce** [7c1202336d90]
1. Map current funnel stages (awareness, consideration, purchase, support) and mark which a customer could already complete inside an AI platform instead of the brand's own site.
2. Check whether the AI platforms the brand's customers use support in-platform purchasing or app integrations; verify current merchant/app-listing requirements directly with each platform. `[web check: current in-platform commerce requirements for ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini]`
3. Represent product data, pricing, availability and brand personality in whatever structured form these platforms consume.
4. Decide a stance on in-platform checkout versus driving traffic back to the brand's own store, weighing margin, data ownership and convenience.
5. Set a trigger for a full go-to-market rethink: the source's own heuristic is roughly 10-20% of customers using this flow (an illustrative figure, not measured data).
6. Reassess quarterly, since the presenter expects the environment to shift materially within about a year.

**D. Turn support into a profit center** [298b23ff39c4]
1. Establish separate baseline metrics: cost per contact and return per contact, so any shift can be proven rather than assumed.
2. Give support agents/bots complete, accurate customer, order and history context.
3. Resolve the issue first, then surface a relevant recommendation, keeping it non-intrusive.
4. Automate high-volume, low-complexity contacts to push cost per contact down; keep humans for emotional or complex cases.
5. Train recommendation logic on individual fit, not generic best-sellers; monitor acceptance and complaint rates.
6. Track the cost-per-contact/return-per-contact ratio over time as the signal of the cost-to-profit shift.

**E. Build a 24/7 coaching assistant** [52869049dbd3]
1. Identify where the brand holds genuine, repeat-need category expertise.
2. Design the assistant around that expertise (source's example: a beauty assistant for hair/skin/nail questions).
3. Scope around ongoing customer value, not campaign messaging.
4. Tie the assistant to post-purchase moments: setup, usage, troubleshooting, replenishment.
5. Feed it the customer's own order history and preferences; make opt-in and data use clear.
6. Measure retention, repeat purchase and engagement, not chat volume.

**F. Build brand agents instead of generic bots** [7de1741d6532]
1. Define the brand's voice in concrete terms (tone, vocabulary, pacing, what it would never say) before writing dialogue flows.
2. Draft sample conversations and have people familiar with the brand judge whether it is recognizably the brand's voice.
3. Match personality to use case rather than one generic helpful-assistant tone.
4. Protect the voice through every system update so it doesn't flatten to generic phrasing at scale.
5. Monitor voice consistency alongside resolution and conversion metrics; review transcripts regularly for drift.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Scoping hyperpersonalization as only marketing/communication personalization, missing the larger customer-value opportunity. [52869049dbd3]
- Making upsell offers before resolving the customer's actual issue, or making them feel intrusive. [298b23ff39c4]
- Spending AI investment only on efficiency and missing the emotional-value opportunity, which the presenter calls a "blue ocean." [27961d818a69]
- Assuming AI-driven service perfection will stay a differentiator; the presenter says it will become "a commodity" and "boring" once broadly achieved. [6893cdd6a985]
- Defaulting to off-the-shelf, generic AI agent technology, which "flattens" the brand's differentiating voice. [7de1741d6532]
- Card-added caution (not stated by the presenter in the transcript): fabricating or soliciting only positive reviews to game AI visibility breaches platform rules and undermines the trust signal being built.
