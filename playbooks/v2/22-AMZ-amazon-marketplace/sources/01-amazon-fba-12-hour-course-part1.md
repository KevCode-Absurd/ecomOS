## 1. What this source is for

Gives a beginner the full order of operations to start selling on Amazon: which seller-account setup avoids getting banned, how to run retail/online arbitrage as a low-capital starting point, how to pick a product (a "passion product," not generic private label), how to brand it, and how to start sourcing a manufacturer. This part is the "before you have a product on Amazon" half of the course: account setup, arbitrage, product research, branding, and the opening of manufacturer sourcing. It is aimed at someone with little to no e-commerce experience and little capital, and repeatedly frames "passion product" (a differentiated product the seller is personally interested in) as superior to generic private label, wholesale, or arbitrage as a long-term model.

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Amazon seller-account signup (US-based example in the source) `[verify UI: this entire flow]`
1. Go to sell.amazon.com, click Sign Up.
2. Choose plan: Professional ($39.99/month, no per-item fee) if expecting >40 units/month; Individual (free signup, $0.99/item fee, link hidden at bottom of the signup page) otherwise. `[8009156d02116fccefaf7338]`
3. Use a business email address, not a personal Gmail/Yahoo (reduces "not a serious seller" flag).
4. Have ready: phone number (Google Voice acceptable), a CREDIT card (never a debit or prepaid card — source says this triggers suspension), passport or driver's license (front+back), a bank statement (downloaded PDF, not a photo).
5. Select business location and entity type. If unsure, select "individual seller" and use your SSN (US-based). Non-US sellers: consider forming a US LLC first for an easier signup.
6. If forming an LLC: options named are wyregisteredagent.net (Wyoming) or LegalZoom (Delaware); if you live in the US, source recommends forming in your home state instead (his own CA LLC: $800/yr). DBA (~$99 via LegalZoom) is a cheaper alternative that enables a business bank account/card but gives NO personal-asset protection; you can upgrade DBA → LLC later. `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
7. Select business type (privately-owned is the default case for most viewers).
8. Enter company registration number = EIN. Get a free EIN in minutes at irs.gov (source: do not pay a third party for this). `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
9. Enter registered business address (home address OK only if the business is registered there; otherwise use a registered-agent service for privacy/non-US sellers).
10. Verify business phone via SMS PIN.
11. Enter personal info: citizenship/birth country, passport or driver's license number + expiration.
12. Confirm sole owner or add co-owner info.
13. Enter bank account info (business or personal OK for an individual seller, but keep them separate where possible); if the bank isn't instantly verifiable, a bank statement upload is required later. US: source recommends US Bank (free business account). Non-US: Payoneer or WorldFirst. `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
14. Enter credit card for the monthly fee/ad spend/(if individual plan) per-item fees.
15. Choose store name (does not need to match LLC/brand name; changeable later).
16. Answer: do you have a UPC? (Needed for a new product; get one at gs1.org, ~$30 for a single code, or a multi-pack of 5-10 for less per unit at other UPC resellers.) `[608f261d2998793868d55fae]`
17. Answer diversity-certification question (optional).
18. Answer: private label / own product → Yes. Wholesale/arbitrage (reselling others' branded products) → No.
19. Answer: do you own a registered trademark? Optional at signup; you can say No and add Brand Registry later once you've filed (not necessarily approved) a trademark. `[9ea0db787b781ae064a6d0e2]`
20. Upload ID (passport preferred, or license front/back) and bank statement PDF. Submit.
21. Possible live video verification call: agent checks your ID against your face and your submitted data; mismatches (e.g. a wrong birthdate) trigger a correction requirement and can add up to 48 hours to approval. Calls cannot be recorded (Amazon policy per source).
22. Amazon may request additional docs post-submission (e.g. a utility bill for address proof); if you can't produce one, a registered-agent service is one workaround the source mentions. `[web check: current Amazon identity-verification requirements]`

### 3.3 Retail arbitrage — sourcing to shipment `[verify UI: Seller Central screens]`
1. Set up a seller account (3.1) and install the Amazon Seller app on your phone, logged in. `[42f7ff54e5abc190d97e1c76]`
2. At a store (worked example: Costco), scan each candidate barcode in the Seller app: check current Amazon sell price, estimated monthly units sold, gated/ungated status, and estimated FBA fees.
3. Reject if store cost + inbound shipping to Amazon + FBA fee ≥ Amazon sell price. Worked example: item sells $15 more on Amazon than Costco cost, FBA fees ~$5 → ~$10/unit profit; 100 units/month selling → bought 15 units.
4. Confirm the item is NOT gated before buying (gated = requires brand approval, not guaranteed).
5. Confirm the UPC (and, if mismatched, the model number) lines up with the existing Amazon listing.
6. Note: consumable/cosmetic products need Amazon's own FN SKU label, not just the manufacturer UPC, even on an otherwise-matching product. `[175a95a101de6388bc083b57]`
7. In Seller Central: Manage All Inventory → find/match the existing listing → set quantity, price, SKU, fulfillment = FBA.
8. Answer the dangerous-goods questionnaire accurately (worked example: alkaline AAA batteries packed with product, declared not dangerous goods).
9. Choose "use manufacturer barcode" or "print & apply" Amazon's own label (print & apply is safer if you're unsure the manufacturer barcode will scan).
10. Enter box dimensions/weight (measured; system may warn on mismatches but can be overridden with "confirm and continue").
11. Print shipping labels (thermal printer or plain paper + tape), apply with barcodes visible, ship via UPS Ground (or carrier of choice).
12. Track the shipment status (in transit → received → closed) in Seller Central's Shipments page; check the Problems tab is empty.
13. Once live, monitor: adjust price manually or set an automatic min/max price band so the listing stays competitive for the Buy Box without manual checks (worked example: min $36.99, max $44.99).
14. Monitor seller feedback (Feedback Manager): if a buyer mistakenly leaves a PRODUCT complaint as SELLER feedback, request removal (must contest within 30 days or it becomes permanent); if legitimate, either accept it or message the buyer to negotiate a resolution. `[8446fdb9deaa5ee290e92ce7]`
15. If Amazon damages the item in their warehouse, that is Amazon's fault — you can request compensation.
16. Restock when Seller Central signals fast sell-through or you observe an item selling out; size the reorder against observed monthly velocity, not against how much cash you want to deploy. `[b3b6582d3684baa5922de659]`

### 3.4 Online arbitrage
1. Same core logic as retail arbitrage, but source from retailer websites (worked examples: target.com, walmart.com, biglots.com) via their deals/clearance/weekly-ad pages instead of in-store.
2. Avoid heavy/bulky items (shipping cost kills margin); favor small, light items.
3. Use the Amazon Seller app to check gating and fees the same way.
4. Compare against Amazon's 90-DAY MEDIAN price, not the current spot price, since a temporary low-stock spike will revert once competitors restock. `[579eb32aa6996331795fa095]`
5. Use the FBA Revenue Calculator (enter package/product dimensions and item price) to get a fee breakdown before buying. Worked example output: fulfillment cost $3.87, Amazon fee $4.88, fixed closing fee $0.88 (only charged on the INDIVIDUAL plan, not professional).
6. If available, pick up in-store instead of having it shipped to you first, to avoid double shipping (retailer→you→Amazon).
7. Ship inventory directly from retailer to Amazon FBA if the retailer will include proper labels + invoice.

### 3.5 Seven product-research methods (source's own numbered list)
1. **Helium 10 Magnet**, title-density filter set to 0-0: Tools → Magnet (not Blackbox/Trendster) → enter a personal-interest seed term → sort by Magnet IQ score → scan every keyword row (including page 2+) for a phrase with real search volume but NO matching product on Amazon yet. `[032cac3738fc85768568aaf4]`
2. **Free — Amazon Best Sellers + New Releases**: browse New Releases specifically for fast-validating, already-selling recent launches; check "first available" date to gauge how new/immature a competitor is; read 1-3 star reviews of a strong seller to find a fixable flaw to build into your own version. Do NOT clone exactly. `[85e208c54bc91df455bb665c]`
3. **Helium 10 Blackbox**: set category (avoid electronics/most appliances/CDs-vinyl-books as a beginner), price min $15/max $85, a monthly-revenue range (worked example $5,000-$80,000), and a MAX average review rating (worked example 3.5 stars) to surface high-selling but poorly-reviewed listings you can beat. `[e3b79ddc217f9563d4fa8927]`
4. **Helium 10 Cerebro** (reverse-ASIN): paste a competitor's ASIN → Get Keywords → sort by Magnet IQ score → look for a high-volume phrase with no purpose-built listing. `[0c2c13f7d9ce7e8bddecefd2]`
5. **Amazon search-bar autocomplete**: type single letters/short stems, note top suggestions (ranked by search popularity); cross-check candidates with Helium 10 X-ray for estimated monthly revenue. `[0d48e4ef7ef2111a2b647ae8]`
6. Cross-reference with **Google Trends** (rising vs falling; a spike could be dying or still rising, can't tell from Trends alone) and **Google Keyword Planner** ("Discover new keywords") for related phrases, then confirm real Amazon demand for that exact phrase back in Helium 10 (Google interest ≠ Amazon purchase intent). `[0d48e4ef7ef2111a2b647ae8]`
7. **Amazon full-category-list narrowing**: from Best Sellers' left-nav (20,000+ subcategories), uncheck every category you have zero interest in, optionally score remaining ones 1-5 for interest, then drill into Best Sellers for a kept subcategory and check standout products' revenue via X-ray. Companion move: take that bestseller's ASIN into Cerebro, filter title density to 0, and look for a high-volume generic phrase the incumbent's title is missing.
   - Plus a personal, tool-free **"idea generator" exercise**: list everything you interact with in a normal day, then for each item ask (a) what makes it 5-star, (b) what would make you pay double, (c) what would make you tell friends, (d) what's your biggest pain point and how could you solve it, (e) what invention would make it 5-star. Repeat with an activities list. If still stuck, mine your credit-card history, Amazon browse/wish-list history, and Google/podcast history for interest patterns.

### 3.7 Crowdfunding a first production run (AJ / Cocktail Cards pattern)
1. Build a $0-cost paper/stationery prototype first (source example: hand-drawn flashcards with Sharpies). `[0508b31155e4a93adbdc4e90]`
2. Find a manufacturer already making an ADJACENT product (not your exact spec) via Alibaba; ask for a free leftover/blank sample from a past project, pay only shipping (~$50 in this case).
3. Get a designer to produce logo/box/card art on a small deposit (not full payment) before a first crowdfunding round.
4. Set a funding goal tied to your very next specific cost (worked example: $1,500 = the designer's deposit), and drive traffic to the campaign with content in your niche (YouTube/TikTok/Instagram).
5. Run the campaign past the initial goal; use funds to pay for design and then for manufacturing + freight. `[bd7b6cb000906ca89dec7840]` `[cfd113d1ee3bee7fd9a534b6]`
6. Once a finished printed sample exists, post a story-driven video (your journey + the finished sample) to try to go viral and spike pre-orders.
7. Choose freight mode: air (~5-7 days, materially more expensive) vs sea (~$2/kg, 6-week minimum) vs a split of both if you have partial urgency (worked example: AJ split half/half, air portion ~$4,200). Full air freight when not truly urgent is "throwing money down the drain" per AJ's own hindsight. `[dd5ab39da4c610dcfe897f72]`
8. Fulfill crowdfunding backers by hand first (700+ orders in AJ's case, with friends/family help), then send remaining stock into FBA.
9. While first FBA stock is in transit/checking in (~1 month), keep building an email list of interested buyers to concentrate day-one sales and reviews once live (ties into Amazon's search-ranking algorithm rewarding early sales/reviews velocity).

### 3.8 Manufacturer/supplier sourcing — methods started in this part `[verify UI: all platform screens]`
1. **Alibaba.com**: search product category → filter "Trade Assurance" (Alibaba escrows payment, refundable if the order isn't fulfilled as agreed) and "Verified Supplier" (third-party-inspected, confirmed real company) → switch to "All Suppliers" view (not the product-listing view) → vet on years in business, sales volume, staff size, rating/review count (worked example: 4.9 rating, 200+ reviews called "probably very reputable"), quality-control process, production capacity, R&D capacity. `[516fc94151074c07881a0fc8]`
2. **ThomasNet.com**: US-domestic alternative; profile shows location, founding year, employee count, capabilities, certifications, diversity status. Prefer domestic sourcing when safety/label trust matters (e.g. added-caffeine dosing) even at higher cost. `[409cdd9f62e7eb228ac8ada9]`
3. **Google search + cold contact**: search "[product] manufacturers / private labelers / suppliers," then call (preferred for US manufacturers — builds connection, more likely to yield a referral even on a "no") or email.
4. **Outreach positioning tactics** (source presents both, calls it a trade-off, not one rule): (a) present as COO/"chief purchasing agent" of a larger company rather than founder/CEO — useful later for negotiating ("my boss says no" depersonalizes pushback); (b) be transparent as a passionate beginner asking for education — builds empathy but risks being taken advantage of on terms.
5. **Mass cold-email case study (Rocket Tea, presenter: William)**: one templated email, sent to 50-60 manufacturers found by paging through Google results pages 1-10 (not stopping at page 1), tracked in a spreadsheet (contacted / replied), non-responders mostly written off unless an ideal fit. `[eb3555923ac1d72f054b4643]`
   - Most traditional tea manufacturers refused the concept (adding synthetic caffeine powder to dry leaves) as against their process/values; one implied a competitor claiming to already do it must be lying.
   - A phone call with a refusing manufacturer revealed the ONLY method they knew (wetting the leaves, risking mold/rot) — treat refusal calls as free technical education to refine your pitch for the next call. `[d17ff4715cfafd747e2c1ef8]`
   - Eventually found 1-2 specialty tea blenders willing to attempt a proprietary process (undisclosed, even to the client).
   - Questions to ask a willing manufacturer: minimum order quantity, lead time, flavor/blend options, packaging compatibility, sustainability options (this vendor offered biodegradable, string/tag-free tea bags).
6. **One manufacturer rarely covers everything**: William's tea blender could blend/fill but not package — ask your primary manufacturer for a referral to fill the gap (a US packaging house with low MOQ, in this case) rather than sourcing that piece cold. `[52da1e0b76d95629d7ca3776]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Before buying an arbitrage unit**: scan with the Amazon Seller app → check gated status, current price, est. monthly units, est. fees. Good = ungated, store cost + fees clearly under Amazon price, decent monthly units. Bad = gated (approval not guaranteed) or margin erased by fees → skip. `[42f7ff54e5abc190d97e1c76]`
- **Before committing to a product idea**: does it sit in $15-$85, avoid restricted/seasonal/trend categories, have high search demand AND low competition together (source says missing either one kills profitability), solve a real problem, and is the category not brand-dominated (check for a Nike/Adidas-style incumbent)? `[9592e17b4a2363d6d9075817]` `[6b24a59a41c19e832a3b7c9e]`
- **Price-disparity check**: search the category on Amazon — if prices cluster tightly (worked example: floss picks $2-$16, called "relatively tiny" spread and a bad sign), buyers default to cheapest and there's no room for a premium play; a WIDE legitimate price spread (e.g. $5 vs $95) signals buyers aren't purely price-driven. `[6b24a59a41c19e832a3b7c9e]`
- **Review-mining check**: read 1-3 star reviews of a strong-selling competitor to find a specific, fixable flaw (worked examples: UPC sticker impossible to remove; product not covering gray hair; goggles causing "raccoon eyes") — build the fix into your version and say so in your listing. `[85e208c54bc91df455bb665c]` `[e3b79ddc217f9563d4fa8927]`
- **Monthly P&L check (all 3 case studies use the same 4-line model)**: revenue − product cost − referral fee (15%) − FBA pick/pack fee − PPC spend = net profit. Read revenue as gross, never as profit; a stockout month should not be read as a demand signal (it understates true demand). `[4e439ec1d0c9079c2bb25f47]` `[2f107a408cee6c5811a9c9dd]`
- **Inventory-health check every reorder cycle**: is a stockout visible weeks ahead (units sold/week vs. units on hand + in production)? All 3 case studies in this source hit exactly this failure mode multiple times each. `[6804638a759cc1a943e9dc92]`
- **Seasonal-decline check before abandoning a product**: map revenue by month for a full year before concluding a product has failed — a source's own case (AJ) showed a January/February dip tied to an external seasonal factor (New Year "dry January" for a cocktail-adjacent product), not product failure; margin can hold up even as revenue dips if landed cost per unit improves (e.g. switching air→sea freight). `[2e76599ac49d0d80fc787d97]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Opening a duplicate seller account without telling Amazon first (ban risk).
- Changing bank info/address in the first 1-2 months post-approval (scam-signal red flag).
- Signing up with a debit or prepaid card instead of a credit card (source claims this triggers suspension — unverified against current policy). `[web check]`
- Picking the cheapest freelancer without reviewing their portfolio (all 3 case studies/examples in this source touch this).
- Choosing a generic, non-differentiated private-label product (Cam's case, and the source's general framing).
- Launching with too many SKUs/color variants at once, fragmenting a small first order (Cam's case).
- Under-ordering inventory out of fear — the single most-repeated, most costly mistake across every case study (Brent, Cam, AJ all independently hit this).
- Choosing full air freight when not truly urgent ("throwing money down the drain" — AJ's own words).
- Delaying a launch to keep researching (analysis paralysis) — framed as the single most common mistake the presenter sees across students generally, not just in this source's 3 case studies.
- Not outsourcing/delegating non-revenue tasks soon enough (AJ's case).
- Selling seasonal or trend/fad products as a new seller with thin capital buffer.
- Selling in a brand-dominated category (e.g. footwear vs. Nike/Adidas/Skechers/Crocs).
- Selling a product with a tight, undifferentiated price cluster (no pricing room — the floss-pick example).
- Linking a refund to a review removal request, even indirectly (flagged here as a policy risk in the source's own described tactic, not endorsed as safe). `[web check]`
