## 1. What this source is for

Walks a complete beginner through starting Amazon FBA in the UK end to end: choosing FBA over FBM, picking a business model, registering and verifying a seller account, finding and screening a resale product (arbitrage or wholesale), listing an existing ASIN, and building and executing an FBA shipment through carrier pickup. It is reseller-focused (arbitrage and wholesale), not private-label or DTC product-building — the presenter explicitly skips private label "how-to" content in this video.

## 2. Principles

1. Fulfilment model must be chosen before registering a seller account or sourcing stock, because it changes every fee line in a profitability calculation. FBA (Fulfilment by Amazon) means Amazon picks, packs and ships each order for a fee; FBM (Fulfilment by Merchant) means you ship yourself. Condition: any new Amazon selling operation. Evidence: the presenter's stated personal preference for a "lifestyle, semi-passive" business, not a controlled comparison — FBM can still suit heavy, fragile, low-volume or made-to-order goods. [79cb4207ec9548d50a9d074a]
2. Pick one of three business models before sourcing, because each has different capital needs and a different set of screening checks: (1) retail/online arbitrage — buy discounted stock from a shop or its website and resell on Amazon, lowest capital, fastest cash but not scalable and often quantity-capped; (2) wholesale — buy branded stock in bulk from a wholesaler or the brand and resell at retail price, more scalable, no need to create demand or run ads, roughly £1,000 starting capital per the presenter; (3) private label — build your own brand, source from China, create the listing and run ads, roughly £5,000–£10,000 needed per the presenter for stock, ads, images, listing, barcodes and trademark. Condition: decide before doing any product research. Evidence: presenter's own path and stated figures, not audited. [a4f91430f890a0954f1f07d4]
3. Beginners should start with wholesale or arbitrage before private label, in the presenter's opinion, to learn how Amazon works cheaply first. Condition: this is his suggested sequencing, not a rule — arbitrage/wholesale sourcing metrics (BSR, seller count, 30% ROI) do not transfer to private label or a DTC store. [a4f91430f890a0954f1f07d4]
4. A UK seller account needs identity and address verification, done over a live video call, before it can trade. Condition: applies to the UK sign-up flow as filmed in mid-2025; other marketplaces or later dates may differ. Evidence: presenter's own onboarding experience. [f543c38ae44d63d19a5801ea]
5. Do not source or ship stock until verification has passed and payout bank details are added in Seller Central, because you cannot be paid or charged correctly before that. [3f1b8c4c61475e58d0fd861c]
6. Screen every candidate resale ASIN on four pass/fail checks — Best Seller Rank, competing-seller count, whether Amazon itself is on the buy box, and ROI — before buying any stock; all four should pass. Condition: this applies to wholesale/arbitrage resale of an existing ASIN, not to a private-label or DTC listing you create yourself. Evidence: presenter's own sourcing heuristic; he allows one adjustment (~20% ROI instead of 30%) for a fast-selling item. [2e113575bf73f95b1a097ba1]
7. ROI must be calculated on the full cost stack, including a UK-specific 20% VAT charged on top of Amazon's own selling and fulfilment fees, which the standard revenue calculator does not show by default. Condition: UK sellers, including those not VAT-registered themselves. Evidence: presenter calls this a "hidden fee" that "a lot of new Amazon gurus" leave out of their profit estimates. [394a2f9025bbb777515d51ba]
8. Place a small test order (10 to 50 units) before committing to full-volume stock on any newly validated wholesale product. Condition: first order on a new ASIN. Evidence: presenter's downside example — ordering 400 units of a product that turns out gated or undercut leaves your cash trapped in unsellable inventory. [d00e98909a62d33950f44053]
9. List by joining the existing ASIN rather than creating a new listing, price at or near the lowest FBA (not FBM) seller on that listing, and choose Amazon's own barcode rather than the manufacturer's, to stop your stock being commingled with another seller's units on the same ASIN. Condition: reselling an existing branded product via FBA. [cee7349150a1e7c21d4aebc3]
10. Build the FBA shipping plan from accurate unit counts, prep needs, box dimensions and weight before choosing a carrier and shipping mode; box size and weight limits are hard constraints in the tool, not suggestions. [03f3588ec1844114cb3418d4]
11. A shipment is not finished when Amazon shows a tracking number — the process only completes once you print labels and actively book a carrier collection using that tracking ID. [f2e93ddb9b519170f33fbab8]

## 3. Procedures

### A. Register and verify a UK Amazon seller account [verify UI]
1. Search "sell on Amazon" and choose the UK site (amazon.co.uk; presenter notes .com for the US, .ca for Canada).
2. Compare the Individual and Professional selling plans before signing up (see section 4 for the fees quoted in this source) [web check: current plan pricing and features].
3. Click Sign up. Use an existing Amazon shopping account or create a new one — the presenter recommends a dedicated new account.
4. Register under a name that has both a genuine, matching proof of ID and proof of address, since verification checks both documents.
5. Complete the live video verification call with an Amazon employee: hold your ID next to your face on camera and upload your proof-of-address document when asked.
6. Wait for verification. The presenter's own experience: usually completes within 72 hours, sometimes as fast as 1–2 hours [web check: current verification timelines].
7. Once verified, add your bank/payment details in the Seller Central dashboard (used both to pay you and to charge you fees). Do this before sourcing or shipping.
8. If the account is rejected, suspended or deactivated: appeal it yourself, or pay a specialist to prepare a detailed appeal. Reasons the presenter cites: submitted details not matching your ID, a previously suspended account, or missing information Amazon requested.

### B. Find and screen a wholesale product (the presenter's recommended core method for beginners)
1. Search "wholesalers near me" (or equivalent) in Google to find local wholesale suppliers.
2. Visit in person, register a trade account, and get access to the wholesaler's price list (often an Excel sheet, sometimes only available on paper or PDF).
3. Ask which brands they stock; check any candidate brand's products on Amazon.
4. On a candidate ASIN, open "see all information" to find the Best Seller Rank and the ASIN.
5. Open "other sellers on Amazon" on the listing and filter by Prime to count FBA competitors.
6. Open the buy box and check who it says "sold by."
7. Run the ASIN and your intended sell price through the Amazon revenue/FBA calculator, entering your cost of goods, to get profit and ROI.
8. Only proceed if all four checks in section 4 pass (or the ROI exception applies for a fast mover).
9. Place a 10–50 unit test order rather than a full-volume order.
10. Validate the test batch — does it actually sell at your price, are other sellers undercutting you, is the product gated or restricted — before reordering at volume.

### C. Retail/online arbitrage sourcing (presenter's secondary method; his own scope note flags this and its BSR/ROI checks as arbitrage-specific, not private-label/DTC — see section 9)
1. Browse a retailer's sale, clearance or discount section online (presenter's UK example: Argos), focusing on items discounted 50% or more.
2. Copy the product name or barcode and search it on Amazon; compare the Amazon price and seller count to the retailer's sale price.
3. Use the Amazon Seller mobile app's built-in scanner in-store to scan a product directly: it looks up the live listing, shows current fees and estimated profit, and flags whether you are eligible to sell that product before you buy it. [verify UI]
4. Stack a cashback credit card with a cashback website on purchases to add a few percent back to your margin (presenter's example: 1.5% + 1.5% ≈ 3%).
5. Sign up for retailers' email/newsletter lists to get sale alerts sent directly to your inbox.
6. Note per-retailer purchase limits (presenter's example: capped at a maximum of 10 units per checkout on one Argos item) — this caps how much profit any single arbitrage run can produce.

### D. List a validated ASIN in Seller Central [verify UI]
1. Go to Seller Central > Catalog > Add Product.
2. Paste the product's ASIN (found under "see all information" on the Amazon listing) and search, rather than building a new listing from scratch.
3. If the result shows "condition new — apply to sell," the ASIN is gated. To request ungating, buy 10 units (sometimes 100, depending on the category) of the exact product from a wholesale supplier and upload an invoice that matches your Amazon account or company name.
4. Once eligible, set condition to New and click "Sell this product" — you are joining the existing listing, not creating a new one.
5. Set your price to match or sit close to the lowest FBA seller on that listing (filter "other sellers" by Prime to isolate FBA-only competitors; do not match FBM prices).
6. Set fulfilment channel to Amazon (FBA) and disable any marketplaces you do not intend to sell in.
7. At the barcode field, choose "Amazon's barcode only," even though the product already carries a manufacturer barcode — this lets Amazon attribute mixed inbound stock correctly to you instead of confusing it with another seller's units on the same ASIN.
8. Save and finish, then list as FBA. Allow up to about 15 minutes for the offer to appear in your inventory.

### E. Build and confirm an FBA shipping plan [verify UI]
1. From the new inventory offer, use the "send/replenish inventory" (Send to Amazon) workflow.
2. Enter the ship-from address.
3. Choose prep and labeling: "No prep needed" is the default; fragile items (the presenter's example: glass) need bubble wrap or similar prep. Decide who labels the units: "buy seller" (you label them yourself) or pay Amazon to do it (presenter cites 38p per unit — treat as historical and check the current fee) [web check: current Amazon labeling fee].
4. Enter the unit count being sent for this SKU; add an expiry date field if the product carries one.
5. On the label-printing step, choose a standard format (A4 self-adhesive label sheets on a normal printer) or thermal format depending on your printer, then print the SKU/unit labels.
6. Pack individual units. If selling a multi-pack, put the units inside a poly bag and label the poly bag itself, not each individual unit inside it.
7. Enter packing information: number of boxes (the presenter's example fits everything in one box) and that box's length, width and height, plus its weight. The tool rejects a box if any side exceeds 63.5cm or the weight exceeds 23kg [web check: confirm current FBA box size/weight limits].
8. Choose a ship date.
9. Choose shipping mode: small parcel delivery for boxed shipments, or pallet for larger loads.
10. Choose a carrier. The presenter recommends UPS (Amazon-partnered; DPD is also available) as the cheaper option in his experience.
11. Review the destination fulfilment centre Amazon assigns (his example: BHX4). Do not attempt to drop shipments off directly at an Amazon warehouse — this is not permitted.
12. Accept the shipping charges to confirm the shipment. The presenter says you then have up to 24 hours to cancel without being charged [web check: confirm current cancellation policy] — use this window if the shipment was only a test.
13. Print the box label and carrier label together on an A4 label sheet or thermal printer, using the size template Seller Central specifies (his example: 1 x 139mm).
14. Copy the tracking ID from "view tracking details" — do not treat this step as the end of the process.

### F. Schedule the UPS collection
1. Go to UPS's schedule-a-pickup page.
2. Confirm "yes" to having a prepaid UPS shipping label.
3. Enter the tracking ID from Seller Central; separate multiple tracking IDs with commas if sending more than one shipment.
4. Enter your collection address, the number of packages/boxes, and their total combined weight.
5. If the total weight exceeds 32kg, flag this on the relevant field.
6. Choose a collection date and a 4-hour collection window (the presenter's UK examples: 11:00–15:00 or 12:00–17:00; the window cannot be narrowed further) [web check: current UPS UK windows], or choose a drop-off point instead of a collection if preferred.
7. Review the details and click Confirm. From collection onward, Amazon handles picking, packing and shipping to the end customer — no further seller action is needed per order.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Skipping the four-check screen (BSR, seller count, Amazon-in-buybox, ROI) and buying stock anyway.
- Jumping to a full-volume order instead of a 10–50 unit test batch on a new product.
- Selling on a single-seller (likely private-label) listing — risks the listing owner complaining and Amazon blocking the account on repeated occurrences.
- Forgetting to manually add the UK's 20% VAT on Amazon's own fees when estimating net profit — called out as an omission common among "new Amazon gurus" making similar videos.
- Using the manufacturer's barcode instead of Amazon's own barcode, risking inventory being commingled with another seller's units on the same ASIN.
- Assuming a shipment is finished once a tracking number appears in Seller Central — a carrier collection still has to be actively booked.
- Trying to drop a shipment off directly at an Amazon fulfilment centre — not permitted.
- Registering the seller account under a name that does not match the submitted proof of ID/address, risking rejection.
- Starting with private label as a complete beginner before learning the platform via wholesale or arbitrage first (presenter's stated opinion, not a hard rule).
- Choosing the free Individual plan for a real business, since the per-sale fee (75p + VAT) plus its unit cap limit growth compared with the Professional plan (about £30/month), in the presenter's view.
