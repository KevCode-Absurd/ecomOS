**A. Register the Amazon seller account**
1. Decide plan before signing up: Individual ($0.99/unit plus other selling fees, no Buy Box eligibility, harder ungating) vs Professional ($39.99/month flat, Buy Box eligible, easier ungating). `[verify UI: current plan names, fees and feature gating]`
2. Go to sell.amazon.com and click sign up. `[verify UI]`
3. Have ready before starting: a government-issued ID (driver's license or passport — source has heard, unverified, that passport may process faster; a student or library card will not work), a payment method for the ~$39.99 first-month charge (a debit card works even if the page implies credit-card-only; if your bank isn't listed, scroll down, select "other," and try different cards), bank routing and account number for payouts, tax information (SSN, or EIN if you have an LLC), phone number, name, and email (source recommends a separate business email, not required).
4. Confirm you are 18 or older; this is required to open the account.
5. Note: per the source, both plans are charged the ~$39.99 first-month fee at signup; under Professional it recurs monthly with active listings, under Individual it does not recur. `[web check: confirm current signup fee behavior for both plans]`
6. LLC/EIN is optional at this stage. The source formed hers in her 6th month selling, after crossing $10k in monthly sales in month 5 — a personal timing choice, not a stated rule for when to incorporate.

**B. Buy materials (if not using a prep center)**
1. Buy a printer, 2x1 labels, poly bags, and a label set. The source points to a materials list in her own video description. `[sponsored/affiliate: verify this list before treating any linked product as neutral]` `[web check: current link, if still live]`
2. Get 4x6 shipping labels and boxes free: from UPS, or by reusing boxes from local retail/beauty-supply stores (source suggests calling ahead and asking which days they receive truck deliveries and discard boxes).
3. Skip this step entirely if outsourcing prep to a prep center from the start; they supply materials and do this work.

**C. Get ungated per brand/category**
1. Check gating status for a brand/category in your sourcing tool (e.g., SellerAmp flags whether you're ungated or need approval, and flags IP/private-label alerts). `[verify UI/tool]`
2. Purchase the number of units the ungating application requires from a wholesaler, distributor, or authorized reseller, to get an invoice. Source's baseline is 10 units; she notes Amazon has sometimes required 100 instead, and suggests trying your existing 10-unit invoices first since some of her students have had that work. `[web check: current per-category invoice/unit requirements — these vary by category and change over time]`
3. Submit the invoice, plus (often required) photos of the total units purchased, through the Seller Central ungating/approval application. `[verify UI]`
4. Expect denial on the first attempts; reapply. Make the details reviewers need to see stand out (source calls this "highlighting") since, per the source, reviewers are non-native English speakers processing applications at high speed (~30 seconds each, her claim, unverified).
5. Target roughly 10-15 ungated brands/categories before scaling up sourcing volume and software spend.

**D. Buy the software stack (only after step C)**
1. SellerAmp: main sourcing/product-analysis tool. Shows monthly sales velocity, competitor count/stock/price, your ungating status, IP/private-label alerts, and Buy Box win percentage by seller. `[sponsored: source has a referral link and mentions a free trial]` `[web check: current pricing/features]`
2. Keepa: price history and trend tracking, used to judge whether a current price is stable or likely to fall. `[web check: current pricing/features]`
3. SellerBoard: add only once you have active listings. Tracks whether Amazon received all shipped units, gives a template for filing reimbursement claims with Seller Support, automates review-request emails (~5 days after delivery), and tracks Amazon's fee deductions. Source cites a 2-month free trial through her own link. `[sponsored/affiliate]` `[web check: current pricing/features]`
4. Check for free or extended trials before committing to an annual or monthly plan on any of the three.

**E. Source inventory**
1. Choose retail arbitrage (scan products in physical stores using the SellerAmp app) or online arbitrage (source from online retailers from a computer, ship to home or a prep center).
2. Retail arbitrage: source reports less listing competition and sometimes better deals, at the cost of not being scalable (you must physically buy, carry, prep, and ship each unit).
3. Online arbitrage: scalable from a computer, at the cost of more competition — the same product found by you can be found by someone else within minutes, and by the time your unit ships the price may have dropped $10-20 from when you found it, erasing your margin.
4. Do full diligence per product (sales volume, seller count, price history) before buying; source attributes most sourcing losses to shallow, fast, low-diligence sourcing rather than to the arbitrage model itself.
5. Trial both methods and shift toward the one that fits your time/capital; source's own current mix (at time of filming) leans more OA than her early RA-heavy start.

**F. Prep and label units**
1. Remove all visible original price/clearance stickers from the product before shipping.
2. Print free 2x1 FNSKU labels from Seller Central and apply them over the product's UPC code. `[verify UI: label-printing flow inside Seller Central]`
3. Use standard materials from step B (printer, 2x1 labels, poly bags, label set, free 4x6 shipping labels and boxes).
4. Create the shipment in Seller Central and send it to Amazon following the current shipping workflow. `[verify UI: source notes the shipping workflow layout changes often]`
5. If using an outsourced prep center, they perform this entire step; source used one in Florida and, at time of filming, was looking for one in a tax-free state instead.

**G. Track inbound inventory**
1. In Seller Central, go to Manage All Inventory. `[verify UI]`
2. Under the SKU condition column, scroll to the "FBA Inventory Breakdown" section. `[verify UI: source notes this section is not widely known and its layout can change]`
3. Read the fields: available units, and reserved units broken into FC transfer, FC processing, or held for a customer order.
4. FC transfer = Amazon is redistributing your shipment across multiple fulfillment centers. This is normal and expected, not an error, even if it means only some of your units show as received at any one FC.
5. FC processing = units have arrived at their final warehouse and are being opened, scanned in, and shelved. This is described as the slowest phase.
6. Typical timing (source's own benchmarks, outside Q4): transit to Amazon ~3-4 days; check-in/processing 1-2 weeks is normal. Beyond 2 weeks, start looking into reimbursement. In Q4 (October-December), check-in can legitimately take up to a month due to volume — treat that as seasonal, not a loss signal.
7. If units are not received in full, use SellerBoard's template to file a reimbursement claim through Seller Support.

**H. Reprice listings**
1. Check newly active listings daily at first; prices can move before your own check-in even finishes (source's example: $50 down to $30).
2. Reprice manually while listing count is low — no software needed at this stage.
3. Once you exceed roughly 25 active listings, manual daily repricing stops being practical. Invest in repricing software at that point; source personally uses one she calls "BQ" (likely BQool, spelling unconfirmed in the transcript). `[web check: confirm actual product name and current pricing]`
4. Repricing software is comparatively expensive, which is why the source does not list it in step D's initial software purchases — it is only justified once listing volume requires it.

**I. Outsource sourcing, then prep**
1. Do not outsource anything until you have learned and can do every part of the business yourself.
2. Outsource sourcing first, to a trained virtual assistant. Source uses FastTrack's trained-VA program (referral link plus discount code "JAZZ100"/"J10" for $100 off — an affiliate promotion). She pays her VA (Philippines-based) ~$3-4/hour for 40 hours/week, and reports spending over $3,000 in one month (January 2025) on products the VA sourced. `[sponsored: affiliate program with a discount code]`
3. Outsource prepping next, via a prep center, specifically seeking one in a tax-free state so sales tax is not added to landed cost when the VA sources product. `[web check: current tax-free states and each prep center's actual tax treatment]` `[needs brand data: which state(s) a given operation's prep center(s) sit in, and their tax treatment]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Buying analytical software (SellerAmp, Keepa) before you're ungated to actually source approved products — wastes recurring subscription cost with nothing to analyze yet. `[69b0a58ad59d]`
- Leaving the original store price/clearance tag on a resold unit — a customer who sees a much lower original price is likely to return the item. `[d9137e094ae3]`
- "Ship and forget": not rechecking listings after they go live, letting the market move against the price and erasing margin. `[9ff28ce4f21d]`
- Lazy or shallow sourcing (grabbing the first apparent deal without checking volume, seller count, and price history) — source attributes most sourcing losses and "saturation" complaints to this, not to the arbitrage model itself. `[e24a1498e0a7]`
- Outsourcing sourcing or prep before mastering every part of the business yourself. `[5bdaa518305f]`
- Reading "FC transfer" status as a lost shipment — it is Amazon normally splitting one shipment across multiple fulfillment centers. `[13ee6100527e]`
- Treating a Q4 check-in delay of up to a month as a red flag — source treats it as seasonal and normal, distinct from the same delay outside Q4. `[13ee6100527e]`
