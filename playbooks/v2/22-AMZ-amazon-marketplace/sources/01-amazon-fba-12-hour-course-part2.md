## 2. Principles

1. **Ask a manufacturer for their MAX monthly production capacity, not just their MOQ.** Condition: before scaling order volume with any single manufacturer, not just at first contact. ### 3.1 Manufacturer outreach and negotiation, continued from part 1 `[verify UI: all platform screens]`

1. Use a dedicated outreach email address (not your personal inbox) when cold-emailing many manufacturers at once — example given: "specialblend2000@gmail.com." Reasons given: keeps resulting newsletters out of your personal inbox, and lets you call the same company later as "yourself" without them connecting you to the earlier anonymous email. Optional upgrade: a branded-domain email for extra professionalism, not required. `[eb3555923ac1d72f054b4643]`
2. If still stuck finding a manufacturer, 4 more methods beyond Alibaba/direct outreach:
   - **Jungle Scout Supplier Database**: Suppliers → Supplier Database → search by company name or by ASIN to see which manufacturer supplies a given Amazon listing. Not 100% accurate per source, but "surprisingly" often is. `[bfd58753bfa025385cba5a63]`
   - **Industry trade conventions**: Google "[product] + conventions," open that event's exhibitor list (worked example: a "2023 exhibitor list"), and contact exhibitors individually, or attend and walk the floor. `[088df087220a9bd879d69424]`
   - **Regional directories for non-US sellers**: e.g. europages.co.uk for UK/Europe — works similarly to Alibaba (search, browse verified profiles). `[92fe3ae2bbccfccaa3a3a020]`
   - **Sourcing agent / concierge**: Makers Row concierge service (~$1,000) or self-serve Makers Row database (~$400/year); alternatively Google "[product] + agent," or message industry people on LinkedIn and offer to pay for their time. `[00a90593aced4a6c50b56ba0]` `[web check: current Makers Row pricing]`
3. MOQ negotiation tactic: state a large target order (example: 50,000 units) to be taken seriously and get the best quoted price, then after some back-and-forth ask to start with a smaller test run (example: 500 units) — the manufacturer may or may not honor the original price. Presenter frames this as not deceptive since the larger order remains a genuine eventual goal. `[8115f16806de9d7f74f573c4]`
4. Order about 3-6 months of inventory for the first production run: too much ties up capital and needs storage; too little forces an early reorder before you've earned volume-discount pricing. `[1b37755b2ef8f19aab58970a]`
5. 3 manufacturer-relationship habits recommended: (a) spell-check and grammar-check every message — sloppy communication signals "beginner" and invites being treated as one; (b) build a genuine relationship, e.g. a small heartfelt gift after the first successful production run, done sincerely and not as manipulation; (c) do not automatically choose the cheapest manufacturer — weigh organization and reliability, since a cheap-but-disorganized manufacturer can cost far more in delay than the price difference. `[3aa386e7b617eef4a4217c04]`

### 3.2 Product design, branding, sampling, payment and shipping (general procedure, not Alibaba-specific)

1. Describe the desired product change (dimensions, look, graphics) directly to the manufacturer, in words and/or sketches — most manufacturers can execute this themselves since it's their business to help buyers customize. `[63e7361bc93027b204e4ade5]`
2. Escalate to a hired CAD designer (Fiverr/Upwork) only if the manufacturer says the change is outside their capability.
3. Invest in a real logo/branding process (e.g. a 99designs campaign, or an Upwork hire) rather than the cheapest option — the presenter reiterates his part-1 warning against $5 Fiverr logos. `[8dd41b666ba3355f553f10d7]`
4. Always request a sample. If a custom/branded sample carries an extra charge (worked example: $200-$300 for a fully printed Veno Cards sample versus a free blank one), pay for the fuller sample when feasible — a skipped paid sample can let small defects reach the full production run. `[6f30eee6c39cfabcd2a524fc]`
5. Confirm which payment methods the manufacturer accepts (usually shown on their product/profile page): wire transfer (near-universal, irreversible), PayPal/Alipay/credit card (reversible, ~3% manufacturer-side fee passed to buyer).
6. On a new relationship, prefer a reversible method; once trust is established over a few orders, move to wire transfer to save the fee. `[00605a61c7e01c858e99372e]`
7. If wiring, split 30% upfront / 70% after inspection. Hire a pre-shipment inspection (third-party, ~$100, or Alibaba's own inspection service, ~$118/day) before releasing the balance — inspectors sample units for quality and check that barcodes actually scan (a poor print job can break FNSKU scanning later). `[00605a61c7e01c858e99372e]`
8. Choose freight mode: air (DHL or FedEx, ~5-10 days, more expensive, source recommends this for a first shipment) versus sea (~4-6 weeks plus customs, cheaper, higher delay risk). `[0e36997070ce85e08bd4a221]` `[dd5ab39da4c610dcfe897f72]`
9. If shipping by sea into the US, budget a customs bond: ~$100 single-use, or ~$400 for a multi-entry annual bond if doing several shipments a year; budget an estimated 10-20% import duty from China depending on product category (source notes this narrows China's cost advantage once duty and freight are counted). `[0e36997070ce85e08bd4a221]` `[web check: current US customs bond costs and China tariff schedule by HS code]`
10. Hire a freight forwarder for sea shipments (described as essentially mandatory, "100%") — they arrange trucking from the factory, ocean transit, customs clearance, and final delivery. `[0e36997070ce85e08bd4a221]`
11. For a US-based manufacturer, arrange LTL (less-than-truckload) trucking for 1-2 pallets; get quotes via freightquote.com; for very small volumes, have the manufacturer ship directly via UPS/FedEx/USPS. `[962618d3768fef46aa65d629]`

### 3.3 Alibaba.com's own 7-step sourcing and negotiation workflow (source's own numbered framing) `[verify UI: entire Alibaba flow]`

1. **Search for suppliers.** Do not simply private-label an existing generic Alibaba product (source calls this outdated advice); instead find a supplier already making something similar and ask them to customize it (worked example: searching "flashcards" to source a not-yet-existing "cocktail cards" product). Toggle from the default Products view to the Suppliers view to compare companies rather than individual listings. `[516fc94151074c07881a0fc8]`
   - Filter/vet each candidate on: Trade Assurance enabled (Alibaba escrows funds), Verified Supplier status (3rd-party-inspected), years in business (examples cited: 7, 8, 13 years), transactions in the last 6 months (examples: 47, 90, 166), stated total revenue (example: $2.5M-$5M), top export markets (example: 50% North America, 15% Western Europe — read as a quality/English-fluency signal), country/region (China most common; also India, Vietnam, others), specialization match in "main products" (does their listed specialty match your product, e.g. "card printing" for a flashcards buyer), star rating (example: 4.9/5), photos/video of the actual factory floor and equipment (to rule out a reselling middleman), and the "production flow" and "production equipment" sections on the profile. `[516fc94151074c07881a0fc8]`
2. **Identify the best suppliers.** Build a spreadsheet of shortlisted candidates; narrow to a top 3-5 before contacting (can contact 20-40 if desired). Use Alibaba's Favorites/star feature to group shortlisted suppliers and send one bulk "Contact Supplier" message. Move the conversation off Alibaba's own (slow) messaging system to WhatsApp, WeChat, or Skype/Zoom as soon as possible. `[904e60a14493a751a69b7c9b]`
3. **Get samples.** Request from your top 2-3 remaining candidates; off-the-shelf products are usually free (pay shipping only, ~$25-$45 internationally, DHL recommended); heavily customized products may carry a sample fee. `[6f30eee6c39cfabcd2a524fc]`
4. **Negotiate.** Leverage competing quotes explicitly (e.g. "another supplier quoted $5/unit vs your $6, can you match?"). Balance price against quality — the source's stated position is that a defect-prone cheap supplier usually costs more than it saves. `[8115f16806de9d7f74f573c4]`
5. **Place the order / pay.** Confirm accepted payment methods on the listing; use the 30/70 wire split (see 3.2 step 7) or a reversible method on early orders. `[00605a61c7e01c858e99372e]`
6. **Get the product inspected** before final payment/shipment (see 3.2 step 7 for cost and process). `[00605a61c7e01c858e99372e]`
7. **Ship from manufacturer to the Amazon warehouse** (see 3.2 steps 8-11 for air/sea, customs, freight forwarder choices). `[0e36997070ce85e08bd4a221]`

### 3.4 5 steps to legally start an online business (worked example: "Carnivore Electrolytes")

1. **Choose a legal entity.** Options: sell as an individual (no formal entity); DBA ("doing business as," ~$99 via LegalZoom, lasts ~5 years, no liability protection, cannot sell shares); LLC (liability protection, cost varies by state, cited examples: California ~$800/year, non-US filers can use a Wyoming registered-agent service ~$149); S-corp tax election (lets the owner take a salary plus a shareholder distribution not subject to Social Security/Medicare tax, at the cost of more paperwork); or — the source's own recommended default — an LLC that elects S-corp tax treatment via a checkbox during LLC filing, combining liability protection with the tax savings and less paperwork than a full separate S-corp. One LLC can cover multiple products/sub-businesses; a new LLC is not required per product. `[89eb3eba5f623d79fc60cead]` `[56008befb8c70451b01274a5]` `[web check: current state LLC fees and S-corp election rules — consult a tax professional]`
2. **Get an EIN** for free at irs.gov (10-15 minutes, returns a PDF); do not pay a third party for this. `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
3. **Get a seller's permit** for free from your state's official .gov site (search "[state] seller's permit"); anyone charging money for this is running a scam per source. Enables tax-free purchase of resale goods and is used to file state sales tax. Source's own (self-disclaimed, "not a lawyer") understanding of the sales-tax collection rule at the time: collect state sales tax only from buyers in your own business's state(s) — `[web check: current economic-nexus / marketplace-facilitator sales tax rules, which have materially changed since Wayfair and likely supersede this simplified framing]`. `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
4. **Open a business bank account** with the entity, EIN, and seller's permit paperwork (source uses US Bank; can be done online now). Non-US sellers: Payoneer or a service referred to as "World First" (spelling per transcript, `[web check: verify exact company name]`). Never mix personal and business spending — an IRS red flag, and (for an LLC) grounds for a court to pierce the liability shield in a lawsuit. `[5922aa6e05a8637c7c42f6c8]`
5. **Get a business credit card**, chosen partly for sign-up bonus/points value (named cards: Amex Gold, Capital One Venture, Chase Ink — all affiliate-linked in source, `[web check: current card terms/offers]`). Minimum spend requirements for sign-up bonuses (example: ~$3,000 within 3 months) are often easy to hit during a new launch.

### 3.5 Trademark and patent filing (self-service paths)

1. Choose trademark type: an image/logo mark (generally easier to obtain, recommended when the product name itself is generic or competitive) or a word/text mark (protects the words in any font, harder to win, stricter USPTO review, blocked by any confusingly similar existing mark of either type). `[bcaa11727f52b6db22302ab3]`
2. File directly at uspto.gov (Trademarks → Apply Online): answer basic questions (goods/services category, e.g. "beverages"), upload the mark image and/or transcribe its literal text. Self-filing cost is quoted twice in this part at two different figures — $250 in one section and $275 in a later, more detailed section of the same source; treat both as the source's own rough/rounded estimate of one real fee, not two distinct fees, and `[web check: current actual USPTO trademark filing fee, which also varies by filing basis and number of goods/services classes]`.
3. Alternative: hire a trademark lawyer. Source's stated added cost: "a couple hundred dollars" is reasonable; $1,000-$2,000 is "overpriced" in the presenter's opinion. Find one via Google ("[city] + trademark lawyer") or a personal referral.
4. For patents: a utility patent (for a genuinely new invention) carries a USPTO search fee (~$540), examination fee (~$220), and filing fee (~$330) — roughly $1,090 in filing fees alone — plus a typical lawyer cost around $10,000 if hired (self-filing is possible; one named student, "Jeremy," self-filed but said he'd hire a lawyer if redoing it). A cheaper first step is a provisional patent (~$75), which can later be followed by a full utility filing. A design patent (protects only the visual form, e.g. the Coca-Cola bottle shape) is easier/cheaper to obtain than a utility patent but offers weaker protection. `[web check: current USPTO fees for all patent types — the source's own figures should be treated as dated]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Before scaling a reorder with any manufacturer**: have you confirmed their MAX monthly production capacity, not just their MOQ? If not, you risk outgrowing them exactly when demand justifies scaling. `[458ab7dcf449470f574a40fe]`
- **Before accepting a bulk production run**: was it independently inspected (not just judged against the earlier approved sample)? Good = a 3rd-party or Alibaba inspection report confirms unit quality and barcode scannability; bad = relying on the sample alone. `[5001ae00f4651b0f6ad5df8a]`
- **Before finalizing an FBA shipment's box labels**: are weight and dimensions correct per case, and do ship-from/ship-to addresses match between the UPS label and the Amazon box-ID label? Mismatches can cause extra UPS charges or a lost/rejected shipment; Amazon may split one shipment across several fulfillment centers, so different boxes in the same shipment can legitimately show different ship-to addresses. `[ebc91c8e96fbf78465c48c40]`
- **Tracking an FBA shipment**: Inventory → Manage FBA Shipments → check status (in transit → delivered [dropped off, NOT yet checked in] → receiving [units being checked in] → closed [fully reconciled]); check the "Contents" tab for a declared-vs-received discrepancy and the "Problems" tab for shipment/box/product-level issues; if Amazon under-reports received units, you need your own shipped-quantity documentation to get paid for the difference. `[bc218f44de6b3b2d3e1abd1c]`
- **Inventory-health check**: Restock Inventory widget on the Seller Central home screen shows days-of-supply remaining and a recommended reorder quantity — treat a very low days-left figure as urgent given ~3-6 week replenishment lead times. `[6bea9b6ea5bb2f3bade1401b]`
- **Buy Box health check** (mainly relevant to shared/arbitrage listings, not unique private-label products): Buy Box win rate should sit near 100% for a unique product; a dip is a more advanced troubleshooting case not detailed in this part. `[6bea9b6ea5bb2f3bade1401b]`
- **Business Reports check**: Sales & Traffic report by week/month separates page-views (traffic) from unit-session percentage (conversion rate) — a period can show rising traffic with flat/falling conversion and still post higher total sales; read the two metrics separately before diagnosing a problem. `[73e0a4ad19dc97cde267b3c5]`
- **Account Health check**: Performance → Account Health shows Order Defect Rate, negative feedback, A-to-z claims, chargebacks; source's own healthy example was 0% defects across 743 orders — proactively reach out to any single negative reviewer to try to resolve the underlying issue. `[verify UI]`
- **PPC keyword relevance check**: before adding a broad or phrase-match keyword, ask whether every plausible search containing that phrase is genuinely relevant to your exact product; if not, that phrase belongs on the negative-keyword list instead, or needs a negative-keyword companion. `[f14aac7c2e850ffb0e622e61]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Asking a manufacturer for MOQ but never for their max monthly production capacity, then discovering the ceiling only after sales have grown past it. `[458ab7dcf449470f574a40fe]`
- Treating an approved sample as proof the bulk production run will match it, without hiring independent QC. `[5001ae00f4651b0f6ad5df8a]`
- Choosing the cheapest manufacturer without weighing organization/reliability. `[3aa386e7b617eef4a4217c04]`
- Skipping a paid, fully-printed sample to save a few hundred dollars, only to discover defects in it at full production scale (Veno Cards case). `[6f30eee6c39cfabcd2a524fc]`
- Wiring 100% of payment upfront to a new manufacturer instead of splitting 30/70 around an inspection. `[00605a61c7e01c858e99372e]`
- Choosing full sea freight for a first-ever shipment purely to save money, when the source recommends air for a first shipment given the higher delay risk of sea/customs. `[dd5ab39da4c610dcfe897f72]`
- Sending too much FBA inventory (ties up cash, risks long-term storage fees past ~6 months) or too little (risks a stockout that the source says can hurt search rank and take a long time to recover from). `[24ca880132fbe704614d66a1]`
- Using the wrong ship-from address on an FBA inbound shipment — presenter's own past mistake reportedly cost $5,000 in lost sales.
- Choosing to have Amazon apply FNSKU labels (per-unit fee) instead of self-labeling or having the manufacturer print the barcode. `[a5c9b2ba33c2a4cee89b9d60]`
- Not keeping shipping receipts/Bill of Lading — can leave a seller unable to prove a lost shipment actually happened, delaying or forfeiting compensation. `[bc218f44de6b3b2d3e1abd1c]` `[77a1ff7d90473cce496e1aba]`
- Waiting until inventory is critically low to start a replenishment shipment — Amazon does not expedite a seller's shipment just because that seller is nearly out of stock.
- Writing listing bullet points around features instead of customer benefits. `[9c6378ecc0532964bfa9f804]`
- Setting a default/blended PPC bid across all 4 automatic-targeting groups instead of splitting them, or running broad/phrase-match keywords without negative keywords. `[b5ced20e1372c2a7336bddab]` `[f14aac7c2e850ffb0e622e61]`
- Defaulting to FBM to "save money" without accounting for FBA's claimed Prime-driven sales lift. `[a4c6fdf106dbc226cbff95c4]`
- Buying inventory for an unvalidated product idea instead of pre-selling via Kickstarter/Indiegogo first. `[c18d2ec7e6ba8c0c53c8f1b7]`
- Paying $5 for a logo/branding job on Fiverr without reviewing the freelancer's portfolio (repeats a part-1 warning). `[8dd41b666ba3355f553f10d7]`
- Launching quietly without building pre-launch social/email audience or review velocity, leaving the product exposed to being copied (Leanne and Logan case). `[c5fd5ed5d1d3d02e00b6bbc8]`
