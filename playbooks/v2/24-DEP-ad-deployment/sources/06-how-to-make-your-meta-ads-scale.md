## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator plan, script and audit Meta creative by the viewer's stage of awareness, so the account can spend into cold audiences instead of re-serving the same warm people. It covers the 5 stage definitions, how to keep 1 ad on 1 stage or walk it down deliberately, the "product awareness curve" for video scripts, unaware statics with long primary text, an 80/10/10 creative volume split, how awareness maps to adset structure, and how to diagnose high frequency. It is strongest on creative strategy. It is thin on account settings, budgets and measurement.

## 2. Principles

1. **Write every ad for the stage the viewer is actually in.** Condition: every ad, video or static, cold or warm. Most brands write as if everyone is product aware and lead with features, ingredients, price and benefits. The presenter says most people are problem aware at best and most are completely unaware. If the creative does not meet people where they are, frequency rises and the ads do not reach new cold people. Evidence: presenter's opinion from account work. [48b5b50768aa] [7443b5bb8016]

2. **The 5 stages, and what each ad must do.** Condition: use as a planning device. Real ads often blend stages. [48b5b50768aa]
   - Unaware: the person does not know a problem exists (source example: not knowing a plastic drink bottle is bad for you). Job: bold claim, pattern interrupt, reframe, teach the problem. You cannot open with problem agitation ("Are you sick of your plastic drink bottle?") because they are not. [48b5b50768aa] [973d03a425e7]
   - Problem aware: they know something is wrong but have not started looking for solutions (acne; an annoying car fault). This is where problem agitation lives. [48b5b50768aa]
   - Solution aware: they know solutions exist but have not chosen one (knows plastic is bad and metal bottles exist; a buyer who has used 5 agencies before). Do not explain the category or agitate the need. Make your unique mechanism, the thing that differs you from competitors, visible in the creative. [79e75a8b3645]
   - Product aware: they know your brand, product and what it does, but are not convinced to buy. Use proof, risk reversal and offer clarity. Presenter equates this with website visitor retargeting. [04071060afad]
   - Most aware: they know you, like you and plan to buy; they need a reason to act now. Presenter equates this with add-to-cart retargeting. Use time pressure to pull the sale forward. [04071060afad]

3. **Down-funnel audiences are cheaper but small; scale only comes from moving up.** Condition: any account trying to grow spend. Targeting solution-aware and product-aware people is cheaper because you do not pay to educate them. But few people know your brand, and the in-market pool for a solution also hits a limit. The only way to scale further is to make ads for a higher stage. The bigger the account gets, the further up it must go. Unaware ads are costlier because you pay to take people through several steps of education. [b81e94d98fa2] [973d03a425e7]

4. **Up-funnel ads are a harder and rarer skill.** Condition: planning who writes unaware and problem-aware work. The presenter says few people can make strong unaware and problem-aware ads. Budget talent and time accordingly. [b81e94d98fa2]

5. **Post Andromeda, Meta reads the creative and picks the audience from it.** Condition: presenter's account of current Meta delivery; verify in your own data. Before, product-aware ads in an interest-targeted adset kept serving to new people in that interest. Now Meta sees a product-aware ad, will not push it to unaware people who will not convert, and keeps it on warm audiences. So if you load, for example, 200 product-aware ads and raise budget, they all serve to the same small warm pool and frequency "goes through the roof". [7443b5bb8016] [faa05bf3cff8] [web check: Meta's own description of Andromeda and creative-based delivery]

6. **1 ad, 1 stage, or a deliberate walk down. Never a blend.** Condition: every ad. 2 valid patterns: (a) pick 1 stage and hammer it, and let separate product-aware and most-aware ads do the retargeting and conversion later; (b) start at a high stage and move down in order as the ad goes on. What fails is moving back and forth between stages or appealing to different stages at once. The presenter calls this "the key takeaway" of the video. [8a649c8f4785]

7. **The ad and its copy must assume the same stage.** Condition: every ad with primary text. The visual (video or static) and the copy must match. A product-aware image with solution-aware copy works for no one. The header note says such a mismatch makes the creative fail across all audiences. [8a649c8f4785]

8. **Walk-down (VSL-style) ads are among the most scalable.** Condition: long video that can carry a full journey. The presenter says these are "some of the most scalable ads that I've seen in accounts" because they take a viewer from knowing nothing to conversion in 1 asset. Order in section 3.2. Evidence: his observation, no numbers. [8a649c8f4785]

9. **Statics can scale cold; they are not only for retargeting.** Condition: when the primary text carries a long story and the ad skill is high. Presenter says statics can spend "a quarter million dollars" at the unaware and problem-aware stages. The limit is primary-text skill, not the format. [50555d28666c]

10. **The viewer's sense of "being sold to" should rise slowly (product awareness curve).** Condition: scripting video. The ideal ad starts at zero awareness, so it resonates with a huge pool. Keep the sales feeling low through hook and body, then let it creep up to the call to action. Sharp jumps ("bridges") into a CTA lose viewers, visible in the video's retention curve. [84685cc67b36]

11. **Creative volume should be proportional to the size of each stage's pool.** Condition: planning production. Starting split: 80% unaware plus problem aware, 10% solution aware, 10% product aware plus most aware. Flex it from account data. Presenter's heuristic. [b81e94d98fa2]

12. **Structure follows concepts; awareness does the targeting.** Condition: accounts already grouping adsets and ads by concept. Keep each concept to 1 stage and group by concept. Product-aware ads grouped together will retarget on their own with high frequency without any audience defined. Unaware ads grouped together will show low frequency and "cut through" on audiences. [faa05bf3cff8]

13. **Judge unaware ads at account level, not by their own ROAS.** Condition: when introducing a high volume of unaware ads. They may not show a good return themselves while other creatives pick up the attributed conversions. Look at account-wide performance. [7443b5bb8016] [b81e94d98fa2] [needs brand data: account-level spend, revenue and new-customer trend before and after the change]

14. **Most-aware audiences need very few ads.** Condition: when you do not need to pull sales forward urgently. A DPA (dynamic product ad) in retargeting can cover this stage. [04071060afad]

15. **The fix for high frequency is the creative mix, not exclusions.** Condition: frequency rising and cold reach stalled. Adding website-visitor exclusions to force cold delivery "won't work because the ads aren't good"; Meta is withholding them from cold on purpose. Fix the stage mix first. [7443b5bb8016]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Choose the stage before writing an ad
1. Name the target viewer and ask: would they nod at a statement of the problem? If no, they are unaware. If yes but they have not looked for fixes, problem aware. If they know the category and are comparing, solution aware. If they know your product, product aware. If they are close to buying (for example added to cart), most aware. [48b5b50768aa] [973d03a425e7]
2. Pick the opening move from the stage: unaware = bold claim, pattern interrupt, reframe; problem aware = name and agitate the problem; solution aware = your unique mechanism; product aware = proof, risk reversal, offer clarity; most aware = reason to act now (time pressure). [48b5b50768aa] [8a649c8f4785]
3. Decide: hammer this 1 stage, or walk down from it (section 3.2). [8a649c8f4785]
4. Write visual and copy to the same stage. Check every element against the chosen stage before launch. (Card adds voiceover, captions and offer to the check; the source names the ad and the copy.) [8a649c8f4785]
5. Tag the creative with its stage so the mix can be audited later (card recommendation; the source implies it through the volume split). [48b5b50768aa] [59dbcd6956ee]

### 3.2 Script a walk-down ad (VSL style)
Order the presenter describes for the 2 problem-aware examples:
1. Meet the viewer at their stage (for problem aware: state the problem).
2. Agitate the problem a little.
3. Move into possible solutions. Handle objections to other solutions they may have tried (the tonsil stone ad: squeezing stones out, salt water, apple cider vinegar only treat the surface).
4. Introduce your product and the unique mechanism that makes it better than everyone else.
5. Give proof: social proof, testimonials.
6. Give a risk reversal.
7. Make the current offer clear.
8. Give a reason to buy now, "likely through some kind of time pressure".
Keep the product awareness curve smooth through these steps (section 3.3). Source example length: about 2.5 minutes. [8a649c8f4785] [84685cc67b36]

### 3.3 Apply the product awareness curve to a video script
1. Start at zero awareness of being sold to, high on the stages, to reach the largest pool. [84685cc67b36]
2. Keep the sales feeling low through the hook and body while you educate. [84685cc67b36]
3. Let it rise gradually to the CTA. [84685cc67b36]
4. Avoid the "staircase": no product at first, then a sudden product intro, then back to problem agitation, other solutions and objection handling, then "but our product's better" and another CTA. [84685cc67b36]
5. Check the video's retention curve for drops at bridges. A drop at a jump means the transition was too abrupt. The card says to smooth that section rather than cut the CTA; the source only says the loss shows in retention curves. [84685cc67b36] [verify UI: where Ads Manager shows video retention] [needs brand data: per-video retention curves]

### 3.4 Write an unaware static
1. Use an image that does not reveal the product or that it is an ad. Source example: a ring or security camera shot in the dark, "probably just an AI photo". [50555d28666c]
2. Open the primary text with a curiosity hook that reads like an organic post. Source examples: "Are you seeing someone? That's what my husband asked me last Tuesday. Dead serious..." and "My father was on blood pressure medication for 16 years and by the end of this you're going to be pissed..." [50555d28666c]
3. Run a long story in the primary text that pulls the reader through all the stages to the product (grounding sleep cover; aged garlic extract). [50555d28666c]
4. Destination: the header note says the story redirects to an advertorial landing page. The presenter does not say where these ads link. [50555d28666c]
5. Expect the format to spend at unaware and problem-aware stages if the writing is strong. [50555d28666c]

### 3.5 Plan creative volume by stage
1. Set the starting split: 80% of ads for stage 1 and 2 (unaware, problem aware), 10% for stage 3 (solution aware), 10% for stages 4 and 5 (product aware, most aware). [b81e94d98fa2]
2. Flex from account data: if retargeting underperforms, raise product-aware and most-aware volume; if solution-aware ads do very well, raise volume there. [b81e94d98fa2] [04071060afad] [needs brand data: performance by stage]
3. When you add a high volume of unaware ads, read account-wide results, not per-ad ROAS. [b81e94d98fa2]
4. Count the current mix against the target (section 3.7). [59dbcd6956ee]

### 3.6 Set up account structure
1. Build each concept inside 1 stage. [faa05bf3cff8]
2. Group adsets and ads by concept, as you normally would. The source says awareness "doesn't really" change Meta structure beyond that. [faa05bf3cff8]
3. Do not define audiences to force warm or cold. A group of product-aware ads will retarget on its own; a group of unaware ads will show low frequency. [faa05bf3cff8]
4. For most aware, a DPA in retargeting can be enough. [04071060afad] [verify UI: DPA / Advantage+ catalog ads setup]
5. Read results at account level when unaware volume is high. [faa05bf3cff8] [7443b5bb8016]

### 3.7 Audit a creative library by stage
1. List active creatives. [7443b5bb8016]
2. For each, decide the stage it assumes. For video, note when the product first appears: the earlier the product appears, the further down the funnel the ad sits (source calls this "a good rule of thumb"; Nero shorts at about 9 seconds read as solution aware, arguably problem aware). [59dbcd6956ee]
3. For statics: if the image alone shows the product, it is product aware; if the image is vague and the primary text carries a long story, it is likely unaware (card extension of the same logic). [59dbcd6956ee]
4. Confirm with what the hook and body actually assume. Expect blends and arguments over exact placement. [59dbcd6956ee] [79e75a8b3645]
5. Flag any ad whose visual and copy assume different stages. [8a649c8f4785]
6. Count assets that lead with features, ingredients, price and benefits. If they dominate, that is the scaling bottleneck. [7443b5bb8016]
7. Compare the count by stage to 80/10/10. [59dbcd6956ee] [b81e94d98fa2]

### 3.8 Diagnose an account stuck at high frequency
1. Signal: frequency rising, cold reach flat, spend will not scale when budget goes up. [7443b5bb8016] [needs brand data: frequency and reach trend by adset]
2. Run the library audit (3.7). Expect too many product-aware or most-aware ads. [7443b5bb8016]
3. Do not reach for website-visitor exclusions first. [7443b5bb8016]
4. Shift production up the stages (3.5). [7443b5bb8016] [b81e94d98fa2]
5. Re-check account-wide results, since bottom-funnel ads may still take credit for sales the new top-funnel ads drive. [7443b5bb8016]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Stage match check.** How: read the visual, then the copy, and name the stage each assumes. Good: same stage, or a deliberate top-to-bottom walk. Bad: visual product aware, copy solution or problem aware (the creatine example), or back-and-forth. Next: rewrite the copy or the visual to 1 stage. [8a649c8f4785]
- **Unaware hook check.** How: ask if the target viewer already feels the problem. Good for unaware: bold claim or reframe that teaches a new problem, organic tone. Bad: "Are you sick of X?" to people who are not. Next: rewrite the hook as a claim ("If you use xylitol, you'll stop tooth decay at its source") rather than "If you suffer with..." [973d03a425e7]
- **Solution-aware check.** How: does the ad spend time explaining the category? Good: mechanism up front. Bad: explaining what a marketing agency or whitening strip is to people who already know. Next: cut category education, show the mechanism. [79e75a8b3645]
- **Awareness curve check.** How: retention curve per video. Good: gradual decline without sharp drops at the product intro or CTA. Bad: drops at bridges or a staircase shape. Next: smooth the transition. [84685cc67b36] [needs brand data: retention curves]
- **Library mix check.** How: tag each active creative by stage, count. Good: roughly 80/10/10 or a deliberate, data-based flex. Bad: mostly product aware or feature-led. Next: shift production up. [59dbcd6956ee] [b81e94d98fa2]
- **Frequency check.** How: adset frequency and cold reach over time. Good: unaware adsets at low frequency, product-aware adsets high (expected). Bad: account-wide frequency high, cold reach flat as budget rises. Next: library mix check, not exclusions. [7443b5bb8016] [faa05bf3cff8] [needs brand data: frequency, reach, new vs returning buyers]
- **Attribution check when adding unaware volume.** How: compare account-wide results before and after, not per-ad ROAS. Good: account results hold or improve while unaware ads show weak attributed ROAS. Bad: judging and killing unaware ads on their own ROAS. [7443b5bb8016] [b81e94d98fa2]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Writing every ad as if the viewer is product aware (leading with features, ingredients, price, benefits). [48b5b50768aa] [7443b5bb8016]
2. Problem agitation aimed at unaware people. [973d03a425e7]
3. Explaining the category to solution-aware people. [79e75a8b3645]
4. Copy and visual assuming different stages. [8a649c8f4785]
5. Moving back and forth between stages in 1 ad. [8a649c8f4785]
6. The staircase pattern and aggressive bridges into the CTA. [84685cc67b36]
7. Making most ads for most-aware and product-aware people. [b81e94d98fa2] [04071060afad]
8. Believing statics are only for retargeting. [50555d28666c]
9. Adding website-visitor exclusions to force cold delivery of weak product-aware ads. [7443b5bb8016]
10. Judging unaware ads by their own attributed ROAS. [7443b5bb8016]
11. Not deciding a stage at all, so the ad becomes "a weird blend of multiple". [8a649c8f4785]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single presenter, no data.** Every rule is opinion or observation. The 80/10/10 split is a heuristic he says to flex. [b81e94d98fa2]
- **Andromeda mechanism is his account.** The claim that Meta holds product-aware ads on warm audiences is not backed by Meta documentation in the source. [web check: Meta's Andromeda documentation] [7443b5bb8016]
- **Stage placement is subjective.** He changes his own calls mid-video (whitening strips, xylitol, Nero) and says people argue about the minutiae. [79e75a8b3645] [59dbcd6956ee]
- **Promised but missing.** He says he will cover "how diversity fits in the mix" and never does. No frequency threshold, no budget split by stage, no bid or campaign settings, no test design, no measurement method beyond "look account-wide".
- **Card drift to watch.** [973d03a425e7] mentions "bleed-through examples"; nothing by that name is in the transcript. [50555d28666c] says "hundreds of thousands of dollars"; the source says "a quarter million". [59dbcd6956ee] says early product intro indicates "solution- or problem-aware"; the source only says earlier means further down the funnel. [50555d28666c] and [59dbcd6956ee] mention advertorial landing pages; only the header note says that. [79e75a8b3645] tells you to use "proof and early product introduction once the mechanism lands"; the source does not state this as a rule.
- **Conflicts with common ecommerce advice.** (a) Many operators build separate retargeting campaigns with defined audiences and exclude buyers or visitors from prospecting; the presenter says awareness-grouped creative retargets on its own and exclusions will not fix weak cold creative. (b) Common advice keeps statics mainly for retargeting and uses video for cold; he rejects that. (c) Per-ad ROAS is the usual kill signal; he says unaware ads must be judged account-wide. (d) His advice fits the common post-Andromeda advice to go broad and diversify creative, but he gives no source for it.
- **Compliance.** Several examples make health claims (xylitol "repair and strengthen your teeth", blood pressure, tonsil stones, whitening in 30 minutes) and use curiosity or relationship-drama hooks. Check Meta ad policy before copying these patterns. Time pressure must be real. [web check: Meta health and personal-attribute ad policies] [04071060afad]
- **Folder fit.** Most content is creative strategy (10-MCR); the scaling, frequency and structure parts are what belong in ad deployment.
- **Brand data needed before use:** current creative mix by stage, frequency and reach by adset, retention curves, account-level results, and which stage each product's buyers are usually in. [needs brand data: all of these]
