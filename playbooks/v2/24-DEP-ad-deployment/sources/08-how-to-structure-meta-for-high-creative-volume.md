## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator design or audit a Meta ad account that must take a high volume of new creative each month (the worked example is 200 new ads a month) without spreading budget thin and without dumping everything into 1 campaign. It gives a 2-layer model: campaign level splits follow business units (categories, margins, budgets), ad set level splits follow the variable you want to learn about, and the source's preferred ad set variable is the creative concept (persona + angle + offer). It then shows how to read results at the ad set level, feed them back into creative production, and choose between doubling down on a winner or capping it.

## 2. Principles

1. **The goal of structure is a fast test loop that keeps spend efficient and returns learnings.** Launching more ads is not the goal. The account must let you test fast, not destroy efficiency, and give learnings you can feed into future creative design. Condition: applies to any account that wants to raise creative volume. Evidence: speaker's framing. [3ae3be9f8f79]
2. **There are 2 failure modes: oversegmenting and overconsolidating.** Oversegmenting: "way too many campaigns", budget forced "way too thin", ads judged after only $1 or $2 of spend, "people test a hundred things but learn nothing". Overconsolidating: 1 fully consolidated campaign, "just run one Advantage Plus campaign and just put all your ads in there", which the source calls a "terrible idea". [52611fb24810] [3ae3be9f8f79]
3. **Account complexity should equal business complexity.** Condition: if you sell 1 product to 1 persona and are "relatively small", 1 Advantage+ campaign where you drop assets may be fine. Once you have 2 or 3 products, several personas, or high creative volume, you need more structure. [52611fb24810]
4. **Complexity means 4 things.** (a) Different product categories. (b) Core versus seasonal ranges, that is always-on versus new arrivals. (c) Differences within the core demographics you target. (d) Concept winners that do very well but that you are overleveraged into, so you want more concepts. [52611fb24810]
5. **Segmentation does 2 jobs: budget control and better KPI reading at the ad set level.** Product categories that the business treats differently can have different gross margin (in dollars and in percent), different target demographics and different budgets and targeting, "particularly in large retail businesses". If any of these apply, add some campaign-level segmentation. [52611fb24810] [1ed05eaf4fa7]
6. **ROAS reliability falls as you move down the account.** Campaign-level ROAS on 7-day click is "relatively reliable" and "correlates to acquisition" in the business "quite well". Ad set level is "a little bit less reliable". Ad level is "very unreliable". [ab729ba34368]
7. **Ad-level data is unreliable because Meta attributes to the last click while it optimizes across a multi-click journey.** "Almost no one buys after one impression." Several ads serve to a person, maybe with several clicks, and all credit goes to the final click. Worked theory: the top-spending ad has lower ROAS because it is the first ad served to cold audiences; a second ad follows up and gets the click and sale, but would not get it without ad 1. The sequence reaches the goal (the example says a 3x return) while ad 1 looks bad alone, "because that's not its job". [ab729ba34368]
8. **Debating ad-level theories gives decisions with "very large error bars".** You cannot know who is right until you turn an ad off or "materially change the account". So stop reading the ad level for decisions and move up to the ad set. [ab729ba34368]
9. **Make the ad set the unit of decision.** Bundle "5, 10, 20 creatives" in an ad set. Ask 1 question: is this ad set, with this set of ads, at KPI? If yes, raise budget. "I don't care what ads are working. The combination of them are working." [ab729ba34368]
10. **Inside a working ad set, use spend as a proxy for performance.** Of 20 ads, "maybe four are spending". Treat those 4 as working and make more like them. Condition: only inside an ad set that is at KPI at the ad set level. Card caveat: spend patterns give rough creative reads but should not drive budget decisions. [ab729ba34368] [94c8e0f902f4]
11. **Choose ad set splits by asking "what variable do we want to gain insights on?"** The ad set structure should create a "recursive feedback loop": make more of what works and less of what does not. The source says the missing piece at most brands is this loop; they throw a lot of creative in, some works, some does not, and they cannot tell why. [ab729ba34368] [3ae3be9f8f79]
12. **Pareto always applies.** "20% of ads get 80% of spend", and more broadly 80% of spend, results or revenue pulls to 20% of ads, ad sets, campaigns and products. With 1 campaign holding everything, you never learn whether the other 80% of ads were any good. [477d8f00df92] [ab729ba34368]
13. **A "concept" is persona + angle + offer.** Change any 1 of the 3 and it is a new concept. The concept is the source's preferred ad set split. You can build "infinite ads" under each concept. [94c8e0f902f4]
14. **Each ad set learns its audience in isolation (the source calls this "Andromeda friendly").** Each ad set goes out and finds its own audience that responds to the ads under it. Because different concepts reach different people, concept ad sets let each one optimize toward its own group (for example gym beginners in one, brain-support buyers in another). "The ad set is where most of the targeting learning is kept, not the ad level." Some learning happens at the ad level, but "a little bit". Condition: this is the speaker's model of Meta delivery, not a Meta statement. `[web check: whether Meta's current delivery keeps audience learning per ad set as described]` [3e877cb4c991] [66c735ceb8ff]
15. **Mixed ad sets optimize toward everyone.** If an ad set holds several angles or personas, it has no specific group to go after, so targeting turns very broad and performance drops compared with splitting by audience. The source calls this "a core component of Andromeda". [66c735ceb8ff] [3e877cb4c991]
16. **Daily bid and budget tweaks have little value; account structure still matters a lot.** "Media buying died like 3 years ago" in the traditional sense of changing bids and budgets day to day. Structure "never died" because it decides how budget flows across categories and ads, how you read KPIs, and how decision loops are built. Condition: the speaker's stated position. Card caveat: you still reallocate budget between ad sets and campaigns from time to time. [5e79782f9a1e]
17. **Overleveraging into 1 ad, ad set, angle or concept builds risk into the business.** When it stops working "you've got nothing to fall back on". [99cd67e7cbac] [477d8f00df92]
18. **No universal structure.** The source rejects one structure for all clients because businesses differ in product categories, niches, audiences and LTV dynamics. Structure "should be bespoke to your individual commercial needs and outcomes". [e336ef61445a] [52611fb24810]
19. **Every structure must pass 2 tests.** (1) Data integrity: you can read the data to make decisions, and the structure is defensible. If an auditor came in, you could explain "exactly why it's structured this way" and how it gives clean insights; they "wouldn't be able to tear down your structure". (2) Commercial alignment: the structure lets you flex budget toward the business's goals. If both hold, "you should be able to introduce a high volume of creative without an issue". [e336ef61445a]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Design or restructure an account for high creative volume

Source basis: the talk's step 1, step 2, ad set options and the 2 tests. [52611fb24810] [ab729ba34368] [94c8e0f902f4] [e336ef61445a]

1. **Audit business complexity.** List: product categories; core (always-on) versus seasonal or new-arrival ranges; distinct core demographics; concept winners you are overleveraged into. `[needs brand data: category list, ranges, personas, current spend share per concept]`
2. **For each category, check if the business treats it differently.** Look at gross margin in dollars and percent, target demographic, budget and targeting needs. `[needs brand data: gross margin per category, category budget targets]`
3. **Set campaign-level splits for real business units** that need their own KPI or budget (examples given: new arrivals versus core collection; in CPG, protein powder versus pre-workout). If you are 1 product, 1 persona and small, 1 Advantage+ campaign may do. [52611fb24810]
4. **If there are many categories, do not make 1 campaign each.** With "like 20 categories", 20 low-budget campaigns is wrong. Consolidate categories (the source suggests grouping across gross margin), move some category testing to the ad set level and keep some at campaign level. With few categories (dresses, tops, pants, accessories) you could run 4 campaigns and use ad sets for other insights. The source gives no numeric cut-off between "few" and "many". [1ed05eaf4fa7] [52611fb24810]
5. **Pick the ad set variable you want to learn about.** Options the source rates, in its order:
   - Format (images, GIFs (the transcript says "Gibson", likely a mis-transcription), videos, DPAs in separate ad sets): for beginners and new accounts only. Stop at roughly $10,000 a month spend. [a5c883d83f91]
   - Landing page (home, product page, collection, custom page): an option, "probably not the best option". Use it mainly when you add a custom landing page. [acad4b08fee6]
   - Product category (dresses, tops, pants, accessories): good when categories need different efficiency goals; often better done at campaign level. [1ed05eaf4fa7]
   - Concept (persona + angle + offer): the source's preferred split for creative learning. [94c8e0f902f4]
   - Launch date: do not use. [66c735ceb8ff]
6. **Build 1 ad set per concept.** Write each concept as persona + angle + offer. Worked example for creatine gummies: people who hate creatine powder; beginners starting at the gym; wellness and focus professionals; brain support. Keep conflicting personas apart (for example men's and women's activewear in separate ad sets). [94c8e0f902f4] [3e877cb4c991]
7. **Load each concept ad set with many ads.** The source says bundle 5, 10 or 20 creatives per ad set for reading, and its volume example uses 50 creatives per concept per month. [ab729ba34368] [3ae3be9f8f79]
8. **Run the 2 tests before you finalize.** Data integrity (can you defend every layer to an auditor?) and commercial alignment (can you flex budget to the categories, ranges and concepts the business needs?). If either fails, restructure. [e336ef61445a]

### 3.2 Add new creative to the account

1. Match each new ad to the existing concept ad set whose persona + angle + offer it fits. [66c735ceb8ff]
2. If the ad is a truly new concept, create a new concept ad set. [66c735ceb8ff]
3. Never create a batch ad set named by production or launch date ("March 7th creatives", "March 9th creatives"). [66c735ceb8ff]
4. Check: after launch, run a gender (or audience) breakdown at the ad level to see who each ad serves. If ads meant for one group serve to both, the ad set is mixed. `[verify UI: Breakdown by age and gender in Ads Manager]` [66c735ceb8ff] [3e877cb4c991]

### 3.3 Read results and decide (the ad set decision loop)

1. Use campaign-level 7-day-click ROAS as the most reliable read, ad set ROAS as the decision level for creative, and ignore ad-level ROAS for decisions. `[verify UI: attribution setting and comparison windows in Ads Manager]` [ab729ba34368]
2. For each concept ad set ask: is it at KPI? The source's example KPI is a 3x return. `[needs brand data: target ROAS or CPA per campaign or category, based on margin]` [94c8e0f902f4]
3. If at KPI: raise the ad set budget. [ab729ba34368]
4. Inside that ad set, sort ads by spend. The few ads taking most spend are the ones to model new creative on. [ab729ba34368]
5. If an ad set is not at KPI after it has spent enough, rotate it out. The source says let new ad sets spend "to a level that reaches statistical relevancy" but gives no number. `[needs brand data: spend or conversion count per ad set before a read]` [99cd67e7cbac]
6. Do not judge after $1 or $2 of spend. [3ae3be9f8f79]

### 3.4 After Pareto shows a winner: double down or cap

Source's expected pattern: with 4 concept ad sets, 1 or 2 "won't perform at all", 1 "will end up significantly outperforming everything else", and about 80% of spend goes to it. Budget moves either by hand at the ad set level or automatically "if you're using a CBO". [3ae3be9f8f79] [477d8f00df92]

Option 1, double down:
1. Shift creative output toward the winner. Source example: instead of 50 per concept, make 120 for the winning concept and 40 for the rest. The source also says "put 80% of your energy" into the winner. [477d8f00df92]
2. This puts more creative volume behind a proven concept and lets you spend more. [477d8f00df92]

Option 2, cap and rotate:
1. Set a maximum spend limit on the winning ad set. Source example: 40%. `[verify UI: ad set spend limits under campaign budget, and whether they can be set as a percent]` [99cd67e7cbac]
2. Keep making new concepts and new ad sets and rotate them in, to find another concept that works as well. [99cd67e7cbac]
3. Let each new ad set spend to statistical relevance, then rotate it out if it fails. [99cd67e7cbac]
4. This gives consistent learnings and a consistent flow of new creative, and lets the account "compound". [99cd67e7cbac]

Choosing: the source allows a mix ("let's double down a little bit, but we don't want to go crazy"). Base the choice on how much single-concept risk the business can carry. Card addition (not the speaker's words in this spot): check the concept's LTV before doubling down, because the speaker elsewhere names a concept that performed well but produced "terrible LTV". `[needs brand data: LTV or repeat rate by first-touch concept]` [477d8f00df92] [e336ef61445a]

### 3.5 Account audit (the defensibility test)

1. For each campaign and ad set, write why it exists and what insight it gives. [e336ef61445a]
2. If you cannot explain a layer to an outside auditor, the data cannot back business decisions; restructure. [e336ef61445a]
3. Check budget can flex to what the business needs: a category that needs a set budget, a core range that must hit set spend. `[needs brand data: category and range budget targets]` [e336ef61445a]
4. If a concept or funnel brings customers with poor LTV, add segmentation to separate it and limit its effect. The source does not say how. [e336ef61445a]
5. Card addition: re-run both tests when creative volume or product mix changes. [e336ef61445a]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Too many campaigns, with budget forced too thin across many tests. [3ae3be9f8f79]
2. Testing 100 things and learning nothing. [3ae3be9f8f79]
3. Judging ads after only $1 or $2 of spend. [3ae3be9f8f79]
4. Putting all ads into 1 Advantage+ campaign and trusting "Meta will figure it out" (for anything beyond 1 product, 1 persona, small scale). [52611fb24810]
5. Making ad-level decisions (switch off the top spender, move the 2nd ad to a new ad set) from last-click ad-level ROAS. [ab729ba34368]
6. Keeping format-based ad sets once spend is around $10,000 a month or more. [a5c883d83f91]
7. Building landing-page ad sets just to learn page conversion rates that Shopify already shows. [acad4b08fee6]
8. Making 20 low-budget campaigns for 20 categories. [1ed05eaf4fa7]
9. Making ad sets by creative launch date. [66c735ceb8ff]
10. Mixing conflicting personas (men's and women's) in 1 ad set. [3e877cb4c991]
11. Overleveraging into 1 ad, ad set, angle or concept with nothing to fall back on. [99cd67e7cbac]
12. Spending time on day-to-day bid and budget changes instead of structure. [5e79782f9a1e]
13. Copying 1 agency or coaching-group structure onto every account. [e336ef61445a]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single speaker, no data.** Every number is a worked example. Treat 3x, 70%/55%, $10,000, 50/120/40 and 40% as illustrations, not benchmarks. [ab729ba34368] [a5c883d83f91] [477d8f00df92] [99cd67e7cbac]
- **Internal number tension.** The source says "put 80% of your energy" into the winner but the example is 120 for the winner and 40 for the rest. If "40 for the rest" means 40 each for 3 concepts, the winner gets 120 of 240, or 50%. If it means 40 in total, the winner gets 120 of 160, or 75%. The source does not say which. [477d8f00df92]
- **Ads per ad set.** The decision section says bundle 5, 10 or 20 creatives per ad set; the volume example puts 50 new creatives per concept per month. The source does not say how many live ads an ad set should hold at once or when to retire old ads.
- **Andromeda claims are unverified.** "Each ad set learns in isolation" and "most targeting learning is kept at the ad set" are the speaker's model. `[web check: Meta documentation on Andromeda and ad set learning]` [3e877cb4c991]
- **Missing how-to.** No numeric "statistical relevancy" threshold, no test duration, no budget per ad set, no bid strategy, no targeting settings (broad or not), no guidance on ABO versus CBO beyond naming CBO, no rule for when to create a new concept, no naming convention, no retire rule for old ads, no Advantage+ campaign setup steps. [99cd67e7cbac] [3ae3be9f8f79]
- **LTV is named, not solved.** The source says to segment a poor-LTV concept but not how to measure LTV by concept or how to split it. [e336ef61445a]
- **"20 categories" consolidation** is suggested "across gross margin" but with no method or cut-off. [1ed05eaf4fa7]
- **Card additions.** Some card steps go beyond the speech: "check LTV before doubling down" (card 477d8f00df92 links 2 separate parts of the talk), "requires sufficient volume per landing-page ad set" (card caveat on acad4b08fee6), "recheck both tests when creative volume or product mix changes" (card e336ef61445a). Treat these as the card writer's advice.
- **Conflict with common advice (1).** Many operators, and Meta's own guidance, push consolidation into fewer campaigns and Advantage+ campaigns. This source calls "one Advantage+ campaign with all ads" a terrible idea for any brand with real complexity. It agrees with consolidation only up to the concept ad set level. `[web check: Meta's current consolidation guidance]`
- **Conflict with common advice (2).** A common testing method launches each new creative batch in its own dated testing ad set (for example in an ABO testing campaign). This source rejects date-based ad sets and wants new ads placed in their matching concept ad set.
- **Conflict with common advice (3).** Common reporting ranks single ads by ROAS to pick winners. This source says ad-level ROAS is too distorted by last click to act on and uses spend share inside an ad set instead.
