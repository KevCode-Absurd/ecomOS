## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator a full audit method for a paid acquisition program (Meta first, Google second, TikTok and Pinterest briefly). The audit finds the 1 constraint on profitable growth, using business data first, then 3 pillars: data integrity, technical account structure, creative velocity and diversity. The output is a ranked 90-day roadmap. It is strongest on the business-side models (GP to CAC cohort model, normalized P&L, first-time P&L, aMER versus platform ROAS) and on the "defend every campaign" structure review.

## 2. Principles

1. **An audit is a diagnosis of the constraint, not a list of settings errors.** A typical agency audit is a PDF with 40+ screenshots of settings errors, trends and generic advice. Source says settings debates are often subjective, not tied to the business, and often do not matter. A good audit names the bottleneck, proves it, and gives next steps ranked by commercial impact. [ce4264077b22]
2. **Rank recommendations by commercial impact so they form a roadmap.** The ranking shows the number 1 bottleneck and what will come up next, so the team can work through it over about 90 days. [ce4264077b22]
3. **Use 3 pillars: technical account structure, creative (velocity and diversity), data integrity and assurance.** Source says getting these 3 right gets "99% of the way there" to growing almost any retailer or ecommerce brand profitably. This is the agency's current thesis; it expects the pillars to change in 2 to 3 years. Source advises in-house teams and agencies to use their own core pillars. [ce4264077b22]
4. **Each pillar changes with size and business type.** Data integrity looks very different at 7 vs 8 vs 9 figures, and with retail or wholesale arms. Account structure changes with revenue bracket, spend bracket and industry. Creative changes less, but fashion and CPG need different strategies, velocity and asset mix. [ce4264077b22]
5. **Do not audit on platform data alone.** If only Facebook and Google access is given, the source will not audit or pitch. Its growth method depends on business-level KPIs, not what platforms attribute. Start from the backend (Shopify or other CMS). This is the source's policy, not an industry rule. [3b7f250f224e]
6. **CAC alone is not useful; pair it with first-purchase gross profit in dollars.** Unit economics move. Example: 2 years ago $100 AOV at 60% GP; now $200 AOV at 80% GP. The CAC from 2 years ago is not comparable. [3b7f250f224e] [96ddc2d014f8]
7. **First-purchase GP to CAC is "the best possible metric for indexing your performance over time".** It shows whether efficiency has moved the right or wrong way, so you know whether to do more of the same or reverse a recent change. It is not true lifetime gross profit. [96ddc2d014f8] [021224f4073c]
8. **Rising CAC is fine if new-customer volume rises with it.** If you triple or quadruple new-customer volume and CAC rises a bit, the trade can be worth it. Rising CAC with flat or barely rising volume means you pay more for the same customers. [96ddc2d014f8]
9. **Repeat revenue can hide weak acquisition.** Large 8, 9 and 10 figure retailers have a lot of accumulated repeat buying, so the blended P&L can look good while paid acquisition is poor. Build a first-time-customer P&L. [04bd5299ca53]
10. **Platform ROAS must correlate with acquisition MER (new-customer revenue / ad spend).** If budgets follow a ROAS number that does not match the business, decisions are arbitrary. [378c1eb72ff9]
11. **Retention sets CAC tolerance.** Strong retention allows a higher CAC and a lower first-purchase GP to CAC. Weak retention (which the source says it sees "quite a lot") means the first purchase must be very profitable. [cb04728e9c8a] [021224f4073c]
12. **Find the bottleneck top-down.** Start at business numbers (Shopify revenue, new-customer revenue), then platform, campaign, ad set, ad. [f547d41f4dd4]
13. **Every campaign must be defendable; the goal is 1 campaign.** Start from 1 cold campaign. Each extra campaign needs a strong reason. Business complexity, not ad spend, justifies more campaigns. Source says accounts at $1M to $5M a month often run only 4 or 5 campaigns. [5ce17b008587]
14. **Judge segment spend by 30-day frequency, not percentage split** across new, engaged and existing. [b8a436d33e2f]
15. **Most accounts' content is not top of funnel enough.** Source calls this "one of the biggest mistakes" right now. It shows as high engaged frequency, or as existing-customer spend bleeding into all campaigns because it is not held in a dedicated campaign. [b8a436d33e2f]
16. **Use a tighter, consistent attribution setting on Meta.** Source's go-to is 7-day click plus 1-day engaged view. [451238284cae]
17. **Do not tighten Google's attribution windows.** Google is data-reliant and needs data to accumulate. Check brand vs non-brand separation instead. [89ab42bd67bc]
18. **Google often sits bottom of funnel (halo effect), but not always.** It can be a strong new-customer channel. It is category dependent and needs real skill so it does not over-claim other platforms' work. [ce1358ea9d05]
19. **If correlation stays unclear, run a test, do not guess.** 8 and 9 figure brands: causal geo-lift, about 25 days. [845571c9595b]
20. **You only ever have 1 creative bottleneck at a time: volume, diversity or quality.** [3ecc8cc48319]
21. **Diversity means concepts (angle, offer, persona), not formats.** People over-index on format (UGC, statics) and under-index on concepts. [45c43e3ff759]
22. **Winners must be analysed and replicated.** Find which variable made the win. [7a6be5942fff]
23. **Creative needs a production system with a feedback loop,** not ad hoc shoots when ads run out. [458dbfdbdf45]
24. **Data integrity is the top bottleneck if it is broken.** You cannot do creative analysis or structure an account on wrong metrics. [7101da7d331d]
25. **Fix order: data integrity, then account structure, then creative.** By commercial impact: data 1, creative 2. By time to fix: data is quickest, structure is fast, creative is slowest. So the working order is data, structure, creative. [7101da7d331d] [2156baae2c7a] [f547d41f4dd4]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Scope the audit
1. Confirm access to backend business data (Shopify or other CMS, P&L). If you only have ad platform data, stop and get backend access first. `[needs brand data: Shopify orders with new vs returning flag, monthly ad spend by channel, P&L with gross margin %]` [3b7f250f224e]
2. Frame the audit around the 3 pillars (or your own). Note business type: fashion vs CPG, retail or wholesale arm, countries, sub-brands. [ce4264077b22]
3. Plan the deliverable: ranked findings, 90-day roadmap, narrated screen walkthrough. [ce4264077b22]

### 3.2 Cohort model: CAC, first-purchase GP, GP to CAC, new-customer volume (pillar: data integrity)
1. Open a spreadsheet (Google Sheets or Excel). Column 1: month. Go back about 2 to 2.5 years. [96ddc2d014f8]
2. Column: CAC = total advertising spend / new customers, per month. [3b7f250f224e] [96ddc2d014f8]
3. Column: gross profit in dollars on first purchase, per month. 2 ways:
   - a. Monthly historical AOV x gross margin % from the P&L (assumes accrual accounting).
   - b. Pull average gross margin and gross profit on first order from Shopify if COGS was tagged historically. `[verify UI]`
   - Check the number is what it really was each month. Do not use a flat GP assumption. Method differs by CMS; the source does not give every method. [96ddc2d014f8]
4. Column: GP / CAC (first-purchase GP to CAC; source says "technically not lifetime gross profit"). [96ddc2d014f8] [021224f4073c]
5. Column: new-customer count per month (already needed for CAC). Example values: 3,000, 4,000. [96ddc2d014f8]
6. Graph new-customer volume, CAC and GP over time. [96ddc2d014f8]
7. Read it:
   - CAC worse in the last 6 months (source's example) is where the audit starts: what went wrong then?
   - CAC up and volume up a lot: may be an acceptable trade.
   - CAC up, volume flat or barely up: spend has gone up for the same customers. Bottleneck on scale is CAC; investigate.
8. Decide: scale up, hold flat and optimise, or pull back. Forcing unprofitable cohorts "puts you in a really bad position in 3 to 6 months time". [96ddc2d014f8]
9. Score the current month and the trend against the GP to CAC bands (section 4). [021224f4073c]

### 3.3 Retention layer (source always does this; not shown in the video)
1. Build a cohort analysis at gross profit level over time. [cb04728e9c8a]
2. Measure % growth in gross profit by day 90, day 180 and day 365. [cb04728e9c8a]
3. Compare against benchmarks. The source compares to its own client-portfolio averages (for example against its fashion brands). It does not share the numbers. `[needs brand data: GP cohort curves]` `[web check: category retention benchmarks]` [cb04728e9c8a]
4. Read patterns: worse or better than peers; 90-day better but 365-day worse. Adjust CAC targets and new-customer KPIs to match. [cb04728e9c8a]
5. Source says pulling these numbers is "quite complicated". No step-by-step given. [cb04728e9c8a]

### 3.4 Normalized P&L (volume analysis)
1. Pull a normalized P&L for the same 2 to 2.5 years, monthly. [9cb9b9fec95a]
2. Structure:
   - Gross revenue
   - minus refunds and returns
   - minus discounts
   - plus shipping collected
   - = net revenue
   - minus COGS
   - minus shipping and fulfillment
   - minus transaction fees
   - = gross margin
   - minus marketing expenses (1 line; can split by Facebook, Google, influencer)
   - = contribution profit / contribution margin (source: "technically contribution margin 3")
   - In-house brand: minus operating expenses to EBITDA, then net profit if wanted ("pretty unnecessary"). Agency auditing a client: stop at contribution margin; opex is not needed. [9cb9b9fec95a]
3. Check the contribution margin trend. If ad spend rose and contribution margin fell, the scale attempt failed. Find at what stage scaling was tried, what did not work, and which assumptions about more revenue at more spend were wrong. [9cb9b9fec95a]
4. Use it to verify the cohort model's decision. It usually tells the same story but can differ on total volume. [9cb9b9fec95a]

### 3.5 First-time-customer P&L and acquisition MER
1. Rebuild the P&L filtering out all returning-customer orders. `[needs brand data: first vs returning order tag]` [04bd5299ca53]
2. Compare with the blended P&L to see how much repeat revenue flatters results. [04bd5299ca53]
3. Compute acquisition MER = new-customer revenue / ad spend. [04bd5299ca53]

### 3.6 Reconcile platform ROAS with acquisition MER
1. Put each platform's account-wide ROAS next to aMER (Meta, Google, TikTok, Pinterest, any other). [378c1eb72ff9]
2. Meta: if ROAS is far above aMER (source example: aMER 3x to 7x, Meta 17x), suspect (a) overspend on existing customers, "very typical", or (b) 1-day view attribution crediting people who would have bought anyway. [378c1eb72ff9]
3. Meta: Columns > attribution setting breakdown. Read 7-day click, 7-day click + 1-day engaged view, and incremental attribution. `[verify UI]` [378c1eb72ff9]
4. Source says in 95% of accounts these tighter numbers correlate much better with aMER. [378c1eb72ff9]
5. Pick 1 option: (a) switch all attribution settings to the one that best matches the business, or (b) keep settings but always judge performance on the breakdown numbers, not the headline ROAS. [378c1eb72ff9]
6. Google: do not tighten windows (defaults are wide, such as 30-day click and 7-day engaged `[verify UI]`). Check brand and non-brand are cleanly split. Brand in non-brand (or the reverse) is an immediate red flag; fix it. [89ab42bd67bc]
7. Google: compare non-brand campaign ROAS to aMER. If it does not correlate, check in order:
   - a. Non-brand is truly outperforming other channels, so you may be underspending on Google.
   - b. It looks non-brand but picks up product titles or bottom-of-funnel traffic driven from Meta.
   - c. Only if a and b are ruled out: a setup or attribution issue. [89ab42bd67bc]
8. Pinterest: pull the 7-day click ROAS column; source says it will "instantly correlate" and will likely show Pinterest well below average. `[verify UI]` [378c1eb72ff9]
9. TikTok: 1-day view inflates it a lot; at 7-day click it drops to about aMER or lower. `[verify UI]` [378c1eb72ff9]

### 3.7 Test when the numbers still do not add up
1. If platform ROAS and aMER still do not match after switching attribution settings, propose a test. [845571c9595b]
2. 8 and 9 figure brands: causal geo-lift experiment, typically about 25 days, with parameters set in advance, to validate whether a platform performs. [845571c9595b]
3. Brand spend: if a large retailer believes brand search is incremental, run an inverse test: pull brand from selected states chosen by a clustering algorithm, and use the result to decide whether to keep spending on brand. [845571c9595b]
4. The source gives no design details (cluster method, power, readout). `[needs brand data: geo-level sales volume]`

### 3.8 Drill down to the bottleneck
1. Start at the top: Shopify revenue, new-customer revenue, CAC. [f547d41f4dd4]
2. Name the problem type (for example a CAC issue). [f547d41f4dd4]
3. Look at Meta and Google separately; find which platform. [f547d41f4dd4]
4. In that platform go campaign, then ad set, then ad, until you find what holds the business back. [f547d41f4dd4]

### 3.9 Meta account structure review
1. Count campaigns. List them in a Google Doc or spreadsheet. [5ce17b008587]
2. Next column: write the exact defendable purpose of each campaign. [5ce17b008587]
3. For each, error-check against its purpose: exclusions, settings, creative. Source's worked example of 10 campaigns: campaign 1 is cold, has exclusions, does creative testing: fine. Campaign 2 does the same thing: not defendable as separate. Campaign 3 switches the objective to add-to-cart optimisation: segmentation not needed. [5ce17b008587]
4. Cut or consolidate any campaign you (or your agency) cannot defend. Source says often half are not defendable and consolidation improves performance. [5ce17b008587]
5. Valid reasons to split (business complexity): selling in many countries (example: 7), several sub-brands (example: 3), fast-turning product range, several business priorities. Example: needing to push spend into "dresses" alone to read category return fast. Example: large retailers split Australia and New Zealand so they can move budget into New Zealand when it underperforms. [5ce17b008587]
6. Check 1, frequency: look at 30-day frequency for new, engaged and existing segments (not % split). Existing very high (example: 30) = overspend; cut it. Engaged high (example: 14) = too much middle-of-funnel creative; cut it and make more top-of-funnel assets. Hold existing-customer spend in 1 dedicated campaign. `[verify UI]` [b8a436d33e2f]
7. Check 2, attribution settings: are they the same across campaigns? Different settings change ROAS. Move to a stricter setting that better matches the P&L; source's go-to is 7-day click + 1-day engaged view. [451238284cae]
8. Check 3, testing structure: of the 3 to 6 campaigns left, how are new assets and new products tested? It must be methodical and give clear insights to iterate on. If none exists, build one. The source does not describe what that structure should be here (it points to its separate account-structure video). [5ce17b008587]
9. Check 4, errors (source says this is all most agencies do):
   - a. New, engaged and existing definitions not set. `[verify UI]` [b025c790c4ac]
   - b. Local or store-traffic campaigns (for example engaged-view, drive to store) bleeding into Audience Network, specifically the "app rewards" placement. Remove app rewards. `[verify UI]` [b025c790c4ac]
   - c. Old targeting (lookalikes, interests) on purchase objectives in ecommerce. Source would not use them in 99% of accounts; there is a time and place elsewhere. [b025c790c4ac]
   - d. Existing customers not correctly excluded from cold campaigns. [b025c790c4ac]
   - e. Naming does not match settings or creative (section 5). [36f63e27c6d5]

### 3.10 Google account structure review
1. Same rule: goal is 1 campaign. Write the purpose of each campaign and why it cannot be rolled into 1. No good reason: roll up. [ce1358ea9d05] [5ce17b008587]
2. Branded terms: open each cold campaign, go to search terms, check brand terms are not pulling through. `[verify UI]` [ce1358ea9d05]
3. Google Merchant Center feed: titles optimised, all relevant variables filled, no warnings, no errors (minimise). `[verify UI]` [ce1358ea9d05]
4. Selling in several countries: use a multi-currency feed. One currency for all triggers auto-conversion and adds tax as it shows in Shopping, which "won't perform well". `[verify UI]` `[web check: current GMC multi-currency and tax display behaviour]` [ce1358ea9d05]
5. Halo effect: ask what cold search demand you want, and whether you convert cold traffic well. If not, ROAS is probably inflated by bottom-of-funnel traffic. Example: at $50k a month on Google with doubt, consider moving some to Meta, after digging into branded terms, the feed and non-brand ROAS vs aMER. [ce1358ea9d05]
6. Propose a Google incrementality test when a halo effect is suspected. Source says "for a lot of brands" a test is usually needed to validate the current Google setup. [ce1358ea9d05] [845571c9595b]
7. TikTok and Pinterest: skipped for structure; source says they are generally about 10% of spend.

### 3.11 Creative velocity and diversity review
1. Volume: compare new ads per month to monthly spend / $1,000. If far below, volume is the bottleneck. [ea5e41f9a967] [3ecc8cc48319]
2. At large spend, use models instead (source's 2 in-house models, no formulas given):
   - a. Churn model: monthly churn rate of winners gives the winner replacement rate; with your hit rate, back into a volume target.
   - b. Average winner model: average winner spend and revenue (example: $20,000 per winner) and win rate, back into a volume target.
   - The source combines both. `[needs brand data: winner churn, hit rate, average winner revenue]` [ea5e41f9a967]
3. Diversity (if volume is fine): count concepts (angle, offer, persona) in the account, total and winning, and compare to the spend band (section 4). Fashion: concepts are usually different products or categories. CPG: different reframes of the same product. [45c43e3ff759]
4. Quality (if volume and diversity are fine): read the scripts and watch the ads. Judge on direct-response principles; say why each weak ad is weak and how to rescript, reshoot, or which angles and personas to move to. Source has no SOP for this. [3ecc8cc48319]
5. Pick exactly 1 creative bottleneck. [3ecc8cc48319]
6. Bonus checks: DPA share of spend (section 5); are winners analysed and replicated [7a6be5942fff]; is there a creative roadmap and production system with a feedback loop [458dbfdbdf45].

### 3.12 Choose and sequence the bottleneck
1. Big gap between aMER and attributed ROAS: data integrity is the number 1 bottleneck, above everything. [7101da7d331d]
2. Data fine, but structure a mess (spend bleeding into existing, undefendable campaigns, messy Google): a short-term bottleneck that is quick to fix; fix it fast. [7101da7d331d]
3. Then creative: volume, diversity or quality. Many brands do well on all 3 pillars and 1 is still the scale bottleneck. [7101da7d331d]
4. Order of work: data integrity (quickest, highest priority), then account structure, then creative (number 2 by impact, but slowest: may need upskilling the in-house team or changing creative agency). [7101da7d331d] [2156baae2c7a]
5. Deliver as a narrated screen recording (source: Loom, 1 to 1.5 hours) through the accounts and models, showing what is wrong, where to look and how to fix, ranked by commercial impact. [ce4264077b22]

## 7. Examples and case results

All are illustrations from the source, not promises.
- CAC deteriorated sharply over the last 6 months while new-customer volume barely rose, so spend must have risen: "paying way more for the same amount of customers". Diagnosis: CAC is the bottleneck on scale. [96ddc2d014f8]
- Large retailer: aMER 3x to 7x ($100k/month spend giving $300k to $700k new-customer revenue), Meta account ROAS 17x. Cause usually existing-customer overspend or 1-day view. [04bd5299ca53] [378c1eb72ff9]
- Account with 10 campaigns: 2 cold campaigns doing the same creative testing, 1 add-to-cart objective campaign with no need to be separate. Often half can be consolidated. [5ce17b008587]
- Large retailer with separate Australia and New Zealand campaigns so budget can move into New Zealand; category campaigns such as "dresses". [5ce17b008587]
- Campaign labelled cold top of funnel that targeted existing customers with bottom-of-funnel ads, so its ROAS looked great. [36f63e27c6d5]
- Creatine brand at $200k/month and 200 ads/month, all on 1 concept, fatiguing the audience. [45c43e3ff759]
- Presenter's own brands in 2019 to 2020: some Google-only, no Meta, scaled to seven-figure run rates. Shows Google can acquire new customers. [ce1358ea9d05]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Audits that are 40+ screenshots of settings errors and generic advice with no ranking. [ce4264077b22]
2. Auditing or judging performance from platform data only. [3b7f250f224e]
3. Comparing CAC across years without first-purchase GP. Using a flat GP assumption. [96ddc2d014f8]
4. Scaling on a blended P&L that repeat revenue props up. [04bd5299ca53]
5. Scaling while contribution margin falls. [9cb9b9fec95a]
6. Allocating budget on headline platform ROAS that includes 1-day view or existing-customer spend. [378c1eb72ff9]
7. Inconsistent attribution settings across campaigns. [451238284cae]
8. Tightening Google's attribution windows. [89ab42bd67bc]
9. Mixing brand and non-brand on Google; brand terms leaking into cold campaigns. [89ab42bd67bc] [ce1358ea9d05]
10. One-currency GMC feed across countries. [ce1358ea9d05]
11. Adding campaigns for spend's sake; campaigns no one can defend. [5ce17b008587]
12. Not holding existing-customer spend in a dedicated campaign, so it bleeds into all campaigns. [b8a436d33e2f]
13. Too much middle-of-funnel content; not enough true top-of-funnel. [b8a436d33e2f]
14. No testing structure. [5ce17b008587]
15. Store or local campaigns bleeding into app rewards placements. [b025c790c4ac]
16. Lookalike or interest targeting on ecommerce purchase objectives. [b025c790c4ac]
17. Existing customers not excluded from cold. [b025c790c4ac]
18. Names that do not match what campaigns and ads really do. Source says this is the most common thing it sees in audits. [36f63e27c6d5]
19. Treating diversity as format mix instead of concept mix. [45c43e3ff759]
20. Too much spend in DPAs (for example 70%). [278c94b0c456]
21. Finding a winner and never iterating on it; chasing new concepts without doubling down. [7a6be5942fff]
22. Shooting ads only when needed, no roadmap, no feedback loop. [458dbfdbdf45]
23. Starting with creative before data is trustworthy. [7101da7d331d]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Card error on priority order.** Card [2156baae2c7a] says account structure is number 2 by commercial impact. The transcript says creative is number 2 by impact, and structure is number 2 only by time to implement. The working order (data, structure, creative) is the same. The same card adds "tracking, pixel/events" checks the source never mentions. Use the transcript and [7101da7d331d].
- **Header mismatch.** The document's metadata header describes a different CRO video (sample size, 40,000 sessions per variant, Klaviyo contamination, peeking). Not this source.
- **Retention benchmarks, velocity models and geo-lift design are withheld.** The source names them but gives no numbers or formulas, and pitches its paid audit for them. [cb04728e9c8a] [ea5e41f9a967] [845571c9595b]
- **GP to CAC bands are opinion.** They use first-purchase GP only. They assume no other acquisition channel is excluded. Retention moves them. [021224f4073c]
- **Frequency examples (30, 14) are not thresholds.** The source gives no "good" frequency range. [b8a436d33e2f]
- **Testing structure is required but not described here.** It points to a separate 90-minute Meta structure video. [5ce17b008587]
- **Quality has no SOP.** It depends on direct-response judgment; a scripting video is promised but not included. [3ecc8cc48319]
- **GMC feed optimisation "how" is deferred** to another video. [ce1358ea9d05]
- **Brand segmentation fix not given.** The source says fix brand/non-brand mixing but does not name the method.
- **Engaged-view definition is from memory** and time-sensitive. `[web check: current Meta engaged-view definition and attribution settings]` [451238284cae]
- **Scale bias.** Written for 8 and 9 figure brands spending $100k to $5M a month. Geo-lift, 4 to 5 funnels and multi-country splits may not fit small accounts. Bands are in USD. `[needs brand data: monthly spend, revenue, countries]`
- **Conflicts with common advice** (keep for pass 2): (a) GP to CAC above 3 on first purchase = underspending, while many teams aim for LTV to CAC of 3 (different metric, lifetime not first order); (b) judge segment spend by frequency, not by a fixed prospecting/retargeting % split; (c) consolidate to 1 cold campaign, versus the common practice of separate testing campaigns (the source still requires a testing structure); (d) DPAs called "not incremental", versus common advice to keep catalog retargeting; (e) do not tighten Google attribution, while tightening Meta.
- **ecomOS note.** Many fixes here (consolidate, change attribution, remove placements, exclusions) are ad-account writes. In ecomOS, Meta is read-only; the agent recommends, a human acts.
