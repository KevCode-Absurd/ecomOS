## 1. What this source is for

It gives an opinionated scaling playbook for Meta under Andromeda. Use it to: pick creative format (statics over video), make many variants of a winning ad, put segment words in copy instead of using interest targeting, rescue ads a CBO ignored ("zombie campaign"), test broad against interest stacking, match ad headlines to the landing page, retarget non-buyers with a different offer, and judge scaling by blended ROAS and net free cash flow instead of platform ROAS. It is thin on settings, budgets and how to read test results.

## 2. Principles

1. **Andromeda needs a very large volume of fresh creative, or ads fatigue.** The presenter calls it "a hungry panda". Condition: accounts that scale on Meta now. Evidence: his own account, where even with a full creative team he struggles to make enough video. [5a4c1bc7103b]
2. **If you must pick 1 format, pick statics.** The presenter says he would pick statics "all day of the week". Reasons he gives: (a) statics are much cheaper and quicker, so you can run thousands; (b) his view is that Facebook can only show so many video ads per user session, so the algorithm favours statics, which it can show more of per session. Evidence: his "down and dirty native statics" beat a VSL that took 2.5 months to make. This is his opinion from his account, which also runs 17+ VSLs at once. The "bias toward statics" is his reading, not a stated Meta mechanic [web check: whether Meta favours statics per session under Andromeda]. [5a4c1bc7103b]
3. **Andromeda reads your image, copy, offer and landing page to decide who sees the ad.** So to reach a different person, you put that person in the creative and the body copy. Source says: "the creative is the targeting now". Condition: broad or light targeting. [ffc5c00e2e11] [9869e4293b51]
4. **Identity triggers.** Put a category or demographic word ("dental leads", "lawyer leads", "25-year-old women") in the headline or body copy. The presenter says it is not a magic word; it tells the algorithm who to look for and opens audience pockets a generic ad cannot reach. He claims it "will dramatically drop your cost per lead". Single presenter claim. [ffc5c00e2e11]
5. **You are not selling the product in the ad.** The presenter says the job of the Meta ad is to bring as many applicable people as possible to the landing page. [ffc5c00e2e11]
6. **Do not ride 1 winner.** Turning off all other ads and scaling 1 winner leads to fatigue and the loss of your only asset. He calls this "amateur hour". Clone the winner into many variants instead. [c61bad8559ba]
7. **CBO picks winners among variants, but it is not perfect.** Many ads in a CBO get no spend. The presenter's fix is a separate ad set of those ads (the zombie campaign). [c61bad8559ba] [841a1ded8706]
8. **Ads that look like ads are "dead on arrival".** People hate ads (he cites hundreds of millions of ad blocker installs). Do not try to make ads "pop off the feed". Make paid creative look like the content people already consume in the niche. Proven organic content is the best candidate for paid. [2f566b8d1247]
9. **Stop trying to outsmart targeting.** Meta's engineers have 1 goal: get your ad to the right people. Let the algorithm target and spend your time on creative. Condition: the creative must carry the context. [9869e4293b51]
10. **Long copy widens the "context window".** Short, basic copy gives Meta little to work with, so it limits who the ad can reach. Long copy that says exactly who you want gives the algorithm more data. This is the presenter's model of how delivery works, not a stated Meta mechanic [web check: whether copy length affects Andromeda audience matching]. [0de9911b652b]
11. **Ad-to-page congruence ("scent") drives conversion.** If the landing page does not match what was clicked, people leave. High CTR and low CPC can still come with bad CPA and conversion rate. Most advertisers put 90% of effort into ads and 10% into landing pages; he calls that a huge mistake. [1fa8a27d2993]
12. **Meta is "the best split testing tool on planet Earth" for headlines.** His reason: an ad headline is seen by about 1,000 times more people than click to the page, so headline tests reach significance faster than page tests. [1fa8a27d2993]
13. **Non-buyers mostly did not buy because the offer or product was wrong for them.** So showing them the same ad more often is the wrong move. Retarget with a different offer. [5dff147cb0c0]
14. **Optimize blended ROAS at the business level, and above that, net free cash flow.** Platform ROAS as a percentage is a vanity number. A lower ROAS at higher spend can make far more money. [259da0427eb7]
15. **Scale up to break-even ROAS.** Find break-even ROAS (the most you can spend to get a customer) and "keep feeding the beast" until you get close to it, to take market share. This is the presenter's aggressive stance. The card adds that it is a hypothesis, not an order to spend with no ceiling. [259da0427eb7]
16. **Data integrity gives confidence, and confidence gives speed.** The owner should check the numbers personally, not rely only on an analyst or media buyer. [259da0427eb7]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Weekly static creative block (hack 1)
1. In your calendar, book 1 hour every week.
2. In that hour, take your winning offer or product and make fresh creatives, mostly statics.
3. Treat this as the core scaling task, not a side task.
The source gives no count, format spec or test method for these statics. [5a4c1bc7103b]

### 3.2 Identity trigger variants, the "one keyword hack" (hack 2)
1. In Ads Manager, find your best winning ad. [verify UI]
2. Find the 20% of clients who bring 80% of revenue. List the niches they are in [needs brand data: top customer segments by revenue].
3. Duplicate the winning ad. [verify UI]
4. Insert 1 niche or demographic word in the headline or body copy. Example: "Here's how to get 462 leads per week on autopilot" becomes "462 dental leads", then "lawyer leads", "property investment leads".
5. Make 1 duplicate per niche.
6. Launch them. Check CTR (and, per the source's claim, cost per lead).
The source gives no budget, ad set layout or read period for this step. The card adds: compare against your own control ad, and do not use niche words that misstate the offer. [ffc5c00e2e11]

### 3.3 Winning format clone (hack 3)
1. Do not turn off the other ads to ride the winner.
2. Give the winning ad to an LLM. He names Claude, ChatGPT and his own tool Kong.
3. Set the premise. His wording: read this ad and be the author; you will write to 100 people and not 1 in 100 should be able to tell it was written by a different person.
4. Prompt for segments: "now write this for 25-year-old women", "write it for a 30-year-old woman". Each version should read almost the same, with a few details that fit that person.
5. Order: body copy first, then headlines, then the creative.
6. Aim for "hundreds" of variations.
7. Put the variants in a CBO campaign. Let Meta move spend to the best ads. [verify UI]
The source gives no CBO budget, number of ads per ad set, or read window. [c61bad8559ba]

### 3.4 Zombie campaign (end of hack 3)
1. After the CBO has run, list the ads that got no spend.
2. Keep only those you have "high conviction" are good ads.
3. Put them all into a separate ad set (he says "zombie campaign" and "ad set"). Launch it. [verify UI]
4. The separate structure forces delivery on ads the main CBO ignored.
5. The presenter says he "typically" gets another 20% of ads that are winners from this.
6. Card step: move new winners back into the main scaling structure (not stated in the transcript; card inference).
Not given: how long the main CBO must run before an ad counts as "no spend", the zombie budget, or campaign-level vs ad set budget. [841a1ded8706]

### 3.5 Make ads that do not look like ads (hack 4)
1. Look for content that already performs organically. His example: a 46-minute, lightly edited YouTube video with millions of organic views, run as an ad.
2. Move proven organic content into paid and scale it ("pour gasoline on it").
3. If you have no organic hits, make a burner account: a clean new TikTok and Instagram account.
4. Follow all the influencers and pages in your niche and like their content.
5. Each time you log in, the feed shows the most popular niche content. Use that as the template for the ads you make, then run them as paid ads.
He says he "can guarantee" this beats your current best ad. That is his claim, not a tested result. [2f566b8d1247]

### 3.6 Broad vs interest stacking test (hack 5)
1. Find your best winning creative that has interest stacking on it.
2. Duplicate it. On the copy, set no targeting beyond country (his examples: Australia, the US, the UK). [verify UI]
3. Run both side by side: interest stack vs broad.
4. After 7 days, compare CPAs.
5. His expected result: broad wins ("you will be shocked"). If it does, spend the time you saved on creative.
The card adds: check spend and conversion volume before calling it, because 7 days can be a small sample. The source gives no budget or significance rule. [9869e4293b51]

### 3.7 Long-form copy for broad (hack 5 principle)
1. Replace short, basic copy with long copy.
2. In the copy, describe exactly who you want to reach, so the algorithm has more context.
3. Run it with broad targeting.
The source gives no length, structure or example of such copy. [0de9911b652b]

### 3.8 Headline to landing page match (hack 6)
1. Run 20, 30 or 40 headlines per ad variant in Meta.
2. Find the best-performing headline. The source does not say which metric picks the winner.
3. Mirror it on the landing page headline. Also carry it into the subheadline and the lead-in of the page copy.
4. Keep at least 3 split tests running at any time (his personal rule).
5. Keep taking insights from your best ads, putting them on the page, and split testing to gain 10, 20 or 30% on conversion rate.
6. Action now: open your top 3 ads. Put each ad's headline next to its landing page headline. If they do not match, fix it within 24 hours.
[1fa8a27d2993]

### 3.9 Retarget non-buyers with a different offer (hack 7)
1. Find what the non-buyer came for (the product they clicked on).
2. Retarget non-buyers of that page with a different product or offer. Needs more than 1 product or offer [needs brand data: product range and which products pair].
3. His agency's full retargeting build ("the deep source"):
   a. Top-of-funnel ad sends people to a landing page with a free, high-value content offer (for example a free report).
   b. His team calls every person who downloads it by phone, to learn pains, fears, hopes, dreams, and why they did not book a call or buy.
   c. Retargeting ad 1: an objection-handling ad built from those reasons.
   d. Retargeting ad 2: proof and testimonial ads, run as carousels, showing that the claims in the ads are true.
   e. Retargeting ad 3: a CBO of the best-performing ads for the other offers, products or services, retargeted to the same audience.
   f. Retargeting ad 4: an audit or similar offer, where the call itself has value whether or not they buy.
The source gives no audience windows, exclusions or budgets. Steps b and f are service-business steps. The card suggests ecommerce stores swap the phone calls for surveys or post-purchase feedback (card adaptation, not in the source). [5dff147cb0c0]

### 3.10 Scale against the metric that matters (hack 8)
1. Stop judging by platform ROAS percentage.
2. Measure blended ROAS at the business level, not the ad account level [needs brand data: total revenue and total ad spend across channels].
3. Above that, track net free cash flow [needs brand data: margins, costs, cash position].
4. Work out break-even ROAS: the most you can realistically spend to get a customer [needs brand data: unit economics].
5. Scale spend as hard as you can until you get close to that number.
6. When ROAS drops or cost per lead rises after a spend increase, ask: did we make more money in that 30-day period?
7. Book 3 hours a month to review the numbers yourself.
Card additions (not in the transcript): separate break-even ROAS (a ratio) from allowable CPA (a money amount) and work out both from the store cost model; test budget increases against allowable CPA, cash, inventory and a set loss limit. [259da0427eb7]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is the account riding 1 winner?** Check if most spend sits on 1 ad and other ads are off. Bad: 1 ad carries the account. Next: clone it into variants (3.3). [c61bad8559ba]
- **Is creative volume enough?** Check how many fresh creatives go live per week and whether the 1-hour weekly block exists. The source gives no target count [needs brand data: current creative output and fatigue signs]. [5a4c1bc7103b]
- **Did the CBO starve good ads?** After a clone launch, list ads with 0 or near-0 spend. Next: zombie ad set for the high-conviction ones (3.4). [841a1ded8706]
- **Do ads look like ads?** Compare your ads with what tops a burner feed in your niche. Bad: polished ads built to "pop". Next: native-style remakes (3.5). [2f566b8d1247]
- **Is interest stacking still earning its place?** Run the 7-day broad vs stack test on the same creative. Good for broad: lower CPA. [9869e4293b51]
- **Is copy giving enough context?** Check if copy is short and generic. Next: long copy that names who it is for (3.7). [0de9911b652b]
- **Scent check.** Put the top 3 ads' headlines next to their landing page headlines. Warning sign: high CTR and low CPC but bad CPA and conversion rate, or high bounce. Fix any mismatch within 24 hours. [1fa8a27d2993]
- **Test count.** Fewer than 3 split tests running breaks his rule. [1fa8a27d2993]
- **Retargeting check.** Does retargeting only repeat the ad people ignored? Next: different offer, objection ad, proof carousels (3.9). [5dff147cb0c0]
- **Scaling check.** High platform ROAS with flat spend (his 40 ROAS example) means room to scale. After a spend increase, judge by net cash in 30 days, not by the ROAS drop. Check blended ROAS at business level and net free cash flow [needs brand data: blended revenue, spend, margins, cash]. [259da0427eb7]
- **Data integrity check.** The owner reviews numbers 3 hours a month. The source gives no list of what to check. [259da0427eb7]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Believing you need slick video and UGC to win on Meta in 2026. [5a4c1bc7103b]
2. Turning off all other ads and riding 1 winner until it fatigues. [c61bad8559ba]
3. Making ads that look like ads, or trying to make them "pop off the feed". [2f566b8d1247]
4. Stacking interests to build "ultra layered" audiences and trying to outsmart Meta's targeting. [9869e4293b51]
5. Going "YOLO" broad with short, basic copy that gives Meta little context. [0de9911b652b]
6. Putting 90% of effort into ads and 10% into landing pages; ads that do not match the page. [1fa8a27d2993]
7. Retargeting non-buyers with more of the same ad. [5dff147cb0c0]
8. Refusing to scale because platform ROAS or cost per lead gets worse. [259da0427eb7]
9. Trusting analyst or media buyer reports without checking the numbers yourself. [259da0427eb7]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single operator, anecdotal.** Every result is his own report. No test design, sample size or time frame is given for the 20%, 15 to 20%, or 6 ROAS figures.
- **Lead gen, not ecommerce.** Identity triggers, outbound calls, free reports and audits come from service lead gen. For a store, the "20% of clients" step needs customer segments by revenue [needs brand data: top customer segments], and call-based objection research needs another channel (card suggests surveys or post-purchase feedback).
- **Statics vs video conflicts with common advice** that video and UGC are needed for cold traffic. He also says to run proven organic video as ads (hack 4), so his real rule is "no costly polished video built from scratch", not "no video". His account runs 17+ VSLs, so he is not video-free.
- **Zombie campaign conflicts with the common advice** to trust the algorithm's spend choices and not force spend on ads it ignored. He gives no rule for "high conviction" and no budget, so it can waste money. The card warns it will not turn a weak ad into a winner.
- **Scaling to break-even ROAS conflicts with profit-first ecommerce advice** that scaling should stay above a contribution-margin target. Break-even ROAS means near 0 profit on the first order unless repeat purchase covers it; he does not mention LTV. The card flags this may not suit a cash-limited store and that break-even ROAS is wrong if COGS, shipping and refunds are left out. Card-only additions (allowable CPA, loss limit, inventory) are not in the transcript.
- **Header summary says ROAS "15x down to 5x"; the transcript says "10 to 5"** (and "15 to 11" in a separate anecdote). Use the transcript figures.
- **Headline test logic is thin.** More impressions give more data on clicks, not on purchases. He does not say which metric picks the winning headline. How to run 20 to 40 headlines per ad is not given [verify UI: headline options per ad and how Meta reports each].
- **"Creative is targeting" and "long copy widens context" are his model** of Andromeda, not quoted Meta statements [web check: Meta guidance on creative signals and Andromeda]. The card warns that long copy only helps if it carries real, accurate targeting context.
- **Identity trigger demographic copy** ("for 25-year-old women") should not misstate the offer, and ad policy limits on naming personal traits can apply [web check: Meta ad policy on personal attributes in copy]. The source does not raise policy.
- **Broad test read** of 7 days has no minimum spend or conversion count.
- **Missing**: budgets, bid strategy, ad set counts, attribution settings, audience windows, exclusions, fatigue signals, creative specs, and how to calculate break-even ROAS or net free cash flow.
- **Sales plug**: the Kong tool pitch (180 headlines, 2 body copies per click) is promotion, not advice.
