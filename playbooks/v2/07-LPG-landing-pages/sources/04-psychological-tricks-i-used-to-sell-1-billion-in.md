## 1. What this source is for

It gives a copywriter or page builder a menu of 7 angles ("human hijacks") for the hook, hero, proof and positioning of a PDP, advertorial, landing page or video ad. It also gives a 3-stage market-awareness test (clueless, curious, saturated) that decides whether the top of the page opens on the problem, a contrarian claim, or a unique mechanism. It is strong on angle ideas and memorable historic examples. It is thin on how to build a page, how to test, and what results to expect. Several tactics it praises are manipulative or were fraud; use the mechanics, not the ethics.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 The 7 human hijacks

Clemens's framing: these 7 "hijack the limbic system", so people pay attention and either respond now or store the message for later. "Not every campaign uses all seven", some are strong alone, and "when you put them all together" is when category-changing results happen. The host adds that "even one of these hijacks could literally change the trajectory of your company" (card 569b2b18 attributes this to the source generally; it is the host's line). [569b2b18df3c]

1. **Make it about them.** Talk about what the buyer gets, not the product or why you are excited about it. Clemens: "a big thing that a lot of companies mess up is they just talk about their own product." Tool: "Mario marketing" (he credits a diagram he saw from Buffer, origin unknown): do not sell the fireflower (the product); sell little Mario becoming big Mario shooting fireballs (the customer's new power). Ask "what's in it for them", and hit "something that keeps them up at night" or "a deep dream, hope and desire". Condition: default for consumer category campaigns. Card afd75cbd adds that technical or B2B buyers may need specs first; that caveat is the card's, not Clemens's. Evidence: Pepsodent, Otis (addressed the fear of falling, did not explain the brake). [e39c49f19656] [10ed69f7ab58] [afd75cbd0aa2] [569b2b18df3c]

2. **Make it an event.** "Nothing attracts a crowd like a crowd." People cannot help looking at a crowd, and an event gets press coverage. Examples: Otis's demo at the Crystal Palace exhibition hall in New York (staged with P.T. Barnum), Bernays's Easter Parade float, the Lucky Strike green ball, Dr. Oz's show as a built-in event. Condition: launches, PR pushes, drops. Clemens gives no lift numbers. Card 4caab1a8 turns this into deadlines, countdowns and limited quantities; Clemens never mentions scarcity or countdowns (see section 9, dark-pattern flag). [a68cd8cc16f4] [4caab1a8dc43] [569b2b18df3c]

3. **Powerful demonstration.** A dramatic, visual, even shocking proof beats stating a feature. It "stands in people's minds" for decades. Clemens's theory: demonstrations are physics lessons, and humans are wired from birth to learn physics, so they cannot look away. The host adds that technical, analytical founders resist this ("that's not what a fat cell looks like, not to scale") and must "use the other part of your brain". Condition: best when the benefit is hard to believe or invisible. Keep the mechanism truthful even if the staging is exaggerated (card caveat). [df90571ef30f] [bbd78f74d75f] [eb4d8f33241f] [569b2b18df3c]
   - 3 demo types the source shows: (a) a do-it-yourself demo the reader performs (Pepsodent: run your tongue over your teeth and feel the film); (b) a staged proof event (Otis on the platform while the ropes are cut); (c) a prop that makes an invisible mechanism visible (Dr. Oz popping balloons as fat cells; knife infomercials cutting a penny or slicing tomatoes in the air; the host's water-filter talk where the presenter drinks the filtered dirty water; the host's Mini Katana sword cutting a fired bullet and a steak).

4. **Unquestionable proof.** Proof that people accept without questioning: doctors and experts, influencers, people of prominence, or the maker risking himself (Otis stood on the platform himself; "he didn't use a dummy, he didn't use a monkey"). Clemens files "people of prominence" under this hijack: "when we see someone of prominence doing something we automatically assume it's okay" and want to copy it, "maybe from when we were all in tribes". Examples: 4,500 of 5,000 doctors agreeing on a hearty bacon breakfast; socialites on the parade float; Prefontaine then Jordan wearing Nike; Dr. Oz as a doctor. Condition: validators must be real and claims substantiated; health claims carry regulatory rules (card caveats). [ef9a1f83952e] [f798243cc16a] [96b010d8cbf0] [569b2b18df3c]

5. **Change a daily behavior.** "If you can change someone's daily behavior you can create something big." Clemens says it can be either: hijack an existing daily behavior (Bulletproof Coffee adds MCT oil or butter to coffee you already drink; bacon added to breakfast; Sunkist juice tied to meals) or create a new daily one (daily toothbrushing; a daily probiotic; daily journaling). Mechanism for spread: Hal Elrod (The Miracle Morning) told Clemens people recommend what they are doing now, so a daily behavior gets talked about every day, "a viral effect that is untouchable by anything else". Condition: products really used daily. Card 5395f132 warns that forcing daily framing on occasional-use products can feel dishonest. [c7118092d981] [74f8bdebd325] [5395f132b821] [569b2b18df3c]

6. **Sell the dream.** Lead with the vivid end state: the "movie star smile" behind the film on your teeth, "fly like Mike", more energy for the workday, "Craig pooping regularly". Keep the mechanism for proof, not the lead (card suggests). Condition: outcomes must be achievable and compliant, especially health and beauty (card caveat). [c283dbad2437] [e39c49f19656] [569b2b18df3c]

7. **Help them rebel or feel superior.** People want to show they are "not one of the sheep". Give them "the tools and the badge" to push against "the man" (an incumbent brand, a norm, the older generation). Examples: Torches of Freedom (women smoking in public as a rights statement), running shoes as rebellion against dad's work shoes, VW "Think Small" against big 1960s cars, Apple's 1984 Super Bowl ad against IBM, barefoot shoes against Nike, teens avoiding emojis because adults use them, the anti-alcohol party trend, the host's Snapchat "boomer-proofing" story. Condition: the stance must be real and competitor claims factual; card 811ca350 notes rebel looks date once they go mainstream (Facebook, then Instagram, then TikTok as parents arrive). [aa227ad75ad4] [811ca350e413] [25e97661e430] [569b2b18df3c]

### 2.2 Market awareness stages

8. **Diagnose the market stage before writing the top of the page.** Clemens's 3 stages, from his own Twitter thread (a heuristic, no thresholds given): [2a1909cea73f] [4f34e051128b] [505ba6d2faaf] [d235019925fc]
   - **Clueless:** the market knows nothing about the product. You must "create the marketing wave". Open on something they already care about (digestion, bathroom irregularity), explain the cause, then bring in the category (microbiome, probiotics). Saying "here's probiotics, try them out" would have got no interest. His 2013 to 2014 ads opened on digestive health. [7b22550734f6] [dbd89ccb417c]
   - **Curious:** they have heard the term. You can "jump right" in with a hook that assumes the belief and disrupts it. His 2018 headline: "Doctor says throw your probiotics in the trash". The page then explained most probiotics lack enough strains and that prebiotics matter. The contrarian hook only works if the reader already holds the belief (card d2350199: on a clueless audience it "reads as nonsense"). [7544583064ea] [d235019925fc]
   - **Saturated:** the category is commoditized. Clemens says probiotics are here now; he is not launching new probiotics and tells would-be entrants "good luck". Some still break through "with strong campaigns". The Topics header of the document says saturation needs "a strong unique mechanism or personality"; in the transcript Clemens only says "strong campaigns". [c38c2d54767b] [2a1909cea73f]
   - The stage can differ by channel, geography or sub-niche (card suggests). [4f34e051128b] [2a1909cea73f]

9. **In a clueless market, add a bigger villain.** Clemens's probiotic story gave the symptom an outside cause: hidden sugars and artificial sweeteners in "healthy" labelled food, listed under other names, plus alleged buried livestock-sugar studies and alleged falsified aspartame animal studies. The product was the simple daily fix. Condition: every villain claim must be factually defensible; do not repeat his claims as fact. [dbd89ccb417c] [web check: each villain claim before any reuse]

### 2.3 Other levers in the talk

10. **Sound bites win.** Short repeatable phrases stick and spread: "yes we can" (Obama's speechwriter had to convince him to use it; will.i.am made it a music video), "think small", "like Mike", "I have your back". The host adds Scott Adams's "verbal kill shots" ("low energy Jeb", "crooked Hillary"): one phrase that brands a rival for good. Condition: labels for competitors must be factual and not defamatory (card caveat). Card 890b189e adds "repeat the same simple message across ads rather than varying it"; that is the card's reading of the Trump example (section 9). [890b189e8bfa]

11. **Ride a live cultural current.** Tie the product to a movement already moving (women's rights marches, the running boom, sober-curious socializing now). Reframe buying as taking part ("torches of freedom"). Borrow existing momentum (Nike and Prefontaine) instead of building awareness alone. Condition: trends reverse; smoking later became unfashionable. The flagship example is harmful. [25e97661e430]

12. **A sensory signature protects repeat orders.** Pepsodent rivals copied the ads but not the minty tingle. A focus group found users judged "it's working" by the tingle and went back to Pepsodent when they did not feel it. The edge lasted about 10 years until rivals copied the tingle too. Condition: consumables (oral care, supplements, drinks). Card suggests calling out the cue in retention messages. [f1d84f9b2b6e]

13. **Borrow a proven attention format, then reveal the product.** Clemens's ecommerce brand copied a TikTok "oddly satisfying" ASMR format (a glass bottle of marbles rolling down concrete stairs, clinking, cracking, marbles bouncing; these videos "always have like 10 million views"), then cut to the product at the smash. "Way more people watch that than if we just start with our product even though it's a complete bait and switch." Condition: top-of-funnel video only. Dark-pattern and policy risk (section 9). No performance numbers given. [7d0a861d7e98] [55a825c596b3]

14. **A small, free entry device can start a habit.** Sunkist (Albert Lasker) had an engineer make a cheap juicer and mailed it free for proofs of purchase, so buyers could start the juice habit. Condition: historical example; no cost or uptake numbers. [74f8bdebd325]

15. **Team up competitors to grow the category.** Lasker stopped California growers competing on "we have the best orange", put them under 1 brand name (Sunkist) and sold juice as a new use. Condition: one historic case; not in any card as a rule. (No card covers this point.)

## 3. Procedures

The source gives no step-by-step procedures. The sequences below are the cards' conversions of Clemens's stories into steps. Treat them as card suggestions to test, not the author's method.

### 3.1 Pick the opening by awareness stage [2a1909cea73f] [d235019925fc] [505ba6d2faaf] [b7d24be2fa78]
1. Classify the target traffic: clueless, curious or saturated. [needs brand data: customer survey answers, search volume for the category term, share of first-time buyers who knew the category]
2. Check category search trends over years, not months, and overlay brand and spokesperson terms (section 5). [web check: category term in Google Trends]
3. Clueless: write a symptom or consequence headline that assumes nothing about the category. Teach the cause, then the category, then the product. Add proof and demonstration for the new mechanism.
4. Curious: write a headline that contradicts a belief the reader holds. Back it on the page right away with the specific difference in your product (Clemens: strain count, prebiotics).
5. Saturated: lead with a named unique mechanism (ingredient, process, format) or a distinct brand personality; support it with proof and demonstration (card suggests; see principle 8 note).
6. After the headline, still run the rest of the hijacks: demonstration, proof, dream (card d2350199).
7. Adjust after hook-level engagement data (card 505ba6d2: first 3 seconds of the ad or the hero line). [needs brand data: hook rate, scroll depth, add-to-cart rate by variant]

### 3.2 Rewrite a page from product-led to buyer-led [afd75cbd0aa2] [e39c49f19656] [10ed69f7ab58]
1. Mark every "our formula", "our technology" line versus "you" lines.
2. Name the fireflower (feature) and the fireballs (what the buyer can now do or become).
3. Rewrite the promise with the buyer as the subject and the change as the object.
4. Check each section answers a buyer question: will this work for me, how do I know, what changes.
5. Keep specs lower on the page as proof for skeptics.
6. Test against the original before rolling out (card suggests A/B or holdout).

### 3.3 Build a demonstration asset [df90571ef30f] [bbd78f74d75f] [eb4d8f33241f]
1. Find the 1 outcome that can be shown in a single glance.
2. Choose a type: reader self-test, staged proof event, or prop that makes the invisible visible.
3. Show it with the maker or a trusted person at real risk or real use (Otis on the platform; drinking the filtered water).
4. Keep what is shown true to what the product does; exaggerate scale, not efficacy.
5. Card suggests: place it as the hero media or primary ad creative, and add a short line explaining what the viewer just saw.
6. Check ad platform rules and claim substantiation before publishing. [web check: platform demonstration and claims policy for the category]

### 3.4 Build proof people will not question [ef9a1f83952e] [f798243cc16a] [96b010d8cbf0]
1. List who the buyer already trusts: credentialed experts, practitioners, admired figures, creators.
2. Collect validation at a scale that reads as consensus, not 1 testimonial (bacon: 5,000 letters, 4,500 yes).
3. Have validators use or demonstrate the product, visible in the media, not buried in text.
4. Card suggests: place each proof next to the claim it supports.
5. Pursue editorial pickup of the finding rather than only paid ads (the bacon result went to newspapers, not into an ad).
6. Disclose paid or material links. [web check: endorsement disclosure rules in the selling market]

### 3.5 Attach the product to a daily behavior [c7118092d981] [74f8bdebd325] [5395f132b821]
1. Name a behavior the buyer does daily without thinking (coffee, breakfast, a supplement).
2. Write PDP and ad copy around that moment ("add it to your morning coffee", "take it with breakfast").
3. Repeat the pairing at first use, in packaging and in post-purchase email (card suggests).
4. Match subscription cadence, reminders and pack size to the routine (card suggests).
5. Consider a low-cost starter device or bundle (Sunkist juicer).
6. Compare repeat rate for customers who got the pairing message against those who did not (card suggests). [needs brand data: repeat purchase and subscription retention by cohort]

### 3.6 Run a launch as an event [a68cd8cc16f4] [4caab1a8dc43]
1. Design a moment people can attend or watch live (livestream drop, stunt video, launch with influencers).
2. Give press a reason: a new visual, a named spokesperson, a quotable line ("all safe here, gentlemen").
3. Film it and reuse the footage as ads and social content.
4. Card 4caab1a8 adds: show honest participation counts, align page, email and ads on the same moment, and measure participation and repeat visits, not only same-day revenue.

### 3.7 ASMR format remix [7d0a861d7e98] [55a825c596b3]
1. Find oddly satisfying formats with very large view counts on TikTok, Reels or Shorts.
2. Note the exact sound and visual beats (roll, clink per stair, crack, marble cascade).
3. Shoot the same opening, then cut to the product at the payoff moment.
4. Connect to a product benefit before viewers drop off (card suggests).
5. Test several formats and keep the ones that hold viewers (card suggests). [verify UI] for any platform retention metrics used.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Market stage from search trends.** Track the category term (for example "probiotics") over years and overlay your brand and spokesperson terms. Clemens: the category curve spiked with the Keybiotics education campaign, fell the day they were kicked off Google, rose again with the next brands, and "Gundry" searches spiked on the days campaigns ran strongest. Good sign your education creates the wave: brand or spokesperson terms move with campaign bursts. Sign the market is already curious: the category curve is flat or runs above your brand's. Limits: search interest is attention, not sales; a curve cannot prove attribution. Cross-check with revenue and new-customer data. Clemens's line that you would "see the big spike" for cigarettes after the Easter Parade is hypothetical (no Google in the 1920s), although card b7d24be2 presents it as observed. [b7d24be2fa78] [web check: Google Trends for the category term] [needs brand data: new-customer revenue by week against campaign dates]
2. **Product-led versus buyer-led copy audit.** Count "our" lines against "you" lines (procedure 3.2). Bad: page explains the formula first. Fix: lead with the change, move specs down. [afd75cbd0aa2]
3. **Hero shows outcome or object?** If the hero only shows the product, switch to an outcome-led asset and test (card suggests). [c283dbad2437]
4. **Is the problem framing landing?** Card 7b225507: on cold traffic, check scroll depth, time on page and add-to-cart. If problem-first framing is not landing, the audience may be past the clueless stage. Problem-first also slows aware buyers. [7b22550734f6] [needs brand data: scroll depth and add-to-cart by traffic source]
5. **Contrarian hook test.** Run the contrarian hook against a plain benefit hook on the same traffic (card suggests). [7544583064ea]
6. **Mechanism versus price response.** Card c38c2d54: track whether traffic responds to mechanism-led or price-led messaging to confirm the market is saturated. [c38c2d54767b]
7. **Is the positioning really different?** Card aa227ad7: confirm the rebel frame is distinct from rivals, so you do not pay to reinforce the incumbent's frame. [aa227ad75ad4]
8. **Habit pairing check.** Repeat rate and subscription retention for customers who got the pairing message versus not (card suggests). [c7118092d981]
9. **Demonstration truth check.** Is the thing shown what the product really does? Would a regulator or platform call it misleading? Clemens's own counter-examples: Power Balance bracelet (pressing on the arm at a different angle; "got popped", "unethical") and Cybertruck (the on-stage hit broke the "unbreakable" window). [df90571ef30f] [eb4d8f33241f]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

**Dark-pattern and fake-urgency flags**
- Card 4caab1a8 turns "make it an event" into countdowns, limited quantities and deadlines. Clemens never says this. Any deadline, stock count or "X people joined" number must be real and verifiable; fake timers or invented counts are fake urgency. [4caab1a8dc43]
- The ASMR remix is, in Clemens's own words, "a complete bait and switch". It risks misleading-creative rules, IP complaints over copied formats, and trust. Use only formats you create yourself, and show the product before any claim. [55a825c596b3] [7d0a861d7e98] [web check: Meta and TikTok misleading-content policies]
- "Manufacture consensus" (card 96b010d8's own title) and "plant phrases in reporters' heads" (Holiday) can become astroturfing. Only real, disclosed, verifiable endorsements. [96b010d8cbf0]
- Exaggerated demonstrations: exaggerate staging, never the result. A demo that shows an effect the product does not have is a false claim. [df90571ef30f] [bbd78f74d75f]
- Villain narratives (sugar, aspartame) and "doctor says throw your X in the trash" invite regulatory scrutiny in health categories. [dbd89ccb417c] [7544583064ea]
- Many flagship examples are harmful (cigarettes to women, Garcinia fraud, Power Balance, bacon health claims). The cards say to copy mechanics only.

**Card claims not in the transcript**
- Card 569b2b18 cites "PT Barnum's elephant"; the transcript has no elephant. [569b2b18df3c]
- Card 505ba6d2 says the opening "must answer" competitors attacking the leader's claims; in the transcript Clemens wrote the contrarian headline himself as an attack. [505ba6d2faaf]
- Card 890b189e: "repeat the same message across ads rather than varying it" is the card's generalization from political ads. It conflicts with common current paid-social advice to run many distinct creative angles; the source never tests either. [890b189e8bfa]
- Card 569b2b18 says the source argues each hijack alone can change a company's trajectory; that is the host's line. Clemens says the full combination is what changes the world.
- Card b7d24be2 presents the cigarette and VW search spikes as observed; Clemens says they are hypothetical.

**Loose or wrong facts in the transcript** [web check before reuse]

**Conflicts with common ecommerce advice**

- "Sell the dream, not the mechanism" sits against common advice for saturated categories to lead with a unique mechanism. The source itself says saturated markets need a strong campaign; the header says mechanism or personality. Choose by stage.
- Bait-and-switch hooks conflict with advice that the hook and product must match to keep downstream conversion and avoid policy flags.
