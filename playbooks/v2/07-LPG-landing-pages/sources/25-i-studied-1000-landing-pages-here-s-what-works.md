## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator 7 rules to build or audit a landing page: the above-the-fold formula, emotion-first images and copy (Problem-Agitate-Solve), long pages with good structure, copy for scanners, verifiable social proof, visual hierarchy, and familiar layouts (Jakob's law). Its strongest part is the 6-element hero formula with a clear order and the FUD-under-CTA placement. It is thin on how to run tests, gives no mobile-specific sizes or tool steps, and most proof comes from service and lead-gen pages, not ecommerce product pages.

## 2. Principles

1. **The 5-second rule: the above-the-fold section is make or break.** Condition: every landing page, strongest for cold paid traffic. If you do not hook the visitor in the first 5 seconds, source says you have lost them. Above the fold = what shows without scrolling. Source says "statistically 60% of people will never even scroll past" it, while 100% see it. Put 80 to 90% of effort on this section "when starting out". [f4cbc2b893ed] [web check: 60% never scroll past the fold] [needs brand data: scroll depth by device on the tested page]
2. **The headline answers "why should I care?" and ties to the dream outcome.** Condition: the main hero headline. Do not use it to say who you are or what you do. No jargon, no vague text that makes people stop to process it. One hard-hitting benefit. Quantify the value where you can (bookkeeping example: value shown as time saved). Evidence: agency says it has seen conversion lifts "upwards of 80%" from testing the headline alone (example, not a promise). [f4cbc2b893ed]
3. **The sub-headline carries who you are, what you do and your USP.** Condition: directly under a benefit headline. It explains how you get the result the headline promised and what makes you different from competitors. [f4cbc2b893ed] [needs brand data: USP and main competitors]
4. **At least 2 forms of social proof above the fold.** Condition: hero section. Types: reviews, testimonials, featured publications. Reason given: people who never scroll need a reason to scroll and act. Evidence: "we've just A/B tested this a lot"; more credibility up front raised the chance to convert (no numbers). [f4cbc2b893ed] [8e94b675714d]
5. **1 clear, benefit-driven CTA button that stands out.** Condition: hero section. It must be actionable and visually distinct. [f4cbc2b893ed] [751c54e90cdc]
6. **Put FUD reducers right under the CTA.** FUD = fear, uncertainty, doubt. Condition: hero CTA, any offer where shoppers fear buying online or reaching out. Examples in speech: free trials, return policies, warranties. The curator header adds money-back guarantee and easy returns. Evidence: lifts of 40 to 60% "just by testing this one small thing" (client examples). [f4cbc2b893ed] [needs brand data: actual return policy, warranty, guarantee terms]
7. **1 image or video that complements the message.** Condition: hero section. It should subtly show who you are and what you do. [f4cbc2b893ed]
8. **A strong hero is the bare minimum, not the finish.** Condition: after the hero is fixed. Many visitors still are not convinced by logical reasons, so the rest of the page must keep selling. [f4cbc2b893ed] [7b3bdfd30f36]
9. **People buy with emotion first, then justify with logic.** Condition: all pages. Visitors arrive feeling something: frustration with a deep problem, or a wish for an end-state improvement. Find that feeling and tap into it. Win with emotion (colors, images, design, copy) combined with logic. [7b3bdfd30f36] [needs brand data: customer language on problems and desired outcomes, from reviews, surveys or support tickets]
10. **Images are the easiest way to create emotion.** Condition: hero and throughout the page. Show people in the outcome state, matched to the buyer (education client: many photos of smiling children for a parent audience; tanning brand: a happy person smiling while using the product). Source cites "60,000 times faster" image processing without a source. [7b3bdfd30f36] [web check: 60,000x claim]
11. **Use Problem-Agitate-Solve (PAS) copy to create emotion.** Condition: when the audience has a clear, felt problem. Call out the problem, agitate it ("turn up the heat"), then present the offer as the clear way out. [7b3bdfd30f36]
12. **Long pages do not kill conversions; bad structure does.** Condition: only when every section adds value and moves the visitor 1 step closer to converting. Reasoning: cold traffic that knows nothing about the brand will not convert straight from an ad. "The ad is simply just to get the click." The page must sell like a salesperson. Source says there is "nothing wrong" with a long page and that his agency's best pages are many sections long. This is his stance against "countless studies" on both sides; no test number is given. [4514239a3d5e]
13. **What is hidden will not get seen.** Condition: mobile especially. Carousels and drop-down menus get "very very low engagement rates" (no numbers). Never hide critical content, above all reviews. Always show reviews open on mobile to "force people to consume" them. [4514239a3d5e] [needs brand data: tap or engagement rates on carousels and drop-downs, if tracked]
14. **People scan, they do not read.** Condition: all page copy. Source says people read 20% of content and scan 80%. Write as if only headlines get read. [6ff63786e24d] [web check: 20% read claim and its original study]
15. **Section headlines must state the benefit themselves.** Condition: every section header. Replace "Here's what we do", "What makes us unique", "How it works", "Our services" with the actual point (instead of "What makes us unique", say what makes you unique). Think like a reader, not a writer: put the 1 thing people should know at a glance front and center. [6ff63786e24d]
16. **Social proof must be real proof.** Condition: when shoppers are skeptical, especially cold traffic from Facebook ads. Source says everyone now uses social proof, so people have become numb to it, in particular "featured on" logo bars. Anonymous reviews "could literally be anyone" and could be fake. [8e94b675714d]
17. **Visual hierarchy guides the eye to the CTA.** Condition: every page, especially cluttered ones. Use font size, weight and color to move attention from the most important element to the least. Target flow: headline, then sub-headline, then CTA. [751c54e90cdc]
18. **Less is more: too much choice equals no choice.** Condition: pages with many buttons, links, texts or options. Too many options cause analysis paralysis and exits. [751c54e90cdc]
19. **Simplicity beats creativity (Jakob's law).** Condition: when conversion is the goal and a layout departs from common patterns. People spend most of their time on other sites, so they expect yours to look and work the same way. Flashy design, quirky copy and unusual layouts lose conversions when they cost clarity. [a556c62817f0]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build the above-the-fold section [f4cbc2b893ed] [a556c62817f0]
The source gives this order as "the basics" in lesson 7 and the formula in lesson 1.
1. Write 1 headline: 1 benefit tied to the buyer's dream outcome. Check: does it answer "why should I care?" Does it avoid who-we-are, what-we-do, jargon and vague words?
2. Write 1 sub-headline, close to the headline: who you are, what you do to get that result, and your USP versus competitors.
3. Add at least 2 forms of social proof at the very top (reviews, testimonials, featured-in with quotes; see 3.4).
4. Add 1 clear, benefit-driven CTA button that stands out.
5. Directly under the CTA, add FUD reducers: free trial, return policy, warranty, guarantee. Use only terms the brand actually offers. [needs brand data: policy terms]
6. Add 1 image or video that complements the message and shows what you sell.
7. Test the headline and the FUD block as separate tests (card advice; the source reports each tested alone). [needs brand data: traffic per page to size tests]

### 3.2 Add emotion to the page [7b3bdfd30f36]
1. Name the feeling the visitor arrives with: frustration with a problem, or desire for an end state.
2. Pick images of people who match the buyer, shown in the outcome state (smiling, using the product). Place them across the page, not only in the hero.
3. Write 1 PAS section: state the problem in the buyer's terms, agitate it, then present the offer as the way out. The coaching example named specific pains (dizziness, going through multiple physical therapists and specialists).
4. Follow the emotional sections with logical support (value props, proof) so the buyer can justify the choice.

### 3.3 Structure a long page [4514239a3d5e]
1. Plan multiple value prop sections, multiple social proof sections, multiple FUD and objection sections, and multiple "why we are the best fit" sections.
2. For each section, ask: does it move the visitor 1 step closer to converting? If not, cut or rewrite it.
3. On mobile, list every carousel and drop-down. Move critical content, especially reviews, out of them so it shows open. [verify UI: how the theme or page builder renders reviews on mobile]
4. Card adds: accordions and tabs count as hidden too, and page length should be tested rather than assumed. The source did not say either.

### 3.4 Upgrade social proof [8e94b675714d]
1. Audit the "featured on" bar. Remove repeated logos (the bad example repeated the same 3 logos in a carousel).
2. Next to each press or media logo, add a short quote or snippet of what that outlet said.
3. For each review shown, add the reviewer's real name, a photo of the person, and the platform it came from (source names Google and "Capira", likely Capterra) so it can be checked.
4. Prefer reviews with images of real customers using the product.
5. Keep at least 2 proof types above the fold and show reviews open on mobile (3.3). [needs brand data: which reviews have consent for name and photo use]

### 3.5 Rewrite for scanners [6ff63786e24d]
1. Replace every generic section header with a full benefit statement.
2. Put main value props in bullet points.
3. Bold key words, especially in headlines.
4. Run the headline-only test: read only the headlines top to bottom. Is the benefit clear from those alone? If not, rewrite the headlines.

### 3.6 Fix visual hierarchy [751c54e90cdc]
1. Set the priority order: headline, sub-headline, CTA, then the rest.
2. Headline: bold, prominent, can be a different color. Sub-headline: a different, more subdued color, less prominent. CTA: large and stands out.
3. Remove clashing colors and elements of equal weight that compete for attention (the "before" case had clashing reds and a subdued headline).
4. Cut extra buttons, links and text blocks until the eye has 1 clear path.

### 3.7 Check a creative layout against Jakob's law [a556c62817f0]
1. Compare the layout to the baseline: 1 headline, body text close to it, social proof at the top, CTA right under, FUD under the CTA, image or video.
2. Flag: 2 headlines instead of 1, oversized social proof under the headline, body text far from the headline, headline-only heroes with no body text and no CTA.
3. If creativity costs clarity, revert to the standard pattern. Card adds: if you keep the creative version, A/B test it against a familiar control.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Headline that says who you are or what you do instead of why the visitor should care. [f4cbc2b893ed]
- Jargon or vague headline text that makes people stop to process it. [f4cbc2b893ed]
- A hero with no social proof, giving non-scrollers no reason to scroll. [f4cbc2b893ed]
- Treating a strong hero as enough; many visitors still need emotional and further logical selling. [7b3bdfd30f36]
- Expecting cold traffic to convert straight from the ad. [4514239a3d5e]
- Hiding reviews and key content in mobile carousels or drop-downs. [4514239a3d5e]
- Generic section headers: "Here's what we do", "What makes us unique", "How it works", "Our services". [6ff63786e24d]
- Bare or repeated "featured on" logos. [8e94b675714d]
- Reviews with no name, photo or source. [8e94b675714d]
- No distinction between body text, headlines and CTA buttons; too many elements; clashing colors; subdued headline. [751c54e90cdc]
- Too many buttons, links and options, causing analysis paralysis and exits. [751c54e90cdc]
- Chasing minimalism or creativity: 2 headlines, giant social proof under the headline, body text far from the headline, headline-only heroes with no body and no CTA. [a556c62817f0]
