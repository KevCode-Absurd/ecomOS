## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator a checklist of recurring landing page "conversion killers" and the fix for each. The core is an above-the-fold anatomy (proof, benefit headline, mechanism subheadline, benefit CTA, FUD reducers, value-prop image, second proof), a page section order (hero, pain point with PAS, value props, repeated CTAs, closer), and ecommerce fixes for the homepage (View Product Rate, visual nav, 7 items max) and PDP (thumbnails, reviews first, first-screen purchase info, stated savings, scannable modules). It is strong on what to look for. It is thin on how to measure, how to test, and on evidence. [3cefd472f380] [12c3e5742646] [d9c4d43e2810] [3ff3c8b062b0]

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Above the fold and ad match

1. **The above the fold is the most important section.** Source says 100% of visitors see it and "statistically" 60% never scroll past it. So the offer, the benefit and the action must sit there. Condition: stated for all 3 page types, with mobile as the main view "because that is typically where most traffic is these days". Evidence: stated statistic, no source. Card caveat: the scroll figures are heuristics, not benchmarks. [3cefd472f380] [3ff3c8b062b0]
2. **What got the click must be the first thing the visitor reads.** The ad's specific promise ("Lose 6 to 20 lbs in 2 to 4 weeks") must be mirrored in the landing page headline. A generic headline ("Retreat to a new reality") drops the benefit that earned the click. Source says he sees this mismatch "every single day", on Google ads and Meta ads alike. Condition: any paid traffic page. [3cefd472f380]
3. **The headline carries the dream outcome, not a description of who you are.** "Stop hair loss without surgery" says what the company does. The better headline states the deep emotional outcome: feeling confident when you look in the mirror. Test it with "what's in it for me, why do I care?". [12c3e5742646] [3cefd472f380]
4. **The subheadline explains how, not what again.** Its job is to explain how you achieve the headline's promise: the unique mechanism or proprietary method. A subheadline that repeats the headline ("Transform your body at the world's number one adult fat camp") wastes the slot. In the retreat case, the 8-step "how the program works" content lower down should be compressed into the subheadline. For the redesign he frames it as "explain the pain point followed by what is our actual solution". [12c3e5742646] [3cefd472f380]
5. **The CTA button should state the benefit, not the action type.** "Contact us" is "very overdone". Use the outcome: "Lose fat fast", "Gain muscle fast", "Get thicker hair now" instead of "Schedule your free hair analysis". Card translation for ecommerce: purchase-task labels such as "Choose your size", "Build your bundle", "Add [product] to cart". Note the card and source differ here: the source wants benefit words, the card wants task words. [12c3e5742646] [ae06c62a4a73]
6. **Reduce FUD (fear, uncertainty, doubt) near the point of action.** Visitors arrive "very uncertain" and skeptical. Put reducers next to the CTA: money back guarantee, free trial, "no surgery or pills". For ecommerce: money back guarantee, warranty, free trial, free shipping. [12c3e5742646] [3ff3c8b062b0]
7. **The hero image or video must reinforce the core value prop.** A generic shot of the retreat venue does not show the transformation. Use a transformation image (the page already had some lower down). If the promise is happy healthy pets, show smiling pets, not pets that "look under the weather". "Especially for e-commerce, your images are going to be critical." [12c3e5742646] [d9c4d43e2810]
8. **Use 2 forms of social proof in the hero: one at the top, one at the bottom.** "We always go with two forms of social proof. Just trust me on this. We've AB tested this a lot." Condition: the source applies this to the hero of every page type. Evidence: agency claim, no numbers. Card caveat: agency experience, not a guaranteed lift. [12c3e5742646]
9. **Bare "as seen on" logos are overdone.** If you use media logos, add the actual quote under the logo (for example what Dr. Phil said about the program). Pair it with a second proof type such as a strong testimonial. Card caveat: only real, attributable, permitted quotes. [12c3e5742646] [8940324d5b00]
10. **Best practices only work when they are impactful.** The hair loss page "is trying to follow best practices but it's actually not doing it in the right manner": a generic testimonial, a vague headline, long body copy. Each element must do a real job. [8940324d5b00]
11. **Keep the hero body copy short.** 8 lines of body text should be cut to 2 to 3 sentences max, because long copy pushes the key content below the fold. [ae06c62a4a73]

### 2.2 Focus and friction

12. **Remove navigation on a dedicated landing page.** On the lead gen page the goal is 1 conversion ("All I care about is getting people to click on this contact us button"). A top nav (About, Explore, Blog) is "bleeding traffic" to other pages. Condition in the source: dedicated conversion pages. Card condition: dedicated single-offer paid-traffic pages only; keep normal navigation on storefront, category pages and homepage. The source itself keeps nav on the ecommerce homepage and only asks to make it visual and shorter (principles 26 and 27). [159090e7a12d]
13. **One core action per page.** Two competing offers ("Schedule your free hair analysis" and "Click here to get your free guide") should be cut to 1. [159090e7a12d]
14. **Do not ask cold paid traffic for a high-friction action first.** For Facebook or Google ads traffic, a calendar booking form "adds a lot of friction up front". Collect simple information first with a form. Condition: lead gen, cold paid traffic. No ecommerce card covers this. [ae06c62a4a73]

### 2.3 Page body and structure

15. **For cold traffic (Meta ads), introduce the pain point right after the hero.** Meta traffic is "typically going to be colder traffic", people who do not know the company or offer. His framework puts the pain point section straight after the hero. [81a3fd40b635]
16. **The pain point section must also tie in the solution.** Listing pain points alone ("Talk to us if...") is not enough. Use PAS (problem, agitate, solve): explain the problem, "turn up the heat", then show how you solve it. Card condition: may be unneeded for high-intent, high-knowledge visitors; keep agitation honest and avoid unsupported harm or medical claims. [81a3fd40b635]
17. **Every section headline must carry the benefit on its own.** Imagine nobody reads the body copy, only headlines. Source says "statistically people only read 20% of the content on your site. The other 80% they just scan." Bad examples: "Analysis first, then focus on doing the right thing" (no meaning), "Ready for your first step?" (no reason). [ae06c62a4a73]
18. **Repeat the CTA in every critical section.** One button at the bottom is not enough. "It seems very trivial ... but the more buttons you introduce, the more likely someone is to convert." Card caveats: this is a heuristic; a repeated CTA must not skip required choices such as size or shade. [ae06c62a4a73]
19. **Include value prop sections that explain the process.** His template has value prop 1, 2 and 3. The hair loss page had success stories but "nothing really talking about the process or how it works". [49c753e32e8d] [ae06c62a4a73]
20. **End with a closer section, a "mini above the fold".** "Want to know more? Schedule your free hair analysis" is "not enough of an incentive". The closer is a final recap: why act today, 1 final reason, 3 value props, CTA button, FUD reducers, and an image, video or form. Example: his physical therapy client page. Card caveat: a framework, not A/B validated; a short PDP with a sticky buy module may not need it. [ecc54f71873d]

### 2.4 Digital products and courses (not ecommerce, kept for completeness)

21. **Sell the transformation, not the format.** "Dual health and life coach certificate" plus "online, self-paced, 5 months, 3 to 5 hours per week" is transactional. State the end outcome: an in-demand career that lasts. His videography client went from "Digital imaging technician course" to "Land six figure DIT gigs, travel the world on global sets and connect with high-profile creatives". [12c3e5742646]
22. **Make the price feel like a no-brainer, whether high or low ticket.** Against a one-time payment of $6,750, a "laundry list" of items is weak. Present the offer as everything in one course: 4+ hours of content, 38 videos, certified completion, then explain what each module teaches (module 1: how a DIT works, finding work, bidding; module 2: all equipment, hard drives, laptops, software). [12c3e5742646]
23. **Value stacking with bonuses.** Give each bonus a dollar value and show it is included free: "get all of this, valued at X, for this price". No card covers value stacking; the source gives it only for courses. The nearest card is bundle savings [fd9d123726a1], which is a different tactic.

### 2.5 Ecommerce homepage

24. **The homepage's job is to get people to a product page.** Source says the metric ecommerce brands he audits are most "underindexed" on is people viewing products. Track View Product Rate: share of homepage visitors who reach a product page. Card condition: use it with add to cart and purchase so more product views do not hide worse traffic. `[needs brand data: homepage sessions and product page views per session, device split]` [d9c4d43e2810]
25. **Same hero rules apply to the homepage.** The pet brand's benefit ("gentle vet approved support they need so they can get back to being their happy healthy self") sat in the subcopy; "no one's going to read that". Put it in the headline. Use images that match the promise. [d9c4d43e2810]
26. **Add icons or images next to navigation items.** "One thing that we've AB tested that works really well for any niche." Pet client: the nav opens with images of a dog wearing the products (bow tie collar, best sellers). Result: 15% lift in conversions in an A/B test (example, not a promise). [d9c4d43e2810]
27. **Show 7 or fewer navigation items at once.** "Anything over seven items shown at once, we typically tend to see diminishing returns": visitors get overwhelmed, get analysis paralysis and exit. Basis: "our experience working with high traffic stores and doing lots of AB testing"; no numbers. Card: a practical heuristic, not a fixed rule; varies with catalogue breadth. [d9c4d43e2810]

### 2.6 Ecommerce PDP

28. **Show image thumbnails, not hidden dots.** Hiding images behind carousel dots, or showing "1 of 5" without thumbnails, is a mistake. [3ff3c8b062b0]
29. **Reviews come first in the information hierarchy.** "On any landing page, we always put the reviews as the very first thing people see." Move the rating above the product title. Card softens this: keep rating and product identity prominent near the gallery and test the exact order. [3ff3c8b062b0]
30. **Fit as much as possible above the fold.** Everything up to the add to cart and its FUD reducers should be in the first screen (his red line). [3ff3c8b062b0] [3cefd472f380]
31. **Turn body text into digestible icons.** Give a quick scan of the unique benefits "in addition to the text", because 20% read and 80% scan. [3ff3c8b062b0] [49c753e32e8d]
32. **Do not make people do math.** A discount (the 75.95 example) that is not prominent on mobile is a mistake. "Just tell me how much I actually save." [fd9d123726a1]
33. **Put the saving in the bundle title.** Small saving text under "2x solar warmers" is weak. Tested format: "1 pack save €10", "2 pack save €22", "3 pack save X". Basis: "we've AB tested quite a bit"; no lift figure. [fd9d123726a1]
34. **FUD reducers under the add to cart.** Money back guarantee, warranty, free trial, free shipping. Their absence under the button is called "the final big mistake" in the PDP pass. [3ff3c8b062b0]
35. **Break large text blocks into scannable, image-led value props.** "No one is going to read this." Use the landing page framework: digestible value props, "lots of imagery throughout the page", so users consume the content and convert. [49c753e32e8d]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Landing page audit against the framework (all page types)

The source's audit order, as he walked it:
1. Start from the traffic source. Open the actual ad (Google search result or Meta ad) and write down its exact promise. [3cefd472f380]
2. Open the landing page on mobile first. [3cefd472f380]
3. Check the above the fold against the anatomy in 3.2. Note each missing or weak element. [12c3e5742646]
4. Check the header: is there navigation that sends traffic away from the 1 conversion? [159090e7a12d]
5. Check for competing offers or CTAs. Cut to 1 core action. [159090e7a12d]
6. Scroll the page and compare the section order to the framework in 3.3. [ae06c62a4a73]
7. Read only the section headlines. Rewrite any that do not give a benefit or reason on their own. [ae06c62a4a73]
8. Count CTAs. Add one to each critical section. [ae06c62a4a73]
9. Check the form or conversion step for friction (for lead gen: calendar booking vs simple form). [ae06c62a4a73]
10. Check the ending. Replace a weak final line with a closer section. [ecc54f71873d]
11. Audit every proof element: does it do a real job, or is it best practice for its own sake? [8940324d5b00]

The source does not give a scoring method, a priority order for fixes, or a test plan.

### 3.2 Above-the-fold anatomy (his Figma framework, as described)

In the order of his hair loss redesign:
1. Social proof 1 at the very top (example: "Rated 5 out of 5, 800+ reviews").
2. Benefit headline that mirrors the ad and states the emotional outcome (example: "Get thicker, healthier hair in 12 weeks and feel confident looking in the mirror").
3. Subheadline or value prop: the mechanism that delivers the headline, or pain point then solution.
4. Benefit-driven CTA button (example: "Get thicker hair now").
5. FUD reducers next to the CTA (examples: 60-day money back guarantee, "no surgery or pills").
6. Image or video that reinforces the value prop (a transformation, a happy outcome).
7. Social proof 2 at the bottom (example: "100% success rate, [N] men helped, 25+ years in business"). The source says these hair loss numbers were "all placeholder, all made up data". Never publish placeholders. [12c3e5742646]

For an ecommerce PDP, the cards add: product context, price or offer clarity, purchase CTA and shipping, warranty or return info in the first screen. [3cefd472f380] [3ff3c8b062b0]

### 3.3 Page section order (lead gen framework, as described)

1. Hero (3.2).
2. Pain point section with PAS, placed straight after the hero for cold traffic. [81a3fd40b635]
3. Value prop sections 1, 2 and 3: how it works, the process. [49c753e32e8d]
4. A CTA in every critical section. [ae06c62a4a73]
5. Social proof (2 forms in the hero; testimonials also used lower down).
6. Closer: recap, why act today, 3 value props, CTA, FUD reducers, image, video or form. [ecc54f71873d]

### 3.4 Rewrite hero copy with AI prompts (as demonstrated)

1. Copy all the copy from your page or website.
2. Paste it into ChatGPT and tell it "do not do anything yet".
3. Paste the "headline hero section" prompt from his prompt pack. Output example: "Regain full confident hair in just 12 weeks using our personalized trichology method. Clinically proven results."
4. Paste the follow-up prompt to refine. Output example: "Look younger and more attractive in 90 days using Swiss approved hair analysis and personalized therapy."
5. Paste the subheadline prompt (pain point, then solution). Output example: "Tired of seeing thinning hair in the mirror? We are the only personalized trichology system that combines clinical hair analysis, laser therapy, and plant-based actives to guarantee fuller, stronger, and healthier hair within 12 weeks."
6. For the pain point section, paste the PAS prompt. He says the first attempt was "really good".
7. Build the rest of the above the fold from the outputs.

The prompts themselves are not in this document. Note the AI outputs contain claims ("clinically proven", "Swiss approved", "the only", "guarantee") and placeholder proof. Card rule: review every factual, comparative and performance claim before publishing; AI drafts must not add invented statistics. `[needs brand data: substantiated claims, real review counts, guarantee terms]` [81a3fd40b635] [12c3e5742646]

### 3.5 PAS pain point section (card procedure for ecommerce)

1. Place it right after the hero and product overview.
2. Name a real problem in the customer's words (inconvenient routine, poor fit, storage issue, unreliable product).
3. Agitate only enough to make the cost concrete (lost time, wasted product, discomfort, replacements). No fear tactics.
4. Pivot at once to the specific feature, material, contents or method that solves it.
5. Support with visuals, short benefit bullets and real use cases.
6. Check every claim for substantiation. [81a3fd40b635]

### 3.6 Ecommerce homepage audit

1. Measure View Product Rate (homepage visitors who reach a PDP), next to add to cart and purchase. [d9c4d43e2810]
2. Check the hero headline states the shopper benefit; move it out of subcopy. [d9c4d43e2810]
3. Check the lead image matches the emotional promise. [d9c4d43e2810]
4. Count top-level navigation items shown at once. Cut to 7 or fewer. [d9c4d43e2810]
5. Add images or icons next to nav items; A/B test by device and category. [d9c4d43e2810]

The source does not say where to find View Product Rate in analytics. `[verify UI: GA4 or Shopify report that gives homepage to PDP rate]`

### 3.7 PDP first-screen audit

1. Gallery: visible thumbnails, not dots only. [3ff3c8b062b0]
2. Rating and reviews first, above the title (source) or near the gallery (card; test order). [3ff3c8b062b0]
3. Everything down to the add to cart and FUD reducers fits in the first mobile screen. [3ff3c8b062b0]
4. Price and discount legible on mobile; saving stated in money. [fd9d123726a1]
5. FUD reducers directly under the add to cart. [3ff3c8b062b0]
6. Benefit icons in place of or next to dense text; keep full specs available. [3ff3c8b062b0] [49c753e32e8d]
7. Check both mobile and desktop. [3ff3c8b062b0]

### 3.8 Bundle savings labels (card procedure)

1. Calculate for each option: total price, per-unit price where useful, exact saving vs buying the same units singly. Do the arithmetic in code.
2. Put the saving in the label: "3-pack: save $20".
3. Show options side by side or in a clear vertical list: quantity, total, saving, any recurring terms.
4. Make the saving legible on mobile.
5. Make the selected option obvious and check the cart matches the quantity and saving.
6. A/B test against the current layout. Watch conversion, bundle mix, AOV, margin, returns, and subscription cancels if relevant. [fd9d123726a1]
`[needs brand data: unit prices, legitimate reference prices, margin per bundle]` `[web check: local rules on reference prices and savings claims]`

### 3.9 Dense text to visual benefit modules (card procedure)

1. Find blocks where a shopper must read several sentences before learning a benefit.
2. For each feature, write the customer consequence first.
3. Build a module: product visual, icon if useful, short benefit headline, 1 short supporting line.
4. Use real imagery of the feature in use, material detail, scale or setup.
5. Repeat the format across key value props for a steady scan path.
6. Keep full specs, sizes, ingredients, safety and care info available. [49c753e32e8d]

### 3.10 Proof audit (card procedure)

1. List every proof and reassurance element: reviews, ratings, user photos, press logos, claims, badges, shipping messages, guarantees, FAQs.
2. For each, name the doubt it resolves (performance, fit, quality, delivery risk, return risk, legitimacy, value). Rework or remove those with no job.
3. Pick real reviews that describe the same outcome as the headline.
4. Pair press logos with an attributable, permitted quote.
5. Replace filler headings and decorative badges with product proof, demos or clear policy text.
6. Improve existing elements before adding new badges. [8940324d5b00]

### 3.11 Navigation removal on a single-offer campaign page (card procedure)

1. Define the 1 conversion event (add to cart, bundle pick, checkout start).
2. Remove the full storefront nav from the campaign template.
3. Keep only logo, cart access, support link if needed, and the main CTA.
4. Put all decision info on the page (variants, price, offer terms, reassurance) so nobody is trapped.
5. Check mobile: the header must not crowd the CTA or hide the price.
6. Keep normal nav on storefront, category and homepage. [159090e7a12d]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Evidence is thin.** Only 1 number from a test (15% lift). "We've AB tested this a lot" backs 2-proof heroes, visual nav, 7-item nav and bundle labels, with no data. Treat all as hypotheses to test. [d9c4d43e2810] [fd9d123726a1] [12c3e5742646]
- **Scroll and scan stats are unsourced.** "60% never scroll past the fold" and "20% read, 80% scan" are given as fact. The 20% figure resembles the common usability claim that users read about 20% of words on a page. `[web check: original research behind 60% no-scroll and 20% read figures]`
- **Page type mix.** Most detailed hero and structure advice comes from lead gen pages. The cards transfer it to PDPs and campaign pages; the source did that only partly (homepage and PDP pass). Course advice (transformation, value stacking) has no ecommerce card; value stacking may fit bundles or kits but the source did not say so.
- **CTA wording conflict inside the cards.** Source: benefit CTA ("Get thicker hair now"). Card [ae06c62a4a73] and [12c3e5742646]: purchase-task labels ("Add to cart", "Choose your size"). Keep both; test for ecommerce. `[needs brand data: CTA test history]`
- **Navigation.** Source removes nav on a lead gen landing page but keeps (shorter, visual) nav on an ecommerce homepage. Common ecommerce advice keeps nav on PDPs because many shoppers browse. The card limits removal to dedicated single-offer campaign pages. Do not strip nav from the storefront PDP template.
- **Reviews above the title.** Source says "always". Many ecommerce themes put stars under the title. Card says test the order. Low risk either way; test.
- **"More buttons = more conversions."** A heuristic. Common advice agrees on repeated CTAs on long pages but not on "more is always better". Card caveat applies.
- **7-item nav cap.** A heuristic; large catalogues may need more with good grouping. [d9c4d43e2810]
- **Placeholder data.** His hair loss hero used "all made up data" and the AI outputs used "clinically proven", "Swiss approved", "the only", "guarantee". Never publish such claims without proof. This conflicts with ecomOS rules on invented claims.
- **Missing how.** No test design, sample size, run length, metric definitions, where to find View Product Rate, how to choose which of 2 proof types, or what FUD reducers work by category. The Figma template and prompts are referenced but not included.
- **Related sources.** Source 25 ("I Studied 1000 Landing Pages, Here's What Works in 2025") has a near-identical title and "1,000 landing pages" hook, and this video's outro teases a next video where he builds a page from scratch. It looks like the same presenter and a companion video. Source 07 in this folder ("The BEST Conversion Rate Optimization Tutorial for Ecommerce") also looks related: its mini playbook describes an agency presenter with "80 different niches", View Product rate as the most under-performing stage, thumbnails over dots, and FUD reducers "AB tested a lot". Likely the same agency. The folder merge should treat agreement between 07, 14 and 25 as 1 voice, not 3 independent sources.
