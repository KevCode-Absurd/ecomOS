## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator rebuild a paid-traffic funnel for a high-AOV direct-response offer: ad to sales page to order page to post-purchase one-time offers (OTOs), instead of ad to product page to stock checkout. It gives an 8-item sales page checklist, a 7-trait headline score, order page rules (clarity, quantity breaks, order bumps) and 2 frameworks for picking OTO products. It also gives a 4-document customer research set (research doc, offer brief, avatar sheet, necessary beliefs). It is strong on structure and AOV arithmetic. It is thin on how: no tooling, no platform steps, no test design, no measured results.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Judging a page

1. **Judge a landing page by 2 numbers: conversion rate and AOV.** Mark says only these 2 quantitative metrics matter, not how the page looks. If both are healthy he will "run the ugliest landing pages that I possibly can". He says most people send traffic to funnels where neither is dialled in. Condition: his opinion from direct-response work; no benchmark for "healthy" is given. The card adds that you should track both per page and per funnel step. [694b21ec8c19] [needs brand data: conversion rate and AOV per page and per funnel step]

2. **Measure profit contribution per funnel step, not take rate.** In ecommerce, factor in cost of goods, so the number to maximise at each step is "profit contribution per funnel step" (AOV contribution when COGS is ignored). Condition: applies to every upsell step, stated most strongly for OTOs. [ea7e5b13b044] [needs brand data: COGS per product, take rate per step]

3. **Higher AOV at the same CPA is the competitive edge.** "If you have an AOV with the same CPA as everybody else, but your AOV is double all your competitors, you're going to destroy them." More AOV gives "margin for safety, margin for scale" on ads. Condition: his reasoning, no data. [ea7e5b13b044] [a0bf3baac741]

### 2.2 Funnel architecture

4. **Sell the outcome, not the product.** A product page (PDP) is designed to sell the product. Mark says marketing sells a "transformational outcome", "a step further than benefits". People buy on emotion first and justify with logic after. Condition: framed as universal by the presenter; it is his view. [a0bf3baac741] [9f5970096a05]

5. **Do not send cold ad traffic straight to a PDP.** His analogy: ad to PDP is like asking someone on a first date to go back to your place in the first sentence. Build "chemistry" first. Replace with ad to sales page to order page to OTO1 (and on). Condition: he frames this for pages "where I'm trying to sell an outcome"; the card limits it to high-AOV direct-response offers. [a0bf3baac741]

6. **Do not stop selling after payment.** Most operators collect payment and "never really talk to those customers again". The post-purchase OTO step is "one of the single most important steps" for AOV. [a0bf3baac741] [ea7e5b13b044]

7. **Right after payment, the buyer is at peak emotion and peak price elasticity.** The moment the card is accepted is "the most emotional state that your customers will ever be in". Price elasticity (how much you can charge and still convert at a step) is low on early pages and high on OTO1. He adds "ethically of course". Condition: his claim; no data. [ea7e5b13b044]

8. **OTO1 can be priced at 10x the front-end price.** A $50 front end supports a $500 OTO1 as "a minimum minimum" multiple that can still convert; "some people even do $5,000". Conditions he gives: it fails if your copy or OTO page structure is weak, and fewer people take a $500 offer than a $100 one. "Price does matter." Do not assume a $1,000 upsell will keep a 10% take rate. [ea7e5b13b044] [needs brand data: front-end price, OTO take rates by price]

9. **Pre-sales pages are optional and out of scope here.** An advertorial, listicle or quiz funnel can sit before the sales page. Mark skips them in this video. [a0bf3baac741]

10. **Every step leaks; email and SMS plug the leaks.** Mentioned in 1 sentence at the end, next to the course pitch. No flows, timing or setup given. [758ee8a3f6fe]

### 2.3 Page anatomy

11. **A landing page is copy + media + design. Copy and media carry the sale.** Copy is the words. Media is images, GIFs and video. Design is aesthetics, button colour, theme. If copy and media are right, design "doesn't matter unless you are adding friction". Media's job is to "substantiate" the copy. Button colour and Shopify theme do not matter if the sales message is right. [1e9621a4c281]

12. **Copy is the most important of the 3.** "So many of you suck at writing copy, even if you're using AI." Knowing AI is not knowing copy. [1e9621a4c281]

13. **Clarity first. A confused mind does not buy.** Getting good AOV and conversion rate is "a game of subtraction of all the confusing [stuff]" on the site. [1e9621a4c281] [f3e8514c33f7]

14. **Prefer ugly, fast and clear over heavy and pretty.** He would "much much much rather" run a very ugly but fast and clear page than a heavy page with animations, heavy images, videos and GIFs, and average copy. Condition: his stated preference; design matters when it slows the page or slows understanding. [1e9621a4c281] [f3e8514c33f7]

15. **Bad copy usually comes from bad research.** Write in the voice of customer (VoC), not your own. Writing through your own filters and biases creates "a disconnect". [0106fc07ad6a]

16. **Marketing is belief change.** Visitors arrive with beliefs about the world, the product and the market. The job is to shift limiting beliefs to empowering beliefs that lead to purchase. You must know current beliefs to shift them. Condition: his framing. [e7770b03502c]

17. **Copy should be as long as it needs to be, no longer.** No fixed length rule; long copy is fine, but "you shouldn't ever make it longer than it needs to be" for clarity and concision. [9246e69228b5]

### 2.4 Sales page: 8 must-haves

The list is "eight things ... to convert as many people as possible" and at high AOV.

18. **Benefit-driven headline.** The first piece of copy is the most important; "if you mess up the headline, nothing else matters". Visitors glance and decide in a split second whether to scroll. Mark: "Most people, 80% on average, typically", if the above-the-fold headline and first image do not get them to scroll, will not buy. Score the headline on 7 traits (section 3.3). [9246e69228b5]

19. **Urgency.** Using and "compressing" time to get the purchase. Ethical examples: a sale countdown timer (Primal Queen Black Friday), and "order within X time, we ship today". Fake urgency timers are "treading on a thin line". He disclaims legal advice. [ba6079d8e9e9]

20. **Scarcity.** The "little brother" of urgency, based on volume: limited stock. People value scarce things more (his analogy: Bitcoin vs the dollar). Ethical example: "Due to high demand, shortage of beef organs, very high risk of selling out." He prefers "risk of selling out" over "only 7 units left" to stay clear of legal risk and protect the customer relationship. [ba6079d8e9e9]

21. **Guarantee as risk reversal.** The point is not to have a guarantee "for guarantee's sake" but to make it risk-free "even if everything totally fails". Do not compete on length (30 days, 1 year, lifetime has nowhere to go). Be creative with structure. [982828a9eb80]

22. **Unique mechanism.** Two halves: unique mechanism of the problem (UMP) and unique mechanism of the solution (UMS). The mechanism is the tactical reason the product works and the reason everything the buyer tried before failed. [67d1415b8bc7]

23. **Social proof, including embedded testimonials.** Keep a testimonial or review section, and also place testimonials throughout the page. Each testimonial must do 1 of 2 jobs: overcome an objection, or install an empowering belief. Do not add proof for its own sake. Handling objections before the visitor reaches the FAQ converts more; many objections are not conscious enough for the visitor to go looking in the FAQ. [cc5d84e31feb]

24. **FAQs to overcome objections.** The FAQ answers common questions but its real job is objection handling. Better still, pre-handle objections earlier with a matching testimonial. [cc5d84e31feb]

25. **Credibility that fits the market.** Do not default to doctors, lawyers, influencers or athletes. In some health and wellness markets the medical industry is "the enemy". Use research to find who the market looks up to ("the gurus"). [1dee1f7972ec]

### 2.5 Order page

26. **The order page has 1 goal: the least friction possible between the buyer and a completed order.** "Clarity is king" on every page, most of all here. Enforce "clarity over being clever. Keep it simple, stupid." [f965f0b7466d] [f3e8514c33f7]

27. **Order page split tests are among the highest impact.** Mark says his order page tests are "usually one of the most impactful split tests" in his business, because the biggest drop-off in a funnel is "always" on the checkout (order) page. Condition: his experience. [f965f0b7466d] [needs brand data: step-by-step funnel drop-off]

28. **Use a custom order page, not a stock Shopify checkout, to get order bumps and quantity breaks.** He is "bullish on order pages instead of like the Shopify checkout pages" because order bumps are possible. [f965f0b7466d] [web check: what Shopify checkout now allows for bumps, quantity breaks and post-purchase offers; see section 9]

29. **Order bumps have the highest take rate of any upsell.** Low price ($10 to $20), checkbox format. Examples: expedited shipping, shipping insurance, digital products. [f965f0b7466d]

30. **Ugly order pages work if the copy is clear and the page is fast.** "All of them are ugly. And they just work." [f965f0b7466d]

### 2.6 OTO pages

31. **The OTO page usually adds the most to AOV.** It is his favourite page. [9f5970096a05]

32. **The most common OTO mistake is the product choice.** "The thing that most people [mess] up with the OTOs is what product they're actually selling." Use 1 of 2 frameworks (section 3.5). [9f5970096a05]

33. **Pick front-end products that solve 1 problem and create another.** Mark says 9 and 10 figure brands do this "extraordinarily well". Condition: his claim, no brand named. [9f5970096a05]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Rebuild the funnel architecture

1. Map the current flow. Typical: ad (Facebook, TikTok, Google) to PDP to checkout, then no further selling. [a0bf3baac741]
2. Point the same ad to a sales page that sells the outcome instead of the product (section 3.3). [a0bf3baac741]
3. Send the sales page to an order page you control (section 3.4). Payment is collected here. [a0bf3baac741] [f965f0b7466d]
4. After payment, send the buyer to OTO1 (section 3.5). [a0bf3baac741]
5. If they buy OTO1, send them to OTO2. If they decline OTO1, send them to an OTO1 down-sell. The source gives no down-sell pricing or content rules. [9f5970096a05] [ea7e5b13b044]
6. Optional: add a pre-sales page (advertorial, listicle, quiz) before the sales page. Not covered here. [a0bf3baac741]
7. Measure drop-off at each step and use email and SMS to recover each leak. The source gives no flow detail. [758ee8a3f6fe] [needs brand data: drop-off per step]

### 3.2 Build the 4 foundational research documents

Mark's SOP runs "a few basic prompts" in ChatGPT. The prompts themselves are behind an Instagram DM and are not in the source. His demo product: a digestive drink for gut health and the microbiome. [0106fc07ad6a]

1. **Research document.** Collect what customers say in their own words on online forums, Reddit threads, YouTube comment sections and TikTok. Cover how they speak, pains, desires, "nightmares" and previous solutions tried. Output is 20 to 60 pages depending on the prompt, with sources cited. [0106fc07ad6a]
2. **Offer brief.** Turn the research into how you will sell: product name, the customer's level of consciousness or awareness, stage of sophistication, big idea, mechanism. He calls these the "ingredients" of copy, used "in specific proportions". [0106fc07ad6a]
3. **Avatar sheet.** From the same research, only avatar data: demographics, key challenges and pain points, goals and aspirations, emotional drivers, and a bank of direct quotes. He calls the quotes a "gold mine" for hooks, headlines and all copy. [0106fc07ad6a]
4. **Necessary beliefs.** 4 to 6 statements, each starting "I believe that". Examples from his demo: "I believe that this brand is safe and transparent." "I believe that lasting results are worth waiting for." All taken from the research, "no assumptions". [0106fc07ad6a] [e7770b03502c]
5. Use the documents to write the sales page, headlines, testimonials selection, FAQ and credibility choices. [1dee1f7972ec] [cc5d84e31feb]

Card additions not in the source: check that AI output is grounded in real harvested quotes [0106fc07ad6a]; label beliefs limiting or empowering, map each to a page element, and re-check coverage after edits [e7770b03502c]. These are sensible but are the card writer's steps, not Mark's.

### 3.3 Build the sales page (8-item checklist)

1. Write a benefit-driven headline. Score it on 7 traits: (1) curiosity, (2) calls out the pain point, (3) promises the solution, (4) specificity, (5) simplicity, (6) credibility, (7) time frame to results. Aim for at least 3 or 4 of 7. More is often better; all 7 are not required. [9246e69228b5]
2. If 1 trait is missing, add an eyebrow (pre-headline) above the headline to cover it, for example the pain point, rather than rewriting the headline. [9246e69228b5]
3. Add urgency based on real time limits: sale timer for a real sale, same-day shipping cut-off. Avoid fake timers. [ba6079d8e9e9]
4. Add scarcity based on real supply, phrased as risk ("very high risk of selling out") rather than a made-up unit count. [ba6079d8e9e9]
5. Add a guarantee designed as risk reversal. Ask: if the product totally fails, how is the buyer not at risk? Choose structure over length. [982828a9eb80]
6. Add the unique mechanism: why the problem persists (UMP), why this product solves it (UMS), and why everything tried before failed. [67d1415b8bc7]
7. Add social proof: a testimonial or review section plus embedded testimonials placed through the page. Pick each one for a job: overcome a named objection or install an empowering belief. [cc5d84e31feb]
8. Add an FAQ that handles objections. Where you can, pre-handle the same objection earlier with a testimonial (example: a testimonial from a pregnant woman answers "I'm pregnant, will this work for me?"). [cc5d84e31feb]
9. Add credibility chosen from research on who this market trusts. [1dee1f7972ec]
10. Cut anything confusing. Keep the page fast. Keep copy as long as needed and no longer. [1e9621a4c281] [f3e8514c33f7]

Card additions not in the source: place urgency at the offer block and scarcity near the product explanation [ba6079d8e9e9]; place the mechanism after the headline and problem, before proof and offer [67d1415b8bc7]; repeat the guarantee near the checkout button [982828a9eb80]; repeat credibility near the offer and payment step [1dee1f7972ec]. Mark gives no section order for the sales page.

### 3.4 Build the order page

1. Make the payment fields obvious. Buyers must see where to enter card number and CVV. He has seen funnels fail because buyers could not find where to enter card details. [f965f0b7466d] [f3e8514c33f7]
2. Add quantity breaks: discounts for more units of the same product. [f965f0b7466d]
3. Add order bumps as checkboxes (his example page shows 2). Price about $10 to $20. Ideas: expedited shipping, shipping insurance, digital products. [f965f0b7466d]
4. Do not slow the page with design elements. Plain is fine. [f965f0b7466d]
5. Split test this page first among funnel pages; he finds it the most impactful. No test method given. [f965f0b7466d]

### 3.5 Build OTO pages

1. Pick the format. [9f5970096a05]
   - VSL OTO: a video sales letter embedded on a page "with almost nothing else on there"; the video sells, short details and the offer sit under it.
   - TSL OTO: text sales letter, "literally just a VSL except it's printed out on the page".
2. Use the interrupt frame. Examples shown: "Wait, your order is not yet complete. Please watch this video to get this special [XYZ] offer." Another page shows "Order approved", a middle stage of "customizing your order" before the order confirmation, then "Wait, order is not yet complete. Please watch this important message", with the VSL and the offer directly under it. The first example is from info products, not ecommerce. [9f5970096a05]
3. Pick the product with 1 of 2 frameworks. [9f5970096a05]
   - **More of the same (MOTS):** the same product in higher quantity. Most common in supplements: buy 1 bottle, then OTO1 offers an extra 3-month or 6-month supply at a discount "while we're packaging your order".
   - **Solve one problem, create another (Mark's preferred):** the front-end product solves 1 problem and creates a new one the OTO solves. Example: weight loss with semaglutide or Ozempic; the weight comes off, then comes back after stopping; the new problem is keeping it off, which is where the next product goes.
4. Price OTO1 using the 10x heuristic as a ceiling to test against, and compare options by contribution per buyer, not take rate (section 4). [ea7e5b13b044]
5. Route: OTO1 yes to OTO2; OTO1 no to OTO1 down-sell. [9f5970096a05]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Judging pages on looks instead of conversion rate and AOV. [694b21ec8c19]
2. Sending ad traffic straight to a PDP and then straight to checkout. [a0bf3baac741]
3. Stopping after payment; letting the buyer go at their most emotional moment. [a0bf3baac741] [ea7e5b13b044]
4. Optimising OTOs for take rate instead of AOV or profit contribution. [ea7e5b13b044]
5. Assuming high-ticket OTOs work without strong copy, or that take rate holds as price rises (a $1,000 OTO will not keep 10%). [ea7e5b13b044]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **All numbers are heuristics or arithmetic examples.** 10x OTO pricing, 50% bump take, 80% above-the-fold loss, 3 to 4 of 7 traits, and 20 to 60 page research docs come from one presenter's experience with no data shown. The $535k per month claim is not substantiated.
- **Bump math error.** He says $10 or $20 at 50% take is "immediately a $5 AOV contribution". That is only true at $10; at $20 it is $10. The $500 per day example uses $5.
- **Order bump ethics.** On expedited shipping and shipping insurance bumps he says "we know damn well you're not changing anything", which implies charging for a service not delivered. Treat this as a warning, not a method: a paid bump must deliver a real service. The card flags chargeback risk. [f965f0b7466d] [web check: consumer protection rules on shipping insurance and expedited shipping fees in the brand's market]
- **Urgency tension.** He preaches ethical urgency, then shows an order page with "most likely fake urgency" as a good example without comment. [ba6079d8e9e9] [f965f0b7466d] [web check: local rules on countdown timers and scarcity claims]
- **"Solve one problem, create another" can slide into manufactured problems.** He adds "ethically of course" only for OTOs in general. The card flags the failure mode. [9f5970096a05]
- **Anti-medical credibility framing** (Primal Queen) carries health-claim, ad-policy and reputational risk; the source gives no compliance guidance. [1dee1f7972ec] [web check: ad platform health and medical claim policies]
- **Mechanism claims** like "fat burn switch" need substantiation; not addressed. [67d1415b8bc7]
- **Card vs source on drop-off.** Card [a0bf3baac741] says the biggest leak is "usually sales page to order page". Mark says the biggest drop-off is "always on the checkout page, also known as the order page". Card [f965f0b7466d] says "sales page to order page". Follow the source: the order page itself loses the most.
- **Card additions not in the source:** step-by-step belief mapping [e7770b03502c]; placement of urgency, scarcity, mechanism, guarantee and credibility on the page [ba6079d8e9e9] [67d1415b8bc7] [982828a9eb80] [1dee1f7972ec]; carrying mechanism language into OTOs [67d1415b8bc7]; the 5-second test [f3e8514c33f7]; matching guarantees to COGS and return rates [982828a9eb80]; and almost all of the email and SMS flow plan [758ee8a3f6fe]. Use them as card-writer suggestions, not as this source's rules.
- **Conflicts with common ecommerce advice:**
  - Design-led and premium brands often win on perceived quality; "ugly converts" is one direct-response operator's view. The cards say so. [694b21ec8c19] [1e9621a4c281]
  - Common Shopify advice favours the native checkout (fast, trusted, accelerated wallets). Mark prefers a custom order page to get bumps. The trade-off (possible loss of accelerated checkout, extra tools, speed) is not discussed. [web check: current Shopify checkout options for bumps and post-purchase offers, and conversion impact of leaving native checkout]
  - Common post-purchase upsell advice uses lower-priced, complementary add-ons. Mark's 10x rule is the opposite and fits high-copy, long-form DR funnels (supplements, info products) more than typical catalogue stores.
  - He argues against sending cold traffic to a PDP; many ecommerce operators run PDP traffic profitably. His advice is framed for outcome-led, high-AOV offers.
- **What is missing:** funnel tools and platforms, tracking setup, how to split test (sample size, duration, metric), how to price down-sells, OTO2 rules, the actual research prompts, sales page section order, mobile layout, page speed targets, and any real before-and-after results. [needs brand data: current funnel steps, conversion rate and AOV per step, COGS, return rate, stock and shipping cut-offs]
- **Fit limits (from cards):** both OTO frameworks fit consumables and products with a natural next problem; they fit one-off durable goods poorly. [9f5970096a05]
