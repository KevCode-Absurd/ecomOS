## 1. What this source is for

It gives 1 health and wellness writer's checklist for an advertorial or pre-lander that sits between a paid ad and the product page. The 3 must-haves are Mirror, Story and Transformation (his "M, S, T"). It adds a section order (headline, hero image, mirror, agitate, failed solutions, mechanism, proof, transformation, soft product mention, "Check availability" CTA, author bio, second CTA) and 3 page tips: paragraph-matched images, an author bio under the CTA, and a sticky button that only shows after the product is introduced. It is strong as an editing checklist. It is thin on proof, on test design, on length, on native vs Meta traffic differences, and on compliance.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 The 3 must-haves (M, S, T)

1. **Mirror: write for 1 specific person so the reader feels seen.** "You have to hold a mirror in front of your target audience and they have to feel seen." Naming many ages and genders at once ("if you're 28, 57, you're a woman, you're a man, if you're 18") makes nobody feel spoken to. Naming 1 ("hey guy, 25") does. Condition: he says this applies to advertorials and also to creatives, listicles, comparison pages and VSLs. Evidence: his opinion and a spoken example only. [20cba28f276b] [d6e1e26520c5]

2. **Write the advertorial for 1 real buyer, and it will speak to many.** His "trick": look at your ad data, find a buyer (his example: a 75-year-old man who bought and left a comment), learn everything about that person by scanning the profile, emailing, or sending a survey, then write the advertorial for that person only. "If you then write an advertorial for that specific customer... you are writing an advertorial for a lot of people." Condition: needs real buyers and real answers. Card caveat: over-narrowing to an unrepresentative buyer can hurt if the audience you scale to is broader. The card adds "a buyer who matches the core demographic you want to scale to"; the source does not state that filter, but it is a sensible guard. [20cba28f276b] [needs brand data: buyer list, ad comments, survey answers on age, gender, problem length and what they tried]

3. **Keep the same person from ad to advertorial to product page.** Make the ad about them, put them in the title, have "the same person in your advertorial", and on the product page talk about "the solution for knee pain for women that are 72 and have been having knee pain for 3 years". If any step widens to a general audience, the mirror breaks. Condition: paid traffic funnels built for 1 segment. The header routes deeper ad-to-page congruency scaling rules to 06-MEA. [d6e1e26520c5] [20cba28f276b]

4. **Story: the advertorial is a story from the first line to the last, including the product part.** His most common failure: the first 100 to 200 words are a story, then the page switches to talking about the product. "Storytelling is the way you sell to people." He quotes Seth Godin: "marketing is no longer about the stuff that you make but about the stories you tell." He writes each advertorial as if it were "the most important chapter of the book because the transformation happens in the chapter", and compares a good one to watching Game of Thrones. He admits it is hard: 2 weeks before, his own draft was a story "until I had to talk about the product". [bf3e5b98a5c7] [98430d1864cc]

5. **The goal at the product part: the reader should feel they have not been sold to yet.** "When they've reached past you introducing the product... you want to make it feel to them as if they have not yet read about the product." "People don't like being sold to." [bf3e5b98a5c7] [98430d1864cc]

6. **Every sentence where the story stops and selling starts is a place where someone closed the tab.** This is his test for principle 4 (procedure 3.3). It is a claim from experience, not a measured drop-off. [bf3e5b98a5c7]

7. **Transformation: people buy a newer version of themselves, not a product.** "People do not buy products, they buy a newer version of themselves." Every advertorial needs a before state and an after state. The product is "a vehicle to get there". "You're selling a transformation and you're using a product as a vehicle to get there and you're doing it for specific people only." [33e80017536a] [20cba28f276b]

8. **Write the after state first, then work backwards.** Ask "what does their life look like when this problem is completely solved?" and write the advertorial backwards from there. His example after state: "I could finally play tennis again. I got my new shoes because I couldn't play tennis for 3 years and now I've played 3 games in a row." Example before state: "I couldn't play tennis because of my knee pain." The after state must pay off the reader's perceived value. [33e80017536a]

9. **Spend a lot of time in the before state and agitate it.** Spoken: "you have to spend more time in the before state that feels comfortable. So you really want to agitate the before state." The card reads this as "more time than feels comfortable". Either way, he wants the pain made bigger before relief arrives. [33e80017536a] [47a0c05d4614]

10. **Success test: the reader ends with hope.** "If they finish the advertorial feeling different with hope than when they started, you've done a great job." An advertorial is "a story where you give people hope about a problem they have not been able to solve for years". Readers arrive skeptical; mirroring and story keep them reading. [33e80017536a] [47a0c05d4614]

### 2.2 Mechanism and product

11. **The unique mechanism is what gives hope.** It comes after mirror, agitation and failed solutions, so it lands as relief for a problem they have had for years. [47a0c05d4614] [6704102a81a7]

12. **Name the mechanism so readers can retell it.** Frame it so that "the next time they are on a birthday" and meet someone with the same problem, they say "Did you know this exists?" He says to give it a name: his example is "RTP", "I don't know, rheumatoid trauma protocol. I don't know. You just give it a name. You can give it any name you want." Condition: the name must describe a real, true mechanism of the product. Do-not-copy: his example name is invented on the spot and sounds medical. A made-up scientific-sounding name for a mechanism the product does not have is an unsupported claim. [6704102a81a7] [needs brand data: the product's real mechanism and what evidence supports it]

13. **Soft-sell: put the product in a character's mouth, not in a pitch.** His line: "My doctor was so impressed by this that he asked me where do I get that exact product." "That's how you sell the product, but you don't actually sell it." Condition: only if a real person said it. Do-not-copy: an invented doctor reaction is a fake endorsement. [98430d1864cc]

14. **No price in the advertorial CTA.** "Don't say buy it here now for $50 off. Don't name the price, whatever it is. Check availability." This is his rule for his health advertorials. It conflicts with other sources in this folder (section 9). [98430d1864cc] [47a0c05d4614]

### 2.3 Page elements

15. **Images must repeat what the paragraph above says.** "Show, don't tell": text first, then an image that shows what was just said. An image that does not match the text above creates "friction and it will probably leave". [146a5b208b74]

16. **Images should look like real phone photos.** "Make it very photorealistic... as if someone took their phone out and took an image to put in the article because it's an article. It's a story after all." Do-not-copy: if the photos are AI-made or staged, they must not be passed off as real customer or patient photos. ecomOS rule: generated images can redraw products, so use the real product photo where the product shows. [146a5b208b74]

17. **Hero image: the transformation, problem on the left, result on the right.** His example: a woman grabbing her knee, and next to it the result. "Make it very realistic." Condition: before and after images for health claims can break ad platform policy and need real results. [47a0c05d4614] [146a5b208b74] [web check: Meta and native network policy on before-and-after images for health and body claims]

18. **Add a short, personal author bio below the first CTA.** "At the end of the advertorial you want to write something about the author", for example "author loves to play tennis in their free time", "because people buy from people". Put a second CTA after it. Do-not-copy: the author must be real, or the page must not present a made-up person as the writer. [98430d1864cc]

19. **Show the sticky button only after the reader is product aware.** Many pages show a sticky button at the start or around 50% down. He says not to; show it only after the reader has scrolled past the product introduction. [340d4991b51b]

20. **Put a push line right above the button.** "Click the button below to check the availability", so "they get pushed to actually click the button. They have no other choice." [340d4991b51b]

21. **Native ad traffic: shorter page, more CTAs.** "If you're running Taboola or native ads, you want to have more CTAs obviously and you want to make it shorter." He gives no length or CTA count for that case. [bf3e5b98a5c7] [98430d1864cc]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build the persona from 1 real buyer

1. Open recent ad and order data. Find 1 buyer who bought and ideally left a comment (his example: male, 75). [needs brand data: order list, ad comments]
2. Learn about that person: read what they wrote publicly, email them, or send a short survey. The card lists what to learn: age, location, how long they have had the problem, what they have tried. [20cba28f276b]
3. Write down 1 age, 1 gender, 1 problem and 1 problem length (his example: woman, 72, knee pain, 3 years).
4. Check that this person looks like the buyers you want more of. This step comes from the card caveat, not the talk. [20cba28f276b]
5. Use only that person in the ad, the headline, the story and the product page copy (procedure 3.5).

The source says "scan his profile". Contacting or profiling a customer should follow the brand's privacy rules and consent. The source does not cover that. [needs brand data: consent to contact buyers for research]

### 3.2 Outline and write the advertorial (his order)

1. **Title.** Name who it is for, the problem and a hint of the transformation. Keep it "stupid simple". Formats he gives:
   - Direct: "Woman, 72, how I got rid of knee pain after 3 years."
   - Header form: "Woman, 72, finally free from knee pain after 3 years."
   - Story title: "Last week a woman, 72, came into my clinic and we got rid of her knee pain after 3 years, finally."
   He says to apply the Hormozi value equation and put the "perceived value" in the title, but he does not explain it. [2595318b416f] [web check: Hormozi value equation terms if you want to use it]
2. **Hero image.** Problem state next to result state, realistic (principle 17). [47a0c05d4614]
3. **Introduction: mirror.** Describe their problem so they feel seen. [47a0c05d4614]
4. **Agitate.** "Make it bigger in their head." [47a0c05d4614]
5. **Failed solutions.** List what they have already tried, "so they feel understood". [47a0c05d4614] [needs brand data: what real buyers tried before, from reviews or surveys]
6. **Unique mechanism, told inside the story.** His story device: the character read an article, the doctor advised against it, the character used it anyway "because they're stubborn", got the result, went back and showed the doctor "See, it worked." Name the mechanism (principle 12). [47a0c05d4614] [6704102a81a7]
7. **Transformation.** Show the after state that you wrote first (procedure 3.4). [33e80017536a]
8. **Social proof.** "More women got rid of their problem using this mechanism." In his sketch proof appears both before and after the transformation. [47a0c05d4614] [needs brand data: real review counts and real customer quotes]
9. **Where to get it, told in the story.** "My doctor was so impressed... he asked me where do I get that exact product." [98430d1864cc]
10. **CTA 1.** Microcopy above: "Click the button below to check the availability." Button: "Check availability". No price, no discount. [98430d1864cc] [340d4991b51b]
11. **Author bio.** 1 or 2 personal lines. [98430d1864cc]
12. **CTA 2** below the bio. For native or Taboola traffic, add more CTAs and cut length. [98430d1864cc]
13. **Page setting.** Sticky button set to appear only after the product introduction scrolls past (procedure 3.6).

His spoken recap of the order: problem and mirror, make the problem bigger, name their failed solutions, introduce the mechanism in a story way, give hope, add social proof and the transformation, tell them where to get it, then "Click the button. Go to the page."

Story narrator options he uses (pick 1 and keep it the whole way):
- Doctor as narrator: "You don't want to know what happened to me last week. Mary came in. She was a regular [patient] and she came to me with something that I wouldn't have expected."
- Patient as narrator: the patient reads an article, the doctor is against it, the patient tries it and returns to the doctor.
Do-not-copy: a fictional doctor or a fictional patient presented as real is a fake spokesperson. Use a real, consenting person or label the piece clearly as a dramatized story. [needs brand data: a real customer or expert who agreed to be featured]

### 3.3 The story-stop audit (edit pass)

1. Read the full draft as a reader, not as the writer. [bf3e5b98a5c7]
2. Mark every sentence where you "stopped telling a story and started selling". The card names examples: price, offer, urgency, aggressive claims. The talk does not list them. [bf3e5b98a5c7]
3. Treat each mark as a place "where someone closed a tab".
4. Rewrite each marked line so the product moves the story forward (a character uses it, reacts to it, asks where to get it). [98430d1864cc]
5. Re-read past the product introduction. Pass if it still feels like you have not been sold to. [bf3e5b98a5c7]

### 3.4 Transformation first

1. Write the after state: what life looks like when the problem is fully solved, in a concrete scene (tennis, 3 games in a row). [33e80017536a]
2. Write the before state as its mirror (cannot play tennis because of knee pain, 3 years). Spend real length here and agitate. [33e80017536a]
3. Build the story backwards from the after state to the before state.
4. Put the product in only as the vehicle, not the hero. [33e80017536a]
5. Check the end pays off the value named in the title. [2595318b416f]

### 3.5 Congruency chain check

1. Write down the ad's persona, problem, problem length and promise.
2. Check the advertorial headline uses the same 4 items. [d6e1e26520c5]
3. Check the story's main character is the same person. [d6e1e26520c5]
4. Check the product page speaks to the same person and problem. [d6e1e26520c5] [needs brand data: live ad copy and product page copy]
5. Fix any step that widens to "everyone". The deeper scaling rules live in 06-MEA per the header.

### 3.6 Sticky CTA placement

1. Find the paragraph where the product is first introduced. [340d4991b51b]
2. Set the sticky button to appear only after the reader scrolls past that paragraph. Not at the top, not at 50%. [340d4991b51b] [verify UI: sticky or scroll-triggered button setting in the page builder in use]
3. If the page is edited and the product introduction moves, move the trigger too (card step, not in the talk). [340d4991b51b]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Talking to many audiences at once, so nobody feels spoken to. [20cba28f276b]
2. Writing a vague advertorial "about everyone". [20cba28f276b]
3. Starting with 100 to 200 words of story, then switching to product copy. He says "a lot of people" do this, and he did it himself 2 weeks before recording. [bf3e5b98a5c7]
4. Pitching the product in a sales voice. "People don't like being sold to. You're losing them." [98430d1864cc]
5. Naming the price or a discount in the CTA ("buy it here now for $50 off"). [98430d1864cc]
6. Images that do not match the paragraph above them. [146a5b208b74]
7. A sticky button that shows at the top or around 50%, before the product is introduced. [340d4991b51b]
8. Advertorials with no story, no clear transformation and no mirror. He says he sees "a lot" of these.

Mistakes the source makes or invites (do-not-copy):
- Fictional patient ("Mary") and doctor narrator presented as real: fake spokespeople.
- "My doctor was so impressed... he asked where to get it" when no doctor said it: invented endorsement.
- "More women got rid of their problem using this mechanism" with no count or source: unsupported social proof.
- A scientific-sounding mechanism name made up for the page ("rheumatoid trauma protocol"): implies medical validity. Health claims like "free from knee pain" need substantiation.
- Phone-style photos that are staged or AI-made but shown as real patients.
- A made-up author with a personal bio.
- A page that reads like a news article or book chapter with no ad label. [web check: ad disclosure rules for advertorials in the target market, for example FTC native advertising guidance in the US and ASCI rules in India]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Evidence is thin.** No test, no metric, no client result. The 10 cards all carry "rule of thumb" caveats. Use this as a checklist and a starting draft, then test. [needs brand data: A/B results]
- **Vertical fit.** All examples are health and wellness for older buyers with a long-running pain. He does not say how it works for products that do not solve a pain, such as fashion, jewelry or gifts. The "before state" and "failed solutions" steps may not apply. Treat fit outside problem-solving products as untested.
- **Conflict with other sources in this folder on price and urgency.** He says never name the price in the advertorial CTA. Mini playbook 05 (the $20k/day advertorial structure) puts an urgency and offer bar at the very top, uses a price anchor and offer section, and says some of his best advertorials talked about price right away. Both are 1 practitioner's opinion. Keep both; test.
- **Conflict with common ecommerce advice.** Common advice is to show price early and be transparent to cut surprise at checkout. "Check availability" with no price, plus "they have no other choice" microcopy, can raise clicks but send less qualified traffic to the product page, and can read as a bait step. Judge by product page conversion and sales, not advertorial click-through alone. [needs brand data: click-through to product page and conversion after the click]
- **Conflict on sticky CTAs.** Many CRO guides say a persistent mobile CTA helps. He says delay it until after the product introduction. His rule is specific to long story pages. Test per page.
- **Inner inconsistency on narrator.** He gives a doctor-as-narrator story and a patient-as-narrator story in the same talk, and the soft-sell line is in the patient's voice. Pick 1 voice per advertorial.
- **Inner inconsistency on proof position.** In his sketch social proof comes before and after the transformation; in his recap it comes with the transformation. The cards treat the order as flexible. [47a0c05d4614]
- **"More time in the before state than feels comfortable"** is the card's reading of an unclear spoken line.
- **Native traffic.** He says shorter and more CTAs for Taboola or native, but gives no word count, no CTA count and no reason.
- **Missing:** length targets, headline testing method, how to pick between the direct and story headline, mobile layout, load speed, what the product page should do after the click, how to measure, and any compliance guidance for health claims.
- **Card additions:** some card steps are not in the talk: the buyer "matches the core demographic you want to scale to" [20cba28f276b], the list of pitch signals (price, offer, urgency, aggressive claims) [bf3e5b98a5c7], "explain it in 1 or 2 sentences" [6704102a81a7], and "re-test the trigger if the page changes" [340d4991b51b]. They are reasonable but are the card writer's, not the presenter's.
- **Scope routing:** the header sends advanced ad-to-page congruency scaling rules to 06-MEA and email flows to 09-EML.
