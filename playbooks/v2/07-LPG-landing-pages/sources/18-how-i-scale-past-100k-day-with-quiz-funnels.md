## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator decide whether to send paid traffic to a quiz funnel, and how to build one: quiz length, the first 3 questions as the hook, nurture and proof screens between questions, the email gate choice, and a simple direct-response page after the quiz. Its main point is the back end: use quiz answers as a first-party research engine that feeds ad angles, frame, landing pages, email segments and product-market fit. It is strong on the "why" and on 2 concrete quiz examples. It is thin on tools, build steps, test sizing and results-page logic.

## 2. Principles

1. **A quiz funnel does 2 jobs at once: it sells and it researches.** Front end: a personalized path that ends in a purchase. Back end: answers on pain points, age, aspirations, triggers, objections, frustrations and failed past solutions. The speaker calls this "getting paid to learn from your customers". Condition: paid traffic goes to the quiz, and buyers can purchase right after it. Evidence: his client work; opinion. [4d9eb8148c84]
2. **Personalization raises conversion.** He says adding personalized elements to the sales process "guaranteed" raises conversion rate in any business, because people feel it is more for them. This is an overclaim; treat it as a hypothesis to test. [4d9eb8148c84]
3. **Personalization is more than asking questions. It is routing.** Send people who pick pain point 1 to a landing page about pain point 1 with a matching offer. The gain comes from congruence between ad, quiz, page and offer. Condition: you have a page and offer per pain point [needs brand data: which pain points have their own page and offer]. [4d9eb8148c84] [11555797742f]
4. **Quiz answers are mostly honest.** He says people "don't really lie because they want to be truthful to their own self" and estimates 90% to 95% accuracy, with 5% to 10% less accurate. This is his estimate, not measured. [4d9eb8148c84]
5. **Start quiz funnels from day 1.** He rejects the idea that you need $100k or $500k a month first. Condition: you still need enough spend to reach about 100 submissions in a useful time (see principle 10). [4d9eb8148c84]
6. **Run a quiz for data even when it is not the most profitable funnel.** In most of his cases the goal is to find a winning funnel and write better ads. But he says even a quiz run only for information is worth it, "because this is where you're going to get all of your answers." Condition: you can afford a funnel that may not beat your current one on front-end return [needs brand data: margin and target ROAS]. [4d9eb8148c84]
7. **Without a quiz, research is slow and less accurate.** The alternatives he lists: Reddit, asking and calling customers, reading comments on your own winning ads and on competitor winning ads. [4d9eb8148c84]
8. **Real data often shifts your message, frame and even the avatar.** He says brands get stuck because they hold beliefs copied from competitors or self-belief that do not match the market, which shows up as a messaging, frame, funnel or offer issue. [4a95dc5791e4]
9. **Your customers beat your competitors as a data source.** Competitor research is "like 20%". The other 80% is "cracking the code on frame", how you present the message and what you say. You cannot see a competitor's margins: an ad with huge engagement may be break-even for them while you need 2.5x ROAS. Opinion; the 20/80 split is his framing. [0cbaff6c8e15]
10. **About 100 submissions is enough for a first report.** Submissions, not purchases. He expects most brands to reach this weekly. At very low spend ("$7 a day like my friend") you need more patience. [a8aab5b91a98]
11. **The "what have you tried before" answer shows awareness stage.** Mostly "I haven't really tried much" means you are pulling top-of-funnel people. Mostly named failed solutions (for example "I've tried this serum") means middle to bottom of funnel. [3c016be4d254]
12. **Quiz results shift over time and with your ads.** The age and pain-point mix reflects the ads you run. Analyze a couple of times a month at least. There will be an average, but it moves. [a8aab5b91a98] [3c016be4d254]
13. **The most-picked pain points become ad angles.** Example from the transcript: if everyone reports chin and mouth area problems, that is "a perfect example of an angle that I would probably test". [71392b19ec6c]
14. **Frame is how you present the message; pick it from the quiz data.** His frame options: lead with a doctor, show a lot of proof, or make statements that "make a lot of sense". Messaging means the angle, what you say. He says both frame and messaging can change based on surveys. He gives no rule for which answer pattern maps to which frame. [14d287ff7489]
15. **Weak product-market fit is often a message problem.** He says brands cannot scale because the product and offer shown to a specific person do not resonate. Tweak messaging, problem and frame, and resonance rises. Opinion; treat as a hypothesis. [4a95dc5791e4]
16. **Conversion rate and revenue per visitor are the scale numbers.** He calls conversion rate and revenue per visitor "as a holistic number" very important to scalability. [4d9eb8148c84]
17. **Build the quiz with AI so your own beliefs do not bias it.** Give the AI context (what you sell, who you target, who you think your avatars are). A self-written quiz carries your unquestioned internal beliefs. Opinion. [11ad5d80b42f]
18. **The first 3 questions are the hook.** He compares them to the bridge between a video's intro and body. They are "the door" to all later questions. Evidence: "across several clients", changing the first 3 questions changed how far the funnel opened. [5b8e0e66fb00]
19. **Quiz completers are high intent but still need proof on the sales page.** "They went through a quiz, they're sold probably. They need just a bit more conviction." So the post-quiz page is short and heavy on social proof. [4c990d3bdb82]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build the quiz (the speaker's method)
1. Write a context brief for an AI tool: what you sell, who you are trying to target, who you think your potential avatars are [needs brand data: product facts, target customer, avatar hypotheses]. [11ad5d80b42f]
2. Have the AI draft the quiz so it is not shaped by your own beliefs. The speaker gives no prompt. [11ad5d80b42f]
3. Aim for 15 to 25 questions, about 20 on average, about 5 minutes to finish. He notes his own run of a 20-ish question quiz took about 2 minutes. [4c990d3bdb82]
4. Include the research questions he names or shows (full list in section 5.1): age, main goal, awareness of the method, satisfaction with the current state, focus area, what they have tried before, and a lifestyle habit.
5. Between questions, add nurture screens: education on how the method works, authority, social proof, before and after, results trajectory (see 3.2). [4c990d3bdb82]
6. Decide on the email step (see 3.4). [3269b97e0ba6]
7. After the quiz, send people to a simple direct-response sales page. Where you have one, send each pain point to its own page and offer (see 3.5). [4d9eb8148c84]
8. Review the draft for product accuracy and claims before launch. The source does not say this; the card adds it [11ad5d80b42f]. It matters because the example screens make result and study claims.

The source does not name a quiz builder, integration steps, logic branching or how results pages are chosen per answer [verify UI: quiz tool branching, tagging and export].

### 3.2 Order of screens (from the face yoga quiz example)
This is the sequence as the speaker clicked through it. It is 1 example, not a rule. [4c990d3bdb82]
1. Age.
2. "What is your main goal?" (he picked tighten skin).
3. "Have you heard about face yoga before?" Awareness question.
4. Education screens: "How does this work?", "What is face yoga?". He calls this "adding elements to a traditional landing page within the quiz to nurture people."
5. "Are you generally satisfied with your skin condition?"
6. "What's your focus?" (areas like forehead, mouth, chin).
7. More education and nurture, then social proof at intervals.
8. Authority screen: made "in collaboration with certified beauty experts" and wellness coaches.
9. Loading screen: "connecting to the database to give you a personalized" result.
10. Before and after of the same person, told as her story. He says it looks very realistic.
11. Program stage: "Now that your program is adjusted to your skin type and concerns", a claim of "2x" chance to minimize a tired look, "based on a 12-week study of active" users.
12. Proof tied to the user's answer: "400,000 women in their 30s" (the age he selected) have made progress.
13. Result trajectory: "reverse your wrinkles" (his selected goal) "with science and self-care", shown as a trajectory with social proof.
14. Email capture (he typed a test address), then a simple sales page.

### 3.3 Test the first 3 questions first
1. Write 2 versions of the first 3 questions. [5b8e0e66fb00]
2. Send traffic to both and measure the share of starters who get through question 3. His illustration: version A 70%, version B 85%. [5b8e0e66fb00]
3. Keep the version that lets more people through. More throughput means more customers and more data.
4. Only then work on later questions. (The card adds "optimize later questions and nurture screens after"; the speaker says "if we just focus on the first three questions initially".)
5. The card also says keep the first 3 low-friction, such as age and main goal. The speaker does not state this rule, but his face yoga example does open with age and main goal. [5b8e0e66fb00]

The source gives no sample size, test length or significance method [needs brand data: quiz starts per week to size the test].

### 3.4 Decide on the email gate
1. Decide which KPI matters more: front-end conversion and cash, or an owned list for retargeting. [3269b97e0ba6]
2. Add the email step if you want stronger retargeting and can accept slightly lower conversion. Some people will not give their details, so expect some drop.
3. Lean toward adding it for subscription offers, where front-end cash matters less. His example: "It doesn't really matter if you're getting a 2 or a 1.8" ROAS. [needs brand data: subscription share and repeat rate]
4. Skip it if front-end conversion matters most. You still get the quiz answers, but you lose email retargeting, "which is a bummer".
5. The card says to test both and compare conversion and revenue per visitor. The speaker says "you decide", not "test". Testing is the card's addition, and a sound one. [3269b97e0ba6]

Note: card 3269b97e0ba6 says to route users to a sales page like "resend instructions". In the transcript, "resend instructions" is a screen label he reads after entering the email, not advice.

### 3.5 Segment and route by answer
1. Integrate the quiz with your email tool so each answer is stored on the contact [verify UI: quiz to email platform field mapping]. The speaker says only "if you integrate your quiz effectively". [11555797742f]
2. Send different emails to people who chose pain point 1, 2 or 3. [11555797742f]
3. Where you can, send each pain point to a landing page about that pain point, with a personalized offer. [4d9eb8148c84]

### 3.6 Report on quiz answers
1. Wait for about 100 submissions. [a8aab5b91a98]
2. Export all answers from the quiz app [verify UI: export format and location].
3. Paste them into ChatGPT, Claude or another AI and ask it to draw conclusions and write a report. He gives no prompt and no report format. [a8aab5b91a98]
4. Read the age mix, main goal mix, focus areas, what they tried, and habits (see 5.1).
5. Read the awareness stage from the "what have you tried" question (principle 11). [3c016be4d254]
6. Repeat at least a couple of times a month. [a8aab5b91a98]
7. Do arithmetic (shares, counts) in code or the quiz tool's own report, not in the AI. That is an ecomOS rule, not the source's.

### 3.7 Turn learnings into changes
1. Generate learnings the same way you do for ads: for each win or loss, ask whether it came from the editing style, the messaging, a before-and-after static, and so on. He says most often it is the messaging. [71392b19ec6c]
2. From the quiz data (focus areas, problems, products tried, habits), write angles. Only after this data is in, "then and only then", pick angles and frames. [71392b19ec6c]
3. Pick the frame from the survey: doctor, proof-heavy, or logical statements. [14d287ff7489]
4. Shift creatives, funnel, messaging, angles and frame, "and more", toward what the data shows, to raise product-market fit. [4a95dc5791e4]
5. Watch conversion rate and revenue per visitor. [4a95dc5791e4]

Card additions not in the transcript: pick frame by clusters of "skepticism, aspiration, or rational objection"; test frame separately from angle; apply the frame across ads, quiz screens and sales page [14d287ff7489]. Use awareness mix to choose education (top) or proof and differentiation (mid and bottom) [3c016be4d254]. These are reasonable, but they are the card writer's, not the speaker's.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Seeing quiz funnels only as a conversion rate test and ignoring the data value. [4d9eb8148c84]
2. Waiting until $100k or $500k a month to start. [4d9eb8148c84]
3. Copying and pasting competitor concepts, common with low to mid 7-figure brands. Inspiration is fine; relying on their data is not. [0cbaff6c8e15]
4. Assuming a competitor's viral ad is profitable. It may be break-even for them while you need 2.5x. [0cbaff6c8e15]
5. Writing the quiz yourself and baking in your own untested beliefs. [11ad5d80b42f]
6. Holding beliefs from competitors or self-belief that do not match the market, which becomes a messaging, frame, funnel or offer problem. [4a95dc5791e4]
7. Optimizing later questions before the first 3. [5b8e0e66fb00]
8. Reading 1 report as permanent. Results shift with ads and time. [a8aab5b91a98]
9. Expecting fast reports at very low spend ($7 a day). [a8aab5b91a98]
10. Running ads without doing learnings on why they won or lost. [71392b19ec6c]
11. Collecting answers but sending everyone to the same page and offer. He wonders whether the hair brand uses the quiz only to "trick people that it's personalized". [4d9eb8148c84]
12. Building a long, complex post-quiz page. Both examples keep it simple and proof-heavy. [4c990d3bdb82]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single presenter, agency pitch.** Most rules come from his clients and 2 observed quizzes. One case result, no sample sizes. Treat every number as an example.
- **Header and card distortion.** The curator header and card [71392b19ec6c] say 70% picked "chin fat". The transcript says 70% tighten skin and 20% face fat. Chin and mouth is a separate example angle. Do not cite "70% chin fat".
- **Card additions.** Several card steps are not in the transcript: low-friction first 3 questions [5b8e0e66fb00], "test both" email variants [3269b97e0ba6], frame by skepticism or aspiration clusters and testing frame apart from angle [14d287ff7489], education vs proof by awareness mix [3c016be4d254], truthfulness and compliance review [4c990d3bdb82] [11ad5d80b42f]. They are sensible but unsourced.
- **Quiz length conflicts with common advice.** Many product-recommendation quiz guides advise short quizzes (often under 10 questions) to limit drop-off [web check: current quiz length benchmarks]. This source says 15 to 25, arguing completers are higher intent. His examples are app and health-style problem categories with education screens. The source gives no example for gift, fashion or jewelry categories, where fewer pains exist. When the product is not problem-solution, this length is untested here.
- **"People don't lie" vs survey bias.** Self-reported answers can be skewed by leading options and social desirability. His 90% to 95% figure is a guess.
- **"Guaranteed" conversion lift from personalization** is an overclaim. The only number is 1 client, about 2.3% to 3%.
- **Claims risk.** The examples use "2x chance", "12-week study", results timelines and doctor proof. Only use such screens with true, substantiated claims [needs brand data: real study or customer results data]. Health, hair and skin claims face ad and consumer rules [web check: claims rules for the category and market].
- **Subscription pre-selected** is shown as normal. It can conflict with auto-renew and consent rules in some markets [web check: subscription pre-selection rules].
- **AI drafting and AI reports.** He gives no prompt, no report structure and no check of the AI's reading. AI counts can be wrong; compute shares in code.
- **Missing how:** no quiz tool, no branching logic, no results-page mapping, no test sizing, no completion or email-capture benchmarks, no quiz-to-purchase rate, no guidance on the result page copy beyond "simple, lots of proof", no mobile notes.
- **Frame mapping is missing.** He says pick frame from the survey but never says which answers point to doctor, proof or logic.
- **Revenue per visitor vs completion.** He optimizes the first 3 questions for throughput. More throughput with lower-intent people could lower purchase rate; he does not address this. Judge hook tests on downstream purchases or revenue per visitor too [needs brand data: purchase rate by quiz variant].
- **Folder fit.** The header files it under 06-MEA. The build and page parts belong here (07-LPG). The data and angle parts overlap with measurement (06) and creative strategy folders.
