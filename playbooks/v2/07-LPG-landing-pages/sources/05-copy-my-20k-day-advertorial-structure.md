## 1. What this source is for

It gives 1 dropshipper's advertorial page skeleton for cold Meta traffic, in the order he builds it: urgency bar, qualifying headline, hero GIF, credibility figure, symptom list, common enemy, gap, product and USPs, proof, exclusivity, price anchor, offer, risk reversal, 2-option close. It also gives his order of work: find the avatar, win an angle with native image ads in a CBO test, then build the advertorial and scale it in its own campaign. It ends with a Claude prompt that rebuilds the structure for a new brand as HTML, then a page builder. It is strong on section order and on angle-first thinking. It is thin on test design, sample sizes, page metrics (no conversion rate, no bounce rate, no scroll depth) and on proof that the advertorial beat sending traffic to the product page.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Order of work: angle first, page second

1. **Know who you are speaking to before anything else.** "I don't even care what your problem is... I need to know who I'm speaking to first. From there, I can reverse engineer." He quotes Dan Kennedy: "When you try to speak to everyone, you end up speaking to nobody." His coaching-business example: not "Instagram ads" but "IG ads for coaches", helping trainers and coaches build a remote coaching business and make $10k a month. Condition: his core method for every page. Evidence: his account of the teeth brand turning only after he switched avatar. [53d57d05d296] [489c9dc0c84e]

2. **Prove the angle with ads before you build the advertorial.** "None of this will work if you don't have a proven angle." He tests 5 angles in a CBO campaign, takes the best one as the winning angle, then builds the advertorial and runs it in a separate campaign to scale. He says not every campaign needs an advertorial, but it "will definitely juice up sales" once the angle is found. Condition: cold traffic, product already has a winning native image angle. The 5 count is his habit, not a tested optimum. [108973ca4a70] [b7214132b1ab]

3. **In a crowded niche, pick an under-served sub-avatar instead of the category's top pains.** Everyone selling teeth whitening targeted adults who want whiter teeth. He tried the coffee-stain angle, broken-down enamel and his competitors' angles; "nothing would work" and he saw "the highest CPMs". Switching to parents of kids with braces who fear white spots cut his CPMs to about $20. Going after the same top desires "will a lot of times group you up with other competitors". Condition: competitive niches. For a product that is not in a crowded niche, he says it is fine to go after the top desires and top pain points. [53d57d05d296] [6aa1da717dab]

4. **A unique avatar needs its own language, and the advertorial is where you speak it.** "Unique customer avatars have unique ways to speak to them. And that's what advertorials are designed to do." He calls them long pieces of copy that "tell a story and bridge a gap and create an epiphany", written to be relatable to that avatar. [53d57d05d296] [b7214132b1ab]

5. **The method fits problem-solving products for buyers about 35 and up.** He says the structure works with "just about any product" if you can write long-form copy, structure the advertorial, and have "a problem solving product that aligns with an older demographic of people 35 and up". Condition: his own stated limit. Not shown for impulse fashion, young audiences or high-ticket goods. [489c9dc0c84e] [b7214132b1ab]

6. **Native image ads plus long copy come before the page.** He moved from copying competitors' video ads (via Kalodata) to native image ads and advertorials. With video copies, either CPMs were too high on day 1 to get a sale, or the product reached $1,000 to $2,000 a day and then flopped. Native image ads carry long-form copy that already introduces the product. So the reader arrives with "some context and rapport built" and the page's job is to confirm and sell. Header only: native ads "break the scroll pattern" and lower defenses. [b7214132b1ab]

7. **Product-hunting tactic: run native images where others run only video.** "Find someone scaling with video ads that isn't running native image ads, and you run the native image ads", so you face less competition. He was the only one targeting parents of kids with braces, and the only one doing it with native images. [53d57d05d296] [web check: Meta Ad Library for the category, to see whether native image ads already run to the avatar]

### 2.2 Page copy

8. **Put urgency at the very top.** A short line above the headline: the demo output reads "Update: Alex123 Deep Relief massagers selling out faster than expected. Lock in your order now. Buy one get one free plus free shipping." He says it plants urgency "as soon as they land on the page". Condition: only when the stock claim is true (see section 8). [6aa1da717dab] [2bb1553ace82]

9. **The headline and the hero image are the most important parts.** "Right away, I think that most important thing is the headline and of course the image or GIF." [6aa1da717dab]

10. **Write the headline to qualify the reader by symptoms, then point them to the article.** His headline: "If you're struggling to get your child to properly clean their braces, noticing white spots forming, or worried about cavities during orthodontic treatment, read the short article before you try anything else." Pattern: If you're [avatar] dealing with [symptom 1], [symptom 2] or [worry 3], read this short article before trying anything else. [6aa1da717dab]

11. **Lead with a money-saving frame when the product honestly supports it.** His winning page said the product protects the child's teeth "but also save you $6,000" (the cost of braces wasted by white spots). "You can't always do something like this... but if you can, lean in on it." He found this hook after angle and hook testing. He says some of his other best advertorials also talked about price right away. Condition: only where a real, checkable cost of failure exists. [6aa1da717dab] [needs brand data: a sourced cost of the problem the product prevents]

12. **Use a hero GIF or image that shows the problem, and stand out in hard niches.** He chose a GIF of a teeth cleaning showing white spots left after braces, because in some niches "you have to really be cutthroat and you have to really stand out". His best ad image was a child smiling with braces, shown yellow versus white. [6aa1da717dab] [28056fad0269]

13. **Add a credibility figure, but make it real.** The source used a made-up "Sarah Mitchell, licensed dental hygienist". He says the person "may not be real, but the information... is true", and the figure is only there to support 1 point: the importance of keeping teeth clean. He says a regular person works too ("mom of three kids"). The demo AI output invented "Emily Richardson, licensed physical therapist and mom of three". ecomOS rule: use a real, qualified expert or a real customer with accurate attribution, for a narrow claim that can be backed up. Never invent a person. [2bb1553ace82] [needs brand data: a real expert or real customer who agreed to be named]

14. **List the avatar's daily problems in their own words.** His list: kids who rush through brushing around braces, white spots, food constantly trapped in the wires, gum inflammation, daily battles over proper brushing technique. The demo list for stay-at-home moms: chronic lower back pain, tight shoulders and neck from hunching over during feedings and diaper changes, muscle knots that make it hard to sleep, exhaustion from being "touched out" but still in pain, guilt about spending money on massage appointments for themselves. He says lines like the last one only come from knowing the avatar. He knew his reader was parents, "specifically women". [2bb1553ace82] [df5d2f71273b] [needs brand data: customer reviews, support tickets or survey quotes in the avatar's words]

15. **Give each problem its own short section, written from authority.** "We're going to highlight article after article, and we're just going to really spend some time highlighting each element and problem." Tone: authority, but speaking to the parent. The layout looks messy on desktop but "more organized and structured" on mobile. [2bb1553ace82]

16. **Reframe blame onto the tool, not the buyer or their family.** "You think that your child is lazy or they're not cleaning their teeth properly, but it's actually the tool itself, the mechanism." [b55f3961caa9]

17. **Always create a common enemy.** "It's important marketing. You always create a common enemy." His enemy on the page: the regular way of brushing. Inadequate cleaning is why common remedies (electric toothbrushes, water flossers, spending more time brushing) "never really solve the issue", because they do not fully clean around the braces. In his coaching business the enemy was organic marketing ("stop trying to grow a brand organically... just grow a brand with IG ads"). Condition: the enemy is a method or tool. [b55f3961caa9] [2bb1553ace82]

18. **Widen the gap, then present the product as the bridge.** He credits Russell Brunson: "The bigger of a gap you can create between the customer and where they want to go, the better... then use your product to bridge that gap." Rattle off the issues and go deeper into the pain. His longer ad copy went as far as the child's prom being ruined; he admits the advertorial left this out. Transition line: "what if there was an instrument, a tool, a device, something that could actually clean thoroughly..." [b55f3961caa9]

19. **After the product appears, prove it is different with concrete USPs.** "Why should I go to you and not just find the same product on Amazon? What makes your product special?" His list: almost 2,000 silicone bristles, UV whitening with 3 cleaning modes, deep gum cleaning, 1 charge lasts 90 days, 360 degrees reaches every surface, sonic technology breaks up plaque, U-shaped design fits the mouth, 3 modes, waterproof and safe for kids. The images for this section were made with Higgsfield. [2bb1553ace82] [090226c382a3] [needs brand data: verified product specs]

20. **Name the "why not Amazon" objection and answer it.** His copy: you won't find this in stores, on Amazon or on eBay; anything that looks familiar is "a cheap knockoff"; ours is the only authentic one, on our website. He says this brings back the share of readers who do not yet trust the brand, and he claims "your conversion rate will skyrocket". No test data given. He adds that Facebook is an impulse platform, so people buy on impulse anyway, but the best marketers make copy good enough that people buy "right then and there". Condition: only if the exclusivity is true. [090226c382a3] [web check: whether the same or near-identical product is sold on Amazon, eBay or in stores]

21. **Anchor the price against the cost of failure, not just a discount.** His line: would you rather spend $40 on a brush (about $3 a month) or $6,000 fixing their braces? He framed it as 1 investment for complete orthodontic protection and stress-free daily hygiene. "We don't want to just say, hey, you're just saving money... there's a deeper implication." [2bb1553ace82] [6aa1da717dab]

22. **Then state the offer.** His: buy 1 get 1 free, free shipping, while limited supplies last, normally $70, now $40. The CTA goes straight to the product page. [2bb1553ace82]

23. **Stack risk reversal and trust markers near the end.** A 60-day trial with a 60-day money-back guarantee, plus a warranty. Repeat limited supply: "click the button now to check if there's even some in stock". Refunds, satisfaction guarantee, secure checkout, "just a lot of social proof markers". [090226c382a3] [28056fad0269]

24. **Close with 2 options.** "You either pass up on this offer today and then potentially risk your child's teeth, or you do it now while supplies still last." [2bb1553ace82] [090226c382a3]

25. **Add more case studies and social proof at the bottom.** He says "with a little bit of code" you can add more at the bottom. His were not genuine (see section 8). [2bb1553ace82]

26. **Put an advertising disclosure at the bottom.** He did not, and says he should have: "This is an advertisement, not a real article." He says Facebook "cracks down on this now" and that "crazy claims on your prelanders" are a likely reason for bans. Condition: always. A disclosure does not make invented experts or reviews acceptable. [2bb1553ace82] [web check: current Meta advertising standards for advertorials and landing pages]

27. **Build for mobile.** "99% of people will be viewing this on mobile." He previews the page in mobile view while building. [3491a4f0a0d9] [2bb1553ace82] [needs brand data: device split for the traffic source]

### 2.3 Header-only principles (not in the transcript)

28. **Feature-to-benefit with the So-That drill.** Header only. Ask "so that...?" until the answer stops changing (example: grow revenue, so that you make more money, so that you can retire your wife, so that you can be happy). Ask "what's in it for them?" at every section. The talk's nearest content is the "deeper implication" price anchor (principle 21). [021844addc99]

29. **5 direct response design rules.** Header only: (1) benefit headline that says who you are, what you do, what's in it for me; (2) relevant hero image; (3) FUD (fear, uncertainty, doubt) reduction beneath the CTA; (4) clear, visually distinct CTA button; (5) benefit sub-headlines with "the deeper why". The talk supports (2) and (3) through his hero GIF and trust markers, but never states these as rules. [28056fad0269]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 From angle to advertorial campaign

1. Pick the avatar first: a specific group with a distinct problem. In a crowded niche, avoid the category's top pains. [53d57d05d296]
2. Build native image ads with long-form copy for about 5 angles aimed at that avatar. [108973ca4a70]
3. Run them in 1 CBO campaign. His ad set settings: automatic placements, broad age and gender, no cost caps or bid caps, "maximum" spend (highest volume delivery). He loaded 10 images into 1 ad set. `[verify UI]` [0d2cc6cf41f5] [d890bf30d59a]
4. Take the best angle as the winner. The source gives no spend level, time window or metric threshold for calling it. [108973ca4a70]
5. Make variations of the winning angle with new images ("spam testing"). In his case the best variation was a child smiling with braces, yellow versus white. [d890bf30d59a]
6. Write the advertorial for that 1 angle (section 3.2).
7. Launch the advertorial in a separate campaign to scale it. Ad CTA "Learn More", not "Shop Now". Use 1 image; turn off creative variation features. Put several ad variations in the campaign, not 1 ad set. `[verify UI]` [0d2cc6cf41f5] [108973ca4a70]
8. Optionally build 2 URL versions of the page for angle refinements (his were /braces and /teeth). [31a4937fc754]

### 3.2 Advertorial page, top to bottom (his order)

1. Urgency bar: short stock or demand update plus the offer. Only if true. [6aa1da717dab]
2. Headline: qualify the avatar by 2 to 3 symptoms or worries, then "read this short article before you try anything else". Lead with money saved if a real figure exists. [6aa1da717dab]
3. Hero GIF or image showing the problem. [6aa1da717dab]
4. Byline / credibility figure: a real expert or real customer, 1 narrow point. [2bb1553ace82]
5. Symptom list in the avatar's words. [2bb1553ace82]
6. Problem sections, 1 per problem, authority tone. [2bb1553ace82]
7. Reframe: it is not the child (buyer), it is the tool (mechanism). [b55f3961caa9]
8. Common enemy: why the usual remedies fail. [b55f3961caa9]
9. Gap: go deeper into pain and consequences. [b55f3961caa9]
10. Product reveal: "what if there was a tool that...". [b55f3961caa9]
11. USP list with specific numbers and features, with images. [2bb1553ace82]
12. Reviews and proof (genuine only). [2bb1553ace82]
13. Scarcity and exclusivity: not in stores, Amazon or eBay (only if true). [090226c382a3]
14. Price anchor against the cost of failure, plus cost per month. [2bb1553ace82]
15. Offer: BOGO, free shipping, normal versus current price. [2bb1553ace82]
16. CTA to the product page. [2bb1553ace82]
17. Risk reversal: 60-day money-back guarantee plus warranty; refunds, satisfaction guarantee, secure checkout. [090226c382a3]
18. 2-option close. [090226c382a3]
19. More case studies and social proof. [2bb1553ace82]
20. Disclosure: "This is an advertisement, not a real article." [2bb1553ace82]

He typed his first version himself. He says AI can now structure it "way better". [df5d2f71273b]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is there a proven angle?** Check: has 1 of about 5 angles clearly won in a CBO test with native image ads? Good: a clear winner. Bad: guessing. Next: if none won, change the avatar or angle before building a page. [108973ca4a70] [needs brand data: ad-level results by angle]
- **Are you in a crowded lane?** Check: do your angles repeat the category's top pains that competitors already run? Sign in his case: the highest CPMs and no traction. Next: pick an under-served sub-avatar. [53d57d05d296] [web check: Meta Ad Library for competitor angles]
- **Does the headline qualify the reader?** Good: names the avatar and 2 to 3 specific symptoms. Bad: generic benefit for everyone. [6aa1da717dab]
- **Does the hero show the problem?** Good: GIF or image of the problem or before/after. Bad: generic product beauty shot. [6aa1da717dab]
- **Does the copy sound like the avatar?** Good: details only an insider would know (his "guilt about spending money on massage appointments"). Bad: generic lines. [df5d2f71273b]
- **Is the enemy a tool or method?** Bad: blaming the buyer or their child. [b55f3961caa9]
- **Are USPs concrete?** Good: counts, battery life, modes. Bad: "high quality". [090226c382a3]
- **Is every claim true?** Check urgency, stock, exclusivity, reviews, experts, savings figure. Any false one: remove. [2bb1553ace82] [needs brand data: stock levels, channel availability, review source]
- **Is there a disclosure?** Bottom of page. Missing: add before launch. [2bb1553ace82]
- **Ad setup errors he made:** link to homepage instead of product page or advertorial, "Shop Now" CTA, creative variation features on, 1 ad set only. [0d2cc6cf41f5]
- **Proofread.** He had double dollar signs and typos. [28056fad0269]
- **What the source does not measure.** No page conversion rate, scroll depth or click-through to the product page is given. Use ad-level metrics he shows (CPM, CTR, CPC, cost per add to cart, cost per purchase, ROAS) plus your own page analytics. [0d2cc6cf41f5] [needs brand data: advertorial to PDP click-through and conversion rate]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Building the advertorial before an angle is proven. Result: "why is this not working for me?" [108973ca4a70]
2. Chasing the category's top pains in a crowded niche. Result: highest CPMs, competing with top brands. [53d57d05d296]
