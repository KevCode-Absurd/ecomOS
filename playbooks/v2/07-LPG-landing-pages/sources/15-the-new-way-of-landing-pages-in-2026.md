## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator plan, write and fix a landing page (or a PDP or advertorial used as a landing page) with a 3-pillar sequence: (1) customer research and offer and copy, (2) a design built around that copy, (3) ongoing data-led A/B testing. It is strongest on above-the-fold rules, section-level copy formulas (hero headline, sub-headline, proof headline, "so what" benefit drill) and on choosing a pain-point format by offer type. It is thin on test statistics, page speed, mobile layout, section order below the hero, and anything specific to ecommerce PDP mechanics (variants, price, bundles, cart). ## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Strategy

1. **The landing page is the common bottleneck at every stage of growth.** Source says the gap between a struggling business and one "scaling profitably" has "nothing to do with their ad spend or their content"; "it simply comes down to their landing page". Condition: this is strong agency framing. Traffic quality still affects conversion (card caveat). Evidence: his audits of 1,500+ pages, no data shown. [cc62aa6c5457]
2. **Build the page as a machine, not a checklist.** Hero, value props, social proof and pain points are the right sections, but having them does not make a page convert. Put the dream outcome at the very top. Then every item on the page must be "intentionally thought out solely to support and be directly correlated to achieving that end result", like cogs working together. Condition: the section list is correct in principle; the failure is execution without strategy. [13c8f4c9d77e]
3. **Offer and copy come first, design second.** Source says "many agencies and businesses start with the design first" and that design "is actually the easy part". His team finalizes the offer and a rough outline of its components, then builds the design around the copy. He claims this gives "a far better higher converting final product". Evidence: opinion from his agency process. [72fd0e79eb5c] [13c8f4c9d77e]
4. **Copy comes from customer research, not invention.** Understand pain points, dream outcomes, goals and motivations, needs and wants, demographics, and the typical sales conversation and objections before writing. [10b436ef5145]
5. **The offer is the dream outcome, broken into all its parts.** An offer "is basically whatever your dream outcome is". Each part (the "cogs") becomes raw material for the page sections. [d779b2b6cbd0]
6. **The blueprint is a skeleton, not a template.** Do not "copy paste a design" and do not treat sections as a checklist. Execution of each section in copy and design "is going to be completely different depending on your offer or industry". [72fd0e79eb5c]

### 2.2 Copy

7. **Every headline answers "what's in it for me".** Never put who you are or what you do in the hero headline; source calls it "a massive waste of space". [59be84f0758c]
8. **Assume visitors read only headlines.** Source states people read 20% of page content and scan the other 80%. So each value prop headline must carry the benefit alone. If a visitor reads only the headlines, they should "already have a glimpse of what's in it for me" and be "already sold". The 20/80 figure is not sourced in the transcript. [ae55963d2252] [web check: origin of the 20% read statistic]
9. **Benefits over features.** "Features are what people get"; people care how it benefits "their day-to-day life". Ask "so what?" until you reach the deeper why. [ae55963d2252]
10. **Pain sections must agitate and solve, not just list.** A list of pain points with no agitation and no solution is box-ticking. [43874661fc5d] [13c8f4c9d77e]
11. **Proof must be relevant to the offer.** A testimonial unrelated to the offer (his hair loss example) is weak proof even if it sits above the fold. [13c8f4c9d77e] [ac8005d3b7e7]

### 2.3 Design

12. **Design follows the offer type.** Transformational, visual offers get visual executions (before/after, comparisons, photos in reviews). Complex offers get long-form scannable copy. [43874661fc5d] [a3d3e6c06299]
13. **Familiar layouts are fine and may help.** Source says "people prefer landing pages and websites to work in a very similar manner to what they're already used to", so the standard image plus text layout is not a weakness even if "some people would consider this boring". This is his preference, not a test result. [a3d3e6c06299]
14. **Show results up front; do not make users work to see them.** Criticized a before/after slider that makes the user "manually drag my mouse" instead of showing it up front. [13c8f4c9d77e]

### 2.4 Testing

15. **There is no ceiling on conversion rate.** His team keeps testing at 5%, 7% and 15% conversion rates because "there's always something to improve". Card caveat: real tests hit diminishing returns. [587103c3bbc8]
16. **The page that got you to $1M to $2M will not get you to $5M to $10M.** Plateaus and walls at each scale step are "completely natural". The only way through is "conversion optimization which are new pages, new offers, and constantly running AB test". [587103c3bbc8]
17. **Test ideas come from data, not gut feeling.** "You don't want to base it off of gut feeling cuz that never gets you anywhere." [44084ab1fbbe]
18. **Small changes compound.** "Even the smallest of changes can quickly result in a snowball effect." No numbers given. [587103c3bbc8]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Diagnose which growth bucket the business is in

1. Check whether traffic arrives but does not convert (bucket 1). Traffic may come from Facebook ads, Google ads, SEO or any channel. Fix: offer and copy before buying more traffic. [cc62aa6c5457]
2. Check whether conversion is average or above average with leads and sales coming in (bucket 2). Source's range: about $500k to $5M annual revenue. Symptoms: profit "isn't where it could be" after costs, and the owner does not know how much revenue is left on the table. Risk: when they try to scale 3x or 10x, CPLs and CPAs "spike through the roof" and the page stops converting as well. Source says "most businesses I speak to fall under this bucket". [cc62aa6c5457]
3. Check whether the business already spends "several hundreds of thousands of dollars per month" with substantial traffic and decent conversion (bucket 3). Fix: extract more conversions from existing traffic through CRO, not more traffic. [cc62aa6c5457]
4. Compare conversion rate to the brand's own history and unit economics (card addition; the source gives no conversion benchmark). [needs brand data: conversion rate history, CPA, contribution margin] [cc62aa6c5457]

### 3.2 Audit an existing page for box-ticking

1. Write the single dream outcome the page sells. Check it is at the very top. [13c8f4c9d77e]
2. Walk each section. Ask whether it directly supports that outcome. [13c8f4c9d77e]
3. Flag these symptoms from the source's examples: above-the-fold testimonial that does not mention the offer's result; pain points listed with no agitation and no solution; generic proof headline; before/after behind a drag slider; a bare-bones page with barely any value props or proof. [13c8f4c9d77e]
4. Rewrite or cut anything that does not support the outcome (card wording). [13c8f4c9d77e]
5. Fix offer and copy before design polish. [13c8f4c9d77e] [72fd0e79eb5c]

### 3.3 Pillar 1a: customer research (Voice of Customer)

1. List what you need to learn: pain points; dream outcomes, goals or motivations; needs and wants; demographics (age, gender, income, personality); what a typical sales conversation looks like; common objections. [10b436ef5145]
2. Secondary research: read Reddit forums and YouTube comments about your product or industry. Note common themes and the exact words people use. [10b436ef5145]
3. Stakeholder interviews: run discovery calls with the client (or founder), their sales team and their customer support team, to see the customer "from different lens". [10b436ef5145] [needs brand data: support tickets, sales or chat logs, founder input]
4. Qualitative user testing with UserBrain: give customers a series of tasks on the website and ask them to talk out loud. His example recording was 30 minutes long. [10b436ef5145] [verify UI]
5. Analytics: pull the interest breakdown and age breakdown from Google Analytics or another analytics platform. [10b436ef5145] [verify UI: GA4 demographics and interests reports need Google signals] [needs brand data: GA4 access]
6. Card addition: sort the raw language into a research document and map each phrase to the section it will feed (headline, pain point, proof, value prop). The source only says "take a note". [10b436ef5145]

### 3.4 Pillar 1b: build and decompose the offer

1. State the offer as the dream outcome. Source examples: dentist, "a brighter, whiter, natural smile"; fitness, "losing weight or getting stronger"; SaaS, "cutting logistics time by X%". [d779b2b6cbd0]
2. List every component that makes up that outcome. Source's personal training example (offer: lose 20 lbs in 12 weeks): certified coaches; personalized onboarding and assessment flow; weekly check-ins and progress reviews; done-for-you meal planning; over 4,000 people got results with the method; only 4 hours a week; 1-week free trial; results 100% guaranteed. [d779b2b6cbd0]
3. List "as many things as possible that are all unique". Do not repeat. The source sets no count; the card's "8 to 12" is a card addition. [d779b2b6cbd0]
4. Use this list as the framework for pillar 2: fit the high-level parts of the offer into the page sections. [d779b2b6cbd0] [72fd0e79eb5c]

### 3.5 Pillar 2: lay out the page from the blueprint

Blueprint sections (source's counts): 1 hero section; 1 pain point section; 2 to 4 social proof sections; 6 to 8 value prop sections, placed "multiple times throughout your page". The source does not give a fixed order below the hero. [72fd0e79eb5c]

**Hero section (topmost section)** [59be84f0758c] [716414ea8582]
1. Headline: [End result] + [Time period] + [Emotional payoff]. Swap the order to whatever sounds natural. End result examples: "3x your revenue", "losing weight". Time period examples: "24 hours", "7 days". Emotional payoff: how the buyer feels once they get the result.
2. Sub-headline: call out the pain point first "if it's applicable", then the solution, then list the USPs and inner workings of the offer that deliver the headline result. Header formula: "Stop doing X, no more Y [pain] -> Our solution does Z [benefit]".
3. At least 2 forms of social proof above the fold. Allowed forms: reviews, stats, featured publications, "anything that builds trust". They must be "relevant and impactful".
4. FUD reducers (fear, uncertainty, doubt) near the call to action button, "the critical point of action". Address "all of the anxieties and fears that people have around buying anything online" up front.
5. An image or video that reinforces the core value prop. It must directly support the offer or dream outcome.
6. Optional: source's ChatGPT prompt framework to draft copy by feeding it company info. The prompts are not in this document.

**Pain point section** [43874661fc5d]
Purpose: show you understand and empathize with their problems, to build connection and trust. Pick 1 of 3 executions:
1. Graphical design, for transformational offers with a clear visual before and after (fitness, weight loss, dentistry, surgery). Use images plus scannable icons, or a clear before-state and after-state visual with scannable text and icons. Compare the old way to your way (Pilates vs the client's method).
2. Sales letter, for offers that are "more deep", "more complex" and need explanation. Write as a letter to the ideal prospect. Long-form but "very, very scannable": no long blocks, "at most, maybe one to two sentences per paragraph". Use PAS:
   - Problem: call it out so clearly the reader thinks "this is exactly me". Opener such as "Do you experience..." or "Does this sound like you?", then list 3 to 5 things.
   - Agitate: "really going to make people feel it". Describe what they are missing out on "in just a few sentences".
   - Solve: present your solution "as a clear way out" and "give them a compelling reason to act".
3. Short-form PAS, for offers that are less complex but still need some explanation and are "not visual in nature". Much shorter PAS. Add scannable icons or images "if your offer is more visual", as with the luxury closet client. (The source contradicts itself here: "not visual" then "if more visual". Read it as: short PAS by default, add icons or images where there is something to show.)

**Social proof sections (2 to 4)** [ac8005d3b7e7]
1. Headline formula: "Helping [X number of customers] achieve [main benefit or dream outcome]". Example: Nutrisense, "over 110,000 members" plus the main benefit.
2. Never use "See what our customers are saying", "Our reviews" or "Our testimonials". Visitors already know they are looking at reviews.
3. For results best shown visually, show the visual inside the review (lawn care client: photos of customers' lawns in each review).
4. Show the reviewer's full name and the review source (platform) on each review.
5. Video reviews: lay them out in the open. Give each a scannable headline that sums up the review, for viewers in public or who cannot play sound.
6. Wall of love: "shower people" with images, videos and text reviews, so many that it builds "an instant sense of trust and credibility". The header adds before/after photos and verifiable source logos (Google, Trustpilot).

**Value prop sections (6 to 8)** [ae55963d2252] [a3d3e6c06299]
1. Content: your unique differentiators and the inner workings of the offer (from 3.4).
2. Copy rule 1: combine benefit with feature. Drill with "so what?" until you reach the benefit. Header example: "Clear reports" -> "Make smarter decisions" -> "Grow your bottom line".
3. Copy rule 2: state the benefit in the headline alone of every value prop section.
4. Design, pick per offer and value prop:
   - Standard image plus text. "Works for just about every niche or every industry."
   - Custom graphics layered on the images to complement the copy.
   - 2 or 3 images in one section where the offer is visual (fitness client).
   - Analogue layouts, e.g. an education client's section styled like a LinkedIn profile or resume to help people "visualize where they could be in the future".
   - Unique designs for a specific value prop, e.g. a fitness client's training blocks.
5. Build the design around the copy and offer written first, "not the other way around".

**Swipe files** [72fd0e79eb5c]
Use category swipe files for inspiration when stuck. Do not copy-paste, "cuz every offer and every business is different". Source sells his own (Figma, 30+ industries); commercially motivated.

### 3.6 Pillar 3: iterate and A/B test

1. Treat launch as the start. Source calls "the work is done" thinking "a massive mistake". [587103c3bbc8]
2. Pull data from 3 tools [44084ab1fbbe]:
   - GA4: bounce rate, average time on page, as a snapshot of high-level metrics. [verify UI: GA4 reports "average engagement time", not "time on page"]
   - Microsoft Clarity (free): session recordings, where people click, how far they scroll. [verify UI]
   - UserBrain: customer interviews and think-aloud tests. [verify UI]
3. Form each test from the data. Test areas the source names: CTA, design, offer, copy. [587103c3bbc8] [44084ab1fbbe]
4. Card additions, not in the source: cross-reference the 3 tools on the same section; write a hypothesis on one element; log which data source drove each test. [44084ab1fbbe]
5. Run the test on a platform: Intelligems for ecommerce clients, Go Stellar for non-ecom clients. Both "track all of the data in one clean dashboard". [220ec901e95b] [verify UI] [web check: current Intelligems and Go Stellar features and pricing]
6. Run every test for at least 1 week, to absorb fluctuations "in data, the economy, in ad performance". [220ec901e95b]
7. Use a sample size calculator to know how much data and time the test needs. Check statistical significance ("was your test due to chance or not"). The source names no calculator, confidence level or power. [220ec901e95b]
8. Card additions, not in the source: at low traffic, combine changes into fewer, bigger tests; decide in advance what to do with an inconclusive result. [220ec901e95b]
9. When growth plateaus or CPA spikes at a new spend level, respond with new pages, new offers and new tests, not only more spend. [587103c3bbc8]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Treating the page as a checklist of sections and assuming their presence makes it convert. [13c8f4c9d77e]
2. Following generic landing page advice (from a "well-known YouTuber") without thinking about the offer. [13c8f4c9d77e]
3. Above-the-fold proof that does not relate to the offer. [13c8f4c9d77e] [ac8005d3b7e7]
4. Pain point lists that tell people what is wrong with them with no agitation and no solution. [43874661fc5d]
5. Generic proof headlines ("Our reviews", "Our testimonials", "See what our customers are saying"). [ac8005d3b7e7]
6. Before/after results hidden behind a drag slider. [13c8f4c9d77e]
7. Bare-bones pages with barely any value props or proof: "why would someone actually buy from this page?" [13c8f4c9d77e]
8. Putting who you are or what you do in the hero headline. [59be84f0758c]
9. Starting with design, or copy-pasting a template or swipe file design. [72fd0e79eb5c]
10. Repetitive offer components that restate the same thing. [d779b2b6cbd0]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

**Hedges and weak evidence**
- All numbers, formulas and case results are self-reported by an agency that sells pages, a swipe file and services. No controls, dates or test sizes. [4d8daca6320c] [72fd0e79eb5c]
- The 20% read / 80% scan figure is given as a statistic with no source. [ae55963d2252] [web check: origin, often traced to Nielsen Norman Group reading research]
- "No ceiling on conversion rate" is opinion. Dozens of tests a month needs traffic that small stores do not have. [587103c3bbc8] [needs brand data: monthly sessions and orders per page to size tests]
- "Nothing to do with ad spend or content" overstates the case; traffic quality and offer-market fit also move conversion. [cc62aa6c5457]
- Bucket revenue bands are from sales conversations, not measured thresholds. [cc62aa6c5457]

**Internal gaps and contradictions**
- The short-form PAS option is described as for offers "not visual in nature" and then "if your offer is more visual". [43874661fc5d]
- The source gives section counts but no order below the hero, and no guidance on CTA repetition, FAQ, guarantee section, footer or page length.
- The FUD examples shown are for a subscription app. The source gives no physical-product FUD list (shipping time, returns, sizing, payment options, COD). Card 716414 lists some of these, but that is a card addition. [716414ea8582] [needs brand data: returns, shipping, guarantee and payment terms the brand can honour]
- Most examples are services or lead gen. Ecommerce mechanics (price, variants, bundles, add to cart, sticky ATC, PDP images, cart and checkout) are not covered. Cards that apply the method to PDP, cart and checkout are card extensions. [587103c3bbc8] [d779b2b6cbd0]
- No page speed or mobile guidance. Card caveats flag that proof walls and custom graphics can slow mobile pages. [ac8005d3b7e7] [a3d3e6c06299]
- Test statistics are one line: 1 week minimum, use a calculator, check significance. No confidence level, power, minimum detectable effect, or rules for peeking. [220ec901e95b]
- "Average time on page" is a Universal Analytics metric name; GA4 uses engagement time. [verify UI]

**Conflicts with common ecommerce advice**
- Hero headline "never say what you do" can clash with PDP and ecommerce practice, where the product name and what it is must be clear at a glance. For a PDP, the product title still has to be present; the source's rule is about the headline, and he gives no PDP example.
- Time-period and outcome claims in headlines ("in just 2 weeks", "20/20 vision in 10 minutes") can breach ad and consumer rules for supplements, health and beauty. Use only claims the brand can back. [59be84f0758c] [web check: ad and consumer claim rules in the brand's market] [needs brand data: substantiated claims]
- The 1-week minimum is shorter than the common advice of 2 full weekly cycles and a pre-set sample size. The source treats 1 week as a floor, not a stop rule. [220ec901e95b]
- Agitation in PAS can clash with a premium or calm brand voice. The source gives no guidance on tone limits; ground it in real VoC language (card caveat). [43874661fc5d] [needs brand data: brand voice rules]
