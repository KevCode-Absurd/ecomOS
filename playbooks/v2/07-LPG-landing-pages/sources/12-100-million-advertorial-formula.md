## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator decide whether a product needs an advertorial before the product page, pick 1 of 6 advertorial formats based on the buyer's main obstacle, and assemble the page from a fixed block order: hook, reader, agitation, why other fixes fail, 3 layers of proof, offer, objections, guarantee, CTA. It adds an asset checklist, CTA placement, a 1-day build workflow, funnel KPI targets (30 to 40% click-through to the next page, 70 to 80% on the next step, about 15% at checkout) and a test priority order. It is strong on format choice and block order. It is thin on proof that advertorials beat product pages, on how to measure awareness, and on compliance.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 When to use an advertorial

1. **Product pages assume intent; advertorials create it.** A standard Shopify product page assumes the visitor already wants the product and only needs the price. Most visitors do not have enough desire yet. They see a price like $50 and are not sure it is worth it, because they have not been taught the value. An advertorial educates and builds excitement first. Source's claim: this raises conversion rate and average order value. No data given. [05ee72da15eb] [needs brand data: PDP conversion rate and AOV for cold traffic, to compare against an advertorial route]

2. **The 3-minute rule.** If the product needs about 3 minutes of explanation, it needs an advertorial. If it is simple and self-evident (a bottle of water, a regular mouse pad, a t-shirt, a hoodie), send traffic straight to the product page. Named advertorial candidates: gadgets, weight-loss products, beauty products. This is the presenter's heuristic, not a measured rule. [05ee72da15eb]

3. **Use an advertorial when the market or claim needs it.** The source lists 4 triggers: the market is skeptical; the claims need explanation; the product is a new mechanism; the category is crowded. He says health, beauty and pets mostly fall here. Any 1 trigger favours an advertorial. [05ee72da15eb]

4. **Match the page to awareness level.** He names 5 levels: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. Product pages work well for product-aware and most-aware buyers, who "just want the best discount possible". Unaware buyers (who do not know they have the problem) and problem-aware buyers must be educated first, with more context, before the product appears. He says these are the scalable markets: crack advertorials there and you "can spend $50, $100,000 per day on ads". That spend figure is his claim, not shown. He gives no method to assign a level beyond research (see 3.1). [05ee72da15eb] [34a2012a9531]

### 2.2 The 6 formats

5. **Storytelling.** Tell the story of 1 person, why the problem was happening, and how it went away. Pattern he describes: the person believed the problem was their nature or genetics; they tried and failed with other fixes; they found this product; they used it as a routine for 3 to 6 months; the result came; limited availability, buy now. Use it for cold traffic, emotional problems, and identity-based problems. He says it "works for most products" and is the one to start with if you run no advertorials yet. Examples: an acne page aimed at people in their 30s in the Philippines, blaming their skin type (he notes all its images are AI-generated); "how this 72-year-old grandmother ditched her mats for this secret body relief remedy" for foot pain. [68ab05281912] Do-not-copy: AI-generated people presented as real customers, and "very low availability" unless stock really is low.

6. **Every format needs a unique mechanism, most of all in saturated markets.** Buyers who see a product pitch think "I've seen this before". A unique mechanism or ingredient makes the product different from everything they tried. The foot-pain page has a block "how Mama Bear nerve relief lotion works". His own mechanism example is a deliberate joke (special magnesium carried by hand from a cave in India, guarded by an army). Condition: the mechanism must be the product's real, checkable way of working. [68ab05281912] [7d6c89cc428f] [needs brand data: the real mechanism or ingredient and its evidence]

7. **Authority expert.** Introduce a person with authority to say the reader is doing something wrong, which gives the reader "permission" to buy. Examples: "your pillowcase could be aging you 10 years overnight, warns dermatologist"; a dog allergy chew page "reviewed by Dr. Daisy", a vet, ending in "check availability" with subscription pre-selected. Best for markets that need more trust and explanation: health, beauty, pain relief, pets. A framing he gives: "what I see in patients", where the expert describes what the patients who get results are doing. He says: "Some people use fake doctors. I don't recommend that. Ideally, use real doctors that actually exist." [68ab05281912] [needs brand data: a real, qualified expert who has agreed to endorse the product and what they will say] Do-not-copy: fake or AI-image doctors.

8. **Buyer's guide or comparison ("Top 5").** A third-party style article reviews about 5 products in the category, names the real competitors, discredits each, and crowns the featured product. Example: "MD and endocrinology reviews the top 5 supplements to get the weight loss benefits of GLP-1 without Ozempic side effects or [the] per month price tag". The featured brand (Bioma) sat at number 2, not number 1. The click then goes to a quiz on the brand's own site, so the visitor arrives pre-sold and only checks fit. Then a simple product page with subscription pre-selected. The ads were mostly simple image ads run from third-party page names. He says this format "works very very very well for retargeting" and that every health, beauty, pain or pet brand should run third-party pages with an authority figure (doctor, dermatologist, vet or celebrity). A variant: a guide on "how to buy this product, how to avoid getting scammed". [cdf7785c11ed] [68ab05281912] [web check: comparative advertising law and Meta policy on naming competitors in the target market] Do-not-copy: third-party "review" or "news" pages that hide that the brand paid for them, and discrediting competitors with claims that cannot be proven.

9. **Scandal or whistleblower ("beauty review").** Position the brand as a small, honest player against a giant industry that hides the truth. Example: a "Beauty Review" magazine page, "The beauty industry's dirty secret: your collagen creams can't reach where wrinkles actually form", citing a $511 billion yearly industry and a "small laboratory in Dublin". Structure: steps to the discovery, friction points and why everyone is against it, the shortcut (tool or product), then 1 CTA to a simple product page, since the traffic is already warm. He calls it "very very effective". [68f9de4a107b] [web check: any industry-size figure before reuse] Do-not-copy: unverified industry numbers, claims about competitors' products, and a fake magazine masthead.

10. **Case study.** Document 1 case in detail: the problem, each standard fix failing in turn, then the product. Example: "Johns Hopkins urologist traveled to Nepal to save this 82-year-old father from surgery", aimed at men waking "3, 4, 5 times every night", calling it "the silent epidemic". The page discredits each prescription drug by name, then surgery, using the fact that nobody wants surgery as the pivot. Then "the discovery that changed everything": "plastic poisoning". He says he does not know if the doctor exists ("some AI image"). Use when buyers have tried and failed with standard fixes. [3be6d44f2b54] Do-not-copy: named-institution doctors who may not exist, discrediting prescription drugs, and disease claims. [web check: health claim rules in the target market]

11. **Reasons why (listicle).** "5 reasons why people are choosing this product" or "8 reasons why". It compares the product with other options (for example a wrinkle serum or a dark spot serum) and handles objections inside the reasons, such as time: "5 minutes every 2 weeks". His example was written from a doctor's point of view for extra authority. [68ab05281912]

12. **Formats can be stacked and should be tested.** You can combine several formats. He says top advertisers test many formats, pages and variants all the time, and shows several live examples of each. [68ab05281912] [0be867d0a8b8]

13. **Pick the format by the buyer's main obstacle.** His "60 seconds" map:
    - Low trust: story, with proof early. He adds "you can use basically any of these types".
    - Need for permission: an expert ("you need someone to tell people what to do; that's how people make decisions").
    - Too many options: compare them, discredit the others, leave yours.
    - Skeptical or "seen it all": buyer's guide or comparison.
    - "Will this work for me?" (too old, too young, a certain ethnicity): cover that objection directly. The talk names no format; the header and card say case study.
    - Needs more reassurance in a very skeptical market: buyer's guide and comparison.
    Treat this as a starting hypothesis to test. [68ab05281912] [34a2012a9531]

### 2.3 Page copy and proof

14. **One universal block order fits every format.** Hook, identify the reader, agitate the problem with the cost of inaction, explain why it happens, explain why every other fix fails, 3 layers of proof, offer reveal, objection handling, guarantee, CTA. Full steps in 3.2. [7d6c89cc428f]

15. **Discredit the alternatives 1 by 1.** Take each fix the reader has tried and knock it out. His weight example: the gym did not work, Ozempic "forget about it", "I tried like 50" diets. Then the product that "stops your hunger from the inside" so no diet is needed. Condition: each knock-out must be fair and true. [7d6c89cc428f] Do-not-copy: the weight-loss claim in his example.

16. **Every advertorial needs 3 layers of proof.** Mechanism proof: the logical reason it works ("it works because it stops X"), as simple and logical as you can make it. Social proof: testimonials, "as many as possible". Demonstration proof: the product visibly working (applied to skin and the skin changing). [b23e07cd5c01] [7d6c89cc428f]

17. **Build a proof stack beyond reviews.** Add specificity (specific numbers, specific timelines). Add third-party proof (experts, publications, the authority figure). Show reviews from repeat buyers ("I bought it again") to show continuity, most of all for consumables. [b23e07cd5c01] [needs brand data: repeat-purchase reviews and real timelines]

18. **Hold the price until later.** Reveal what the product is and does, but not necessarily the price on the first advertorial page. In his foot-pain example the price appears only on a second pre-sell page. Condition: a 2-page pre-sell funnel. [7d6c89cc428f] [68ab05281912]

19. **Most people fail at objection handling.** Cover risk, price, time and safety. His examples: what if it does not work; what if delivery takes "50 weeks"; what if it is made in China. Then the guarantee (money back). He says most people "sell, sell" and skip this block. [7d6c89cc428f] [needs brand data: real shipping times, origin, guarantee terms]

20. **Layer CTA strength.** CTAs can be soft, medium or hard. "Each one of them could work, but you have to layer all of it in the same advertorial." He gives no wording for each level. [7d6c89cc428f]

21. **Copy matters more than design.** "You can have the horrible design, the ugly design... but if the copy is on point, if it's based on real research... it will print." Write the copy yourself; outsource the build. [a5a6310c4758]

22. **Research first, from real buyer words.** Find awareness level and objections on Reddit, in ad comments and in customer support emails. Example: if customers email "does it work for Asian people?", handle it on the page "so that you don't lose sales". [34a2012a9531] [needs brand data: ad comments, support tickets, customer emails]

23. **Model live advertorials before inventing your own.** "Like everything in life, you just rip it first." Model the structure of pages already running. [0be867d0a8b8] Condition for ecomOS: model structure and format only; never copy text, claims, images or people.

24. **Ship fast.** A decent advertorial takes about 1 day to write. "Don't think for 6 months." Launch an imperfect page and improve it. [a5a6310c4758]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build your first advertorial (source's step-by-step workflow)

1. Pick 1 product. [a5a6310c4758]
2. Choose the awareness level and the main obstacle. Research what buyers think on Reddit, in ad comments and in support emails. The source offers a DM-gated "deep research" prompt for this; its output is not verified. [34a2012a9531] [needs brand data: customer comments and support tickets]
3. Choose the advertorial type that attacks that 1 obstacle (map in principle 13). [68ab05281912]
4. Gather assets:
   - visual assets (he says most can be made with AI; see section 9 on product accuracy),
   - 10 to 20 review quotes,
   - 5 objections from the research,
   - the mechanism explained in 3 sentences,
   - demonstration points. [34a2012a9531] [b23e07cd5c01]
5. Write in blocks, not paragraphs. Write block by block, then check that each block flows smoothly into the next. [7d6c89cc428f] [a5a6310c4758]
6. Place CTAs (see 3.3). [65c6cbb0e670]
7. Build the native design. Use any builder (section 6). Unless cash is tight, pay a Fiverr or Upwork freelancer about $100 to $200 to build it; they will not write the copy. [a5a6310c4758] [verify UI]
8. Launch, read the first 48 hours, then optimise step by step (3.4). [82a0e9f86e18]

### 3.2 Universal block order

1. **Hook** at the top: a promise or shocking discovery that grabs attention. Often attributed to a doctor or expert (his example names "Michael Richards"). [7d6c89cc428f]
2. **Identify the reader**: who it is for, what it is for. Example: a blood pressure page pre-qualifies "adults over 50". [7d6c89cc428f]
3. **Agitate the problem**: the cost of inaction ("struggling with this for many years", "in pain every day"). [7d6c89cc428f]
4. **Why it happens**: the cause, in a way that sets up the mechanism. His example ("a molecule from eating plastic") is one he says he made up; yours must be true. [7d6c89cc428f]
5. **Why all other solutions fail**: discredit them 1 by 1. [7d6c89cc428f]
6. **Proof, 3 layers**: mechanism, social, demonstration, plus specificity and third-party proof. [b23e07cd5c01]
7. **Offer reveal**: what it is and what it does. Price can wait for a second page. [7d6c89cc428f]
8. **Objection handling**: risk, price, time, safety. [7d6c89cc428f]
9. **Guarantee**. [7d6c89cc428f]
10. **CTA**: soft, medium and hard, layered. [7d6c89cc428f]

### 3.3 CTA placement

1. Put a CTA after the hook, after the mechanism, after the proof, and after objections and guarantee: about 4 on the page. [65c6cbb0e670]
2. Or use 1 floating ("hovering") CTA that stays on screen through the page; he says it can replace the others. [65c6cbb0e670]
3. Pages with CTAs only near the bottom force people to read; he "understands that", but says putting CTAs higher gets more clicks. His examples: 1 page with only a bottom CTA; 1 with 3 clickable CTAs, mostly low. [65c6cbb0e670]
4. Do not over-deliberate. "Just put it somewhere and get it out to the market." [65c6cbb0e670]

### 3.4 Measure and optimise (source's sequence)

1. First 48 hours: check engagement and CTR. Are people clicking? What are the ad CPMs and CTRs? Are people clicking from 1 page to the next? [82a0e9f86e18]
2. Then follow people through every page. Do they click from the first advertorial to the product page or to the second pre-sell? Find the page that loses them and fix it step by step. [82a0e9f86e18]
3. Measure on the advertorial: scroll depth, and CTR to the product page (target 30% or higher). [82a0e9f86e18]
4. Measure after: add to cart and checkout conversion (about 15% in his funnel). [82a0e9f86e18]
5. Test in this order: advertorial type (for example story vs guide or comparison), then headline ("very very meaningful"), then where the first proof block sits (higher or lower), then CTA style. [82a0e9f86e18]
6. Only test CTA style or CTA details (for example claim-offer wording) if you have thousands of visitors per day. He expects small differences and says you will not reach statistical significance otherwise. [65c6cbb0e670] [82a0e9f86e18]

### 3.5 Find advertorials to model

1. Use an advertorial library tool (his own, Get Hooked AI). Search "top 5" to find comparison pages; he says you get about 100 ads leading to "top 5" pages. [0be867d0a8b8] [verify UI]
2. Or browse news sites such as Fox News, scroll to the bottom and click the sponsored links. [0be867d0a8b8]
3. For each page, note the format, hook, proof blocks and funnel steps (quiz, second pre-sell, product page). [0be867d0a8b8]
4. Model the structure. Do not copy text, claims, images or people (ecomOS rule).

### 3.6 Next steps he gives the viewer

1. Pick 1 product (or the one you already sell).
2. Identify the 1 objection a story could handle (for example trust, or a transformation story).
3. Choose the advertorial type that attacks that objection.
4. Rewrite it block by block and get it out. Adjust after launch. [a5a6310c4758]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **PDP or advertorial?** Check: can the value be understood in seconds? Is the market skeptical, the claim in need of explanation, the mechanism new, or the category crowded? What awareness level is the traffic? Good for PDP: simple product, product-aware or most-aware traffic (for example retargeting of product viewers, branded search). Good for advertorial: any of the 4 triggers plus unaware or problem-aware cold traffic. Next: the card suggests running both routes against the same cold audience. [05ee72da15eb] [needs brand data: cold-traffic PDP conversion rate]
- **Right format?** Check the main obstacle from comments, support emails and Reddit. If the page format does not attack that obstacle, change the format first; format is the top test variable. [68ab05281912] [82a0e9f86e18]
- **Block audit.** Check the draft has all 10 blocks in 3.2. Bad: no "why other fixes fail" block, no objection block (the most common miss), only 1 proof type. Fix: add the missing block. [7d6c89cc428f]
- **Proof audit.** Mechanism proof present and logical in about 3 sentences? Social proof present, specific, ideally from repeat buyers? Demonstration visible, not just described? Specific numbers and timelines? Third-party proof? Every item real and sourced? [b23e07cd5c01]
- **CTA audit.** Is there a CTA soon after the hook? About 4 in total or 1 floating? [65c6cbb0e670]
- **Funnel read after 48 hours.** First page to next page below about 30%: look at the hook, the headline and the match between ad and page. Second to third page below 70 to 80%: look at that page. Checkout well below about 15% in a similar funnel: look at offer, price reveal and objection handling. The source gives the order to check (page by page) but not what to change at each leak. [82a0e9f86e18] [needs brand data: page-level click-through, scroll depth, add to cart and checkout conversion]
- **Test plan check.** Is the test on a high-impact variable (type, headline, first proof position)? Is traffic high enough for a CTA-style test (thousands of visitors a day)? If not, do not run it. [82a0e9f86e18]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Sending cold traffic that needs education straight to a basic product page; the source blames this for low profitability and low ROAS. [05ee72da15eb]
2. Skipping objection handling (risk, price, time, safety) and the guarantee. "Most people mess it up here." [7d6c89cc428f]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **No evidence of lift.** The source never shows an advertorial beating a product page for a given product. The "increase conversion rate and AOV" claim is opinion. [05ee72da15eb]
- **15% is ambiguous.** The talk says "on this page, specifically funnel Checkout Champ, the conversion rate should be like about 15%" right after naming add to cart and checkout conversion. The header reads it as the final checkout page. It is not a store-wide or session conversion rate, and it is tied to 1 checkout tool. Do not compare it with a store's overall conversion rate. - **Click-through targets are loose.** He gives 30% (ideally 40%) from the first page, 70 to 80% from the second to the third page, and "30% or higher" CTR to the PDP, without saying how the pages are counted (sessions, users, unique clicks). [82a0e9f86e18]

- **CTA placement.** He says 4 CTAs, then says placement "doesn't matter", and shows good pages with CTAs only near the bottom. Treat 4 as a default, not a rule. [65c6cbb0e670]
