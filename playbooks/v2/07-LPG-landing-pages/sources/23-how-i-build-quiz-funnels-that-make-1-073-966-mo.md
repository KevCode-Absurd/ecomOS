## 1. What this source is for

Design the questions of a quiz funnel that sits between a cold paid ad and a sales page or PDP. It gives a 3-job test for every question (micro-commitment, seeding a belief, pre-handling an objection), an ordering rule (gradualization), a format-variety rule, a length range, a completion KPI, a drop-off diagnostic, an AI drafting workflow built on customer research docs, and an email gate with answer-based email sequences. It is strong on question writing. It is thin on the results page, the offer, the sales page, the build tool and tracking setup.

## 2. Principles

1. **The quiz wins by priming, not by personalizing.** The presenter says quizzes do not win because they personalize, match the product to the answers, or raise AOV. He says they win because a run of small answers puts the cold visitor in an "emotionally primed", "highly suggestible" state before any offer is shown. Condition: cold traffic that would otherwise land straight on a PDP or sales page. Evidence: his opinion plus his revenue claim. He later says personalization does matter at the email step (principle 11), so the claim is about the front end only. [38bb690a9cc6]

2. **Every question must do 1 of 3 jobs.** (a) Get a micro-commitment: each yes, tap or slider move is "one step closer to a sale". (b) Seed a belief: move the customer's current beliefs toward the beliefs needed to buy. (c) Pre-handle an objection: answer it before it comes up. "If it's not doing one of those three things, it ain't worth it." Condition: applies to every question. Evidence: his framework; the pre-handling idea is credited to a sales mentor and to copywriting practice. [62b402dc7dc6] [38bb690a9cc6]

3. **Marketing is belief change.** "Marketing at its core is simply a game of belief change." Seeding aligns the buyer's beliefs with buying so that by the sales page there is little friction left. Condition: needs real research on what the buyer believes now and must believe to buy. [6bd78cdf299b] [needs brand data: current customer beliefs and the beliefs required to buy, from reviews, surveys and support tickets]

4. **Pre-handled objections beat handled objections.** "The best copywriters don't do objection handling at all because all of them are already handled before the offer." Keep the answer on the sales page too (FAQ or elsewhere). Condition: the objection must be a real one from research. [56f4e8549a30]

5. **Gradualization: start non-invasive, then escalate.** Credited to Eugene Schwartz as a copywriting term, applied here to quiz order. The first question is "the most important question in your quiz". Never open with something invasive or against social norms. His example: asking an older woman her age is seen as rude, so do not lead with it when selling to older women. Condition: all quizzes, more so for personal or health topics. No threshold is given for when to escalate. [25ad02d8040c]

6. **Do not use the same question format more than 2 or 3 times in a row.** Switch between multi-select, multiple choice, yes/no and slider so people stay engaged and "aren't passively clicking through". Evidence: his practice and the example quiz, which switches formats. [1081c0601454] [097e646ec1ec]

7. **Make each tap feel rewarding.** He wants quizzes "as dopamine inducing as humanly possible", for example the selected option changes colour. He says he'd add "little confetti cannons" if he could. Condition: his preference; the card adds that heavy effects can slow load and cause drop-off. [1081c0601454]

8. **Avoid typed (input) answers by default.** Typing has "much higher perceived effort" than tapping a choice. He would change his own AI-drafted input question. Exception: use input on purpose when you want friction to qualify buyers, and only after the quiz is proven. [ce1ad833b2ec]

9. **Launch with minimal friction, then add qualifying friction.** First prove the quiz works. Then cut high drop-off questions, or add friction that qualifies the buyer once you have data. [cb45dd220b9b] [ce1ad833b2ec]

10. **Ground questions in research, not in swiped quizzes.** "You shouldn't just be randomly selecting the questions that you ask or just blindly swiping the top people." Study other quizzes for structure; write your own questions from your own research. [9f7d25ae19bb] [097e646ec1ec] [6bd78cdf299b]

11. **Personalization pays at the email step.** Collect name and email at the end to send the "personalized quiz result", then run a 5 to 7 email sequence. The advanced version branches the emails by quiz answers. [b800e9ca6a61]

12. **The quiz hides the sales ask.** Ad CTAs and copy can say "take the quiz" instead of "shop now" or "buy now", which he calls much less invasive. Condition: the ad must promise a quiz that exists and delivers a result. [38bb690a9cc6] (from the source text; the cards do not cover the ad CTA in detail)

13. **Formats run in cycles; move fast.** Advertorials, then VSLs, now quizzes. He says the window is limited and urges urgency. This is his opinion and a sales framing. [bdc066a61075]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build the research base first (foundational docs)
1. Produce 4 documents: an offer doc, an avatar doc, a research doc and a beliefs doc. He calls this "the deepest level" of understanding of customer, market and offer, and starts every funnel and every piece of copy from it. [9f7d25ae19bb]
2. The SOP for making these docs is linked in his video description and is not in this document. The source does not say how to make them. [needs brand data: offer details, avatar, customer research (reviews, surveys, support tickets), list of current and required beliefs, list of objections]

### 3.2 Draft the quiz with AI (his 3-prompt workflow)
1. Open a new chat in ChatGPT (he uses "ChatGPT 5"). `[verify UI]` [9f7d25ae19bb]
2. Prompt 1, context profile: say you are a copywriter for the brand and what it sells (his example: a gut relief brand). Upload the 4 foundational docs. Ask the model to gain context of everything in them. No action steps. [9f7d25ae19bb]
3. Prompt 2, example analysis: screenshot every step of a proven quiz (he used the "Fioma" weight-loss quiz). Upload the screenshots. Ask the model only to analyze it and give its thoughts. No action steps. [9f7d25ae19bb]
4. Prompt 3, generate: give the 3 principles (micro-commitment, seeding, pre-handling) plus execution guidance, including "no more than 2 or 3 of the same format in a row". Ask for the quiz. The exact prompt text is shown on screen only and is not in the transcript. [9f7d25ae19bb]
5. Review each question. Label its job (commit, seed, pre-handle). Check the order against gradualization. Replace typed input questions with tap controls. Cut questions with no job. [62b402dc7dc6] [25ad02d8040c] [ce1ad833b2ec]

### 3.3 Write and order the questions
1. List the beliefs the buyer must hold to buy, from the beliefs doc. [6bd78cdf299b]
2. List the top objections, from research. [56f4e8549a30]
3. Write question 1 as the least invasive and most important question, usually a goal question that also seeds a belief. Example: "What's your biggest gut health goal right now?" with options reduce bloating, improve regularity, boost energy or mood. It seeds "gut health affects mood, focus and energy". [25ad02d8040c] [6bd78cdf299b]
4. Write seeding questions that make the problem feel bigger in daily life. Example: a 1 to 10 slider on how much gut symptoms affect day-to-day life. It "reframes gut issues as a quality of life problem". [6bd78cdf299b]
5. Write pre-handling questions for each objection. Example: "When starting something new for your gut, how quickly do you expect to feel results?" (multiple choice) pre-handles "this takes too long". "What's been your most frustrating experience with gut health products so far?" pre-handles "I've tried everything" (but convert it from input to choices). [56f4e8549a30] [ce1ad833b2ec]
6. Add intra-quiz cards (info screens between questions) that overcome beliefs and pre-handle objections. The example quiz shows one after the symptom questions. The source gives no rule on how often. [56f4e8549a30] [097e646ec1ec]
7. Put personal and demographic questions (age, height, weight target) later, once the visitor is invested. [25ad02d8040c]
8. Rotate formats: never more than 2 or 3 of the same format in a row. [1081c0601454]
9. Set length: 3 to 20 questions, usually 8 to 12. [c9f4734eacf6]
10. Write the quiz headline as a question plus a time promise. Example: "Gut Guardian Quiz. Why hasn't your gut fully healed yet? Find out in 2 minutes." [9f7d25ae19bb]

### 3.4 Match the ad to the quiz
1. Use "take the quiz" style CTAs and copy in the ad instead of "shop now". [38bb690a9cc6]
2. The source gives no more detail on ad hooks, creative or platform CTA buttons. `[verify UI: which CTA button options the ad platform offers for a quiz link]`

### 3.5 End of quiz, email and follow-up
1. At the end, ask for name and email to send the personalized quiz result. [b800e9ca6a61]
2. Send the visitor to the sales page or PDP. The source says completers "get to the sales page"; it does not describe a results page, product recommendation logic, offer, discount or pricing. [cb45dd220b9b]
3. Put captured leads in a 5 to 7 email sequence aimed at a purchase. [b800e9ca6a61]
4. Advanced: branch sequences by answers. His examples: people who reject non-natural ingredients get a sequence on the natural formula; people who put speed first and do not mind pharmaceutical ingredients get a different workflow. [b800e9ca6a61]
5. Use the list for remarketing. [b800e9ca6a61] [needs brand data: email platform, consent rules, current flow performance]

### 3.6 After launch: fix drop-off
1. Pull per-question drop-off from the quiz tool. The tool is not named. `[verify UI]` [cb45dd220b9b]
2. Expect at least about 1% drop-off on every question. [cb45dd220b9b]
3. Look for a spike well above the rest. His illustration: 1%, 2%, 1%, 1%, 2%, 4%, 1%, 1%, 2%, then a clear outlier (the transcript's last value is garbled as "0"). [cb45dd220b9b]
4. For the spike question, ask it in a less invasive way or remove it, unless it is deliberate qualifying friction. [cb45dd220b9b]
5. Check completion against the 30% target (25% is "still quite good"). [cb45dd220b9b]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Sending cold ad traffic straight to a PDP or sales page with a "buy now" ask. [38bb690a9cc6]
2. Thinking a quiz works only because it personalizes, matches products or lifts AOV, and so missing the priming job. [38bb690a9cc6]
3. Picking questions at random or blindly swiping a top brand's quiz instead of using your own research. [6bd78cdf299b] [097e646ec1ec]
4. Writing questions with no job. [62b402dc7dc6] [c9f4734eacf6]
5. Opening with an invasive or norm-breaking question (e.g. age for older women). [25ad02d8040c]
6. Using the same format 3 or more times in a row, so people click through passively. [1081c0601454]
7. Using typed input questions at launch; they add high perceived effort and drop-off. [ce1ad833b2ec]
8. Launching with lots of friction before proving the quiz works. [cb45dd220b9b]
9. Making the quiz so long that too few people reach the PDP. [c9f4734eacf6]
10. Capturing emails and not following up with a sequence; not using the list for remarketing. [b800e9ca6a61]
11. Designing a quiz without enough customer understanding: "you're never going to design a good quiz". [9f7d25ae19bb]
12. Waiting too long: he says the format window is limited. His opinion. [bdc066a61075]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Self-conflict on personalization.** He opens by saying personalization is not why quizzes win, then says personalization "does play a role" at the email result and in branched emails. Read it as: priming drives the front end; personalization drives follow-up.
- **Self-conflict on input questions.** He advises against typed answers, yet the example quiz he praises uses typed age and height, and his own AI draft has a typed question. He only flags the last one. An agent should treat numeric fields like age as input questions too, and place them late or convert them to ranges.
- **Self-conflict on first question.** The praised example opens with male or female, a demographic question, while his rule says open with the least invasive, most important question (a goal). Gender may count as non-invasive; he does not say.
- **Completion math does not add up.** His illustrative 1% to 2% per-question drop-off gives about 77% completion over 10 questions, not 30%. 30% over 8 to 12 questions needs about 10% to 14% average drop per question. He does not say whether 30% is measured before or after the email gate, or from quiz view or quiz start. Define it before using it as a KPI. [needs brand data: step-level funnel from ad click to purchase]
- **Card drift.** Card [62b402dc7dc6] says to alternate between the 3 jobs; the source says alternate formats, not jobs. Card [c9f4734eacf6] says use about 3 questions for a simple priming quiz and the middle of the range for belief change; the source does not say this. Card [1081c0601454] frames questions as "product-selection" and says "omit questions that only manufacture agreement"; the source does the opposite and wants micro-commitments for their own sake. Follow the source text where they differ.
- **Ethics and compliance.** He describes a "hypnotic", "highly suggestible" state and inserting beliefs "directly into their subconscious". Seeding on health products (gut issues affect mood, focus, energy) can become a health claim. Keep reframes true and supportable; no invented medical outcomes. [web check: health and supplement claim rules for the brand's market and ad platform] The cards add "seeding by exaggeration risks mismatched expectations and refunds" [6bd78cdf299b].
- **Email gate and consent.** He gates the result behind name and email and gives no option to skip. He does not mention marketing consent. [web check: consent rules for the brand's market, e.g. separate opt-in for marketing email]
- **Missing how.** No quiz builder, no branching logic, no results page design, no product recommendation logic, no offer, price, discount or bundle, no sales page structure, no tracking events, no test length or sample size, no email content. The ad side is 1 line ("take the quiz" CTA). The final prompt text is on screen only.
- **Evidence.** No numbers from his own quizzes. The revenue claim, the lift anecdote and the 30% target are his word. Competitor traffic is not proof of conversion. The "limited window" claim is a sales framing.
- **Niche.** Examples are health (weight loss, gut). He says the health niche is where quizzes are "roaring". Fit for other categories (e.g. jewellery, gifts, home) is not addressed. [needs brand data: does the product need belief change or problem framing, or is it a style or gift choice]
- **Conflict with common ecommerce advice.** Common advice sells product quizzes as personalization, product matching and zero-party data. This source says those are not the main reason. Common advice also often makes email optional or shows results before the gate to protect completion; this source gates the result.
- **Conflict with other quiz sources in this folder.** Length: this source says 8 to 12 (range 3 to 20); source 18 says 15 to 25; source 17 walks through about 30 steps. Personalization: source 18 treats routing by pain point to a matched page and offer as the gain; this source says personalization is not why quizzes win. Email gate: source 18 frames it as a trade-off (retargeting versus a small conversion loss); this source gates by default. Keep these for Pass 2 section 7.
