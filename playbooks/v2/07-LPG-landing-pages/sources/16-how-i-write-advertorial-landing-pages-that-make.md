## 1. What this source is for

It gives a 5-step SOP for writing a long-form advertorial (a pre-sell page that reads like an editorial but sells a product) with an AI chat model: (1) AI-run customer research, (2) 4 reusable "foundational documents", (3) a competitor advertorial as a structural swipe, (4) drafting in halves, (5) AI review then human edit ("chiefing"). Its core claim is that AI copy fails when you skip research and just ask the model to rewrite a swipe. It is strongest on the order of inputs and on the review checklist. It is thin on page structure, headline options, claims safety, disclosure and testing, and it gives no results data.

## 2. Principles

1. **Do not ask AI to "rewrite this swipe for my company".** The presenter calls this "a terrible terrible terrible" way to write an advertorial because "you're not doing any of your research around your product your audience your offer". Condition: applies whenever you use a competitor page as the starting point. Evidence: his opinion; no side-by-side test shown. [fc318bc5a7c9] [d1001c281579]

2. **An advertorial blends an ad and an editorial so it reads as third-party and impartial, while selling the product "throughout the entire landing page".** This is his definition of the format. Condition: he does not mention an "advertisement" or "sponsored" label anywhere. See do-not-copy note in section 8 and the conflict in section 9. [fc318bc5a7c9] [web check: current FTC native advertising and endorsement guidance and Meta ad policy on landing pages that look like news or editorial content]

3. **Research and foundational documents come before any drafting.** He says the foundational docs are "the step that most people skip and as a result they're never able to write an advertorial that actually converts". Condition: his diagnosis, not a measured finding. [d1001c281579] [de19e0165ffe]

4. **Output quality tracks input quality.** "The quality of the output that you get from AI is directly proportional to the quality of inputs that you feed it." People who say AI "doesn't write good copy" are, in his words, bad at prompting. Condition: opinion. He invites adding more detail to any foundational doc by hand. [190e25f2e375] [b42b02137f4d] [de19e0165ffe]

5. **Build the foundational docs once per brand or offer, then reuse them for every asset.** "You only have to do this once." Once built, he says they drive VSLs, ads, advertorials and "landing page copy". Condition: same brand and offer; the docs are only as current as the research inside them (card caveat). [de19e0165ffe] [d1001c281579]

6. **Marketing is "a game of belief change".** Prospects act "in accordance with our own beliefs"; the copy's job is to shift limiting beliefs into empowering beliefs. He caps the list at 6 beliefs: if the prospect holds all 6, "they will positively buy". Then "the only thing that we have to do ... is simply make sure that our copy aligns with those 6 necessary beliefs ... and our job is done". Condition: the cap of 6 is his rule; "positively buy" is his framing, not proof. [4d2414f0f91d]

7. **Treat the model as a junior copywriter.** "Don't think of it as an AI think of it as your junior copywriter. You want to guide it ... have a conversation, give it feedback, critique it." Condition: applies at the draft and angle stage. [54cefb04dc2c]

8. **Use the swipe for structure, fill it with your own research.** He uploads a competitor advertorial that is "running successfully currently" only after the 4 docs are loaded, so the model writes from your research on the swipe's frame. The transcript does not spell out "structure only"; card [54cefb04dc2c] and [3bcd4bbc9ddb] state it. ecomOS rule: competitor pages are signals for structure and mood only; never copy their text, claims, names, images or faces.

9. **Never ask for a full long advertorial in 1 prompt.** If a swipe is about 2,000 words and you ask for a full rewrite, "it's only going to send you back like a thousand words" and quality is "very low" because of the token limit. Write it in chunks, "first half second half or ... by chunks". Condition: model and plan limits change; the principle (check that the draft is not being silently shortened) still holds. [54cefb04dc2c]

10. **The headline carries most of the weight.** "When it comes to split testing things the headline pulls like 90% of your entire advertorial." Condition: his opinion, no test shown. He uses this to decide whether the first half is good enough to continue. [54cefb04dc2c] [3bcd4bbc9ddb]

11. **Open with a large open loop.** He praises the demo headline because a "massive open loop" was created, "which is exactly what you want with an advertorial". The pattern is personal fear or struggle, then a discovery. Condition: 1 example; the model produced it without being asked. [3bcd4bbc9ddb]

12. **Handle objections inside the advertorial, before the sales page.** "A lot of people think that objections is something that's only covered like an FAQ section. No, ideally ... you cover and handle all the objections before they even get to the sales page." He links risk reversal to the affordability objection in the demo. Condition: his direct-response view; he does not say how to weave them in. [99f68cd7f6fd] [5a017a86f31d]

13. **AI review is not the end. A human must read it.** "You should have AI chief it but you're not done, you still have to go through and chief it yourself." The human test is the "slippery slide": copy should read like a greased slide, and "any point ... where it feels like there's a bump" costs conversions. Condition: he attributes the slide rule to "a famous copywriter" he cannot name. [5a017a86f31d] [d3e73dfc9e86]

14. **Prefer premium products for premium audiences; avoid markets that buy on lowest price.** In the demo, research showed people want the heater "to save money". He says he would "never want to compete on price", calls it "a race to the bottom", and names Walmart and Amazon as rivals no one can out-price. Condition: his personal preference, stated as a side rant. He does not give a rule for what to do if you are already in a price-led market. [b5474d3f3abb] [needs brand data: price position against competitors, gross margin]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Research (step 1)

Goal: a long qualitative research document on the market, built by AI. [190e25f2e375] [a0033b088c87]

1. Open a new ChatGPT chat. He uses GPT-4.5. [verify UI] [web check: current best model for long-form research and copy]
2. Convert the brand's sales page to PDF with a webpage-to-PDF tool. For your own brand, upload any owned content: sales page PDF, existing advertorials, other copy. Give "as much information as possible on the product itself". [needs brand data: live PDP or sales page, existing advertorials, ad copy, product facts]
3. Paste prompt 1 from his SOP. Fill the 1 placeholder line: "explain what you're selling to and who". Attach the sales page PDF. Send.
4. Ignore the analysis it returns. He says "we actually don't care about that", the prompt is "priming" the model.
5. Paste prompt 2 and attach the 2 "how to properly conduct copywriting research" documents from his SOP. Send and let it finish. (The documents are not in this library.)
6. Paste prompt 3, fill the product line again. This prompt asks the model to write a prompt for its own Deep Research run ("AI inception").
7. Copy the prompt it returns. Paste it back into the chat with Deep Research selected. [verify UI]
8. Answer its clarifying questions. In the demo: yes, US-based; all price ranges; yes (the third question is not shown). [needs brand data: target country, price range, customer type]
9. Wait. He says 8 to 20 minutes depending on product and market; his run took about 11 minutes.
10. Copy the full output into a new document and download it as a PDF. This is foundational doc 1, the research doc. He calls this "the most difficult part, which is ironically also the easiest part".

### 3.2 Foundational documents (step 2)

Goal: 3 more PDFs built from the research. Each uses a template from his SOP. [0d9228434376] [b42b02137f4d] [4d2414f0f91d]

Avatar sheet (doc 2):
1. Download his avatar sheet template as a PDF (in a doc editor: File > Download > PDF). [verify UI]
2. Paste the avatar sheet prompt, attach the template, send. The model fills in the avatar "based off the research".
3. Check that it holds demographics, pain points and direct quotes. His demo quotes: "I need a solution now before another freezing winter hits", "I'm desperate to stop throwing my money away on inefficient heating", "I can't afford another huge bill". He calls the last one "a massive pain that we're going to be able to tap into".
4. Copy into a document titled "Avatar sheet", download as PDF.

Offer brief (doc 3):
1. Paste the offer brief prompt, attach the offer brief template, send.
2. The brief covers: funnel architecture, level of consciousness, stage of awareness, stage of sophistication, unique mechanism, and "all the copy ingredients" needed for a good VSL or advertorial.
3. Add your own detail where you have it. Copy into a document, download as PDF. [needs brand data: offer, price, bundle, guarantee, funnel pages]

Necessary beliefs (doc 4):
1. Paste his long "necessary beliefs" prompt. He says it makes the model analyze a transcript on belief change. "Nothing needs to change." Send.
2. Paste prompt 4 and attach docs 1, 2 and 3 (research, avatar sheet, offer brief).
3. The prompt asks for at most 6 beliefs that, if all held, mean the prospect "will positively buy". His example belief: "I believe my current heating method is inefficient and costly."
4. Copy into a document, download as PDF.

Speed tip he gives: during the doc-building prompts he sends 1 and moves straight to the next, because "we're not specifically looking for the output yet". Condition: only for the priming and doc-building prompts, not for the draft.

### 3.3 Refresh and swipe (step 3)

1. Paste prompt 5 and upload all 4 foundational PDFs together. The model analyzes each one "to refresh its memory". Use this same step to start any future copy session. [de19e0165ffe]
2. Pick a competitor advertorial that is "running successfully currently". He uses his own swipe file or an advertorial already running for the same product type. He gives no test for "successful". [web check: confirm the swipe page is live and how long its ads have run, for example in the Meta Ad Library]
3. Convert the swipe URL to PDF and upload it.
4. Paste the swipe prompt from the SOP and send. The model analyzes the swipe.

### 3.4 Draft (step 4)

1. Paste the "first half" prompt and fill in its blanks (not shown). Send.
2. He selects "answer in chat" rather than the side view because "sometimes the outputs come out cleaner"; he admits this "could be totally biased". [verify UI]
3. Read the first half. Judge mainly on the headline. If the angle is wrong, change angle now through conversation before continuing.
4. Prompt for the second half. Copy the first half into the advertorial document while it writes.
5. Paste the second half in. Download the full advertorial as a PDF.
6. Optional: keep going back and forth to "brainstorm new angles". His SOP has a bonus section on writing extra angles; its content is not in the transcript.

### 3.5 Chief (step 5)

AI chief:
1. Upload the advertorial PDF back into the same chat. Paste the chiefing prompt. [5a017a86f31d]
2. Read what it returns. In the demo it checked: emotional storytelling; identification of the problem; each necessary belief; clear unique mechanism; objections pre-handled; "external blame and villain" (from the competitor analysis); risk reversal; fit with levels of consciousness and stages of sophistication; fit with the avatar research and offer brief.
3. Treat its "areas to improve" as angle options. In the demo it suggested a conspiracy angle (heating companies keep bills high) and he names the "whistleblower angle" (an insider who escaped and tells the truth). See section 8 before using either.

Human chief:
1. Read the whole advertorial yourself. [d3e73dfc9e86]
2. Wherever you feel stuck, the flow is not "completely natural", or the word order is illogical, rewrite it yourself.
3. Ship only when the read has no bumps. He says the full process took "under an hour" once set up.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Rewriting a competitor's advertorial with AI and no research. [fc318bc5a7c9]
2. Skipping the foundational documents. He calls it the step most people skip and the reason their advertorials fail. [d1001c281579]
3. Thin inputs, then blaming the model. [190e25f2e375]
4. Blind swiping without understanding awareness, sophistication and mechanism. [b42b02137f4d]
5. Asking for a full ~2,000-word advertorial in 1 prompt. [54cefb04dc2c]
6. Leaving objections to an FAQ. [99f68cd7f6fd]
7. Stopping at the AI review. [5a017a86f31d]
8. Leaving "bumps" in the read. [d3e73dfc9e86]
9. Competing on price in a market that buys on price (his view). [b5474d3f3abb]

Do-not-copy flags (ecomOS rules, not raised by the source):

- **Undisclosed ad format.** He defines the advertorial as copy that "feels like it was written by a third party ... completely unbiased" while selling throughout, and never mentions a label. Do not publish an advertorial without a clear "Advertisement" or "Sponsored" label and honest authorship. [web check: FTC native advertising guidance, ASCI rules in India for Gehna, Meta landing page policy]
- **Invented first-person narrator.** The demo headline is written as "I" and "my family" by someone who does not exist. Only use a first-person story from a real, consenting customer, or frame it clearly as a brand-written story. [needs brand data: real customer stories with consent]
- **Fake insider or whistleblower.** The "whistleblower angle" relies on "somebody on the inside, quote unquote". Do not invent an insider, doctor, expert or spokesperson.
- **Conspiracy and villain claims.** "The heating companies are trying to keep you broke" and "external blame and villain" are claims about third parties. Do not state them without evidence.
- **AI research quotes as testimonials.** Deep Research quotes may be paraphrased, merged or made up. Use them only as voice-of-customer cues for wording. Never present them as reviews or testimonials.
- **Unsupported benefit claims.** The demo headline implies savings on "skyrocketing energy costs" with no data. Every savings, health or result claim needs brand proof. [needs brand data: product test results, claim substantiation]
- **Swipe copying.** Use the competitor page for section order and pacing only. Do not reuse its text, stories, claims, names, images or faces.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Prompts are missing.** The title promises prompts; the transcript has none. Any reuse needs new prompts written from the step descriptions in section 3.
- **No evidence.** The "$1.3m/mo" claim is not backed in the transcript. The demo product is not his. No test data, no before and after.
- **Silent on page structure.** Apart from headline, open loop, mechanism, objections and risk reversal, the source gives no section order, length, image, CTA or layout guidance. Look to the other 07-LPG mini playbooks for that (05, for example, is titled as an advertorial structure).
- **Silent on disclosure and compliance.** Other 07-LPG sources (08) note that advertorials are labeled as sponsored. This one does not. Common ecommerce advice, ad platform policy and consumer law require clear ad labels. Treat this source as wrong by omission here.
- **Headline "90%".** Common CRO advice ranks the offer, the traffic match and the audience alongside the headline. The 90% figure is folk wisdom, not a benchmark. [needs brand data: headline split test results]
- **Objections before the sales page versus FAQ.** Common ecommerce advice keeps an FAQ on the PDP. His view does not forbid an FAQ; it says the advertorial should already have answered objections. Card [99f68cd7f6fd] adds a caveat he did not state: over-handling objections early can raise doubts.
- **Premium only.** His avoid-price-markets stance is a preference. Card [b5474d3f3abb] adds an idea he did not say (reframe around a non-price desire, such as mechanism or whistleblower). Many profitable stores serve price-led markets.
- **Card extrapolations.** Some card bullets go beyond the transcript: [0d9228434376] "changing product but not audience means you can skip rebuilding"; [d3e73dfc9e86] "the cause is usually structure, not missing information" and "cross-check the belief"; [3bcd4bbc9ddb] "do not resolve the loop before the mechanism" and the traffic click-through framing; [99f68cd7f6fd] "weave responses into the story". Use them as card-author advice, not as the presenter's.
- **Card error.** [190e25f2e375] says he prefers GPT-4.5 "over GPT-4". The transcript says "40", most likely GPT-4o.
- **Model and tool advice is stale.** ChatGPT-over-Claude is his 2025 opinion ("Claude tries to be so PC"). Token limits, Deep Research and the chat layout have likely changed. The durable part is the input order and the review checklist.
- **Slide rule attribution.** He cannot name the copywriter. It is commonly attributed to Joseph Sugarman. [web check: attribution] The piano headline is a classic direct-response ad; he does not name its author.
- **"Successful" swipe is undefined.** He gives no way to tell a winning competitor page from a running one.
- **No testing step.** The SOP ends at "ready to be put out into the wild". It has no launch, measurement or iteration step. [needs brand data: landing page conversion, CTR from advertorial to PDP, CPA by page]
