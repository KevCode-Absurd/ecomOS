## 1. What this source is for

It teaches a beginner how to plan, draft and edit direct-response copy: ads, launch emails, welcome emails and sales pages. Its core rules are: copy is sales in writing, write to "you" not "we", use the customer's own words from reviews, match the ad to the landing page, and outline before drafting. It gives fill-in templates for an email (goal, subject, preview, story, content, CTA, PS), a 10-email launch, a 7-email welcome series and a sales page. It is strong on writing process and templates. It is weak on ad platforms, has no test design, and its results are one consultant's anecdotes, mostly from info products.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 What copy is and who it serves

1. **Copywriting is sales in written form.** It is not copyright law, not content writing (SEO, blogs, organic social) and not creative writing. Source line: "content writers write blogs and copywriters write advertorials." Judge a piece by whether it moves the reader toward a conversion goal. [f27d97c52312]

2. **You do not need to be a good writer. You are a salesperson.** Writers care about creativity and wit. Copywriters care about results. Source's evidence: a client's Mailchimp sender name had the company name misspelled (set up 3 years earlier). Her first email for them went out with it and sold 3x more than any email they had sent before, about $15,000, even while people replied calling it spam. This is one anecdote about grammar mattering less than message; it is not advice to misspell. [f27d97c52312]

3. **The best copy is simple, conversational and concrete.** Throw out "SAT words" and school essay rules. People read on a phone at the grocery store, on the way to work, with kids and dogs and doorbells around them, in "micro moments" between tasks. Condition: applies to all sales copy; the source does not discuss premium or technical categories. [f27d97c52312]

4. **Small wording changes can matter a lot.** Source says one word changed in a headline can create a 25% increase in conversions, from her experience. Anecdote, not a benchmark. [f27d97c52312] [a0589e4916be]

5. **Perspective gap: you are the expert, the prospect is not.** The owner has full context and passion for the transformation. Prospects come from a different background. Your job is to understand the gap between where you are and where they are, and lead them. You cannot sell with your logic; learn theirs (see sentiment matching). The source calls this "absolutely key to everything else". [3786ef47e9f9] [44549e0c9982]

6. **The funnel is a mountain, not a slide.** Gravity works against you. The primary conversion goal (a sale, or a free consultation for some models) sits at the top. Every step is a micro decision where people drop. On a social ad: stop scrolling? read the headline? look at the picture? read the sub headline? read the ad text? click? Then the same on the landing page. People also climb by different paths, not one straight line. Mental model only; the source gives no way to measure it. [16254d1bf3e4] [3786ef47e9f9]

### 2.2 Voice: the customer is the hero

7. **It is their story, not yours.** Source calls "make your product the hero of the ad" the single worst piece of marketing advice. Everyone narrates their own life as the hero. The brand is the guide ("Yoda to their Luke"). [a0589e4916be]

8. **Write in second person. Never start sentences with I, me, my, us, ours.** This also covers your company name and product names as the subject. Source: "people don't care about you, they care about them." [a0589e4916be] [44549e0c9982]
   - If you cannot find a way to use "you", at least drop the pronouns about yourself. "Our Story, Contact Us, Our Mission, About Us" becomes "Story, Contact, Mission, About".
   - First person is allowed when tied to the reader. "This mastermind group is the place to be if you want my personal guidance on your headshots..." becomes "If you want my personal guidance on your headshots, clips, resumes, online acting profiles and career questions, then this mastermind group is the place to be."
   - "You can check this out for a flavor of what I'm going to teach" becomes "...what you'll learn."
   - Expertise and frameworks matter to buyers, but only after the copy relates them to the reader.
   - Source admits this style is "very unnatural" and took her at least 6 years to spot at a glance.
   - Stated exception: the launch "Introducing: [product]" email names the product on purpose (see 3.3).

### 2.3 Planning assets

9. **Each asset has 1 function: give value or sell. Never both, never 5 things.** Applies to emails, ads, content marketing, landing pages and organic social. Make the goal specific, for example "value: watch this YouTube tutorial on writing emails". Tension: her own lesson emails teach and then close with a "reminder" CTA to buy (see 3.3). [c3c4e6142fe4] [f27d97c52312]

10. **Decide the count and function of every piece before writing.** Defaults: 10 emails for a launch, 7 for a welcome sequence. Deviate on purpose if you want. Seeing the whole sequence shows the through-line and each email's role. [5a403376e7b5] [70449eae6ade]

11. **Start writing as soon as possible after the outline.** The outline beats "blank page syndrome". Draft without editing. [e20d746ada93] [c3c4e6142fe4]

### 2.4 Ads

12. **Messaging congruency is the biggest problem with PPC ads.** Congruency means 2 things are the same. After the click, the next step must not confuse. Use similar colours to the ad. Repeat the promise in new words, like a musical "reprise". Applies to Facebook ads, Google AdWords, sponsored tweets, Instagram promoted stories, in-feed ads and Amazon sponsored listings. [dda3430eb956]
    - Good: ad says "want to be an actor? here's a complete road map to getting booked in network television from scratch"; the page opens with "your step-by-step road map to becoming a working actor from scratch".
    - Bad: ad sells a Spider-Man t-shirt, click lands on the homepage.
    - Congruency also applies inside an asset (a quiz must give a result) and across the brand (production value must match the rest of the brand that a warm audience already knows).

13. **An ad needs 1 clearly stated purpose and must stand out from the feed.** See the Jake Paul case in section 7. [36b9e7b8a840]

14. **Ad format observations (as of the talk).** Video ads were "performing very, very well". Emojis were pulling; the waving hand emoji was popular ("hi, I saw you were checking out my thing"). Use short lines: 1-sentence paragraphs, 1 to 3 sentences at most. For the first top lines she uses a rhythm she describes as "1, 1, 2, 1" (the transcript does not say if these are words or sentences; the card reads it as words). Keep the ad off yourself and your product. [36b9e7b8a840] `[web check: current Meta ad copy norms; this is 2019-era observation]`

15. **Skip brand awareness ads unless you are a monopoly or duopoly.** Source: even her "$12 million" clients do not have the budget for awareness. Do not run logo ads or funny creative meant only to make people know you. Aim every ad at purchase. This is her opinion; it conflicts with many current full-funnel views. [f27174953be4]

16. **Persistence in the Ads Library is a signal.** If a competitor keeps running an ad for several weeks or months, it is probably working, because at big spend Facebook cuts reach on disliked ads within 2 days, sometimes 24 hours, and big spenders (tens to hundreds of thousands per month) refresh creative every few days. Condition: only holds for brands spending at that scale. [f27174953be4]

### 2.5 Email

17. **Launch emails: send new messages plus resends to unopens.** Resend with a different subject line and preview text. [3375be5b64cd] [2ac1adf98a07]

18. **Pacing does not change revenue much, in her experience.** Aggressive and moderate both produced multi-six-figure launches. Choose by how urgently you need the cash. [ad79c950b7c8]

19. **The triple close: 3 strong sales emails on the last day.** She calls it "consistently successful" and says she never ran a launch without it after the first test. [8046e8352c3b] [8ccd32a8db57]

20. **Text-only emails.** All her launch emails are plain text, except the reading-list email (book covers) and video emails (a thumbnail with a play button). Reason she gives: HTML templates put you in Promotions and spam, and big emails get clipped. Tension: her own ecommerce welcome email 1 uses a 3-column product grid with pictures. [2bcad09a68e8] [60d5057bb3c2]

21. **New subscribers are at peak engagement.** Send daily for 7 days in the welcome series. Her claim: more opens make Gmail keep you in the primary inbox and out of Promotions and spam. Heuristic, not shown with data. [dfb61fe7d4a8]

22. **Subject lines: no periods.** "A period means stop reading." Exclamation points are "kind of okay". She normally writes correct grammar but uses lowercase for late-night "you up" style lines. [676688759b08] [ae22f03a0868]

23. **Always write your own preview text.** Do not let the email tool generate it. [c3c4e6142fe4]

24. **Names do not sell.** She renamed a client's course "Academy" and it sold 20,000 (the transcript does not say units or dollars). Do not spend effort on a clever product name. [2ac1adf98a07]

### 2.6 Sales pages

25. **Story length follows awareness stage.** Less aware needs more build-up; most aware needs the offer. See 3.7. [3912f9a679c0]

26. **Sentiment matching.** Learn how prospects narrate their problem in their own heads and tell that story back. Read 5-star and 1-star reviews. Position as "I'm not like the stuff you hate, but I am like the stuff you love". [fb6ca632b8da] [a438dd94bf69]

27. **A sales page needs a through-line.** Read only the headers; they should tell a story. Source says lack of cohesion is "the number one problem with sales pages". Opinion. [457ab3b6b02d] [caee8d24b0f0]

28. **Do not name the product in the hero** unless you are Mercedes or Apple level. [8ff8c1675824]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Email outline template (works for any asset, per source)

1. Set the primary conversion goal for the asset.
2. Decide how many emails (10 for launch, 7 for welcome).
3. Write one block:
   - `Email 1`
   - `Primary conversion goal:` value or sell, plus specifics (for example "value, watch this YouTube tutorial").
   - `Subject: X`
   - `Preview: X` (write it yourself; she likes it "attention grabbing").
   - Bullet list: `Story`, `Today I want to talk to you about X`, `Content` (what the email says), `Call to action`, `PS`.
4. Always pre-write the PS (she says she does it "mostly" and tells you to always do it).
5. Copy and paste the block for each email and renumber them.
6. Fill ideas into any block in any order as they come. Do research first if writing for someone else (their platform, content pillars).
7. Draft. If a line seems dumb, leave it, turn the text red or add a note, and move on. [c3c4e6142fe4] [5a403376e7b5] [70449eae6ade] [e20d746ada93]

### 3.2 Editing pass (all copy)

1. Let the draft rest 24 hours ("like a good steak", but 24 hours, not 10 to 30 minutes). Sleep on it.
2. Read the whole piece out loud.
3. Main check: anywhere you stumble or trip. If the author, with full context, stumbles, a cold reader will leave, delete or mark as spam.
4. Fix the line, then start again from the top. Repeat until you can read the whole piece aloud without a mistake.
5. Also check: first-person pronouns to flip to "you", parts nobody cares about (cut) and things they do care about (add), confusing sentence structures (rewrite simply), repeated words.
6. Resolve the red-marked lines. [e76793749fc7] [a0589e4916be]

### 3.3 10-email launch sequence

Phases: email 1, emails 2 to 7, emails 8 to 10. All text-only. Each email uses the outline template. [2ac1adf98a07]

1. **Email 1, "Introducing".** Subject: `Introducing: [product name]`. Expect opens to be cut about in half (she has seen about 15% vs a normal 25% to 30%+), but openers are very interested. It is a strong sales email.
   - Story: the problem, told the way people narrate it in their heads.
   - "Today I want to talk to you about [product]" (she often leaves this line as is).
   - Content: everything in the product, told with as much excitement as possible.
   - Close, sign-off.
   - PS: short summary. Pattern: "I know you regularly struggle with [problem]... that's why I created [product]. It does 1, 2, 3. If you want the full details, click here." [2ac1adf98a07]
2. **Emails 2 to 7: 4 formats.** Choose how many of each; "you should have at least one" (she promised to say her usual mix but never did). [2ac1adf98a07]
   - **Lesson.** A quick win the reader can apply right away, related to the product (blog-post or tutorial style). Teach fully in the email; do not send them off the email. End with a soft CTA; her "crutch" openers are "What's next", "Your next step", "Reminder". Example: "If you love these strategies, remember [product] is available and has 4 other strategies way better than this one." PS names the mistake the reader makes and the fuller fix in the product, then links (her example: "15 audition sins", package, headshots, resume). [d5a6684a85cc]
   - **Repurposed content.** Teaches but takes them off the email to the content. Your content, external content or both. Her examples: a recommended reading list of about 10 books with cover, name, Amazon link and a "why" drawn from 5-star Amazon reviews; "5 mistakes you're making with your resume" or "how to network with agents" from a YouTube channel; a 5-day structured live-stream lesson series from a 58,000-member Facebook group, repurposed because not everyone saw it. Same CTA and PS pattern. [1d49a137d194]
   - **Success.** Either 1 long, strong testimonial or case study, or several testimonials. Best when each testimonial answers a known objection. Then the reminder CTA. [0c6041550953]
   - **FAQ.** Collect questions from site chat, emails and Facebook page messages. Opener: "Over the last few days I've been telling you about [product], and you sent in so many great questions that I wanted to answer them all at once." Answer each, then the reminder, sign-off and a PS reminder. [0c6041550953]
3. **Emails 8 to 10: the triple close.** 3 strong sales emails on closing day. Origin: a client asked whether to send closing emails morning and afternoon, or afternoon and evening; time zones make it unclear, so she sent all 3. The late-night one uses a subject like "you up", "you're still up" or "just checking in", lowercase, no punctuation. [8046e8352c3b] [8ccd32a8db57] [ae22f03a0868]

### 3.4 Launch pacing and resends

1. Write 2 subject lines and 2 preview texts per launch email (she does this for clients). On a small list, changing only the subject line is fine. [3375be5b64cd]
2. Pick a pace. The transcript is garbled here; her own words: "every time I practice this I get all discombobulated". Best reading: [ad79c950b7c8]
   - **Aggressive, 10 days:** day 1 send email 1. Day 2 morning resend email 1 to unopens, afternoon or evening send email 2. Day 3 morning resend email 2 to unopens, afternoon send email 3. Continue. 20 sends in 10 days; each person gets only the resends they did not open.
   - **Moderate, about 20 days:** day 1 email 1. Day 2 email 1 to unopens (openers get a break). Day 3 email 2 to all. Day 4 email 2 to unopens. Continue.
   - **Relaxed, 20+ days:** add breaks, or more rounds of unopen resends.
3. Choose by urgency: need the money in 10 days, go aggressive; "feeling chill", go slower. She mostly uses aggressive and moderate.
4. On big lists, resend more. Her lists are usually over 10,000; some clients have 600,000 and 1M contacts. By day 3, thousands still have not seen email 1, and "you paid someone good money to write it". [ad79c950b7c8] [3375be5b64cd]

### 3.5 7-email welcome series

1. Automation, 1 email a day for 7 days. No unopen resends needed. [dfb61fe7d4a8]
2. Info-product version: each day links a video from the client's YouTube channel. Order inside the email: "Click for your video lesson" (hyperlinked), then the thumbnail with a superimposed play button (hyperlinked), then what they will learn, "if you want to check it out, click here", sign-off, PS with the benefit and another link. [dfb61fe7d4a8]
3. Ecommerce version (Head Candy, hair tools): [60d5057bb3c2]
   - Email 1: "Hey, you're confirmed, welcome." Thank them for joining "the squad / the crew / the family". Big coupon for "10 off" (the transcript does not say $ or %). Below it, 3 columns of best-selling products: title, picture, buy link. Say "best-selling products", not "our best-selling products".
   - Emails 2 to 7: each is a hair tutorial ("how to curl a pixie cut", "how to straighten really curly hair without frizz") linking to a video that demonstrates the products.
   - Her summary: "first email, make them an offer; then sharing is caring" (content).

### 3.6 Ads Library research routine

1. Open the Facebook Ads Library (source: facebook.com/ads/library). `[verify UI]` `[web check: current Meta Ad Library URL and filters]`
2. Search a company. Filter by country. You see the live ad, can watch video and click through to the landing page.
3. Pick companies that make most or all revenue online through direct-response social ads, ideally in or near your niche. Her examples: ClickFunnels, Kettle & Fire bone broth. Skip awareness brands such as Coca-Cola and Honda.
4. Check the same set once a week or twice a month.
5. Note what gets cut and what keeps running for weeks or months. Long runners are the ideas to pull from.
6. She rates this "a million times better" than swipe files, because the ads are current. [f27174953be4]
ecomOS rule: competitor ads are signals, not proven winners; use for layout and mood only.

### 3.7 Sales page

1. Place the audience on the awareness scale (car example from the film "10 Cloverfield Lane"): [3912f9a679c0]
   1. Unaware: in a bunker, does not know there is an outside. Do not sell to them.
   2. Problem aware: out of the bunker, knows there is somewhere to go.
   3. Solution aware: knows about bikes, buses, planes, "Uber copter", cars.
   4. Product aware: has decided on a car, choosing between options.
   5. Most aware: "I want a Mercedes."
2. Set story length. Stages 2 and 3: build up more story. Her example: "you tried keto and it didn't work, Zumba worked until you stopped and gained it all back, here are common reasons nothing works, here is a different solution." Stage 4: shorten the story, add lots of testimonials. Stage 5: "shut up about your story" and sell. Her default for most readers: stage 3 to 4, so a medium or fairly long story. (Card 3912f9a679c0 mislabels this as "between problem- and solution-aware".) [3912f9a679c0]
3. Fill the template in this order: [8ff8c1675824]
   1. Headline
   2. Sub headline
   3. Call to action (1 to 3 = hero section; no product name unless you are mercedes.com or apple.com)
   4. Story (length from step 2)
   5. Show why they need it
   6. Show what happens without it
   7. Testimonials and success stories
   8. Position the price
   9. Objection handling (for example "what if I want my money back?")
   10. FAQs
   11. More testimonials
   - Put a CTA or pricing table every 2 or 3 content sections (her slide could not show them all).
4. Write the story with sentiment matching (3.8), not your own story.
5. Check the through-line by reading only the headers (3.9).
6. Position the price (3.10).

### 3.8 Sentiment matching

1. Read your own customer testimonials. [a438dd94bf69]
2. If you have none, read reviews of others who do the same thing, or of analogous products: Amazon books, videos, online courses. Her example: a relationship coach can read reviews of another coach with the same format. [a438dd94bf69]
3. Read both 5-star (what they love) and 1-star (what they hate). [fb6ca632b8da]
4. Record the words and phrases people use to describe the problem.
5. Tell that story back in headlines, story and objection handling. Position against what they hate and toward what they love. [fb6ca632b8da] [a438dd94bf69]
`[needs brand data: own reviews, support tickets, chat logs]` `[web check: competitor and marketplace reviews]`

### 3.9 Header through-line check

Read only the headers in order. They should tell a story. Her examples: [457ab3b6b02d] [caee8d24b0f0]
- Purpose course: "Discover your purpose, do meaningful work and earn an income" / "Get the roadmap you need to discover your passion and earn an income doing what you love" / "It's Monday morning. Ugh." / "It's Monday already. Just 5 more minutes." / "Hey, at least it pays the bills" / "We need a massive culture shift".
- Acting course: "You're starting an exciting journey" / "Hollywood doesn't make it easy" / "If you're serious about your craft". Each line echoes a belief actors already hold: excited, facing institutional barriers, taking classes 2 to 3 days a week, reading Backstage, going to casting director workshops.

### 3.10 Position the price

Two "easy" ways; she says there are more. [c7a79cc9ee3e]
1. **Offer stack:** break the package into features, put a value on each, and end with "priceless" (MasterCard-ad style). Her example: "Total Passion to Paycheck value: 75,000. Total investment starting at 199" (currency not stated).
2. **Apples-to-oranges comparison**, when itemising does not fit:
   - Positive orange: the cost of a trainer plus a personal chef plus a nutritionist, vs the course price (transcript says "3.99"; a later line says "400", so likely $399).
   - Negative orange: the cost of inaction, for example a heart attack, not being there for your children, missing your daughter's wedding.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Evidence is thin and mostly off-category.** Nearly all examples are courses, coaching and memberships. The only product-ecommerce case is Head Candy, and its big result came from CRO plus automation together, so the copy's share is unknown.
- **Dated (about 2019 to 2020).** Open-rate numbers predate Apple Mail Privacy Protection. Ad format and emoji observations are from then. AdWords and Infusionsoft have been renamed. `[web check: current Meta and Google ad copy practice; current email benchmarks]`
- **Card vs transcript differences found:**
  - 36b9e7b8a840 says the "1, 1, 2, 1" rhythm is words; the transcript does not say words or sentences.
  - 2ac1adf98a07 says "at least one of each" format; the transcript says "at least one" and never gives the mix. It also says the Jay Shetty business grew to "~$2M annualized"; the transcript is garbled there, and earlier states the unit grew from $2.8M to $4.68M.
  - 8ccd32a8db57 says closing emails should be short and "not new persuasion"; the transcript calls emails 8 to 10 "strong sales emails" and never says short.
  - 3912f9a679c0 labels stage 3 to 4 as "between problem- and solution-aware"; on her scale 3 is solution aware and 4 is product aware.
  - 60d5057bb3c2 says "$10 off"; the transcript says "10 off" with no unit.
  - c7a79cc9ee3e gives "$3.99"; the transcript later says "400", so $399 is likely.
  - ae22f03a0868 says write the subject last; the transcript only shows "Subject: X" as a placeholder filled later.
  - 16254d1bf3e4 lists landing page, offer, add-to-cart steps; the transcript only says the landing page works the same way.
- **Internal tensions:** "never value and sell in one email" vs lesson emails that teach then pitch; "text-only emails" vs her ecommerce welcome email with a 3-column product image grid; "never name your product" vs the "Introducing: [product]" launch email (she flags this one as a deliberate launch exception).
- **Conflicts with common ecommerce advice:**
  - Plain-text emails: most ecommerce brands send image-led HTML emails and product grids. Her reason for clipping ("about 2 MB") does not match the widely cited Gmail clip at about 102 KB of HTML code, which is about code size, not images. `[web check: Gmail clipping limit]`
  - No product name in the hero: on ecommerce product pages the product name is normally the H1. Her rule fits long-form sales pages and advertorials, not standard PDPs.
  - "Skip brand awareness": many current Meta and full-funnel views disagree. Treat as her opinion.
  - Triple close and aggressive unopen resends: she does not measure unsubscribes or spam complaints. `[needs brand data: unsubscribe and complaint rates per send]`
  - Ads Library persistence as proof of a winner: ecomOS treats competitor ads as signals only; long-running ads can also be evergreen retargeting or neglected.
- **Missing how:** no test design, no sample sizes, no sequence for ad copy beyond format tips, no guidance for PDPs, no segmentation, no discount strategy beyond "10 off", no policy or compliance notes (fear-based "heart attack" framing may hit ad or brand limits).
- **Ecommerce translation is the reader's job.** Offer stack "value" figures and fear framing can break ecomOS writing rules (no invented claims, no inflated values). Use real values only. `[needs brand data: real component prices before an offer stack]`
