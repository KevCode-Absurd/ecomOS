## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator pick the one core message for an ad, landing page, advertorial or product page before writing, and audit existing copy that has piled up too many selling points. It also gives a filter for choosing a single USP. It is a framework, not a testing method; results still need the brand's own tests.

## 2. Principles

1. **Build each ad or page on one big idea (the Rule of One).** Condition: any sales message, and the presenter names ad copy and landing page copy directly. Evidence: Masterson reviewed the top 100 offers of all time at his company; 91 centred on a single concept (retold second-hand, not a guaranteed outcome at your scale). [491bae29b0be]
2. **Do not stack unrelated selling points ("tossed salad copy").** Condition: when you are tempted to add every good point on the theory that "if that one doesn't work, one of them will". Masterson's name for this is tossed salad copy: throw everything at the wall and see what sticks. The source says it weakens the message; the cards say it "dilutes conversion power". [491bae29b0be] [1ee01d6bfef2]
3. **The one idea must pass 3 tests.** It must be (a) easy to understand, (b) easy to believe, and (c) interesting or unique. In the USP passage the presenter words (c) as "counterintuitive". When all 3 hold, "people will take one step towards you". If a candidate fails any test, keep looking. [491bae29b0be] [b720ce9767c7]
4. **Every supporting element serves the one idea.** Condition: all sub-bullets, stories, arguments and facts. They should be there "solely to reinforce the singular concept". A list is fine if every item is a sub-point of one core benefit or topic. [491bae29b0be] [1ee01d6bfef2]
5. **One USP, not 19.** Condition: defining or revising a unique selling proposition. The source applies the same rule: 1 core proposition everyone can understand at once, that is counterintuitive and believable. [b720ce9767c7]
6. **The hard part is choosing the idea, not writing the copy.** Having to pick only 1 forces you to ask whether this is the best, most focused concept to be "the tip of the spear". Once it is fixed, the presenter says the rest of the sales message is "very transparent and simple to write". [491bae29b0be]
7. **Your best past ads likely show the same pattern (presenter's opinion).** Condition: you have run ads before. The presenter says the best converting ads are usually "the simplest, the most direct and the clearest to understand". This is his observation, not a tested rule. ## 3. Procedures

The source gives the rule and one example, not step lists. The steps below are how the cards turn the rule into work; each step maps to something the presenter said.

### 3.1 Choose the one big idea before briefing or writing
1. List every candidate idea or selling point for the product or offer. [needs brand data: product facts, proof and customer language] [491bae29b0be] [b720ce9767c7]
2. Test each candidate: easy to understand? easy to believe? interesting, unique or counterintuitive? Drop any that fail 1 test. [491bae29b0be]
3. Pick 1 as the anchor. If several pass, pick the most focused, central one; the source gives no tie-break method beyond that judgment. [491bae29b0be]
4. Write the ad or page so every bullet, story, fact and argument supports that idea. [491bae29b0be]
5. Use the chosen idea to decide which proof, testimonials and facts go on the page and which to drop. [b720ce9767c7]

### 3.2 Audit existing copy for tossed salad
1. Read the ad, page or advertorial and list every distinct claim and benefit. [1ee01d6bfef2]
2. Flag any point that is not a sub-point of the same core idea. [1ee01d6bfef2]
3. Pick the point that best passes the 3 tests as the candidate big idea. [1ee01d6bfef2]
4. Rewrite the remaining points to reinforce it. If a strong point cannot be made to fit, cut it; do not keep it "just in case" (card wording). [1ee01d6bfef2]
5. Check that a stranger could state the single argument back in 1 sentence (card's check, not the presenter's). [1ee01d6bfef2]
6. Test the rewrite against the old version in real ads or page tests. [1ee01d6bfef2] [needs brand data: test results]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Tossed salad check.** List the claims in the copy. Good: every claim is a sub-point of 1 idea. Bad: claims point in different directions, or read like "15 crazy headline ideas". Fix: run procedure 3.2. [1ee01d6bfef2]
- **3-test check on the chosen idea.** Good: a new reader gets it at once, believes it, and finds it new or surprising. Bad: it is unique but not credible, or credible but generic. Fix: pick a different idea. A counterintuitive claim that is not believable fails. [b720ce9767c7]
- **Past winners check.** Pull the brand's best converting ads and compare how many ideas each carries. The source expects the winners to be the simplest and clearest; confirm on the brand's own data rather than assume. [491bae29b0be] [needs brand data: ad-level results with copy]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Stacking every good selling point in 1 ad hoping one works (tossed salad copy). [491bae29b0be]
- Having many selling propositions ("19") instead of 1 core one. [b720ce9767c7]
- Supporting bullets that introduce new angles instead of reinforcing the main idea. [1ee01d6bfef2]
- Chasing novelty alone: uniqueness without belief fails the framework's own test (card caveat). [b720ce9767c7]
