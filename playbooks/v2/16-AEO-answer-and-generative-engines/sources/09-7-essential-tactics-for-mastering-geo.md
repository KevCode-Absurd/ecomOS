## 2. Principles

1. GEO is SEO's extension for AI engines, not a replacement. Traditional SEO targets Google/Bing link lists; GEO targets AI engines (ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews) that generate full contextual answers instead of links. Condition: applies whenever a query is likely to be answered by an AI engine rather than a ten-blue-links page. Evidence: the presenter's own framing, no data. [9297d576fa76]
2. Start from the query, not the keyword. Identify conversational, question-shaped, long-tail phrases a shopper would actually type into an AI engine, then check how that engine currently structures and cites its answer, and only then build content to match. Condition: applies to planning PDP copy, category descriptions and blog topics for AI-engine visibility. Evidence: presenter's method, illustrated with skincare-industry examples ("best natural moisturizer for dry skin," "how to treat acne with organic products"), no measurement given. [9297d576fa76]
3. Answer directly; do not pad for keyword density. Content that clearly and contextually answers the specific question is what the source says AI engines reward; long generic pieces built to raise keyword density are explicitly discouraged. Condition: applies to PDP copy, buying guides, FAQ content meant to be cited by AI engines. Evidence: presenter's opinion of what AI engines favor, not a measured ranking factor. [06e30e5bb046]
4. Structure for machine extraction. Clear headings, bullet points, short paragraphs, and schema/structured markup make a page easier for an AI engine to parse and reuse. Condition: applies to PDP, category pages, and guides, especially where facts (price, schedule, reviews) need to be lifted cleanly. Evidence: presenter's claim ("AI algorithms prefer content that is well structured"), no data. [4ddb9e4eaae6]
5. Cover intent breadth, not just one query. Match content to what the user actually wants across the funnel (informational to transactional), since covering more intent types raises the chance of being featured. Condition: applies when deciding which pages to build or rewrite for a topic cluster. Evidence: presenter's framing plus one example pairing (informational vs. transactional query), no measurement. [04d9f9f38982]
6. Reviews, UGC and social distribution feed AI visibility. Distributing content on social platforms and actively collecting reviews/UGC adds "freshness" and "diverse perspectives" that the source says AI engines value, plus it builds authenticity/trust with human users. Condition: applies to review collection, social distribution and on-site UGC display. Evidence: presenter's assertion, no measurement of the AI-visibility effect. [98beae980f22]
7. Authority signals (citations, expert quotes, statistics) build trust with both users and "AI algorithms." Condition: applies most where credibility affects citation and conversion, e.g. category guides, buying advice, health/specialty content. Evidence: presenter's assertion, no ranking proof; source explicitly frames it as unproven ("offers no proof or ranking data" per the card's own caveat). [9da08c76878]
8. GEO is a recurring loop, not a one-time project. Monitor how AI platforms structure and cite answers, track visibility/engagement benchmarks over time, and adapt content when a platform's format or citation behavior shifts. Condition: applies as an ongoing workstream, folded into existing SEO analytics cadence rather than replacing it. ## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Writing "giant fluff pieces" purely to raise keyword density instead of directly answering the query. [06e30e5bb046]
- Treating GEO as a one-time project instead of continuous monitoring and adaptation as AI platforms evolve. [d94670809e77]
- Skipping structure (no headings/bullets/schema), which the source says AI algorithms do not prefer. [4ddb9e4eaae6]
- Targeting only one intent level per topic instead of spanning informational through transactional, which narrows the chances of being featured. [04d9f9f38982]
- Relying on content quality alone without authority signals (citations, expert quotes, statistics), which the source says leaves trust-building on the table for both users and AI algorithms. [9da08c76878]
- In regulated categories (health, supplements), asserting claims without compliant sourcing — flagged as a risk in the source's own card caveats even though the transcript itself does not use the word "regulated." [9da08c76878]
