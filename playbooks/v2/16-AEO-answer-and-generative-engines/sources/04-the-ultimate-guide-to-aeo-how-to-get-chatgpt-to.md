## 1. What this source is for

A practitioner's plan for getting a brand or product named inside AI chat answers (ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, Claude, Meta AI, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode), not just ranked as a blue link. It explains the mechanics (RAG citation summarization vs. core-model training), gives a concrete on-site/off-site tactic list, a question-research method, a share-of-voice measurement approach, and a control-group experiment design so an operator can test claims rather than copy folklore. ## 2. Principles

1. **Share of voice beats rank #1.** In Google, the #1 blue link wins the click. In an LLM answer, the model summarizes many citations and names whichever brand is mentioned most often across those citations, even if your URL is the top individual citation. Condition: applies to "best X" and comparison-style answers built on RAG citation summarization, not to the model's own trained knowledge. Evidence: Smith's stated observation from client work (Webflow) plus his general model of how RAG answers get composed. [fe9e277451de]
2. **AEO is mostly SEO plus a citation-optimization layer, not a separate discipline.** The underlying technology (search plus an LLM summarizer) is similar across engines, and traditional SEO landing pages for high-volume terms "free-ride" into AI answers. Condition: true for the on-site, content-quality and technical-SEO parts of AEO; the head and the tail still behave differently between search and chat. Evidence: Smith's direct claim, framed partly as a rebuttal to vendors selling AEO as an entirely new discipline. [276cdec15a35] [77971a0d570d]
3. **Early-stage and low-authority stores can win AEO even when Google SEO is out of reach.** Google SEO needs domain authority that takes time to build, so Smith's standing advice to early startups is "don't do SEO yet." AEO does not have that authority gate: a single Reddit thread, YouTube video, or blog mention can get you cited within days. Condition: applies specifically to citation-based, long-tail visibility, not to competing for high-volume head terms. Evidence: Smith's stated change from his own historical SEO advice, illustrated with a brand-new YC company appearing in answers right after launch. [efe70bd32711]
4. **The tail is larger in chat than in search, and much of it has never been searched before.** Smith cites roughly 25 words per chat query versus about 6 for a Google search (he attributes the exact figures to a third party, likely Perplexity), and chat's follow-up-question format produces truly novel queries. Condition: relevant when deciding what specific product, feature, integration and use-case questions to write content for. Evidence: cited third-party word-count figures plus Smith's direct experience of "early stage companies who just launched some really specific... thing" showing up because they answer a question nobody else answered. [5440ba5b8b20]
5. **Content should pass an information-gain and typicality test, not a content-scoring-tool test.** Ask: did this page say something nobody else said (information gain), and how closely does it mirror what everyone else already said (typicality)? Typical, scored-to-match content is what causes the current "everyone rewrites everyone" problem in both Google and, Smith predicts, eventually in AI answers. Condition: applies to any page meant to be cited, especially buying guides and comparison content. Evidence: Smith's own framework, offered as his answer to "how would I solve the rewritten-content problem if I were Google or OpenAI." [b0dba8938d4e]
6. **100% unassisted AI content does not win citations today, and would break the system if it did.** Graphite's study found roughly 10 to 12% of cited/ranked content across Google and ChatGPT is AI-detected, about 90% is not. Smith also argues that if fully automated content did work, everyone would use it, derivatives would feed on derivatives ("model collapse" at the RAG layer), and answers would converge to one generic opinion. Condition: "AI-generated" here means no human in the loop; AI-assisted, human-edited content is treated as fine and as the likely future norm. Evidence: an internal Graphite study (Common Crawl sample, Surfer SEO AI detector, ~8% measured false-positive rate) plus a cited academic "model collapse" paper on training-side collapse, extended by Smith's own reasoning to the RAG/citation side. [91dd48ff3974] [61fbe7ae9c54] [de7e91d4d30f]
7. **Reddit is heavily cited because it is a trusted, community-policed source, and it punishes obvious spam.** Both Google and ChatGPT deliberately weight Reddit/Quora/UGC because they want authentic content; fake-account farms get banned and their comments deleted. The working tactic is a real, disclosed employee giving one useful, non-promotional answer in a relevant thread. Condition: applies to Reddit/Quora-style UGC citation building specifically, not to owned content. Evidence: Smith's description of Webflow's approach (employees commenting under their own name and employer) and his claim that even five good comments "could be great." [c3a578a68afc]
8. **Most controllable AEO work happens in the RAG/retrieval layer, not in the model's trained weights.** The core model does next-word prediction from its training corpus; RAG performs a live search and summarizes it, and that summarization step produces citations. Influencing the trained model directly is described as extremely hard and slow (effects possibly a year out) and not where most operators should spend time. Condition: applies to prioritizing where to invest optimization effort. Evidence: Smith's explanation of the two-layer architecture, offered as his own working model rather than a technical spec of any single vendor. [d1f4e5dbbbbd]
9. **AI referral traffic is small in volume today but converts much better, and standard analytics undercounts it.** Users arrive pre-qualified after a multi-turn conversation, but they frequently open a new tab and search or type the brand/domain directly, which analytics then misattributes as branded search or direct traffic. Condition: applies specifically to measuring the value of this channel against last-touch referral reports. Evidence: Webflow's reported 6x LLM-vs-Google conversion-rate difference and 8% of signups from LLM traffic, both single-company figures. [d3293f344c01]
10. **Blocking AI indexing entirely forfeits the channel to competitors; blocking AI training is a separate, available choice.** It is not really optional whether to participate: if your site is not retrievable, you cannot be cited while competitors who are retrievable will be. But a site can typically allow the retrieval/index bot while disallowing the training bot via robots.txt or a vendor tool. Condition: applies to robots.txt and bot-access policy decisions. Evidence: Smith's direct recommendation and reasoning; bot names and available toggles are not detailed in the transcript. 11. **Every couple of years a "search is dying" panic cycle repeats, and each new channel has added to the pie rather than taken Google's slice.** TikTok search, Instagram and Facebook search-and-discover behavior were each treated the same way; Smith cites a stated claim from Google's VP of search that publisher referral traffic is flat to slightly up, not down. ## 3. Procedures

### A. Ethan Smith's actionable AEO start-up sequence (as given, in order)
1. Figure out which questions to target. Pull your own and competitors' paid-search "money terms" (what are they bidding on). Paste the keyword list into ChatGPT and ask it to turn the keywords into the questions people would actually ask; Smith says it does this well. [bf1d8a839388]
2. Also mine questions that never show up in search data: sales-call questions, customer-support tickets, and Reddit threads in your category. Assume search-adjacent question frequency roughly tracks the keyword's search volume, but accept there is no volume truth set for pure-chat questions yet. [bf1d8a839388]
3. Put the resulting question set into an answer-tracking (AEO) tool to get a baseline before making any changes. [f758425ed01b]
4. Identify who is currently cited as the answer for your target questions, broken into citation groups: your own site, video (YouTube/Vimeo), UGC (Reddit/Quora), tier-1 affiliates (e.g. Dotdash Meredith titles), tier-2 affiliates, and blogs. Build a separate strategy per group; they behave differently. [77971a0d570d]
5. Look at what page type currently dominates the citations for a given question set (listicle, category page, article, or tool page) and build your own version of that type rather than inventing a new format. [a082ffa8e1fc]
6. Make that page rank/get cited by answering every follow-up and sub-topic question in the theme, not just the head question; a competitor who answers a sub-question you skip is more likely to be cited for it. [77971a0d570d]
7. Work the off-site groups with a tactic per group: paying an affiliate (e.g. a Forbes-style "best X" placement) is expensive but easy and controllable; a YouTube/Vimeo video is comparatively easy because there is no community that can veto it; Reddit is cheap and effective but not guaranteed to stay live since the community moderates it.
8. Design and run a controlled experiment on the interventions (see Procedure B) rather than assuming any tactic worked.
9. Staff it: keep on-site AEO with the existing SEO team/agency; hand the off-site video and Reddit/community work to a separate community-generalist marketing person, since Smith says most SEO people are not strong at that work. [77971a0d570d]

### B. Control-group AEO experiment
1. Build a large question set; Smith's own example uses 200 questions. [0408383723f2]
2. Split it into a test group and a control group (his example: 100 and 100).
3. Turn on answer tracking for all of them and let it run a couple of weeks before making any change, to see baseline variance.
4. Leave the control group completely untouched for the whole experiment.
5. Intervene on the test group with one tactic at a time (e.g. Reddit comments only, or a YouTube/Vimeo video only, or a paid affiliate placement only); consider separate buckets per tactic rather than mixing them.
6. Wait a further couple of weeks, then compare the change in the test group's tracked metric (average rank, share of voice) against the change in the control group over the same window.
7. Treat only the tactic whose test group moved while the matched control group did not as a working result.
8. Reproduce the result: rerun the same test more than once, or replicate across another comparable question set, before adopting it as a rule. Smith explicitly frames this as an academic-research habit and warns that most SEO/AEO "best practices" were never actually tested this way. [0408383723f2]

### C. Authentic Reddit citation building
1. Find a Reddit thread that is already part of a citation you want to appear in (i.e. one that comes up when you ask your target question in an LLM), not a random thread.
2. Comment from a real, named account: state who you are and where you work.
3. Give one genuinely useful piece of information relevant to the thread's actual question; do not lead with a promotional pitch.
4. Do not scale this with multiple fake accounts, auto-posting, or self-upvoting; Smith says this is detected and the accounts/comments get banned or deleted.
5. Do not expect to guarantee persistence: the community can still remove a disclosed, honest comment, so treat this as easy but not fully controllable. [c3a578a68afc]

### D. Help center AEO
1. Move the help center from a subdomain to a subdirectory of the main site; Smith says subdomains underperform for this. `[verify UI: this depends on your CMS/help-desk platform's subdirectory support]`
2. Fix internal cross-linking between help-center articles; Smith says this is usually poor and should connect related articles into a crawlable cluster.
3. Audit head-term coverage (you likely already have articles for common questions) versus tail coverage (you likely do not have articles for obscure integration/use-case questions).
4. Source the missing tail questions from sales calls and customer-support tickets, and write plain answers even where demand looks close to zero, since a unique citation is the win, not traffic volume. Smith's own example: "which meeting-transcription tool integrates with Looker" has no direct answer, but a workaround via Otter's Zapier integration into BigQuery is a real, publishable answer nobody had written. [6ed9256ad5d8] [a43cf3acdd9f]
5. Optionally open a community Q&A on top of the help center so users surface tail questions you would not have anticipated yourself. [6ed9256ad5d8]
6. Common help-desk platforms (Smith names none directly as tested, but the transcript raises Zendesk and Intercom as likely candidates) should already support subdirectory hosting and internal linking; verify this for your own platform rather than assuming it. `[verify UI]` [6ed9256ad5d8]

### E. Bot access split (index yes, train separately)
1. Identify that different crawlers use different user agents for training versus retrieval/indexing.
2. In robots.txt, disallow the training-purpose bot and allow the indexing/retrieval bot for each AI vendor you want to be citable by. `[verify UI: exact user-agent strings per vendor change over time]` `[web check: current OpenAI/Google/Perplexity/Anthropic bot user agents and their training-vs-index distinction]`
3. Alternatively, use a vendor tool or app that exposes this as a toggle rather than raw robots.txt; the transcript mentions such a tool existing but does not name or verify one usable today. `[web check: current tools that separate "allow indexing" from "block training"]`
4. Do not block indexing entirely as a default risk-averse move; Smith frames that as forfeiting the channel outright. [c722163c16a0]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check: are you actually retrievable by AI crawlers?** How: review robots.txt for AI training/indexing bot rules. Good: indexing/retrieval bots allowed, training bots blocked if you want that split. Bad: everything blocked (you cannot be cited) or everything allowed with no policy decision made. Next: implement Procedure E. [c722163c16a0] `[web check: current bot names]`
- **Check: what page type currently gets cited for your target questions?** How: ask the target question on 2-3 AI surfaces and note whether the cited pages are listicles, category pages, articles or tool pages. Good: you have a page of that dominant type. Bad: you only have a different format (e.g. a blog post when the dominant type is a comparison table). Next: build the matching page type, per Procedure A step 5. [a082ffa8e1fc]
- **Check: does your content pass the information-gain and typicality test?** How: read the draft and ask "did we say something nobody else said" and "does this look like a rewrite of the top few competitor pages." Good: original data, specs, testing or expertise present. Bad: it reads as typical, scored-to-match copy. Next: add first-party data, real testing, or a named expert quote before publishing. [b0dba8938d4e]
- **Check: is your help center subdirectory or subdomain, and is it internally linked?** How: inspect the URL structure and crawl the help-center cluster's internal links. Good: subdirectory, richly cross-linked. Bad: subdomain, isolated articles. Next: run Procedure D. [6ed9256ad5d8]
- **Check: are you measuring AI-driven traffic with the right method for your business model?** How: for a B2B-style, non-clickable answer flow, compare last-touch referral traffic against answer-tracking share of voice and a post-conversion "how did you hear about us" question; for a commerce flow with clickable/shoppable answer cards, last-touch referral traffic is a reasonable read on its own. Good: a tracking method matched to whether your answers are clickable in your category. Bad: relying on last-touch referral alone for a non-clickable, high-consideration category. Next: add the missing measurement layer. [7a7c5e12d8bc] [d3293f344c01]
- **Check: did a tactic actually work, or did the metric just drift?** How: compare the test group's movement to a matched, untouched control group over the same window, more than once. Good: reproducible lift in test vs. flat control. Bad: a single before/after reading with no control group. Next: rerun Procedure B before trusting the result. [0408383723f2]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Building hundreds of fake Reddit accounts to auto-post and self-upvote a product; this is detected, and accounts and comments get banned or deleted. [c3a578a68afc]
- Publishing bulk, unedited, 100% AI-generated content expecting it to rank or get cited; the source's own study found this does not correlate with current citations, and argues it would collapse into a single homogenized answer if it scaled (model collapse). [61fbe7ae9c54] [91dd48ff3974] [de7e91d4d30f]
- Treating AEO as an entirely separate discipline from SEO and buying a separate, proprietary toolchain on that premise; the source frames this framing as partly a sales tactic by vendors. [276cdec15a35]
- Judging channel impact by last-touch referral clicks alone in a B2B or other non-clickable-answer context, which systematically undercounts AI-driven traffic (users often reopen a new tab and search the brand directly). [7a7c5e12d8bc] [d3293f344c01]
- Trusting a single before/after change with no control group; the source states plainly that most SEO/AEO "best practices" in circulation were never actually tested this way and get repeated as folklore. [0408383723f2]
- Blocking AI crawlers from indexing your site outright as a defensive default; this forfeits the citation channel to competitors who remain indexable. [c722163c16a0]
- Paying commodity, keyword-tracker-equivalent prices inflated by AEO's "mysterious" framing; the source's own sanity check is that nobody would pay $50,000 for plain keyword tracking, yet similar commodity tracking is being sold at high prices under the AEO label. [56c995c391df]
- Reacting to "Google/SEO is dying" headlines by defunding search; the source frames this as a recurring, historically wrong panic cycle (TikTok search, Instagram, Facebook were each treated the same way). [e9c9ed5464d2]
- Spreading a fixed content budget thin across many landing pages instead of concentrating it on the small number that can actually win, given the source's 1-in-20-pages-drive-85%-of-traffic pattern. [15d85f61b2c8]
