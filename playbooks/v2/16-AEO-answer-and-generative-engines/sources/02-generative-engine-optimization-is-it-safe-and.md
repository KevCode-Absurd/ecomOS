## 1. What this source is for

Helps an operator decide whether a proposed "GEO" (generative engine optimization) or "AEO" (answer engine optimization) tactic is safe to run, or whether it risks a Google penalty. Gives a vendor-vetting question set and red/yellow-flag checklist for hiring a GEO agency or tool. Lays out the source's view of which tactics are safe (mostly ordinary SEO plus targeted off-site placement) versus which are high-risk (scaled AI content) or ethically gray (paid/sponsored listicles, fabricated press releases). Gives concrete, if fast-aging, techniques for seeing what an AI assistant actually searched for a given prompt.

## 2. Principles

1. GEO is mostly SEO. The source's rough estimate: 70-80% of GEO effort should be ordinary, sound SEO (technical health, product/service/location pages) before spending on off-site AI-specific tactics. Evidence: source's stated opinion, not a measured study. `[79bd30ddf57b]`
2. Mass-scaling AI-written content to chase "query fan-out" keyword variants is the single riskiest, most-promoted GEO tactic. Sites the source has watched go from ~100 to 1,000+ blog posts in weeks get an initial traffic spike, then a decline ("Mount AI" / "rank and tank"). Google's own guidelines name this "scaled content abuse." Condition: applies to bulk, templated, keyword-variant content; the source does not extend this to normal editorial content growth. `[d6f727b1e8c0]`
3. AI assistants (ChatGPT, Perplexity, etc.) mostly retrieve pages for their background "fan-out" searches through search-engine APIs (Google, Bing). This means ranking in traditional organic search — not raw content volume — is what makes a page eligible to be retrieved and cited. `[36e3c790fd2d]`
4. Influencing an LLM's baked-in training data (as opposed to what it retrieves via live web search) is slow, only partially demonstrated, and requires repeating the same corrected claim across many channels (own site, third-party articles, founder social video, PR) over a long period. Source's own tracked example took about 2 years to move a stated fact from "9+ months" to "6 months," not to the intended "3 weeks." Source explicitly says it has not seen a truly verifiable case study of anyone reliably getting new information into training data. `[6b58426c3650]` `[5f5e69e87c2f]`
5. Citation counts and automated AI-visibility dashboards are easy to inflate and often meaningless: one URL can be cited across near-infinite phrasing variants, and trackers can report "useless data" when the model never actually ran a live web search for the tracked prompt. Condition: applies to any vendor or in-house report using raw citation/mention counts as the success metric. `[28b2634c43eb]`
6. A citation is not the same as a mention. Being linked from an AI answer while a competitor is the one actually named as "best" is not a win for the brand. `[96e957d20941]`
7. GEO is a company-wide function, not a single hire's job. What the AI repeats depends on product quality, executive/brand reputation, PR messaging, review-platform standing (Trustpilot, G2, Glassdoor), affiliate relationships and web content together — no amount of Reddit or listicle work fixes visibility if the underlying product or reputation is bad. `[304fb1f93013]`
8. The source's durability test for any tactic: does this give a user who lands on the page — via Google or an AI search engine — the best possible experience? Tactics that fail this test (spammy scaled comparison pages, self-serving listicles) are the ones assistants are already working around. `[605820cb3efb]`
9. Bottom-of-funnel, high-intent pages (product, category, scenario, service pages) generally beat broad top-of-funnel informational content for AI citation now, because AI overviews already answer "what is X" directly and high-intent terms are the least searched and so the least competitive, even at high CPC. Condition/exception: brands with existing authority and audience can still win with opinionated top-of-funnel content that introduces a genuinely new point of view; most smaller brands cannot. `[efa046d88d7c]`
10. Screen any aggressive or spammy tactic by time horizon: acceptable for a "churn and burn" site built for short-term gain; not acceptable for a real business with a 10-15 year plan, since a Google algorithm penalty can suppress a domain for years. `[7844505735de]`
11. Even a bare logo or link placement beneath an AI overview, without a click, is a brand impression comparable to a paid billboard or TV-ad impression — relevant when organic click-through on top-of-funnel pages has structurally fallen. `[816ae1a10dd0]`
12. Observationally (per the source, not a stated rule), Google's AI mode is increasingly linking straight to brand homepages, category and product pages rather than to listicles, which changes what deserves optimization effort relative to blog content. `[60bd75ec7279]`

## 3. Procedures

### A. Audit a GEO vendor's or your own scaled-content case study before trusting it
1. Do not accept an aggregate/blended traffic chart as proof a tactic is safe.
2. Split the case study's keywords into difficulty bands (source uses Ahrefs difficulty > 30 as the harder-keyword cut). `[verify UI: metric and threshold are tool-specific and can change]`
3. Compare trend lines for the two bands separately — look for the harder, more commercially valuable keywords declining while easy keywords still prop up the total.
4. Ask whether the traffic increase produced more organic or LLM-referred revenue; if not, the source says it is not a win regardless of the chart. `[needs brand data: revenue by traffic source/keyword]`
5. If you hold an SEO tool subscription, verify the pattern yourself rather than trusting a vendor's screenshot.
`[7cbba7101a4d]`

### B. Vet a GEO/AEO vendor before hiring
1. Ask: how is GEO different from SEO? (Vague/hype answer is a signal.)
2. Ask: how long have you operated, and when did you start in optimization? Source flags companies that appeared only after ChatGPT launched, versus ones operating since 2015-2017.
3. Ask for recent case studies, then ask specifically how those clients are performing *now* and whether the results held up over time — not just what was achieved at the time.
4. Ask their stance on off-site SEO (affiliates, third-party listicles, review sites) and whether they have a video/YouTube/social strategy.
5. Ask directly: are you going to put up hundreds of blog posts? A "yes" is treated as a red flag.
6. Ask them to explain their method in plain English. Inability to do so is itself disqualifying per the source.
7. Run three "grifter probes": ask if they recommend an `llms.txt` file, a dedicated "AI info page," or "chunking" all content. Source treats enthusiastic yes answers to these as leaning red flag. `[web check: whether these are now legitimate, evolving standards rather than fads]`
8. Check jargon comprehension: take confusing vendor terminology and run it through an AI chat tool yourself to see if it holds up in plain language.
`[5872777fb613]` `[3be904342c0a]`

### C. Detect whether an AI answer came from a live web search or stale training data
1. Search the same prompt in the free version of ChatGPT.
2. Open a separate incognito tab and run the identical prompt.
3. Also run it in your paid/logged-in account.
4. Compare all three answers side by side, including presence/absence of citations.
5. Repeat a few times, varying sentence structure between runs, and note how much the answer shifts.
`[5786137d7326]` `[verify UI: behavior differs by account/session state and changes over time]`

### D. See the literal background searches (query fan-out) ChatGPT runs for a prompt `[verify UI: undocumented browser technique, may break at any time]`
1. Send a prompt to ChatGPT and wait for it to show "searching the web."
2. Right-click the page and choose Inspect; open the Network tab in dev tools.
3. Copy the part of the current URL that comes after the forward slash following "c" (i.e., the conversation ID segment).
4. Paste that string into the Network tab's filter box and refresh the page.
5. Look for the entry with orange brackets containing the copied string.
6. Open that entry's Response tab and search it for the word "queries."
7. Read the literal sub-queries the model ran.
(Perplexity shows its fan-out searches directly in its UI, without this workaround. `[verify UI]`)
`[f587896fb244]`

### E. Correct a persistent inaccurate brand fact across AI answers
1. State the corrected fact plainly and explicitly on your own website first (the model repeats what it sees, so start at the most controllable source).
2. Distribute the same corrected claim beyond the website: third-party articles, founder social video addressing the misconception directly, other channels. Repetition across channels matters more than a single update.
3. Set expectations for a slow, partial result — the source's own tracked example took about 2 years to move a number partway to the target, not all the way.
4. Re-run a fixed set of brand questions periodically with browsing controlled where possible; record model, date, and cited evidence each time. A changed answer does not prove retraining occurred.
5. Update the likely underlying sources feeding the stale answer: G2, Reddit, Trustpilot, local directories, local board-of-trade listings.
`[6b58426c3650]` `[5f5e69e87c2f]`

### F. Get listed in the third-party listicles AI already cites (instead of building your own)
1. Use your GEO/SEO tool's "sources" panel to see which third-party URLs are influencing AI answers for your priority prompts. `[verify UI]`
2. Skip listicles owned by direct competitors — the source will not ask a competitor to be added and ignores such inbound requests.
3. Identify durable, high-authority publishers in your vertical (source's examples: Healthline, WebMD, Sleepopolis for mattresses, large credit-card affiliates) and pursue their affiliate programs, even at a higher cost than you'd like.
4. For smaller or niche blogs that turn out to influence many of your priority prompts, email them directly and ask what paid inclusion would cost.
5. Run a backlink/link-intersection analysis on competitors to see which listicles and guest posts they appear in, then pursue equivalent placements — bigger publications for bigger companies, cheaper/local sites for smaller or local players.
6. Coordinate with whoever runs your affiliate program directly, since it often sits outside the SEO team's control.
`[07e818713c36]`

### G. Run a cheap press release to shape an AI-visible narrative
1. Pick a low-cost wire service (source names AB Newswire — verify current name/spelling and pricing `[web check]`).
2. Put the exact target phrase in the release's title, subheading, and first sentence.
3. Publish for reputation management, company announcements, or local sponsorships — the source explicitly warns against publishing fabricated claims (see caveats).
4. Expect syndication to partner news sites (source names Yahoo News and Globe and Mail-type outlets at the time — availability changes `[web check]`).
`[8a54ade0eeff]`

### H. Do GEO research before touching any page
1. Run your target prompts through the LLM(s) your buyers actually use.
2. Extract the query fan-out for each (visible directly in Perplexity; via the network-tab method in ChatGPT — Procedure D).
3. Note which sites are consistently cited for each fan-out variant.
4. If a big retailer or review site is consistently cited for a term you want, prioritize getting your brand mentioned on that cited site over trying to outrank it with your own page.
5. Ask your internal or agency SEO team for fan-out data on your main keywords; some prompt-tracking tools now surface fan-out automatically, or it can be extracted manually.
6. Repeat this analysis periodically since fan-out behavior and cited sources change.
`[dd5f18c44b74]`

### I. Reuse mined fan-out language in on-page copy
1. Observe the literal search phrasing the LLM uses (Procedure D or Perplexity's visible fan-out) — note it typically differs from the user's original prompt wording.
2. Put that exact phrasing into the page's title tag, URL slug, H1, and the opening sentence.
3. Build supporting SEO authority (links, shares) to that page so it's more likely to surface when the LLM's own web search runs.
`[f587896fb244]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Scaling AI-generated content to chase query fan-out keyword variants — the single most-warned-against mistake. `[d6f727b1e8c0]`
- Trusting a vendor's or agency's case study at face value without breaking down keyword difficulty or asking how the featured client is doing *now*. `[7cbba7101a4d]` `[5872777fb613]`
- Treating citation counts or "mentions" growth as real visibility without checking whether it maps to revenue, or whether it's a genuine mention vs. a bare, uncredited link. `[28b2634c43eb]` `[96e957d20941]`
- Paying for a generic Reddit-mentions service without verifying which threads actually get cited, or whether the target subreddit even tolerates brand mentions. `[28b9ca1fa2478ff92669f28b]`
- Emailing competitors to ask for inclusion in their listicle — source says this does not work and personally ignores such inbound requests. `[07e818713c36]`
- Publishing scaled comparison pages/listicles naming competitors nobody actually searches for, purely because a GEO tool recommends scaling that format. `[605820cb3efb]`
- Assuming that entering "LLM training data" through content volume is achievable or proven — the source states he has not seen a truly verifiable case study of this. `[6b58426c3650]`
- Relying on a single review platform for AI citations, since platforms can drop out of results (Reddit's temporary disappearance from OpenAI's citations is the cited example). `[138fd5c7b44a]`
- Hiding pricing entirely and letting the model invent a number or scrape one from Reddit/G2. `[5afb1c4861f4]`
- Treating a vendor's use of jargon like "llms.txt," "AI info page," or "chunking" as a sign of sophistication rather than a possible red flag. `[3be904342c0a]`
- Ignoring the 2-3-year downside horizon of an aggressive tactic because of a short-term traffic spike. `[7844505735de]`
- Believing a blended, aggregate traffic chart proves a scaled-content strategy is safe when the harder, more valuable keywords underneath are already declining. `[7cbba7101a4d]`

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- The whole source is one practitioner's opinion plus personal anecdotes and unnamed case studies — there are no cited external studies, and most brand names are deliberately withheld by the guest ("I won't name the tool," "not going to name the company").
- A mid-roll segment promotes the host's own paid course ("Compact Keywords"); its claims (average page length, course length, a customer testimonial) are advertising copy, not independent GEO findings, and are excluded from the rules above.
- The source is explicit that he has not seen a verifiable case study of anyone reliably entering LLM training data through content; the "9 months → 6 months" example is presented as his strongest evidence and is still only a partial, slow result.
- The source names two tactics as effective but explicitly **not recommended or condoned**: sponsoring/buying listicle placements on third-party news sites, and making your own listicles on other people's websites. He flags that disclosure and legal exposure depend on industry and jurisdiction — this is a real ethical and compliance line the source draws for himself, not a blanket endorsement.
- **Conflict with common ecommerce content-marketing advice**: the source argues broad top-of-funnel informational content ("what is X" articles) is now largely wasted effort for most small-to-mid brands, since AI overviews already answer such questions directly. This runs against long-standing SEO/content-marketing advice to publish broad informational content to build topical authority. The source frames this as his opinion and carves out an explicit exception for brands with existing audience/authority who can introduce a genuinely new point of view.
- The source is also skeptical of the entire GEO/AEO vendor category's measurement claims, calling citation-based "AI visibility" metrics "dangerous" and largely vendor-driven — this conflicts with any client-facing reporting built primarily around raw citation counts.
- No numeric definition of "safe" is given; the test offered ("does this help the searcher?" / "what will this do to me in 2-3 years?") is qualitative.
- No stated benchmark for how much content, how many press releases, or how many affiliate placements are "enough" — the source repeatedly says "put in the time" and "it depends."
- Transcription artifacts to be aware of before quoting this source elsewhere: "Chachi PT / Chachi BT / chatbt" = ChatGPT; "L&M" = LLM; "Simrush" likely = Semrush; "AB Newswire" spelling/name should be verified. `[web check: exact product and service names before using in a client-facing document]`
- All platform-behavior claims (ChatGPT's network-tab fan-out trick, Perplexity's fan-out display, the `site:` fan-out behavior, Reddit's presence in OpenAI's citations, the stated August 2025 model cutoff) are point-in-time observations on fast-changing consumer AI products and must be re-verified before acting on them.
