## 1. What this source is for

It gives a 5-step starter framework for getting a brand cited inside AI assistant answers (ChatGPT, Google AI overviews, Perplexity, Claude), rather than ranked for clicks: understand how AI search retrieves content (query fan-out), find your brand's citation gaps against competitors, pick keywords by whether an AI overview already satisfies them, structure and format content so AI can extract and cite it, and check that AI crawlers can even reach the site. It closes with a simple triage: technical check first, then measurement, then match your biggest brand-gap to the right fix (content, mentions, or video).

## 2. Principles

1. **AI assistants do not search once; they fan a single prompt into dozens of parallel sub-queries and synthesize a response from many sources.** Condition: any AI assistant search (ChatGPT, Perplexity, etc.), not classic Google search. Evidence: reported example of ChatGPT's deep research mode running 420 searches for one prompt about a red phone case. [4b61146b]
2. **To be relevant to a prompt, you must cover the whole topic cluster the prompt implies, not just the head keyword.** Condition: any topic where buyers ask AI multi-part questions. Evidence: the Japan-trip example, where one prompt fans into "best neighborhoods to stay in Tokyo," "November weather in Kyoto," "is the Japan Rail Pass worth it." [4b61146b]
3. **Three factors drive AI's retrieval probability: consensus, freshness, authority.** Consensus: the more places say the same thing about you, the more AI believes and repeats it. Freshness: AI-cited content averaged 25.7% fresher than pages ranking in classic Google results. Authority: 76% of AI overview citations came from pages already ranking in Google's top 10. Evidence: attributed to "Ahrefs research" in the transcript, no link given [web check: source study]. [4b61146b]
4. **Traditional SEO is the foundation of AI SEO.** Condition: this follows directly from the authority finding above (most AI citations already rank top-10 on Google). If you already rank well, you start with an advantage. [4b61146b]
5. **Run a brand gap analysis before doing anything else, to see where you stand versus competitors across Google, AI results and the web.** The source names Ahrefs Brand Radar as the tool: enter your domain, add competitors, get a comparative view. [5ce00206]
6. **Not all keywords are worth chasing for a click; some are worth chasing for an unlinked brand mention instead.** Condition: informational queries that an AI overview already answers in full. Evidence: AI overviews appear on about 21% of all keywords but on about 58% of question queries, and 99.9% of keywords that trigger AI overviews are informational in intent. [3a369bfc]
7. **Content length has no correlation with getting cited by AI.** Evidence: analysis of 174,000 pages cited in AI overviews found near-zero correlation between word count and citation; over half of cited pages were under 1,000 words. Condition: this is about being cited in an AI answer, not about ranking classically, where length effects may differ (not addressed by the source). [e3c82eeb]
8. **Freshness is high-leverage for citation.** Evidence: for ChatGPT specifically, 89.7% of its top cited pages were updated in 2025, and 76% were refreshed within the last 30 days. The source states content untouched for 6 months is already at a disadvantage. [e3c82eeb]
9. **Format matters: list, comparison and review formats are disproportionately cited.** Evidence: 43.8% of all pages cited by ChatGPT are lists ("best X," "top X"), comparisons and reviews. [e3c82eeb]
10. **BLUF (Bottom Line Up Front): put the direct answer at the start of every section, not the backstory.** Reason given: both humans and AI weigh the beginning and end of a passage more heavily than the middle, so a point buried mid-paragraph can be missed by either. [5da2c2bb]
11. **Write atomic content: every section (H2/H3) should make full sense pulled out of context.** Reason given: AI chunks a page into fragments during retrieval and you cannot control where the cut falls, so context-dependent openers ("as mentioned above," "this tool") can lose meaning when isolated. [79e59016]
12. **Write entity-rich, not generic.** Example given: "This tool helps with SEO" becomes "Ahrefs Keywords Explorer helps you find keywords with low difficulty and high traffic potential." More specific named entities and relationships give AI more to work with when assembling an answer. [79e59016]
13. **Keep sentences simple and declarative: one idea per sentence, subject-verb-object.** Litmus test given: if a sentence takes two reads to understand, it is too complex and needs simplifying. This applies to both BLUF and atomic-content writing. [5da2c2bb] [79e59016]
14. **Branded web mentions (not backlinks) have the strongest correlation with AI overview visibility.** Evidence: a study of 75,000 brands found branded mentions correlated more strongly with AI visibility than backlinks, domain rating, or referring domains. Conclusion drawn: the single highest-leverage AEO action is getting your brand named on other people's pages, whether linked or not. [aaf30f11]
15. **There are 3 tiers of pages worth targeting for mentions, in order of difficulty and value: editorial (hardest, most valuable), UGC/community (Reddit named specifically as heavily cited by ChatGPT and a foundational LLM training source), and owned properties (YouTube, podcast, LinkedIn).** Community participation should be genuine engagement, not gaming. [aaf30f11]
16. **YouTube is the single highest-correlation lever the source studied for AI visibility.** Evidence: YouTube is described as the most cited domain in Google's AI overviews; YouTube mentions had a 0.737 correlation with ChatGPT visibility ("the strongest correlation of any factor we studied"); GPT-4 was trained on over a million hours of YouTube transcripts. [2130f7b9]
17. **On YouTube, target "search hits" (evergreen, consistently searched topics), not "viral hits" (a spike that fades).** Reason: search hits keep generating Google and YouTube search traffic month after month, and those are the videos AI is most likely to cite; viral videos spike then die once the interested audience is exhausted. [2130f7b9]
18. **Technical access is a precondition, not an optimization: if AI crawlers cannot reach your site, none of the content or mention work matters.** Framed as the first, 30-second check to do before any other AEO work. [c801b61f]
19. **Blocking is usually accidental, inherited from old robots.txt templates or default platform settings, not a deliberate choice.** Evidence: about 5.9% of 140 million websites analyzed were blocking GPTBot. Cloudflare's "instruct AI bot traffic with robots.txt" feature is enabled by default and can silently block GPTBot for Cloudflare-hosted sites. [c801b61f]
20. **AI's impact on a business is partly invisible to standard analytics, so 3 separate measurement approaches are needed together: AI referral traffic, AI bot activity on pages, and self-reported attribution.** No single one gives a complete picture; the source explicitly frames analytics-only measurement as insufficient for AI search. [98c8979b]
21. **AEO strategy should be sequenced, not scattered: technical check, then measurement setup, then match your single biggest brand-gap to the matching fix.** Content gap (AI doesn't associate you with a topic you should own) -> refresh outdated pages that used to rank, called the fastest win. Mention gap (competitors get recommended, you don't) -> pursue listicles, reviews, Reddit threads. Format gap (AI cites formats you don't produce, e.g. video) -> get on YouTube, yourself or via creators. [9ffb85f5]
22. **The overall model is SEO as foundation, with AEO layered on top as 4 additive workstreams: content, mentions, YouTube, technical.** The source states explicitly this is not one-size-fits-all; which workstream to prioritize depends on your own gap analysis. [9ffb85f5]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Understand and apply query fan-out (foundational, no separate action steps)
The source explains the mechanic but gives no explicit "do this" checklist here beyond principle 2: audit whether your content covers the sub-topics/objections/comparisons a real buyer prompt would fan into, not only the head keyword. [4b61146b]

### 3.2 Run a brand gap analysis
1. Open Ahrefs Brand Radar (or equivalent tool). [verify UI]
2. Enter your domain.
3. Add a couple of competitor domains.
4. Read the comparative overview of where your brand shows up vs competitors across Google, AI results and the web.
5. Apply the "mentioned but not cited" filter: AI responses that name your brand without linking your site. These are missed citation opportunities.
6. Add a competitor and filter for queries where they are mentioned and you are not. That is a competitor gap.
7. Look at specific topics to see whether AI associates them with your brand or a competitor's. That is a topic gap.
8. Write down all gaps found; do not try to fix everything at once. These gaps drive the prioritization step in 3.6.
The source gives no cadence for re-running this; the card recommends periodic re-runs since AI answers shift. [5ce00206]

### 3.3 Keyword and prompt research with the "AI Filter"
1. Prompt your AI assistant of choice with this exact template (source's wording): "I'm doing keyword research for my [type of] site which makes money through [revenue model]. My target audience are [these people]. Give me 10 seed keywords that are one to two words max and five plus modifiers that'll help me surface appropriate content formats that I can use in my keyword research. The seeds and modifiers should not share the same words."
2. Take the generated seed keywords into a keyword-ideas report in a keyword research tool (source uses Ahrefs Keywords Explorer) with the modifiers applied.
3. Vet the resulting keyword list on the usual factors: business potential, search intent, keyword difficulty.
4. Run each surviving keyword through the "AI Filter": Google the query and ask "can an AI overview fully satisfy the searcher?" If yes, the keyword may be a trap (nobody needs to click through).
5. For keywords that fail the filter (AI already satisfies them), do not drop them; retarget the goal from earning a click to earning an unlinked brand mention inside the AI response.
6. To find brand-mention opportunities specifically: go to Brand Radar, hover over the AI platform to research, click "others only" (queries where competitors are mentioned but you are not).
7. Filter that list for terms like best, top, versus, review, alternative to get a short list of brand-mention opportunities. [verify UI]
[3a369bfc]

### 3.4 Content creation for AI parsing (macro + micro)
Macro (what to write):
1. Do not assume length helps; do not default to long-form for citation purposes.
2. Prioritize list, "best X," "top X," comparison and review formats.
3. Set a refresh cadence; do not leave content untouched more than 6 months.
4. When refreshing, update the visible date, prices, specs and recommendations, not just cosmetic text.
5. Prioritize refreshing pages that used to rank but lost ranking due to becoming outdated; this is called out as the fastest win.

Micro (how to write each section), 4 principles in order:
1. BLUF: open every section with the direct answer, not the backstory.
2. Atomic content: edit every H2 section to stand alone and make sense out of context.
3. Entity-rich writing: name specific products, tools and relationships instead of generic references.
4. Simple and declarative: one idea per sentence, subject-verb-object; if a sentence needs two reads, simplify it.
[5da2c2bb] [79e59016] [e3c82eeb]

### 3.5 Earning mentions across 3 tiers
1. Tier 1 (editorial): identify industry publications, review sites (source names The Wirecutter, TechRadar as examples) and listicle/comparison blogs in your niche; pursue inclusion. Hardest to earn, most valuable.
2. Tier 2 (UGC): find Reddit, Quora and niche-forum threads where people ask questions your product or expertise can genuinely answer; contribute real answers. Join and contribute regularly to relevant communities rather than one-off posting.
3. Tier 3 (owned): build out YouTube channel, podcast, LinkedIn content, since these are all crawlable and indexable.
4. Reuse the same specific entities (product names, claims) consistently across site, mentions and video, to build the consensus signal AI weighs.
[aaf30f11]

### 3.6 YouTube AEO checklist
1. Decide to target "search hits" (evergreen, steadily searched topics) over "viral hits" (spike-and-fade).
2. To find proven search-hit topics: in Ahrefs Site Explorer, set the YouTube "watch" subfolder, open the organic keywords report, filter for keywords ranking in the top 3, and add terms related to your niche. [verify UI]
3. Make the video, then apply this video SEO checklist:
   a. Put the target keyword in the title.
   b. Put the keyword within the first couple of lines of the description.
   c. Add timestamps for YouTube chapters.
   d. Actually say the keyword out loud in the video's audio (Google indexes audio/transcript content).
4. Work to retain viewer attention through the video, since retention is described as supporting the video's ranking across many search queries.
5. If you do not produce video yourself, pursue brand mentions on YouTube via sponsorships or creator outreach as an alternative.
[2130f7b9]

### 3.7 Technical AEO check (30-second check)
1. Go to yourdomain.com/robots.txt directly in a browser. [verify UI]
2. Look for any "Disallow" rule listed next to GPTBot, OAI Search Bot, ClaudeBot or Google Extended.
3. If any of those are disallowed, that crawler is blocked from the site.
4. If the site is on Cloudflare, separately check Cloudflare's "instruct AI bot traffic with robots.txt" setting, since it is enabled by default and can block GPTBot without the site owner having chosen to. [verify UI]
5. Override any inherited or default disallow rules found.
6. Re-check robots.txt after making changes to confirm the fix took effect.
7. Do this before investing effort in content or mention work, since blocked crawlers waste that effort.
[c801b61f]

### 3.8 Measuring AEO
1. Set up AI referral traffic tracking: track sessions that click through from ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude or other AI platforms. Options given: a custom GA4 regex channel for AI referral domains (source says its own AEO course covers this setup), or install Ahrefs Web Analytics, which the source says has this built in and is free. [verify UI]
2. Know the limitation: not all platforms pass referral data properly (source's example: ChatGPT's source links track, but in-content links on paid ChatGPT accounts do not), so treat any number as an undercount, useful for direction and relative comparison, not precision.
3. Set up AI bot activity tracking: monitor which citation bots (crawlers) hit which pages. Source suggests the Cloudflare integration in Ahrefs Web Analytics, said to work on Cloudflare's free plan. [verify UI]
4. Treat repeated bot hits on a specific page as a signal that page is likely being used as an AI source.
5. Add self-reported attribution: place a "How did you hear about us?" question in the sign-up flow, at checkout, or as a post-purchase survey.
6. Use self-reported attribution to catch AI-driven awareness that standard analytics would misattribute to "Direct" or "Organic."
7. Set all 3 measurement pieces up from day one, before or alongside content/mention/video work, so you can judge whether that work is moving AI visibility.
[98c8979b]

### 3.9 Build a custom AEO strategy (the closing sequence)
1. Run the 30-second technical check first (3.7); a blocked crawler undermines everything else.
2. Set up measurement (3.8): either the GA4 AI-traffic channel or a tool with it built in, plus the "How did you hear about us?" question.
3. Look at the biggest gap from the brand gap analysis (3.2) and match it to the matching fix:
   a. AI doesn't associate you with topics you should own -> content problem; start by refreshing pages that used to rank but became outdated (fastest win).
   b. Competitors keep getting recommended and you don't -> mentions problem; pursue listicles, reviews and Reddit threads you are absent from.
   c. AI is citing formats you don't produce (e.g. video) -> get on YouTube, either creating content yourself or partnering with creators to amplify your brand.
4. Treat this as ongoing, not one-and-done: re-check gaps and repeat.
[9ffb85f5]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is your content covering the whole topic, or just the head keyword?** Check: list the sub-queries a buyer's prompt would fan into (comparisons, fit, use-case, objections) and see which ones your site actually answers. Bad: only the main keyword is covered. Next: build out category pages, buying guides and PDP sections for the missing sub-topics (3.1, 3.4). [4b61146b]
- **Where are you mentioned but not cited, and where are competitors winning?** Check: run the brand gap analysis (3.2) in Ahrefs Brand Radar or equivalent. Good: few gaps, strong topic association. Bad: many "mentioned but not cited," "competitor gap," or "topic gap" results. Next: prioritize the single biggest gap and match it to content, mentions or YouTube (3.9). [5ce00206]
- **Is a keyword worth a click-focused push or a mention-focused push?** Check: Google the query, look at the AI overview, ask if it fully satisfies the searcher. Good (click-worthy): AI overview does not fully answer it. Bad/trap: AI overview fully answers it and it's informational. Next: for trap keywords, shift the KPI from ranking/click to unlinked brand mention (3.3). [3a369bfc]
- **Is content structured for AI extraction?** Check each section (H2) in isolation: does it open with the answer (BLUF)? Does it make sense with no surrounding context (atomic)? Are entities named specifically, not generically? Bad: backstory-first openers, sections that depend on "as mentioned above," generic references like "this tool." Next: rewrite per 3.4 micro checklist. [5da2c2bb] [79e59016]
- **Is content fresh enough to be cited?** Check last-updated date against the 6-month disadvantage threshold. Bad: untouched over 6 months, especially on pages that used to rank. Next: refresh visible date, prices, specs and recommendations; prioritize pages that lost ranking due to going stale (3.4). [e3c82eeb]
- **Are you present across the 3 mention tiers?** Check for presence in editorial listicles/reviews, in relevant Reddit/forum threads, and on your own YouTube/podcast/LinkedIn. Bad: absent from tier 1 and tier 2 entirely. Next: pursue listicle inclusion, answer real questions in communities, build owned video/audio content (3.5). [aaf30f11]
- **Are you building search hits or viral hits on YouTube?** Check whether videos target evergreen, steadily searched topics vs one-off trend spikes. Bad: relying on viral spikes that die once the interested audience is exhausted. Next: use the Ahrefs Site Explorer YouTube-subfolder + top-3-ranking filter method to find proven search-hit topics, then apply the video SEO checklist (3.6). [2130f7b9]
- **Are AI crawlers blocked?** Check yourdomain.com/robots.txt for Disallow rules against GPTBot, OAI Search Bot, ClaudeBot, Google Extended; separately check Cloudflare's AI-bot-traffic setting if hosted there. Bad: any of those crawlers disallowed. Good: none disallowed. Next: override the rule/setting and re-check (3.7). Do this before any other AEO investment. [c801b61f]
- **Is AI-driven traffic and awareness actually visible in your reporting?** Check whether an AI-referral GA4 channel or equivalent tool exists, whether bot-activity tracking exists, and whether a "how did you hear about us" question is live at signup/checkout/post-purchase. Bad: none of the 3 in place, all AI-driven signups showing up as "Direct" or "Organic." Next: set up all 3 (3.8). [98c8979b]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Assuming AI assistants work like a Google results list, one search in, one set of links out. [4b61146b]
2. Optimizing for a single head keyword instead of covering the full topic a real prompt would fan into. [4b61146b]
3. Chasing a click-through goal on informational keywords an AI overview already fully answers, instead of shifting to an unlinked-mention goal. [3a369bfc]
4. Writing long articles on the assumption that length helps AI citation. [e3c82eeb]
5. Leaving content untouched for 6+ months, especially pages that used to rank. [e3c82eeb]
6. Writing section openers that depend on prior context ("as mentioned above," "this tool") that lose meaning when AI extracts that section in isolation. [79e59016]
7. Writing generically instead of naming specific entities, tools and relationships. [79e59016]
8. Chasing backlinks and domain rating as the primary lever while ignoring branded mentions, which the source's cited study found more strongly correlated with AI visibility. [aaf30f11]
9. "Gaming" community platforms (e.g. Reddit) with inauthentic posting instead of genuinely answering real questions. [aaf30f11]
10. Chasing viral hits on YouTube instead of building evergreen search-hit videos. [2130f7b9]
11. Never checking robots.txt, and assuming a Cloudflare-hosted site is not blocking AI crawlers by default. [c801b61f]
12. Relying only on standard web analytics to measure AI's business impact, missing the traffic that is invisible to it (self-reported attribution shows this can be a large undercount). [98c8979b]
13. Trying to fix every brand gap at once instead of prioritizing the biggest one. [5ce00206] [9ffb85f5]

- **All worked examples are generic, not ecommerce-specific.** The Japan trip, red phone case, and "Ahrefs Keywords Explorer" entity example are not store or product examples; ecommerce application (PDP sections, category pages, buying guides) is the card layer's interpretation, not something the source itself demonstrated for a store.
- **Tool-specific workflow.** Nearly every "how to do this" step names an Ahrefs product (Brand Radar, Keywords Explorer, Site Explorer, Web Analytics). The underlying gap-finding, keyword-vetting and bot-tracking concepts likely transfer to other tools, but the source gives no non-Ahrefs alternative or generic method [verify UI: whether your available SEO/analytics tools have equivalent filters].
