## 1. What this source is for

Gives an operator a mental model shift (follower-graph reach is dead, content-graph/interest-graph reach is what matters) plus four practical layers to act on it: a 5-step AI-assisted testing loop for finding winning hooks fast, a method for turning winners into repeatable branded series, a multi-channel account architecture to avoid confusing the algorithm's audience matching, and a monetization/funnel layer so organic attention converts into leads and revenue instead of staying a vanity metric.

## 2. Principles

1. **Reach now comes from a small test audience's reaction, not follower count.** The source's stated mechanism: a new post is shown to a small audience of strangers first; if they engage, distribution expands regardless of how many followers the account has; if they don't, the post dies, again regardless of follower count. Condition: this replaces what the source calls the "follower-graph" or "gallery era" model where more followers meant more guaranteed reach. Evidence: presenter's own explanation of the algorithm, no platform citation. [e1440ddfb8b1]
2. **Loyalty does not guarantee visibility.** Because discovery is now stranger-first, existing fans are described as no longer the primary discovery audience. Condition: applies to organic discovery reach specifically; the source recommends owned/paid channels to reliably reach existing customers rather than counting on the feed. Evidence: presenter's framing, no data. [e1440ddfb8b1]
3. **Over-produced "gallery era" content underperforms raw, fast content under content-graph algorithms**, specifically for categories where behind-the-scenes process (making, packing, shipping, staff banter) is itself interesting. Condition: the source flags this may not apply to luxury or highly regulated categories, where raw content can conflict with brand or compliance needs — test on a lower-stakes account first. Evidence: one coffee-roaster case example, not a controlled test. [ccecf6f16262]
4. **High-volume AI-assisted testing beats one single high-effort campaign.** Producing 10+ variations (hooks, cuts, captions, angle emphasis, translations) of one concept is now fast and cheap enough to do routinely, and running many small bets removes the emotional stakes of a single campaign succeeding or failing. Evidence: presenter's own ad-campaign anecdote (result below), not a benchmark. [c5eaa02ce9d1]
5. **AI can pre-screen a script or transcript for hook strength and pacing before publishing**, by asking it targeted yes/no and improvement questions rather than open-ended ones. Condition: works on any script/transcript-based content (video, ad, possibly landing-page copy by extension, though the source only applies it to video). Evidence: one YouTube-mentee anecdote reporting a "100x" lift, not a repeatable outcome. [8907bbacfafe]
6. **Platforms score new content against the patterns of top-performing content in the same category** (visuals, transcribed audio, captions, pacing), so studying and matching those patterns before publishing raises the odds of being pushed further. Condition: presented as a premise to accept, not something the source proves; pair pattern-matching with your own product proof/customer language so content doesn't become indistinguishable from everyone else's. Evidence: presenter's stated premise plus the same "Bob and Bob 2" anecdote. [1445bdd3ed8e]
7. **Once a format consistently works, brand it and rerun it as a series instead of chasing a new original idea every time.** Chasing originality over repeatability is named as one of the most common entrepreneur mistakes, producing unrelated one-off experiments that never get refined. Evidence: one B2B software company case (details below), not a benchmark. [3df74f892c3f]
8. **Mixing incompatible content types on one account confuses both the algorithm's audience matching and the viewer**, because each post is judged and distributed on its own in a content-graph world, and a single feed can't equally serve conflicting audiences. Condition: fix is separating proven formats into focused satellite accounts — but only after they've proven out on a main account, not from day one. 9. **Attention without a monetization layer is a vanity metric.** Every piece of content needs a clear next step routing top-of-funnel discovery viewers into a mid-funnel nurture asset before a bottom-of-funnel hard offer; skipping mid-funnel and pitching immediately is named as the most common brand mistake. ## 3. Procedures

**A. The S.G.T.M.S. test loop** (run per content concept)
1. **S — Start** with one strong idea: a single insight, story, or product angle.
2. **G — Generate** multiple variations with AI: 10+ hooks, alternate cuts, captions per platform, versions emphasizing different benefits, translations for other markets.
3. **T — Test** quickly: publish the variations within a short window, ideally at the same time, to minimize time-of-day and trend variability between them.
4. **M — Measure** results using CTR (click-through rate) and cost per lead if tracked — not raw views alone.
5. **S — Scale** the winner: reuse it across other platforms, formats and audience segments; keep the losers as data for the next round rather than treating any single loss as a failure. [c5eaa02ce9d1]

**B. AI pre-publish hook/pacing/caption review**
1. Upload or paste the script, caption, or full transcript into an AI writing tool (source names ChatGPT, Gemini, Grok, Claude). `[verify UI]`
2. Ask targeted questions, not general ones: "Is my hook clear and attention-grabbing in under 3 seconds?" and "Would my target market immediately understand what this is about and want to keep watching?"
3. For hooks specifically: ask for "10 hook ideas similar to the top performing videos in my niche" or "five alternative openings that would make this more curiosity-driven."
4. For captions: ask to "make this shorter and easier to read on a phone screen" or to "get to the point faster while keeping the main idea."
5. For pacing: give the full transcript and ask "where could I cut or speed up so viewers don't drop off" and to identify slow sections that might cause people to lose interest, then re-edit those sections. [8907bbacfafe]

**C. Reverse-engineering top-performing content in a niche**
1. Download and transcribe the top-performing videos in your niche (or your own past top performers).
2. Have AI analyze them against the pre-publish checklist in Procedure B (hook clarity, pacing, slow sections).
3. Remake/re-shoot the content with the AI's suggested changes applied, rather than starting from a blank page.
4. Treat any resulting performance lift as a hypothesis to confirm with your own controlled tests, not a guaranteed multiplier. [1445bdd3ed8e]

**D. Turning a winning concept into a branded, repeatable series**
1. Test small and fast: start with one core idea (tip, insight, or product demo); use AI to generate hook, length and angle variations; publish quickly and watch for traction.
2. Measure performance on completion rate, saves, comments and replays — not overall view count.
3. Refine the winner: make small adjustments (shorten it, strengthen the hook, tweak the call to action), then re-post.
4. Package it as a series: give it a name, a distinct visual style, and a clear promise to the audience for what to expect.
5. Scale across platforms, adapting format per channel (source example: a 60-second tip for TikTok vs. a 3-minute version for LinkedIn).
6. Run several different formats in rotation rather than committing to one format forever, to spread risk and avoid audience fatigue. [3df74f892c3f]

**E. Multi-channel account rollout**
1. Map current content types (product updates, tips/education, testimonials, founder/behind-the-scenes, offers) against the distinct audiences they serve.
2. If starting out, do not launch multiple accounts at once — start with one main account and test multiple formats there. [5bb14404abb5]
3. Once a format on the main account consistently performs, spin it into its own dedicated satellite account (source example structure: main account for polished product stories, a tips account for daily insights, a testimonials account, a founder account for behind-the-scenes/personal takes).
4. Keep testing new, unproven formats on the main account so it stays a live sandbox.
5. If engagement drops on a satellite account, the cause is more diagnosable because that account carries only one format/audience — check message-market fit for that specific content type first. [5bb14404abb5]

**F. Building the monetization/funnel layer**
1. Map the three funnel stages for your content: top-of-funnel (short-form discovery content aimed at curiosity/trust, not a sale), mid-funnel (nurture: longer videos, emails, case studies connecting product/service to the viewer's need), bottom-of-funnel (direct offer: product pitch, consultation, or limited-time promotion).
2. Choose one mid-funnel asset that doesn't need to be complex: a newsletter, webinar, free trial, or waitlist.
3. Attach a single, clear next step to every piece of content produced.
4. Route viral/attention spikes to the mid-funnel asset, not straight to a hard-sell pitch.
5. Use AI to segment audiences and personalize follow-up so each viewer sees a more relevant next step. [5f22157f3977]

**G. Comment-to-DM lead capture setup**
1. Decide the trigger phrase and what it delivers (source example: "Comment GUIDE to get the free sizing guide").
2. Set up comment-driven automation on Instagram, Facebook Messenger, or WhatsApp using a tool such as ManyChat (source describes it as Meta-approved). `[verify UI]` `[web check: current ManyChat pricing, trial terms and platform approval status, since the source is a promotional/affiliate context]`
3. Connect the automated DM to an email list, CRM, or calendar so the lead is captured, not just messaged.
4. Test the full automation flow before the triggering post goes live, so no one hits a broken link during a spike.
5. Monitor which comment triggers actually produce leads that convert later, not just raw reply volume. [00f161224b14]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Feed audit for "gallery era" signals**: check for studio lighting, fixed brand color palettes, staged lifestyle models, and approval cycles before posting. If present and engagement is low, test raw/behind-the-scenes content instead. `[needs brand data: current feed content style and engagement by post]` [ccecf6f16262]
- **Metric selection check**: confirm you are tracking completion rate, saves, comments, replays, CTR and cost per lead — not just view count or follower count — before judging any test round. [c5eaa02ce9d1] [3df74f892c3f]
- **Satellite-account signal check**: if engagement drops on a single-format satellite account, the cause is more likely the content/message itself, since format and placement are held constant on that account. Compare against the main account's mixed-format baseline. [5bb14404abb5]
- **Monetization-layer check**: for each piece of published content, confirm there is one clear next step (link, DM trigger, CTA) routing the viewer somewhere, rather than ending on a like/comment with no path forward. [5f22157f3977]
- **Pre-publish AI review checklist**: run the specific questions in Procedure B on every script/transcript before publishing (hook clarity under 3 seconds, target-market comprehension, caption brevity, pacing/drop-off points). [8907bbacfafe]
- **Automation flow check**: test the comment-to-DM flow end to end before a post goes live, to confirm no broken links during a spike. [00f161224b14]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating follower count or a polished, curated grid as the driver of reach ("gallery era" thinking) instead of content-graph performance. [e1440ddfb8b1] [ccecf6f16262]
- Launching one single high-effort campaign and becoming emotionally invested in its success, instead of running many small variations and expecting some to fail. [c5eaa02ce9d1]
- Chasing originality over repeatability — running one-off experiments that are tried once and never refined. [3df74f892c3f]
- Dumping all content types (product updates, tips, behind-the-scenes, offers) onto one main feed, which confuses the algorithm's audience matching and the viewer. [5bb14404abb5]
- Launching multiple satellite accounts all at once instead of proving formats on one main account first. [5bb14404abb5]
- Skipping the mid-funnel nurture step and pitching a hard sale immediately upon discovery — the source's own framing: "asking someone to marry you on the first date." [5f22157f3977]
- Treating views, likes and followers as the end goal ("reach without revenue is just a vanity metric") instead of building a monetization layer. [5f22157f3977] [c5eaa02ce9d1]
- Assuming weak past performance dooms future posts, instead of accepting that one strong post can reset an account's trajectory. [ff7b85a40e7b]
