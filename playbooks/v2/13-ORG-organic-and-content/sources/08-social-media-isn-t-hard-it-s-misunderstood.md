## 1. What this source is for

Gives a strategic (not tactical/how-to) framework for organic growth: why algorithms now match content to strangers instead of followers, how to get an algorithm to find your right audience, why maximum-reach virality can hurt a business account, why to pick one platform instead of many, why not to cross-link platforms, and why creators who rely on ad revenue and brand deals capture little value compared to those who sell owned products. It does not cover paid media (see 10-MCR) or email/SMS flow mechanics (see 14-EML), and it does not cover video editing, scriptwriting or hook-writing tactics — the author says those live in his other content, not this one.

## 2. Principles

1. **Social media is now "media," not "social."** Since 2020, driven by TikTok, platform algorithms stopped showing content mainly from people you follow and started matching content to strangers based on interest ("media matching" replacing the "social graph"). Condition: the author claims this applies across Instagram, TikTok, YouTube and LinkedIn. Evidence: personal observation across platforms he has grown, no cited platform documentation. [34a52f48158a]
2. **The algorithm's business goal is watch time, and that is the lever you pull.** Platforms earn ad revenue only while a viewer keeps watching, so their algorithm's job is to keep showing viewers videos they will finish. A creator's job is therefore not "get followers" but "help the algorithm match this video to the viewer most likely to finish and engage with it." Condition: applies generally to ad-supported feed algorithms per the author. [34a52f48158a]
3. **Follower count no longer gates distribution.** Because matching can reach non-followers, a small account is not capped by its follower count, and a large follower count does not guarantee distribution either. [34a52f48158a]
4. **The algorithm reads topic mainly from transcription and captions.** Most algorithms (per the author) determine a video's topic from what is spoken (auto-transcribed) and what is written in the caption, then build a lookalike audience from viewers who engage positively. Practical implication: state, in speech and caption, what topic the video covers and who it is for. [34a52f48158a] [d84a8df8c294]
5. **Militant topic discipline trains the algorithm.** One account should cover one topic area for one specific customer avatar, derived from that avatar's pain points, not from broad industry trends. Condition: applies per account — a brand with multiple, unrelated categories needs separate accounts or content lines rather than one account covering all categories. [d84a8df8c294]
6. **On-target virality beats pure virality for business growth.** Pure virality means picking maximally broad/relatable topics to reach as many of "8 billion people" as possible; most 10M+ view videos come from this. On-target virality means maximizing penetration only inside a narrow target-avatar slice, without spilling beyond it. Condition: this is a business-growth/monetization framing; the author explicitly still wants virality, just aimed at his own category — this is not a rule against ever going broad. [d6fdd4b41759]
7. **Broad hits confuse the algorithm's future targeting.** When a video's audience spans many unrelated groups (the author's example: a home-design-tech video pulling in tech people, architects, and real-estate people), the algorithm gets a noisier signal about who to push the creator's next video to, which the author says hurts monetization even though it feels like a win. This is a hypothetical illustration in the source, not a documented case. [d6fdd4b41759]
8. **The algorithm rewards precision over breadth.** The author states algorithms would rather make a creator a "fan favorite" in one niche than spread them thinly across a broader adjacent audience — precision execution on a narrow topic gets pushed harder into that niche ("category viral"). [d6fdd4b41759]
9. **Fish where the fish are: one hero platform + email, not omnichannel.** The author argues against the common advice to be everywhere "because you never know where your buyer is." Instead, find the single platform with the highest density of your customers, add email as the only other channel, and work only that combination. Condition: the author frames this as advice for people/small teams starting from zero; he separately notes that a store with an established team and multi-platform resources may not need this constraint. [e46ef6c2491a]
10. **Islands, not ecosystems.** Treat each social platform as a separate island rather than a connected web, because (a) platform owners compete with each other and do not want to send users elsewhere, and (b) users consume differently on each platform and dislike being redirected. Condition: framed for individuals/small teams building from zero; the author says this may not hold the same way for an already-established, multi-platform brand. [03e0df5b81d9]
11. **The only allowed bridge is rented-to-owned.** Sending an audience from a social platform to an email list or a private community is fine. Sending an audience from one social platform to another social platform is not — that is treated as forcing an ecosystem link and, per the source, gets penalized and resented by users. [03e0df5b81d9]
12. **Outbound links get throttled.** Posting a URL in a tweet, a LinkedIn post, or Instagram Stories reduces that post's engagement/reach, because platforms do not profit once a user leaves. The author explicitly disagrees with the common creator-coach position that "losing reach on one post is worth it if it grows you everywhere." [03e0df5b81d9]
13. **"World building" is the alternative to forced cross-promotion.** Keep visual and narrative branding consistent across platforms so that when someone discovers you natively on a second platform, it feels aligned — without actively pushing people from one platform to the other. [03e0df5b81d9]
14. **Value is not captured at the media (creator) layer.** The content stack has three layers: platform (Instagram/TikTok/YouTube), media (creators), and offering/supply (the brands/products paying for attention or being sold). Attention is generated at the media layer, but most value is captured at the platform and offering layers. Reason given: value capture correlates with risk, and it is far easier to start making content than to build a successful platform or a successful product/brand business, so creator payout rates stay low. [0e331c15890]
15. **Use the media layer as an attention engine, not the income source.** Direct attention generated through content toward owned products, services, or affiliate deals with uncapped upside, rather than depending on AdSense CPMs or one-off brand deals as primary income. [0e331c15890]

## 3. Procedures

**A. Train the algorithm on an account (new or existing)**
1. Pick a single topic area for the account. Do not rotate between unrelated interests (author's own example of what not to do: sports, tech and design on the same channel).
2. Lock in one specific customer/audience avatar for that topic, and derive video ideas from that avatar's pain points, not general industry commentary.
3. For every video, make the topic and intended audience explicit in both the spoken content and the caption, since the algorithm reads topic mainly from transcription and captions.
4. Repeat for 30 to 50 consecutive videos, all on the same topic, for the same avatar, before expecting the algorithm to have "learned" the audience and started pushing into that community. [d84a8df8c294]
5. Throughout, avoid three things: (a) mixing multiple topics on one account, (b) making content that is not valuable or interesting to the single target avatar, (c) making broad content that does not apply to that avatar. [d84a8df8c294]
6. Diagnostic checkpoint after this run: see Section 5, check 1.

**B. Choose a hero platform**
1. List the platform universe the author considers: Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram, X.
2. Identify which one has the highest density of your actual or likely customers. If unknown: take a small random subset of existing customers and ask them directly which platform they use most; alternatively, use who you personally follow and where you learn about your own industry as a proxy signal.
3. Select that platform as your single "hero platform" and add email as the only additional channel.
4. Commit to only that platform-plus-email combination for six months before adding anything else. [e46ef6c2491a]
5. On the hero platform: post video (short-form or long-form both count, per platform), stay as consistent as possible, reply to comments on your own posts, comment on others' posts in your niche on that platform, and use the platform's secondary formats (author's example: use Stories in addition to Reels).

**C. Decide on cross-platform linking**
1. Do not post content designed to move an audience from one social platform to another (e.g., "watch my YouTube" call-outs on Instagram, cross-posting a TikTok link to a tweet).
2. Do not place outbound URLs in tweets, LinkedIn posts, or Stories, expecting full reach — expect a reach/engagement penalty on that specific post.
3. The only outbound bridge to build is from a social platform to an owned asset: an email list or a private community.
4. If a unified cross-platform brand identity is wanted anyway, apply consistent visuals and storytelling across platforms ("world building") so a viewer who finds you a second time recognizes and trusts you — but do not actively try to route your audience between platforms to make that discovery happen.

**D. Set up monetization from content**
1. Do not plan the business model around AdSense CPMs or one-off brand deals as the primary revenue source.
2. Build (or already have) an owned product, service, or an affiliate arrangement with uncapped commission upside.
3. Treat all content/media output as the attention-generation layer that funnels toward that owned offering. [0e331c15890]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Topic/avatar consistency check.** What to check: scroll the account's last 20 videos. How: compare topic and implied target viewer across them. Good: all 20 point to the same topic and the same avatar — if views/growth are still weak despite this, the source says the problem is content quality (script, editing, value), not targeting. Bad: topics are scattered across different subjects or avatars — fix topic focus first, before touching content quality. [34a52f48158a]
2. **Viral-hit conversion check.** What to check: did a high-view video actually drive sales or just views? Good: engagement stays inside your target-avatar slice. Bad: engagement is broad and mixed — expect the algorithm's next-video targeting to get noisier and future monetization to suffer. ## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Mixing multiple unrelated topics on one account (the source's own example: sports, tech and design together) — confuses the algorithm's audience-matching signal.
2. Making content that is not valuable, interesting or entertaining to the single target avatar, even if it might have broader appeal.
3. Making broad, generic content that does not speak to that specific avatar.
4. Chasing "pure virality" (maximally broad, relatable topics) purely for view counts — the source says this can actively hurt a business account's future targeting and monetization, even though it feels like success.
5. Trying to be present on every platform at once ("you have to be everywhere") without a team to support it — the source calls this overcomplication that produces worse results than focusing on one platform.
6. Posting outbound links/URLs to move audiences off-platform (tweets, LinkedIn posts, Stories) — expect the post's reach to be throttled.
7. Treating platforms as one connected ecosystem and actively trying to funnel followers from one social platform to another (e.g., Instagram to TikTok) — the source says users dislike switching and will churn, disengage, or resent being redirected, rather than following along.
8. Relying on AdSense CPMs and brand deals as the primary way to make money from content — the source frames this as chasing value where the least value is actually captured (the media layer), instead of building or affiliating with owned products/services.
