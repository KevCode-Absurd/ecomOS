## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator a starting playbook for growing a zero-follower Instagram account and turning that growth into revenue: how long to commit, which formats to prioritize, how to build a scroll-stopping hook, a 5-point content checklist (SHARE), which metric to track by goal, and how to replace "link in bio" with DM automation (ManyChat) to convert engagement into leads and sales. It is strong on mindset, hook construction and the DM-automation funnel; it is weak on hard numbers for anything except growth-rate expectation and the presenter's own posting-frequency experiment.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Set a multi-month to 1-year time horizon before judging the channel
Small brands/businesses should expect an average Instagram growth rate around 8% per month, and the presenter's recommended commitment period escalated over his own coaching history: 90 days, then 120 days, then 6 months, and now "at least a year." He explicitly criticizes people who message him after 1-2 weeks asking what's going wrong. Condition: this is his stated coaching heuristic, not a published study. [13325d6dff66]

### 2.2 Comparing your outcomes to established creators is the top way to kill a new account
The single biggest failure mode he names for a zero-follower account is comparing your own outcomes (growth speed, follower count, engagement) to creators who started earlier. Following others for inspiration or market-gap research is fine; comparing outcomes is not. Consequence he describes: a "why not me" victim mentality that "crushes" motivation before results can show up, since comparable outcomes take "probably years." [38b7805726e7]

### 2.3 Niche down before growth, not before posting
You do not need your topic/niche "ironed out" before you start posting and experimenting, but you do need it ironed out before you actually start growing. Early posting can be exploratory (what do I like making, what am I good at); sustained growth requires focus. The source gives no specific method for choosing a niche. [13325d6dff66]

### 2.4 Allocate about 80% of early effort to Reels; deprioritize Stories until you have followers
For a new account: roughly 80% of effort on Reels, because Reels reach more non-followers on average than other formats. Continue posting carousels and photos alongside Reels; do not abandon them. Stories are the one format to deprioritize early, because Stories are viewed almost exclusively by existing followers, so a small account gets very few Story views. This is a "new account" rule, not a rule for accounts with an established following — once you have followers, Stories become valuable for clicks and calls to action. [fa1ae83e8a278]

### 2.5 Interest-based distribution ("TikTokification") makes "the more you post, the more you grow" true
The presenter frames this as an 8-word rule: "the more you post, the more you grow." His explanation: platforms (following TikTok's model) now evaluate each individual post on interest/relevance rather than judging the account for posting volume. Practical effect: a weak post just gets shown less; it does not "punish" the account the way old-school blogging/social advice warned against overposting. This removes the downside of high volume that existed on older, follower-graph platforms (his comparison: old Facebook, where posting 4 times meant followers saw all 4 and could be overwhelmed). [c318b8e4519689]

### 2.6 The hook is "beyond a shadow of a doubt" the most important part of a Reel, and it is visual before it is verbal
About 80% of the time and effort spent planning/scripting a post should go into the first few seconds. He says small businesses and marketers over-index on the words of the hook (on-screen text, spoken script) and neglect the visual and audio elements. Visual hook levers named: props, showing a human face with eyes (same logic as a YouTube thumbnail — faces increase clicks), dynamic movement, sudden lighting changes. Verbal hook guidance: keep it short, simple, and understandable by a "drunk grandma" — his literal example is the phrase "should I open it or should I keep it sealed?" [c173b995a2f51]

### 2.7 Hook swaps stitch a genuine dramatic moment into your own content; this differs from clickbait
A "hook swap" takes a proven viral or highly curiosity-driven moment and transitions seamlessly into your own content before the viewer swipes away. Two routes: (1) stitch/duet someone else's already-viral clip and transition into your own content (example: the brand "Wants and Needs" stitching into a product moment); (2) create your own higher-production dramatic moment (example: musician "Citizen the Artist" filming himself apparently about to trip into a tide pool, then transitioning into his music video). His distinction from bait-and-switch clickbait: the dramatic thing must actually happen in the video. His contrast case is a YouTube-style promise never delivered ("my grandma died" → punchline "died laughing") versus a real dramatic event (an actual trip-and-fall) that then transitions. He argues short-form audiences are more forgiving of this because only a few seconds are at stake, versus wasting 20 minutes of a viewer's time on YouTube. [d686739bfd6c6]

### 2.8 The SHARE checklist: 5 qualities of a shareable Reel
S = Simple (understandable by a "drunk grandma" — dumb it down, use easy words). H = Hook (visually arresting, per 2.6). A = Authentic (reduce the time between having an idea and executing it; his example is a person talking straight to camera in a car, unedited — heavy editing/filtering suits long form, not short form). R = Relatable (comes from extreme specificity, not vagueness — his example: "me when my reel doesn't get views" is vague and weak; "me when I spend three hours editing a reel and it gets seven likes" is specific and lands harder with fewer but more resonant viewers). E = Entertaining (he attributes to a private, unofficial conversation with a Meta "head of Instagram for business" that brands must now make entertainment the primary driver of scripting and framing, not education). He adds a redundancy filter: ask of every sentence "is this necessary or redundant?" — his bad example is "fit, lean, lose weight, healthy," four ways of saying the same thing, which causes viewers to scroll away. [9fce1289b0635]

### 2.9 Create for the stranger, not the superfan
Most viewers of any given post are literal strangers who have never seen your content before, or infrequent followers ("this is the first post I've seen in a month") — not people who watch every post. Each post's first few seconds must balance grabbing attention with generating curiosity, without confusing or losing the stranger. Do not reference earlier videos, inside jokes, or assumed audience knowledge. Lead with the clearest version of the idea, not the cleverest. [37ea3485764f6]

### 2.10 Which metric to optimize depends on your goal, not one fixed dashboard
Retention (watch time, and specifically watch rate after the first 3 seconds) is called the foundational metric regardless of goal. Beyond that: if the goal is growth, focus on shares as the engagement signal and double down on high-share content/topics. If the goal is sales ("a season of selling"), focus more on comments and on whatever drives Story views, rather than shares or virality. Practical routine: label a content period as a growth phase or a selling phase and judge posts against the metric matching that phase, not both at once. [3380206cba51f]

### 2.11 Replace "link in bio" with comment-triggered DM automation
Diagnosis of the old approach: telling an interested viewer to "click the link in my bio" tells them to stop engaging with the post and go through a multi-step, confusing path (open bio → find link menu → pick the right item) — and roughly half the audience just isn't interested anyway. Replacement: ask viewers to comment a specific keyword; a DM automation tool sends the asset/link directly to their inbox. Effect described: comments on the post rise (an engagement signal Instagram rewards), the DM arrives with a notification badge that reminds the person to click, and this creates a feedback loop of more engagement leading to more views. He names ManyChat as the tool he uses, calling it the most popular and (per him) the only officially approved Instagram DM-automation partner that meets regularly with Meta. `[verify UI]` `[web check: current official partner status and platform DM-automation rules]` [79467cee9f1727]

### 2.12 "The fortune is in the follow-up": DM automation exposes exactly who to retarget
Unlike link-in-bio, which only shows an aggregate click count (his example: "137 people clicked yesterday" with no way to identify who), DM automation backend analytics show individually who opened the DM, who clicked, and who purchased. His example: of 10 people who clicked, only 3 purchased, leaving 7 identifiable people to follow up with manually or via automation (e.g., "I noticed you were interested... the sale ends tomorrow night, any questions?"). He states example rates from his own account (98% of messages opened, 37% clicking) as illustrations of his own campaigns, not benchmarks. [13e3b50fabea]

### 2.13 Automate a thank-you DM on new follow, not a sales pitch
He turns on an automatic DM triggered by a new follow that says only "Hey, thanks so much for the follow. I appreciate you" — explicitly no offer, no pitch, despite the temptation to sell immediately. Stated benefit: recipients reply or react, opening a DM thread; he says Instagram's own behavior is that DMing someone increases the likelihood they see your next post, which compounds future organic reach `[web check: is this a documented Instagram mechanic]`. Operational payoff: when a later campaign asks people to DM/comment a keyword, an existing thread means the automated reply won't land in the requests folder or confuse the user. Caveat he doesn't state but the card raises: at scale this creates a high-volume inbox that may need muting or batching. [b539e41f421e]

### 2.14 Instagram is best for top/mid-funnel and low-ticket sales, not closing high-ticket deals in-feed
Position stated: Instagram converts well to free lead magnets and low-ticket offers; the higher the price point, the harder it is to close the sale inside the app. For high-ticket items (his context: coaching, courses, a mastermind) he finds it far more effective to book a consultation/call, because buyers want to confirm you're a real person. His launch cadence: typical seasonal sales (e.g. Black Friday) plus 1-2 annual launches, with free lead magnets driven continuously in between launches. No numeric price threshold is given for what counts as low- versus high-ticket. [3dd25615d38ba]

### 2.15 Build a repeatable series or challenge instead of one-off content
His 2026 prediction: repeatable formats (series, challenges) will proliferate and perform well, though he hedges that he hopes the window lasts the full year (a forecast, not observed data). A challenge must be genuinely challenging — something you would not otherwise do — or it won't carry the same weight; his contrast is a low-stakes "different salad every day" premise versus higher-stakes examples (a runner doing a mile per follower gained, someone locking himself in a home-built prison cell for a year). He explicitly says not to replicate that level of extreme, and rules out anything harmful or dangerous. Smaller, relatable challenges also work (example: documenting an attempt to speak for 60 seconds without filler words, day by day). Challenge yourself first; community participation is a bonus, not the design goal. A recognizable, repeatable format (his own show format is the example) makes production faster and easier for viewers to identify. [a0a28a40b29f]

### 2.16 "Commit to the bit": raise production stakes to stand out from generic content
Content that takes the least effort looks the same as everyone else's, so it earns little engagement. A modest but real commitment — his example is simply filming Reels outdoors (a park, a front yard) instead of a static indoor setup — has "been shown to increase engagement and views" because it introduces variability and dynamism (no study cited). Real-world unpredictability is treated as a production asset; he cites street interviews as popular precisely because outcomes are unknown. He does not recommend physically dangerous stunts (his own example of tying a rope around his neck to a moving car is offered partly as a caution). [4289340e4d3b6]

### 2.17 Follower count is less valuable than it was, but still a real funnel step
Framing: the follow is worth less than 10-15 years ago because interest-based distribution no longer guarantees followers see your posts, but it remains the next step in the funnel because (a) followers are still slightly more likely to see your content, (b) followers are more likely to become "superfans" (his phrase: more likely to "use your word" or "wear your shirt"), and (c) viewers very rarely go directly from non-follower to customer — a follow usually sits in between. Where followers pay off most directly: Stories, since only followers see them and Stories are where clicks/CTAs live — he contrasts this favorably with YouTube Shorts, where getting clicks from viewers is much harder. No conversion rates are given for viewer-to-follower or follower-to-customer. [4c8610731e7cb]

## 3. Procedures

The source is mostly principle-and-example, with fewer literal numbered steps. These are the sequences it gives or that its cards make explicit.

### 3.1 Starting a new Instagram account from zero
1. Identify a topic/niche direction; it does not need to be fully fixed before your first posts, only before you expect growth. [13325d6dff66]
2. Post experimentally at first to find what you enjoy making and are good at. [13325d6dff66]
3. Allocate about 80% of effort to Reels; keep posting carousels and photos; deprioritize Stories until you have a follower base. [fa1ae83e8a278]
4. Set an internal expectation of ~8% monthly growth and a commitment window of at least a year before judging the channel. `[needs brand data: current follower count and target timeline]` [13325d6dff66]
5. Track uploads (an input you control) as the primary KPI in this phase, not views or followers (outcomes you don't control). [13325d6dff66] [38b7805726e7]

### 3.2 Deciding and raising posting cadence
1. Start at whatever daily cadence your team can sustain. [c318b8e4519689]
2. Treat posting frequency as directly correlated with growth under interest-based distribution; if capacity allows, raise cadence rather than only polishing fewer posts. [c318b8e4519689]
3. Build systems (templates, batching) so that posting takes minutes rather than hours per post — a prerequisite for raising volume sustainably. [c318b8e4519689]
4. Do not chase extreme volume examples (hundreds of posts/day) that rely on large dedicated staff; use them only as proof the ceiling on volume is very high, not as a target. [c318b8e4519689]

### 3.3 Building a Reel's hook
1. Plan the visual opening before writing the script — decide the prop, movement, lighting change, or face-forward shot that will interrupt the scroll. [c173b995a2f51]
2. Keep the spoken/on-screen hook short and in plain language a "drunk grandma" would understand. [c173b995a2f51]
3. Consider a hook swap: either stitch a proven viral clip and transition into your content, or stage your own genuine dramatic moment that actually occurs on camera. [d686739bfd6c6]
4. Run the finished concept against the SHARE checklist (3.4) before publishing. [9fce1289b0635]

### 3.4 Pre-publish SHARE check
1. Simple: could a "drunk grandma" follow it? [9fce1289b0635]
2. Hook: is the opening visually arresting, not just verbally clever? [9fce1289b0635] [c173b995a2f51]
3. Authentic: is production time-to-execution short, avoiding over-editing for short form? [9fce1289b0635]
4. Relatable: is the caption/line specific rather than vague? [9fce1289b0635]
5. Entertaining: does it entertain first, rather than lead with education? [9fce1289b0635]
6. Redundancy pass: for each sentence/word, ask "is this necessary or already said?" Cut repeats. [9fce1289b0635] [37ea3485764f6]

### 3.5 Choosing which metric to review, by content goal
1. Label the current period as a growth phase or a selling phase. [3380206cba51f]
2. Always check retention and 3-second-plus watch rate as the baseline health check. [3380206cba51f]
3. In a growth phase, review shares; double down on the highest-sharing topics/formats. [3380206cba51f]
4. In a selling phase, review comments and whatever drives Story views. [3380206cba51f]
5. Confirm which of these metrics your current Instagram analytics surface actually reports before building a recurring dashboard. `[verify UI]` [3380206cba51f]

### 3.6 Setting up the comment-to-DM funnel (ManyChat-style)
1. Choose a DM automation tool; the source names ManyChat and calls it the (per him) only officially approved Instagram partner at time of recording. `[web check: current partner status]` [79467cee9f1727]
2. Pick a distinct, memorable keyword per offer/campaign so engagement can be attributed to that specific campaign. [79467cee9f1727]
3. In the caption/video, ask viewers to comment that keyword instead of directing them to "link in bio." [79467cee9f1727]
4. Configure the automation to DM the link/asset to anyone who comments the keyword. `[verify UI]` [79467cee9f1727]
5. Turn on a separate automatic DM triggered by a new follow that says thank-you only, with no pitch. `[verify UI]` [b539e41f421e]
6. Monitor backend analytics: open rate, click rate, purchase rate per campaign. `[needs brand data: campaign-level open/click/purchase rates]` [13e3b50fabea]
7. Build (manual or automated) follow-up messages to people who opened or clicked but did not purchase, referencing their earlier interest and any deadline (e.g. sale ending). [13e3b50fabea]

### 3.7 Mapping offers to funnel stage on Instagram
1. Route free lead magnets and low-ticket products to be sold directly inside Instagram, continuously (not only during launches). [3dd25615d38ba]
2. For high-ticket offers, use the Instagram DM/lead magnet only as the start of a conversation; route the actual close to a call or consultation. [3dd25615d38ba]
3. Layer in 1-2 annual launch windows plus standard seasonal sales (e.g. Black Friday) on top of the continuous lead-magnet drive. [3dd25615d38ba]

### 3.8 Designing a repeatable series/challenge
1. Pick a premise that is a genuine challenge for you personally — something you would not do without the commitment device. [a0a28a40b29f]
2. Scale ambition to what's sustainable and safe; extreme physical or high-risk stunts are not required and are explicitly discouraged. [a0a28a40b29f]
3. For a product-led brand, reframe a repeatable demo as the challenge (his cited example: "day [N] of exercising in a new location with my invention" — a parking lot one day, a mountaintop another) so each installment both fulfills the challenge and demonstrates a product attribute (portability, versatility). [a0a28a40b29f]
4. Keep the format visually/structurally consistent so it's recognizable and fast to produce. [a0a28a40b29f]
5. Let community participation be optional, not the design goal. [a0a28a40b29f]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Comparing your account's outcomes (growth speed, followers, engagement) to established creators instead of using them only for inspiration/gap-finding. [38b7805726e7]
2. Judging the channel's viability after days, 1-2 weeks, or even 90 days instead of committing to a year. [13325d6dff66]
