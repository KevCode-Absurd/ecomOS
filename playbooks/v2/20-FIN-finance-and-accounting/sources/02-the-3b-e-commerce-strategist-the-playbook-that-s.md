## 2. Principles

1. **Forecasting is execution, not modelling.** Set the target, then "every day we have to make it true". Track performance to expectation daily so you learn you are off on day 5, not day 22. Condition: needs 2 rhythms in the team (see principle 6), or daily tracking drives short-term over-optimising. [4d4d40fe1138] [fc4fd43c986c]
2. **The revenue forecast should come out of the marketing calendar, not a finance extrapolation.** Revenue comes from actions ("units of growth": an ad, an email, an SMS, a post, an influencer, a launch, a promo, a PR hit). If last January had a product launch and this January does not, do not copy last January's number. Source says a plan with spend up 50% and CAC flat "is not real", even from a Fortune 100 CFO. Source's opinion that marketing should own the consumer forecast depends on org structure. [4d4d40fe1138] [15d4d04ea13a]
3. **Revenue layer cake: forecast new-customer revenue and existing-customer revenue separately,** then add event effects. 3 models: spend and AMER, cohort-specific LTV, event effect (section 3, procedure 1). [fc4fd43c986c] [4d4d40fe1138]
4. **Regress outliers to the mean.** One brand's history is a weak base, and outlier years (2020 to 2021) mislead. Use comparable brands where you can, and give a range, not a point. Evidence: an analogy (a 60 home run season projected at 37 to 44 by FanGraphs' 12 models), plus CTC's cross-brand data. [ac073be6ad16]
5. **Category profits get competed away.** With no barriers to entry, supply of competitors grows faster than category demand, so efficiency per dollar falls. Example: a supplement brand that was alone on its search term in 2022 now shares it with 50 listings. Do not expect past efficiency back; growth must come from new products, channels or stories. [15d4d04ea13a] [5e8fb784d881]
6. **Run 2 rhythms with different owners.** One person plans the big moment months out ("the moment in June"); another person manages tomorrow. "They can't be the same person." [da68923c4cc5] [ab2ae93189de] [709862670eb2]
7. **Peaks beat the ad auction.** On Meta you are a "market taker": you do not set CPM. Value = price paid for inventory vs conversion rate (CTR leads to CPC, times conversion rate). BFCM lifts CPMs and every brand's conversion rate, so it gives no relative edge. A peak nobody else has keeps CPM flat while your conversion rate rises; you can then take more margin or more volume. Ask "why does someone need to buy this today?" [da68923c4cc5]
8. **Aim for 4 peaks a year, 1 per quarter,** to balance cash flow and keep a rhythm. Build them on cultural or brand-owned moments tied to the ICP. [da68923c4cc5] [fd83b3a498e8]
9. **Progressive peaking: an acquisition peak feeds the next realization peak.** BFCM mostly harvests existing customers and people who already know you. A large new-customer moment 3 to 8 weeks before BFCM (Sept to early Nov) makes that cohort over-deliver at BFCM. [7c84039e1b24] [fd83b3a498e8]
10. **Daily contribution margin (CM) is the scoreboard for the whole team; cash flow is the CEO's.** Cash flow has human choices in it (when you pay invoices), so the CEO or CFO owns a 13-week cash flow forecast ("first tab on my browser"). Everyone else manages daily CM to goal. [25d900c4c527]
11. **Metric hierarchy (source's pyramid, opinion):** cash flow above all (CEO) > CM (top of team pyramid) > business metrics (revenue, spend, MER, AOV) > customer metrics (new customer revenue, new customer acquisition efficiency, new customer CM, ad spend) > channel metrics at the bottom (Meta ROAS, Triple Whale ROAS, MTA numbers). No one metric shows health: you can win CM in a month while the new-customer file shrinks. [25d900c4c527] [5297f747603f]
12. **Keep fixed costs out of the daily view.** Spreading fixed costs per day creates a false "cut costs" signal. Fixed costs fall as a percent of revenue as volume rises, so if media makes positive incremental CM, the fix may be to spend more. Tie the monthly CM goal to fixed costs instead: CM minus OPEX = profit. [25d900c4c527]
13. **Show the numbers to everyone.** "You can't ask people to affect things they aren't looking at." Source says cash flow is far simpler to understand than attribution, so hiding it on the grounds that staff will not understand it is "a huge myth". Opinion. [25d900c4c527] [83186e2addd5]
14. **Grow the active customer file, not the cumulative one.** About 80% of customers who will ever come back do so within 6 to 8 months; after that treat them as lapsed. The best predictor of future returning revenue is the growth of the active file. Set goals on new customers so you are not "squeezing the sponge". Source heuristic; check your own repurchase curve. [5297f747603f]
15. **Optimise for what the platform allocates on, not for what your MTA reports.** Meta spends your money against the conversions it sees under the attribution setting you picked (1-day click, 7-day click, 7-day click 1-day view, 1-day click 1-day view, engaged view). Your MTA view does not change that. So pick the platform setting with the tightest link to new-customer revenue (source prefers 7-day click "with good exclusions"), and if you use a third-party tool, pass its signal back (Northbeam Apex). [3929ca774887]
16. **Calibrate platform numbers with incrementality factors.** Factor = incrementality result / platform-reported result. Example: Meta reports 1.0, geo holdout says 1.2, factor 1.2. Apply it to the platform number when judging. [ccf3794cd11d] [3929ca774887]
17. **Widen measurement when you widen distribution.** Media drives sales on Amazon and in retail too. Measured on .com only, Meta and YouTube look worse as Amazon grows, the team cuts, and "the whole engine stalls". One engine, one blended CM, not rival .com and Amazon P&Ls. [e840728f10ad] [ab546c35c94e]
18. **Amazon ads and Google branded search are a defensive tax,** not demand creation. Low incrementality until competitors bid on your terms; then a rising cost that erodes margin in categories with no moat. [cb05725b572a]
19. **Four-quarter accounting: every P&L is 4 buckets** (cost of delivery, CAC, OPEX, profit). Find the bucket that eats profit and fix that, instead of reading line items. [9a74113cfe78]
20. **CAC as a percent of revenue depends on LTV and stage.** High-LTV brands that hold new-customer acquisition flat see marketing % fall and margin grow over time as repeat revenue builds (source's "Mona Lisa of e-commerce" chart). Low-LTV hard goods stay near 40 to 50% because every month needs new buyers. [ea7eb29c3679]
21. **Ecommerce is a low-OPEX game.** The OPEX standard moved from 25% to 20% to 15% to 10 to 12% now. Fixed OPEX dollars per day should fall every year, even while scaling, because AI raises output per person. Whoever can pay the most for a customer wins. [434847dcd5bb] [798f1af7e8667]
22. **P&L and cash are different games; trade margin for cash on purpose.** Growing the bank account is the top priority, most of all when M&A and funding are hard. Liquidation, discounts and paying more for longer terms all hurt the P&L and help cash. [20d195846808] [f2fc5286cc97] [c73b11cb714e]
23. **Every PO should maximise the marginal value of that batch.** Do not pay high CAC to sell stock that is about to run out. Do not sit on stock with no plan to sell it. [61a1eefb7db0] [c73b11cb714e]
24. **Constraint is a superpower.** Set a CAC or ROAS line from unit economics and payback, and do not move it. The team then solves with story, peaks, creative, product and channels. Evidence: a 2021 partner analysis found 60% of media dollars "never paid back over any period of time". The most common mistake is "capitulation to efficiency of acquisition". Opinion from operating experience; the line must come from real marginal economics. [75cf518af8d3] [09e7f0d32ea7] [35efd9091446]
25. **Product development is growth marketing; the CEO is the head of growth and a capital allocator.** The return on a new category is "usually so much better than the next Meta dollar", with a different payback cycle. Match the payback horizon to your cash reserve. [8d574ab5e9da] [5e8fb784d881]
26. **Each new product must solve 1 of 3 problems: margin, volume or cash.** [9d3c956f2cd5]
27. **Every SKU is its own small business with its own demand,** so allocate media and price per SKU. [ca6522622ee6] [8e1550070943] [224e4db9b5c9]
28. **You die by bad inventory buys; lower the cost of each bet.** Return on invested capital has 2 parts: potential return and cost. Cutting the cost of testing raises ROIC as much as raising the upside. [135d3773766f] [032c38a487a4]
29. **Story, not iteration.** Hook and body iteration finds a local maximum: small changes, small outcomes. A large share of creative effort should go into new stories and new cohorts. Strong opinion; iteration still has a place. [ab2ae93189de] [e6b2cd97910f]
30. **Create for your audience's audience.** Give buyers something they are proud to share about themselves ("make them a hero in their world"). [e6b2cd97910f]
31. **Transaction moves to the point of discovery.** Be where people discover (TikTok Shop, Amazon, retail, LLMs). Source calls it "omnipresent commerce". [ede47bc222466] [1e9a211ffece]
32. **Format is not the question; answering the buyer's questions is.** List everything a buyer must know to purchase. Simple product: a static image can work, the page does the rest. Complex product: long video answers the questions, then send to checkout. If static for a complex product, the landing page "better be freaking awesome". [fd8e8755b2a2]
33. **Novelty beats copying formats.** Source leans on Seth Godin's "purple cow": replicating a format that works lowers its value. [a66b37722a78] [3a3fcb1ef07a]
34. **Incentives drive behaviour; align dashboards with pay and ownership.** [83186e2addd5]

## 3. Procedures

### Procedure 1. Build the 3-model forecast (spend and AMER, cohort LTV, event effect)
1. Define AMER = new-customer revenue / ad spend. Define MER = total sales / ad spend. [fc4fd43c986c]
2. Spend and AMER model: for every historical month, pair spend with AMER. Fit a curve (source: "linear regression model... a combination of models"). Add a seasonal effect and an AOV term. Read the slope: at spend X you get efficiency Y; model what happens to Y at 2X instead of holding it flat. Output: new-customer revenue expectation. [fc4fd43c986c]
3. Cohort-specific LTV model: group customers by acquisition month. Map each cohort's revenue by month after acquisition (the slanted cohort table). Expect sale-period cohorts (November, BFCM) to be worth less over time. Project future existing-customer revenue from the curves. [fc4fd43c986c] [5297f747603f]
4. Event effect model: pull every past action from APIs (email subject lines and send dates, ad launches, Google campaign launches) and the team's marketing calendar spreadsheet. Overlay them on revenue as annotations. For each event type, measure the effect on media efficiency, existing-customer revenue and organic demand. [15d4d04ea13a]
5. Add email and SMS send plans explicitly: existing-customer revenue rises on send days, so the daily forecast needs the send calendar. [4d4d40fe1138]
6. Sum new-customer revenue + existing-customer revenue + planned event effects into a daily expectation for the month. [4d4d40fe1138]
7. When anyone asks for a higher forecast, ask: "what action will we add, and what effect will it have?" No revenue without an action. [15d4d04ea13a]
8. Track actuals to the daily expectation. If off by day 5, change course. [4d4d40fe1138]
9. For short or broken history: treat outlier periods as outliers, cut data before known structural breaks (host suggests 2024 as a start point for a Q1 2026 plan, given iOS and tariffs), and, where possible, use 6 or 7 comparable brands' curves to set a range. [ac073be6ad16]

### Procedure 2. Daily contribution margin dashboard

2. Set a monthly CM goal sized to cover fixed costs: profit = CM - OPEX. [25d900c4c527]

4. Below CM show: revenue, spend, MER, AOV; then new-customer revenue, new-customer acquisition efficiency (AMER), new-customer CM, ad spend; then channel ROAS last. [25d900c4c527]

### Procedure 3. Returns accrual
1. Set the business return rate from history (example: 10%). [1d3d04198311]
2. Each day, subtract rate x that day's revenue as estimated returns. Worked example: $1,000,000 December x 10% = $100,000 of expected returns, about $3,333 a day over 30 days. [1d3d04198311]
3. Reconcile to actual returns at month end. [1d3d04198311]
4. Do not read Shopify's default "total sales" (today's revenue minus returns processed today) as the efficiency base. Worked example: a $500,000 January that processes $100,000 of December returns looks like a 20% return rate, when the rate is still 10%. Source wants Shopify to switch the default to order revenue. `[verify UI: Shopify default sales metric]` [1d3d04198311]

### Procedure 4. Four-quarter accounting view
1. Ask your accountant for a monthly view with 4 totals only: cost of delivery (COGS plus variable expenses, mainly product cost and shipping), CAC (pure variable media dollars), OPEX (fixed: rent, labour, software, agencies, contractors), profit. [9a74113cfe78]
2. Put creative in CAC only if it is a true variable cost; if fixed (retainer, salaried team), put it in OPEX. Agencies and contractors go in OPEX. [9a74113cfe78]
3. Compare to the reference mix (section 4) and to the 25/25/25/25 picture. [9a74113cfe78]
4. Pick the bucket that is out of line and work on that one. [9a74113cfe78]
5. Track net shipping cost = shipping revenue - shipping cost, every month; use price tests on shipping. [1e5dad6d98ce]

### Procedure 5. Aged inventory and liquidation funnel
1. Grade inventory A, B, C, D by how long it has sat, or by days of inventory outstanding. The source does not give the day cut-offs for each grade. [f2fc5286cc97]
2. Target: days of inventory remaining = production lead time, so a PO placed today lands as you run out (just in time). [f2fc5286cc97]
3. Flag stock far above lead time (example: 1,000 days outstanding) as a cash problem. How deep you discount, or how low an efficiency you accept, should rise with days outstanding. [f2fc5286cc97]
4. Liquidate non-moving aged stock and make a new bet, "always" (source), even if you do not need cash. [f2fc5286cc97] [c73b11cb714e]
5. Liquidation funnel: build an unlisted PDP (not on site navigation), run ads to it, exclude existing customers. Either discount deeply on that page only, or keep price and accept lower efficiency. Example targets: core stock at 2.0 ROAS, liquidation at 1.0 or 0.8. [f2fc5286cc97]
6. Other outlets: a discount email to your list with a narrative, if it does not hurt the brand; a third-party liquidator for bulk. Keep standing liquidator relationships. [f2fc5286cc97] [c73b11cb714e]
7. Put the cash into better inventory. [c73b11cb714e]

### Procedure 6. Weekly inventory and media meeting
1. Each week the media buyer and ops review days of inventory outstanding on the best SKUs and where ad spend is going. [61a1eefb7db0]
2. If a top SKU will sell out before restock (example: 30 days of stock, 80-day reorder), either raise the ROAS target a lot to squeeze more margin from the batch, or turn its ads off. [61a1eefb7db0]
3. Move spend to SKUs with stock, including aged stock. [61a1eefb7db0]
4. Keep demand creation (ads) in sync with demand planning (POs). [61a1eefb7db0]

### Procedure 7. Vendor terms negotiation
1. Learn each counterpart's cost of capital and how they make money: do they need cash, margin, or committed future dollars? [377b3fd122bf]
2. Compare to yours. Source's examples: credit cards about 30% interest, merchant cash advance about 70%. Chinese suppliers often get subsidised, cheaper capital. [377b3fd122bf]
3. Offer what they value in exchange for terms: a slightly higher unit cost ("2 bips or 10 bips on my gross margin"), a longer contract, or a bigger commitment. [377b3fd122bf]
4. Target terms longer than production plus sell-through. Example: 90 days to make, 120-day terms, so you sell before you pay: a negative cash conversion cycle. [377b3fd122bf]
5. Build long relationships in person; long terms and consignment come from trust. [377b3fd122bf]
6. Ultimate goal: consignment (supplier's stock, you pay when it sells). [377b3fd122bf]
7. Apply the same logic to agencies, software, licensors and Meta. [377b3fd122bf]

### Procedure 8. SKU product matrix (MVP) and 4 SKU types
1. Take last month. For every SKU: unit volume, revenue, media dollars spent against it, and a SKU-level efficiency (ROAS or MER). Note LTV differences where known. [ca6522622ee6]
2. Classify: [ca6522622ee6] [8e1550070943]

3. Use gaps across gender, tops vs bottoms, footwear vs headwear, colourways and limited editions to choose what to make next. [ca6522622ee6]
4. Re-run on a cadence. [8e1550070943]

### Procedure 9. Pick the next product by business problem
1. Name your main problem: EBITDA/margin, growth/volume, or cash. [9d3c956f2cd5]
2. Cash problem: if revenue is bimodal with 2 far-apart peaks (example: January-heavy, weak summer), you place the winter PO in the summer valley. Develop a product that sells in the valley to smooth the curve. [9d3c956f2cd5]
3. Volume problem: find a category with rising velocity (supplement examples: creatine gummies, colostrum, fiber); track category velocity with a tool such as Particle. Prefer one that fits your ICP. [9d3c956f2cd5]
4. Margin problem: example: 50% gross margin with poor value to weight. A higher-margin product that breaks even at 1.4 ROAS instead of 2.0 lets you buy more new customers. [9d3c956f2cd5]
5. Score all ideas against the problem (the host compares to ICE scoring). Best case hits all 3. For a cash problem, the lever may be vendor terms, not a new product. [9d3c956f2cd5]

### Procedure 10. Demand plan before any PO
1. Write units by channel: X via email and SMS to existing customers, Y via influencer seeding, Z via paid media at a stated efficiency. [20338553ad10]
2. Compute media dollars needed for Z. Worked failure: a brand expected $20M revenue from a new SKU and had "no idea" of the media needed. [20338553ad10]
3. Put wide error bars on new-category efficiency (no history; example: a wallet brand entering luggage). [20338553ad10]
4. Model PO timing and cash cycle with the demand plan. [20338553ad10]
5. Start with a small bet that scales. [20338553ad10] [032c38a487a4]

### Procedure 11. Test demand cheaply
1. If suppliers will not give terms, use pre-orders so customers fund the stock. Works best when demand is already strong. It also sharpens PO sizing. [135d3773766f]
2. Model a cancellation rate for pre-orders; revenue cannot be recognised until fulfilled. BNPL and try-before-you-buy carry the same risk of higher returns. [135d3773766f]
3. Small batch: make about 100 units, list them, run ads, read efficiency, then place the PO (ColourPop example). [135d3773766f]
4. Run 2 supply chains: a fast, flexible, low-MOQ chain where gross margin does not matter (job: learn demand), and a large PO chain for proven items. [135d3773766f]
5. Trend chase: spot a trending SKU (ColourPop watched ulta.com), make it, list within 72 hours. [135d3773766f]
6. Set a sell-through signal before launch. Host's Steve Madden example: made at 8 a.m., on the Times Square floor next day, more than 15 units sold = a win. [135d3773766f] [032c38a487a4]

### Procedure 12. Incrementality for a new channel (geo holdout)

3. Turn spend off in control DMAs, on in test DMAs. [ccf3794cd11d]
4. Effect = revenue gap between test and control. Measure .com and Amazon (and retail if present). [ccf3794cd11d] [e840728f10ad]
5. Build a factor per channel and apply it to platform numbers. Add 1 channel at a time. [ccf3794cd11d]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Finance forecasts that ignore the marketing calendar, or hold CAC flat while spend rises 50%. [4d4d40fe1138]
2. Forecasting from an outlier year (2020 to 2021) with no regression to the mean. [ac073be6ad16]
3. Spreading fixed costs into daily profit, then cutting spend that makes positive incremental CM. [25d900c4c527]
4. Reading January MER on Shopify total sales that include December's returns, then pulling back. [1d3d04198311]
5. Running the company on channel ROAS (Meta, Triple Whale, Northbeam) and then asking why there is no cash. [25d900c4c527]
6. Assuming the customer file compounds forever; most customers lapse. [5297f747603f]
7. Measuring in one tool while Meta optimises on another signal. [3929ca774887]
8. Adding channels without isolating each one's effect; a diverse mix is "almost impossible" to untangle later. [ccf3794cd11d]
9. Measuring media only on .com after adding Amazon or retail, and cutting spend; separate .com and Amazon P&Ls. [e840728f10ad] [ab546c35c94e]
10. Running TikTok ads to .com ("almost never work") instead of TikTok Shop with affiliates. [fb29afeb843e]
11. Opening a retail partner or own store where .com is already strong, which only cannibalises; retailers "sit on your brand terms". [ab546c35c94e] [c73b11cb714e]
12. Giving away free shipping without watching net shipping cost. [1e5dad6d98ce]
13. Holding aged stock to prop up the balance sheet; paying storage on stock with no ads or emails planned. [f2fc5286cc97] [c73b11cb714e]
14. A media buyer on a ROAS target with no inventory view, who sells the best SKUs out fast at high CAC. [61a1eefb7db0]
15. Selling out a hero size or colour mid-flight: Meta sees the conversion rate crash, marks the ad a loser, and will not come back to it on restock. [224e4db9b5c9]
16. One flat price across all colours and sizes. [224e4db9b5c9]
17. Placing a PO with no media plan (the $20M SKU case). [20338553ad10]
18. Pre-orders or BNPL modelled without cancellation or return rates. [135d3773766f]
19. Lowering the CAC or ROAS line whenever the team says it cannot be hit; blaming the macro. [09e7f0d32ea7]
20. Yelling at the Meta team for efficiency in a saturated category. [5e8fb784d881]
21. Hook-only iteration as the whole creative system; drilling one winning pocket "into the ground". [ab2ae93189de]
22. Full-time payroll sized to peak months, accepting losses in low months. [798f1af7e8667]
23. Long-payback investments when cash is tight. [8d574ab5e9da]
24. Copying ad formats that already work; fast followers get lower yield. [a66b37722a78]
25. Making new products because they are "cool", not to solve a margin, volume or cash problem. [9d3c956f2cd5]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Internal number conflicts.** OPEX is "10 to 12%" in one passage and "closer to 15%" in another; cards use 10% to 15%. Cost of delivery target is 30% to 40%, yet the same speaker cites the industry average COGS as 58% (42% gross margin), and at one point says "COGS are probably 60% or 40%". Keep both; do not treat 30% to 40% as typical. [9a74113cfe78] [1e5dad6d98ce] [434847dcd5bb]
- **Cost of delivery scope is loose.** Defined once as COGS plus "all those variable expenses", then as product cost plus shipping. Payment fees, packaging, pick and pack are not placed explicitly. CM "variable expense" is also not itemised. `[needs brand data: full variable cost list]`
- **CAC definition conflicts with common practice.** Source's CAC bucket is pure media only; agencies, contractors and fixed creative go to OPEX. Many operators use fully loaded CAC. Keep the definition stated in every report. [9a74113cfe78]
- **Hierarchy wording.** Source calls CM "top of the pyramid" and puts cash flow above it for the CEO. Card 25d900c4c527 lists cash flow first. Same idea.
- **Correlation number garbled** ("7.8"); likely 0.78. Not a stated threshold.
- **Opinions stated as rules:** 4 peaks a year, under $10M stay single-channel, TikTok ads to .com fail, Amazon ads are a tax, iteration is "the worst creative strategy", "never turn off an ad", AI-only creative beats humans, OPEX heading to 3% (host), service businesses beat ecommerce, "profit engineer" replaces 4 roles. The source itself says it is "not sure" AI is better at creative. [ab2ae93189de] [edca726509d9] [e9c3ba2a4c3e] [a66b37722a78]
- **Disagreement kept:** guest says the most valuable skill is linking the goal to authority over the outcome; host says it is creative judgement. [e9c3ba2a4c3e]
- **"Never turn off an ad"** is a one-line quick answer with no reasoning, and it conflicts with the source's own advice to pause ads on stock-outs and cut spend past the marginal frontier. [a66b37722a78] [61a1eefb7db0]
- **Staffing heuristics** (12% of lowest month; host's "fire 20% to 40%"; "market replacement cost") skip legal, notice, quality and culture costs. Guest frames it as flex staffing. Treat as a planning lens. [798f1af7e8667]
- **Missing how:** no regression spec or error bars for the AMER curve; no event-effect maths; no 13-week cash template; no day cut-offs for A to D inventory grades; no numeric cut-offs for the 4 SKU types; no geo test size, length or spend; no rule for how deep to discount by days outstanding; no marginal-frontier calculation.
