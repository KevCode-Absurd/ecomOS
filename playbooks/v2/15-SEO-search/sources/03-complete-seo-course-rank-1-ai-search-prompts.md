## 1. What this source is for

A single, opinionated, end-to-end walkthrough of "traditional SEO plus AI search visibility" (AEO/GEO) for a small business or agency operator who does their own SEO: keyword research, site structure, AI-assisted content writing (one-off and at scale), local SEO, technical SEO (speed, metadata, schema, conversion), link building, measurement, and finally three no-code automations to run local SEO on autopilot. It is built around specific tools and specific custom GPTs the presenter has made, several of which are only referenced by name in the transcript (the actual prompt text lives in the presenter's separate "resources," not fully inside the words of the video). Its worked examples are all local-service businesses (a mortgage broker, a plumber, a dog trainer, a gardening/NDIS support provider, a PC-parts retailer) — nothing in the transcript is written for an online store, so every ecommerce application below is this playbook's inference, not the source's own words, and is marked as such.

## 2. Principles

1. Search engines have become answer/generative engines (Google AI Overviews, GPT search on Bing, Perplexity, Claude, Copilot, "any LLM with internet access"). The old goal was to be the destination (the click); the new goal is to be the cited source. AEO, GEO, AI SEO and LLM SEO are all still SEO — you cannot skip traditional SEO fundamentals to do the "AI" layer, because AI engines source their answers from what already ranks on page 1 of traditional search. [66b8ab8f9771e221f947bf34]
2. Keep keyword research to two buckets: transactional (buying intent, goes on money/category/product pages) and informational (learn/compare, builds topical authority and blog traffic). More granular keyword taxonomies exist but the source says they don't matter for getting started. [b2ab9292f6baea94828d8182]
3. SEO tool data (search volume, keyword difficulty, competition index, domain authority) is a guide, never an absolute source of truth. A tool showing "0 search volume" or "0 difficulty" does not override your own niche knowledge of what people actually search or ask for. [b2ab9292f6baea94828d8182, d2b1c9b1538ff1ce1ae5973f]
4. New or low-authority sites should chase low-difficulty keywords first, even at lower volume, rather than "shiny object syndrome" on high-volume, high-competition terms — you want the right traffic, not all traffic. [d2b1c9b1538ff1ce1ae5973f]
5. Plan full site architecture (core pages, hub pages, subpages, URL structure, internal linking) before touching any website builder. No structure now means months of rework later. Applies to service businesses (service hub -> subservices -> locations) and, per the source's brief aside, equally to ecommerce (categories -> subcategories -> sub-subcategories -> products). [3a48ad90c5ed4567c17115cb]
6. Identical page templates/layouts across many similar pages (e.g. many location pages) are not content duplication as long as the on-page content differs. This is the presenter's stated experience and opinion, not a cited Google policy — flag as a claim to verify. [3a48ad90c5ed4567c17115cb, c6d42d7d4d1f1934ad5a9b7d] `[web check: current Google guidance on templated/near-duplicate local pages]`
7. Search engines do not penalize AI-written content; they penalize low-quality content. Use AI as an extension of the writer, never a full replacement, and never for spammy mass content. [0b71c176e2e3b50f1e2b8b42]
8. GEO content principles (source's summary of a Princeton research paper, said to show an average 40% increase in AI-overview visibility when followed): write in your own distinct voice, answer the real questions your audience asks, back statements with specific cited data, write at roughly an eighth-grade reading level, cut fluff, always draft then polish, add supporting visuals. [7e809a393a1d31620602f9cb] `[web check: verify the Princeton GEO paper and the 40% figure before quoting it to a client]`
9. Every piece of content needs two kinds of links: an outbound citation to the source of any factual claim (non-negotiable for financial/YMYL topics), and an internal link to the commercial page it supports. A blog post with no internal links is, in the presenter's words, "a room within a house without doors." [205809992abac042eaa04fc3]
10. Content should be organized as pillar (one main commercial/hub page) plus satellite (many narrower supporting articles), all linking back to the pillar. [057f983a9aae3f7700ae544b]
11. Do not trust any content tool that claims "our content will rank you number one." Tool choice matters less than understanding what makes content good (defined audience, tone of voice, sourcing, internal links, human editing). [b95c25b2d3c82e9316839d89]
12. Local SEO narrows to three non-negotiables: an optimized Google Business Profile (GBP), local citations with NAP consistency, and locality baked into on-site titles/H1s/metadata. Claimed by the source to be enough to "do really really well" at local SEO — it explicitly says there is more to local SEO than these three, but treats them as the actionable core. [697ec7cc36485ed3a6729621]
13. Technical SEO, in this source's deliberately narrow scope, is: site speed, metadata (title/H1/meta description/heading order), schema markup, and conversion rate optimization. Anything requiring code/theme changes is, in the presenter's opinion, the web developer's job, not the SEO's. [07eb3c368d7af47fb8a3e8fb]
14. Backlinks remain "the trust signals of the internet" — more reputable sites linking to you on a topic signals trustworthiness to search/AI engines. Quality beats quantity: one relevant link from a reputable site beats a hundred spammy ones. Relevance (industry, locality, niche) matters as much as raw authority. [691b416017c3b96ea709f878]
15. Avoid analysis paralysis in measurement. Only track numbers you will act on. Domain authority/domain rating is a vanity metric (proprietary, inconsistently calculated across tools, doesn't correlate with conversions) — track ranked keywords and actual conversions instead. [fb7381a080bfe5e6f737ebf3]
16. Automation should follow, not precede, working fundamentals. "Just because everything can be automated doesn't mean it should be" — the presenter says he has seen "absolute horror cases" of people scaling automation before their SEO basics were sound. [3b53ab90208b20908f3aaa9e, 39455049b596506d432ef63d]
17. Only let AI auto-publish for low-risk, reversible actions (e.g. positive-review replies, media uploads). Reputation-sensitive or accuracy-sensitive actions (negative-review replies) keep a human in the loop. [3707612ddae8e67144e67fb7]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Keyword research (DataForSEO + Google Sheets + ChatGPT)
1. Create a DataForSEO account (pay-as-you-go credits, not a subscription) and get the API login + API password (emailed). `[verify UI]` `[affiliate link: presenter's signup link grants $5 free credit]`
2. Start a new Google Sheet. Go to Extensions -> install the DataForSEO Google Sheets connector -> accept "insert pre-made templates." This builds tabs: keyword search intent, keyword difficulty, Google search volume, Bing search volume, keyword suggestion, similar keywords, organic traffic estimation, bulk backlinks overview, ranked keywords, and more. `[verify UI]`
3. Critical setup gotcha: the tab name at the bottom of the sheet must match the API function selected in the right-hand menu, or the pull fails (e.g. a "keyword suggestions" tab must have "keyword suggestions" selected in the menu, not "similar keywords"). `[verify UI]`
4. Pick a seed transactional keyword for the client's core offer. Run both "similar keywords" (stricter match) and "keyword suggestions" (looser match), location = target country, up to 80 results returned each. Repeat for other seed terms.
5. Review columns: estimated monthly search volume, competition index (source says ignore this one), keyword difficulty (0-100, closer to 100 = harder), CPC.
6. Look for "gold nugget" keywords: near-zero/null keyword difficulty with meaningful search volume. Apply common sense (a null-difficulty term containing a real bank/brand name is not actually that easy). For each nugget that matches something you actually offer, plan one dedicated transactional page plus supporting informational content.
7. For informational keywords: take the seed keyword to a question-mining tool (source uses Answer Socrates; names AnswerThePublic and Google's "People Also Ask" as similar alternatives) `[affiliate/verify UI]`. Enter keyword + country + language. Download the tool's clustered-questions CSV (one question = one potential blog post; the tool also estimates a combined search-volume per cluster).
8. Import that CSV into a new Google Sheet; add columns for cluster/main keyword, variation keywords with volume, and the H1/H2 that will answer it. Pick 3-4 posts per cluster to actually write rather than trying to cover all questions at once.
9. Competitor keyword-gap analysis: list 3-4 known competitors plus your own site in one tab (color-code your own row). For each domain, pull organic traffic estimation, bulk backlinks overview, and ranked keywords (start at 80 returned; if the tool reports more results exist than you requested — e.g. 101 — rerun asking for the higher number so you capture everything). Put each domain's full ranked-keyword list on its own tab, linked from the competitor row.
10. Manually scan competitor keyword lists for terms/locations you hadn't considered (a human review step the source insists on, not an automatic export).
11. Export each relevant tab as a separate CSV (exporting a Google Sheet only exports the active tab, not the whole workbook). Feed all CSVs into a new ChatGPT chat with a prompt that (a) gives it an "expert SEO" persona, (b) defines the input you're giving it, and (c) defines the output you want (prioritized keyword gaps + backlink opportunities). Model used in the demo: GPT-5, "Auto" routing — the source is explicit that you do not need the "smartest" model for this kind of data organization.
12. Feed the raw keyword export (deliberately not pre-cleaned) into ChatGPT with a second prompt giving business context (type, location, primary goal — e.g. leads) and asking it to segment keywords into a money-page target, subpages, and blog article ideas. Expect and answer the model's clarifying questions before it writes anything; better input produces better output. Review the resulting page/keyword map, including suggested H2s for the main page. [5b3f8191a5724f1f2d46638f]

### 3.2 Site structure ("measure twice, cut once")
1. Before opening any website builder, decide core pages: home, about, services/categories, contact, blog.
2. Build one hub page per main offering as an umbrella (e.g. "Plumbing services") that links down to each subservice's own page.
3. For each main service+location page: H1 and title = "[main service] in [main location]" (source calls this non-negotiable). Suggested on-page order: CTA + title, intro, reviews, benefits, how it works, second CTA, FAQ, subservice list — presenter says you don't have to follow this order exactly.
4. Each subservice page: URL pattern `/services/subservice`, title "[subservice] in [location]".
5. To capture sub-location demand within a metro, replicate the same page template for each sub-location (e.g. `/brooklyn`) and cross with subservices (e.g. "drain cleaning in Brooklyn").
6. Map everything (main page, subpages, URL, target keyword, intent) in a spreadsheet or visual sitemap/wireframe before building.
7. For an ecommerce store, apply the same logic to categories, subcategories, sub-subcategories and products (this ecommerce mapping is this playbook's/the card layer's extrapolation from the source's service-business example, not a worked ecommerce example in the transcript itself). `[needs brand data: current site's category tree, existing URL structure]`

### 3.3 AI content workflow (single article)
1. Build a negative keyword list: start with a general list of AI-cliché words/phrases (source's own example: em dashes) and keep appending anything the model produces that you personally dislike; re-attach this list on every generation.
2. Build a tone-of-voice document: paste one or more pieces of writing you personally wrote and liked (a blog post, email, or LinkedIn post — the source's own example got only 7 likes; performance doesn't matter, only that it sounds like you) into a "tone-of-voice GPT" referenced in course resources. Read the generated tone doc, correct anything wrong, save as a Google Doc. `[verify UI: exact custom GPT name/link]`
3. Start a new ChatGPT chat with the course's "copywriting prompt" (referenced, not fully quoted in the transcript — see Section 9), insert the target keyword/question in brackets, and attach both the tone-of-voice doc and the negative keyword list as files.
4. The prompt is built to make the model ask clarifying questions first (audience, primary goal) — answer them fully. Model used: GPT-5, Auto.
5. Model researches and proposes an outline; approve or amend it, then say "write the whole thing." It should open in ChatGPT's Canvas feature so you can edit inline (e.g. deleting em dashes by hand).
6. Non-negotiable per source: back every factual claim with an outbound citation link, especially for YMYL ("your money, your life": finance, health, legal, safety) topics. The prompt is also set to append an FAQ section at the end.
7. Move the draft into a Google Doc (not straight into the CMS). Read it as the subject-matter expert; add a first-hand experience/story if you have one (an extra ranking-signal tick per the source).
8. Add one supporting visual: paste the article text into Napkin.ai ("new napkin"), select all, generate; review/adjust styling; download as PNG. `[verify UI]` `[affiliate-likely]`
9. Convert the PNG to WebP (source names convert.io / "PNG to WebP" tools) before uploading, since PNGs are called out as a major cause of slow pages.
10. Add internal links from the new article to the transactional/service page it supports.
11. Human QA pass before publishing.

### 3.5 Local SEO — Google Business Profile (GBP) setup
1. Claim and verify the profile (requires a real physical address; presenter notes this step is getting harder due to anti-fraud measures and cannot be demoed on camera). `[verify UI]`
2. Find primary + secondary categories using a custom GPT ("Google Business Profile category finder") fed the business URL/description plus Google's official categories CSV. Categories differ by country. `[verify UI]`
3. Fill in: description (must fit under ~750 characters — GPT can trim to fit), opening date, phone, chat/WhatsApp option (optional), option to hide the exact address, full service-area list at suburb level (not just the main city) — verify each suburb/place name against the correct country (example given: "Vermont" exists in both the US and Victoria, Australia), and any accurate business attributes (e.g. woman-owned, LGBTQ+-friendly).
4. Add a direct booking/contact link if one exists.
5. Seed the Q&A section by asking a friend or past customer to post your most frequently asked questions, then answer them publicly yourself (asking your own questions is pointless).
6. Upload a logo and cover photo, then upload real business/team/service photos continually, aiming for 100 total images (see Section 4 for the benchmark).
7. Post at least weekly: rewrite an existing blog post into a GBP "update" using this near-verbatim prompt from the source — "rewrite this for a Google My Business post. Make sure that it follows the same tone of voice and it adds the same value from the blog post. And make sure that it's less than 1,450 characters in length." Attach an image; set the CTA button to "Learn more" and link it back to the full blog post (drives extra site traffic). Also use "offer" and "event" post types for sales/events.
8. List services under the GBP "services" section, including services that don't map to an official category (source examples: "therapeutic gardening," "NDIS support coordination") — and make sure services/locations claimed match exactly between the website and GBP.
9. Get the GBP review link and its QR code (Google auto-generates one, or make your own); print it on physical materials. Source's strongest stated finding: asking for a review in person after service beats every automated text-message reminder they've tried. Respond to every review, negative and positive.

### 3.8 Technical SEO — site speed
1. Test every important page (homepage, top category/service pages) in at least two independent tools, since results can disagree: GTmetrix (free tier fine for a handful of sites; paid tier lets you change test server location) and Google PageSpeed Insights (free, splits mobile vs desktop). `[verify UI]`
2. If the two disagree significantly, run a third free check: Pingdom Tools. `[verify UI]`
3. Read the GTmetrix report's list of heavy assets. Fix the worst offenders first (source's own example: a heavy video, and a heavy PNG image).
4. Convert PNG -> WebP with a free tool (source uses convert.io) before uploading — WebP was "developed by Google to keep websites speedy," with no visible quality loss in the source's side-by-side comparison. If the platform doesn't accept WebP, compress the original with TinyPNG instead (free tier: ~10 images/day).
5. Optimize mobile first (Google's stated mobile-first indexing standard, and most consumer traffic is mobile except heavily B2B sites).
6. To make a jargon-heavy GTmetrix PDF report actionable, feed it to a custom GPT ("GTmetrics report analyzer") for a plain-language, prioritized checklist (example output: put static assets behind a CDN, compress images, convert to WebP). `[verify UI]`
7. Re-test after each fix to confirm it actually helped, rather than assuming it did (this specific "re-test" instruction is this playbook's standard-practice addition, consistent with the source's own iterative approach elsewhere — mark accordingly if surfaced as a rule).

### 3.9 Technical SEO — metadata, schema, and CRO
1. Metadata audit: install the SEO Wallet browser extension (free); open the sidebar on each important page. `[verify UI]` Fix: one H1 per page, using the actual target keyword/phrase (not a tagline); sequential heading order (H1->H2->H3->H4, no skipped levels); a meta description that ends with a CTA and fits ~155 characters.
2. Schema audit: check the extension's structured-data panel AND Google's official Rich Results Test — the source found these two tools can disagree, so cross-check both rather than trusting one. `[verify UI]`
3. To identify which schema types a given page needs, use this near-verbatim prompt from the source (ChatGPT, web search enabled, paste the page URL): "Have a look at this website and tell me all the different schemas that it needs from an SEO perspective." Then ask it to generate the JSON-LD code for a specific type (source's example instruction: "build the local business schema").
4. Apply schema only to money pages (homepage, transactional category/product/service pages) — not to About/Contact. How to inject the code into the page header is CMS-specific; the source explicitly does not cover this ("you're going to have to GPT that or Google that").
5. CRO audit: screenshot the full page (source uses the "GoFullPage" Chrome extension), feed the PNG + URL to a GPT-5 Auto session for a critique of the above-the-fold value proposition and CTA clarity. `[verify UI]`
6. Apply the fixes: one clear headline naming the actual offer (not a vague tagline), one visible primary CTA (avoid the "paradox of choice" of multiple competing CTAs), and validate with heatmap tools (Hotjar, Microsoft Clarity) which the source says show declining engagement the more a visitor has to scroll before understanding the offer.

### 3.10 Link building
1. Free method — Featured.com (formerly HARO): create a free account (~3 answers/month on the free tier). Clear category filters, scan publisher questions in the "Questions" tab for ones you can answer with real expertise, save them, then answer from the "Saved" list. `[verify UI]`
2. Before answering, check the publisher's domain authority with a free tool (source uses an Ahrefs free website-authority checker) — treat DA as a rough guide only, per the source's own recurring caveat about tool data.
3. Write the answer using genuine first-hand experience — the source states this is what differentiates a published answer from the crowd. Submission goes to editorial review; publication (and the backlink) is not guaranteed. Set a monthly recurring reminder to keep using free answers, since new questions cycle constantly.
4. Paid method — vendors named: Get Me Links (source's disclosed affiliate/personal relationship with the CEO) and The Hoth (used but not affiliated, offered "so it doesn't seem too one-sided"). `[affiliate link — Get Me Links]` `[web check: current vendor reputation/pricing]` Both offer a free strategy/consultation call before committing; packages are segmented by industry (some industries are named as harder to get links for); track each delivered link's anchor text, target URL, and linking site.
5. Vetting any link source or prospect (paid or organic): check the referring-domains growth trend against a paid tool (Ahrefs cheap ~$29/month tier named vs. a pricier ~$129/month tier the source calls not worth it for this — `[web check current pricing]`). Gradual, natural growth over years = good sign. A sudden, extreme spike is a red flag unless traceable to one specific, legitimately viral piece of content.
6. Expect no ROI signal from backlinking for roughly 3-6 months — unlike paid search's fast feedback loop.

### 3.11 Measurement / analytics
1. Use Google Search Console (GSC) for "how Google sees my website" (rankings, average position, CTR, indexing) and Google Analytics (GA) for "what visitors do on my website" (traffic sources, on-site behavior) — keep the two separate rather than conflating them.
2. In GSC, check three query categories: your own brand name (you should rank #1 for it — if not, that's a real problem), core product/service keywords, and informational/blog keywords. Use the Pages report to spot indexing issues (crawling does not guarantee indexing).
3. Export GSC's last-28-days performance data as CSV -> feed it to a custom GPT ("Google Search Console data expert") for plain-language, prioritized recommendations instead of manually parsing the export. `[verify UI]`
4. Low-hanging-fruit tactic: in the Pages/Queries report (sorted by average position, rows expanded to ~50), look for pages sitting around position 9-10 (borderline page 2) up through roughly position 11-30. These are already indexed and getting some visibility — push them up via on-page fixes, added internal links from new supporting content, or targeted backlinks, rather than starting a new page from zero.
5. In GA, go to Reports -> Traffic acquisition (28 days) to see channel/source breakdown; drill into Source/Medium. Compare quality (engagement/session length, conversions), not just volume — a smaller source can convert better than a larger one. Investigate any referral source sending good-quality traffic as a potential partnership (source's example: reaching out to a niche referral site that was quietly sending well-engaged traffic).
6. Check device/tech breakdown roughly quarterly; a mismatch between where traffic comes from and where conversions happen (e.g. desktop-heavy traffic but mobile converting better) flags a device-specific UX problem to fix.
7. Watch for seasonality in the data (source examples: a PC-parts retailer spiking around GPU launches; a landscaping business slowing in winter) and plan content/promotions around it.
8. Do not build an elaborate dashboard unless you already have one free — a free Google Looker Studio template, or feeding exports straight into an LLM, is enough to make decisions; the source explicitly avoids over-complicating this section. `[verify UI]`

### 3.12 AI automations (no-code, using Pabbly + OpenRouter in the demo)
1. Choose a platform by skill/scale: Pabbly (easiest, lowest friction, used for the demo), Make/Integromat (more flexible, more integrations, the presenter's own day-to-day tool), or n8n (open source, self-hostable, needs some technical comfort, best for productizing automation at scale). `[verify UI/pricing for all three]`
2. Create an OpenRouter account for one API key across many LLMs. Test/compare flagship models side by side on the same prompt in OpenRouter's own chat before choosing. Add credits ($5 recommended as going "a very long way" with cheap models). Create a named API key per use case; copy and save it immediately (shown only once). `[verify UI]`
3. **Automation A — Instagram media to GBP.** Requires admin access to both accounts. Trigger: Instagram, "new media updated to the account." Action: Google Business Profile, "upload media/photo," mapping the Instagram post's media URL. Test, verify the asset appears on the GBP photos tab, then turn the workflow on. Purpose: work toward the 100-image GBP target automatically.
4. **Automation B — blog post to GBP post via RSS.** Trigger: RSS feed, "new item in feed" (for most WordPress sites the feed is at `yoursite.com/feed`; other platforms need their own feed URL looked up). Action: OpenRouter chat completion using a model with native internet access (source picks Perplexity's "Sonar" model, also notes GPT has a web-enabled option) so it can read the post URL directly rather than needing a separate scraper. Prompt instructs the model to read the post and rewrite it in the same tone of voice under ~1,450-1,500 characters, optionally adding a CTA line and suppressing hashtags. Action: GBP "create an action post," mapping the model's output as the message, CTA type "Learn more," CTA URL = the original blog post (drives return traffic). Test, confirm, enable.
5. **Automation C — auto-reply to positive reviews only.** Trigger: GBP "new review." Filter: star rating greater than 4 (only 5-star reviews proceed automatically) — explicit safety rule: never auto-post AI replies to negative reviews; those need a human. Action: OpenRouter chat completion with a cheap/fast model (GPT-5 Nano or Mini named as sufficient; free models work but hit volume limits) using a filled-in "review responder" template that also defines a fallback tone for below-3-star reviews (thank, apologize, give one proactive next step and a contact channel) in case that branch is ever used manually. Optional safety step: route the AI draft to a Google Sheet for human review before publishing, rather than auto-posting. Action: GBP "create a reply" (the current, non-legacy action), mapping the model's output; the latest review is targeted automatically. Test, enable.
6. Precondition for all three: confirm site/technical/content fundamentals are already sound before automating (Principle 16-17) — the source frames automation as an amplifier of whatever state your SEO is already in, good or bad.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Keyword data sanity check**: does a "0 search volume" or "0 difficulty" keyword actually match what your niche knowledge says people search for? If yes, target it anyway; don't let the tool overrule domain knowledge. [b2ab9292f6baea94828d8182]
- **Structure check before build**: does every planned page have a single parent, a clear URL, and a planned internal link path, with no orphan pages? [3a48ad90c5ed4567c17115cb]
- **Content quality check**: does the draft avoid your growing negative-keyword list, match your tone-of-voice doc, cite an external source for every factual claim, and link internally to a commercial page? If any of these are missing, it's not ready to publish. [0b71c176e2e3b50f1e2b8b42, 205809992abac042eaa04fc3]
- **Metadata check (per page)**: exactly one H1 using the real target keyword (not a tagline); sequential heading order H1->H2->H3->H4 with no skipped levels; meta description under ~155 characters ending in a CTA. Use SEO Wallet or an equivalent extension. [329b823585e94ee681c95fa9]
- **Schema check**: cross-check the browser extension's structured-data panel against Google's Rich Results Test — don't trust either tool alone, since the source found them disagree. [1da946decc85bb358ebbb31b]
- **Speed check**: run GTmetrix and PageSpeed Insights (add Pingdom if they disagree); grade below B or load time over 3s = fix now; check mobile score specifically (target 80+), since mobile is usually the dominant, and Google's primary, index. [07eb3c368d7af47fb8a3e8fb, b75a8d6d1f402ece740ac520]
- **First-fold CRO check**: does a first-time visitor see, without scrolling, what the business offers and the one action to take? Screenshot the page and ask an AI assistant to critique the hero copy and CTA if you're unsure. [abc8640905828cd106a0656a]
- **Backlink-prospect check**: is the referring-domains growth curve gradual/natural, or does it show sudden unnatural spikes? Is the linking site actually relevant to your industry/locality/niche? [691b416017c3b96ea709f878]
- **GBP consistency check**: do the services and locations claimed on the website match the GBP exactly? [697ec7cc36485ed3a6729621, b38166a6f51b76a1faa0cdf0]
- **Indexing check**: in GSC's Pages report, is a page that gets little traffic actually indexed at all? Crawling is not the same as indexing. [e247cdff3fb7b0e6e555be74]
- **Traffic-quality check**: for each acquisition source in GA, look past raw volume to engagement/conversion — a smaller source converting well beats a larger one that doesn't. [411ebd9a5b1eaec0f5f27e08]
- **Automation readiness check**: are the fundamentals (speed, indexing, metadata, schema, converting first fold) already solid before you wire up automation? If not, fix those first — automating a broken foundation amplifies the breakage. [3b53ab90208b20908f3aaa9e]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating SEO-tool metrics (search volume, keyword difficulty, domain authority) as an absolute source of truth instead of a guide. [b2ab9292f6baea94828d8182, fb7381a080bfe5e6f737ebf3]
- Chasing high-volume, high-competition "shiny object" keywords on a new/low-authority site instead of easier, lower-volume wins. [d2b1c9b1538ff1ce1ae5973f]
- Building a website (including via AI website builders) without planning structure first — leads to months of rework and possible content-duplication problems. [3a48ad90c5ed4567c17115cb]
- Mass-producing spammy, unedited AI content, or copying 100%-AI sites that happen to rank ("SEO is a bit weird like that... don't try to copy them, it probably won't work for you"). [b95c25b2d3c82e9316839d89]
- Using an H1 as a marketing tagline instead of the actual target keyword phrase. [329b823585e94ee681c95fa9]
- Skipping heading hierarchy (jumping from H2 to H4). [329b823585e94ee681c95fa9]
- Adding only one schema block to a homepage and assuming that covers the whole site. [1da946decc85bb358ebbb31b]
- Buying cheap bulk backlink packages ("1,000 backlinks for $100 or $1,000" — "politely run the other way"), or accepting links from contextually irrelevant sites. [691b416017c3b96ea709f878]
- Assuming any hired agency built its own backlinks rather than having bought them from a vendor like the one it recommends — presented as the "dirty little secret of SEO," the presenter's own claimed 99% estimate, not a cited statistic. [691b416017c3b96ea709f878]
- Over-celebrating ranking-position improvements to a client without tying them to actual conversions/revenue — the presenter names this as his own past mistake. [fb7381a080bfe5e6f737ebf3]
- Auto-replying to negative Google reviews with AI without human review. [3707612ddae8e67144e67fb7]
- Automating SEO workflows before the underlying fundamentals (speed, indexing, metadata, schema, converting first fold) are solid. [3b53ab90208b20908f3aaa9e, 39455049b596506d432ef63d]
- Analysis paralysis: pulling more data than you will actually act on. [e247cdff3fb7b0e6e555be74]
- Not verifying an ambiguous place name against the correct country/region before publishing local pages (the "Vermont, USA vs Vermont, Australia" example). [b38166a6f51b76a1faa0cdf0]
