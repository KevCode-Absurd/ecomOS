## 3. Procedures

### A. Decide whether SEO is worth pursuing
1. Ask: do people already search for what you sell? If your product is genuinely novel with no existing search demand, skip SEO. [6d35da6040757178]
2. Check Google search volume and commercial intent for your main category terms. 3. Check your margin math: the source says low AOV or thin margins make content investment unlikely to pay back, but gives no numeric threshold.
4. If checks 1-3 pass, commit to bottom-funnel, conversion-intent keywords rather than general blog traffic.

### B. Multiply one collection page into keyword-variant pages
1. Pick your bestselling product category (source example: skincare, or hoodies).
2. List the different ways people search for it (source examples for hoodies: men's hoodies, oversized hoodies, zip hoodies, running hoodies, hoodies under $50; for skincare: anti-aging face serum, vegan face serum, facial creams under $30).
3. Build a separate collection page per variation, reusing the same underlying products — you are changing presentation, not inventory.
4. Validate each keyword in Ahrefs or Semrush: set the keyword difficulty filter below 35, and check for commercial intent (the tools have a commercial-intent indicator per the source). `[verify UI]` [0120e72eaf946147]
5. Sanity-check by Googling the keyword: if other collection pages already rank, you can likely rank too; if only giants (source example: Nike) rank, it's probably too competitive.
6. Enforce one keyword per page. Never let a product page and a collection page compete for the same keyword.

### C. Build the four buyer-intent blog formats
1. Stop writing seasonal/lifestyle/generic listicle posts (source examples: "how to stay cozy in winter," "five fun facts about skincare," "top fashion trends this year").
2. Before writing any post, check the SERP for your target term: if blog posts are ranking, write a blog; if only collection pages rank, build a collection page instead.
3. Write in one (or more) of four formats:
   a. You vs. competitor — target "brand A vs brand B" searches; mention the competitor by name to try to rank for their branded search and present yourself as the stronger option.
   b. Alternatives to competitor — target "alternatives to X" / "best X alternatives" searches; list options with yourself positioned first.
   c. Best [product type] for [use case] — target clear buying-intent, longtail searches (source example: "best eye serums for women over 50").
   d. Buying guides — long-form evergreen content for curious-but-hesitant buyers (source examples: "the complete buying guide to a sauna," "how to choose the right skincare routine").
4. The source claims a system of 10-15 well-targeted posts across these formats is enough; it gives no method for choosing exactly which topics beyond the four formats and the SERP check in step 2.
5. Anecdote given as a result, not a guarantee: the source's SEO coach, Kai Cromwell, helped a client called Simbotanica implement two competitor-focused formats, and the client reportedly ranked #3 for a competitor's brand name within about a week. `[web check: verify Simbotanica outcome independently]`

### D. Build an internal-linking system to money pages
1. Identify your "money pages" — the collection/product pages where conversion happens.
2. When writing a new top- or mid-funnel blog post, decide the internal-linking flow before publishing, not after.
3. Link from the blog to a money page within the first 100 to 200 words of the article.
4. Add 3 to 5 internal links to related blog posts later in the same article.
5. Use descriptive anchor text of 2 to 4 words that matches the product being linked to. Do not use "click here" or your brand name as anchor text.
6. Link collections from general to specific (source example: a collagen supplements collection linking down to a "hair loss collagen for women" sub-collection).
7. Retrofit existing content: start with your top 2-3 highest-traffic blog posts and add one clean, relevant link from each to your bestselling collection or product page.
8. Repeat across the site until it forms a connected web of links.

### E. Optimize for AI-generated search answers (GEO)
1. Split broad collections into specific use-case pages, reusing the same products but matching the exact query language a shopper would use (source examples: "mineral sunscreen for sensitive skin," "mineral sunscreen for dry skin").
2. Refresh or write comparison-format blogs (brand A vs brand B, best-X-for-use-case) since the source's cited study found comparison content and buying guides are disproportionately cited by AI tools.
3. Test visibility: Google your main product type phrased as a question (source example: "What hoodie is best for me?"). If an AI Overview appears, study which pages it cites.
4. Audit each collection: could it be split into more specific individual needs or situations?
5. Make cited-candidate pages skimmable, useful, and product-focused, matching the query as closely as possible and structured to answer a specific question or comparison directly.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating SEO as a default box to tick rather than deciding up front whether the category has real search demand and margin to support it.
- Chasing a single broad, highly competitive keyword (e.g., "hoodies") on one collection page instead of splitting into narrower, winnable variants.
- Keyword cannibalization: letting a product page and a collection page compete for the same keyword, which the source says "will tank your results."
- Writing generic, seasonal, or lifestyle blog content that gets a few clicks but does not rank long-term or convert, and dilutes site authority.
- Publishing blog content without checking the SERP first to see whether blogs or collection pages actually rank for that term.
- Leaving product pages, blog posts, and collections internally unlinked (orphaned pages, links only in header/footer) instead of deliberately funneling authority to money pages.
- Using vague anchor text ("click here") or brand-name anchor text instead of descriptive, product-matching anchor text.
- Running paid ads that build brand awareness without also making sure your own site ranks for the resulting branded search, letting a competitor capture the conversion.
- Ignoring AI-generated search answers as a citation surface and continuing to write only for the traditional ten-blue-links SERP.
- Treating a broad collection or blog post as good enough for AI citation instead of splitting it into specific, question-shaped, use-case content.
