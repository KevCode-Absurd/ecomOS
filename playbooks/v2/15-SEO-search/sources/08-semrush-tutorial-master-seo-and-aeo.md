## 1. What this source is for

A single-session walkthrough of Semrush One (SEO toolkit + AI Visibility toolkit) for a merchant who has never used it: research what people search on Google and ask AI, turn that into a content plan, fix technical site and backlink problems, then track Google rankings and AI/LLM visibility together. Its job for 15-SEO-search is the concrete tool-by-tool workflow, the default filter values and thresholds the presenter used, and the one worked example (Brightland/olive oil) that shows what "good" and "needs work" output looks like inside this specific tool. It is not independent SEO strategy; it is a product demo that happens to teach the underlying SEO/AEO concepts (search intent, keyword difficulty, backlink authority, AI answer visibility) along the way.

## 2. Principles

1. **Keywords and AI prompts are the same underlying intent on two platforms.** What people type into Google and what they ask ChatGPT/Perplexity/Gemini are treated as the same research question, just captured through a different tool (Keyword Magic Tool vs. Prompt Research). Condition: applies whenever planning what topics to cover. Evidence: source states this directly as "the same pattern... same intent, just different platforms." [1449fb3da4a7] [9e06293fa0bb]
2. **Start with low-difficulty, decent-volume "low hanging fruit," not the biggest keyword.** New or growing sites are more likely to rank for, and get real traffic from, easier terms than for the single highest-volume seed keyword. Condition: applies when just starting keyword research on a new project. Evidence: presenter's stated recommendation, illustrated with "Is olive oil a seed oil?" at 14,800 searches/month and low KD. [1449fb3da4a7]
3. **An AI Overview above the organic results means you are also competing for the AI answer, not just page one.** When the SERP preview shows an AI-generated answer before organic listings, ranking well no longer guarantees visibility to the searcher. Condition: applies per keyword, check the SERP feature before treating a keyword as "just SEO." Evidence: shown directly in the Keyword Magic Tool's SERP analysis panel. [1449fb3da4a7]
4. **Comprehensive topic coverage beats one post.** The source frames one blog post as making a brand "a footnote," while multiple articles covering health benefits, cooking tips, varieties, etc. make the brand "the expert." Condition: applies to content planning for a topic area, not a single page. Evidence: presenter's stated framing, not a measured result. [041545a9] [e82c9ec6e9]
5. **A competitor's organic strength is also a likely AI-citation target.** If a competitor ranks highly for a topic in Google, the source treats that as a signal AI tools may also be pulling from that competitor's content, so competitor research doubles as an AEO target list. Condition: applies when prioritizing which competitor topics to challenge. Evidence: stated directly, no data given. [e656b0848]
6. **Backlinks help both Google trust and AI citation.** The source claims AI tools "tend to cite and reference sources that other websites also reference," so backlink building is framed as serving SEO and AEO at once. Condition: applies to backlink and outreach prioritization. Evidence: presenter's stated claim, not a cited study. [2b4bd20978] [c5e862cbcad]
7. **A few high-quality, relevant backlinks beat many low-quality ones.** Condition: choosing where to spend outreach effort. Evidence: presenter's stated opinion; no quantification of "worth more" given. [c5e862cbcad]
8. **Errors before warnings before notices.** Site Audit issues should be worked in strict severity order: red critical errors first, then orange warnings, then blue notices last (optional). Condition: applies to any Site Audit issue list. Evidence: stated directly as the recommended order; source explicitly says don't aim for a perfect score, just reduce the count. [978afa7cb]
9. **Don't optimize a score to perfection.** Both Site Audit health and SEO Writing Assistant score are meant to be improved, not maximized to 100%/10 — the source repeatedly says results won't be perfect and that's fine. Condition: applies whenever using either scoring tool. Evidence: stated directly for both tools. [978afa7cb] [4fdf9241a]
10. **SEO and AEO are a loop, not a one-time project.** The source frames the whole workflow as: make a change, then watch what happens, repeat — "without tracking, you're just guessing." Condition: applies to the whole program, not any single tool. Evidence: stated directly as the closing framing. [e8139b1b3]
11. **Regional AI visibility numbers can hide the real picture.** Checking "worldwide" AI visibility can understate a brand's true relevance if its audience is concentrated in one country; the source recommends selecting the brand's primary market region instead. Condition: applies before reading any AI Visibility Overview score. Evidence: shown directly — Brightland's worldwide AI visibility score reads low largely because the US (its dominant market) is diluted by the rest of the world. [7a78cc534]

### 3.2 Keyword Magic Tool: build a keyword opportunity list [1449fb3da4a7] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Keyword Magic Tool**.
2. Enter one broad seed keyword tied to the business (example: "olive oil").
3. Read the output table columns: **Volume** (monthly searches), **KD** (Keyword Difficulty %, higher = harder), **Intent** (I/N/C/T — see rules table).
4. Apply the **KD filter**: set a custom range of 0–49 to strip the hardest terms. (The source notes the "difficult" range starts at 50% and up.)
5. Apply a **Volume filter**: minimum 5,000 searches/month, to keep only terms with real demand.
6. Use the **topic cluster column** on the left of the results table to drill into categories for more ideas.
7. Click the small icon next to any keyword to open the **mini Keyword Overview**: global + per-country volume, KD, search features, search intent, CPC, trend over time, related questions with their own volume, related keywords, and a **SERP analysis** (a preview of the actual Google results page for that term, including whether an AI Overview appears above the organic results).
8. Prioritize low-KD, decent-volume terms over the single biggest keyword (see Principle 2).
9. Add chosen keywords to a named list via the plus button to the left of each row. First time: name the list (example: "Month 1 Focus"), confirm with the check mark. Aim for **10–15 keyword opportunities** in the first list.

### 3.3 Prompt Research: what people ask AI [9e06293fa0bb] [verify UI]
1. AI Visibility toolkit > **Prompt Research**.
2. Enter a high-level topic (example: "olive oil"). Click **Analyse**.
3. Read the summary bar: total monthly AI-search volume across the topic (example: 3.2 million), number of subtopics (706), prompts (19,900), intent types (5), brands mentioned (15,300), source domains (23,300).
4. Drill into a subtopic (example: "olive oil and its benefits") using the left-side arrow to see individual prompts, the AI response, brands mentioned, and cited sources.
5. Click **View Full Response** on a specific prompt (example: "best olive oils to buy") to read the full AI-generated answer: top paragraph, named brands, resources listed, and all cited sources.
6. Scan whether your brand is mentioned in that answer and how prominently versus competitors.
7. Decide targeting: if the brand is absent or buried for a commercially relevant prompt, treat it as a content or page-copy opportunity.
8. Optionally click **Create Content** to route the prompt into Semrush's **Content toolkit** — an add-on beyond Semrush One, with its own 7-day trial, giving access to the AI Article Generator and SEO Brief Generator. [web check: current Content toolkit pricing and trial terms]

### 3.4 Competitor Organic Rankings [e656b0848] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Organic Rankings**.
2. Enter a competitor's domain (example: graza.co).
3. Review their top keywords, which SERP features they capture, and how their keyword mix by intent has shifted over time (example: the competitor gets most traffic from informational keywords).
4. Track how their positions and SERP features change over time to spot rising or declining topics.
5. Treat any topic they rank highly for as a candidate AEO target too (Principle 5).

### 3.5 Keyword Gap: Missing and Weak tabs [72620276] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Keyword Gap**. Enter your domain in the first field.
2. Click **Add Domain**; pick Semrush-suggested competitors from the dropdown (add up to 3 more) or type domains manually. Click **Compare**.
3. Open the **Missing** tab: keywords competitors rank for where your column shows 0 (you don't rank at all). Example: graza.co ranks 16th for "olive oil smoke point"; Brightland does not appear.
4. Open the **Weak** tab: keywords you do rank for, but far below competitors. Example: two competitors rank #1 and #2 for "high quality extra virgin olive oil"; Brightland ranks 36th. That term has KD 41 and 1,000+ monthly searches — flagged as a strong target because the gap is large and the difficulty is moderate.
5. Add chosen keywords to your keyword list via the plus button.
6. Pick a handful of important terms to actively compete on; push into the content plan.

### 3.6 Keyword Strategy Builder: cluster into a content calendar [041545a9] [verify UI]
1. Open **Keyword Strategy Builder**, select an existing list (example: "Month 1 Focus").
2. Read the quick stats. Worked example: 21 keywords, 136,230 combined monthly search volume, 20.05% average KD.
3. Sort/filter by volume, keyword, KD%, or CPC to organize priorities.
4. Click **Cluster This List**. If the tool warns "This list has too few keywords to create profitable pages," either add more keywords via Keyword Magic Tool or click **Cluster List Anyway** (results improve with more keywords per the tool's own warning). Processing can take a few minutes.
5. Review the resulting clusters — each cluster is meant to become one page or piece of content. Expand a cluster to see its recommended target keywords and primary intent. Worked example: a cluster for "high quality extra virgin olive oil" targets 6 keywords, primary intent commercial, ~22% average KD, 55,900 combined monthly search volume.
6. From a cluster, send keywords to **Position Tracking** (click Send Keywords > Position Tracking > Apply), to **Create Content or Brief** (Content toolkit, paid add-on), or **Export** as a spreadsheet.
7. Pick the **first 5 clusters** as the content calendar for the next month.

### 3.7 Topic Research: proven headlines and questions [e82c9ec6e9] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Topic Research**. Enter a high-level topic (example: "olive oil"), click **Get Content Ideas**.
2. Review subtopic ideas (example outputs: Mediterranean Diet, Type 2 Diabetes, Heart Disease, Anti-Inflammatory, Olive Oil Benefits).
3. Re-search using a longer keyword from your keyword strategy list (example: "best olive oil") for more specific ideas (example outputs: Side By Side, Grocery Stores, Cold Pressed, Organic Extra Virgin Olive Oil), each with a potential-volume figure shown.
4. Click into a subtopic to see headline ideas ranked best-to-worst by a resonance signal. Worked example: one referenced article has 552 backlinks; you can open the original article to study why it resonated.
5. Favorite headlines you like and audience questions surfaced (example: "What is the best olive oil to buy in the USA?", "What is the best olive oil brand?", "What are the best organic 100% olive oils?").
6. Collect favorites in the **Favorite Ideas** tab as a content planning board. Aim for **10–15 favorited ideas** across a few topics — this becomes the article queue.
7. Write from the pattern: mix and match the strongest parts of top-performing headlines into your own headline, then answer the favorited questions inside the article body.

### 3.8 SEO Writing Assistant: score and fix a draft [4fdf9241a] [verify UI]
1. Open **SEO Writing Assistant**, click **Analyse My Text**.
2. Import from an existing URL, or paste the draft text directly.
3. Paste target keywords from the Keyword Strategy Builder cluster into the provided field so the tool scores against them.
4. Click **Get Recommendations**. Read the overall score (10-point scale; worked example: 6.6/10, described by the source as "good" with room for improvement) and the axis chart (tone of voice, readability, SEO, originality — worked example shows tone and readability strong, SEO and originality weaker/red).
5. Work through the left-side issue list. Click an issue to see the proposed fix in a floating bar; **accept**, **reject**, or **retry**. Worked examples: a title flagged as "longer than 60 characters — could be truncated in search results" (fixed by removing filler words like "actually" and "taste"), and a "hard to read sentence, consider rewriting" flagged with a **Simplify** option.
6. Iterate. The source notes new issues can surface after fixing others (the worked example goes from 51 issues to 50 after one fix), and the score is unlikely to hit a perfect 10 — the worked example improved by 0.3 overall. Aim to reduce the issue count and raise the score, not to reach perfection (Principle 9).

### 3.9 Site Audit: fix errors first, warnings second [978afa7cb] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Site Audit**, select the project created from the domain entered at setup.
2. Read the **Site Health** indicator. Worked example: 87% vs. a stated "top 10% of websites" benchmark of 92% (a 5-point gap).
3. Check the **AI search health** score separately. Worked example: 75%, with 80% flagged as the number needed to "better compete with other websites in AI search."
4. Open the **Errors** count first (most severe). Issues are grouped by type and number of pages affected. Worked example: "Two pages returned 4XX status code."
5. Verify a broken link by clicking the small square-with-arrow icon to open the page directly; a 404 confirms it goes nowhere real.
6. Use the **How to Fix** link on each issue type — it explains the cause and gives remediation steps. Use the **Share** button to hand the issue to whoever owns the fix.
7. Work order: red critical **errors** first, then orange **warnings**, then blue **notices** last (Principle 8). Don't aim for zero issues — reduce the count.
8. Return to the **Overview** tab. Check the **AI Search Health** panel for "Blocked from AI search." Worked example: 100 pages crawled, 4 blocked per AI tool. Click **How To Unblock Pages** — the tool's recommendation in this example was to review the robots.txt file.
9. Treat this as a recurring check, not a one-off, ahead of any content or merchandising push.

### 3.10 robots.txt and llms.txt [8f6ede777] [verify UI] [web check: current AI-bot support for llms.txt]
1. From the Site Audit AI Search Health panel, note pages blocked from AI search and follow the **How To Unblock Pages** link.
2. Understand the split the source gives: **robots.txt** tells Google (and other traditional crawlers) which pages to crawl. **llms.txt** is described as doing "the same job for AI tools" — telling ChatGPT, Perplexity and others which pages are most important and what they cover.
3. If missing, create **llms.txt** as a plain text file at `yourdomain.com/llms.txt` describing the site's most important pages and topics.
4. Use the issue-specific **How to Fix** link for exact guidance.
5. Re-run the Site Audit afterward to confirm the blocked-page count drops.
6. **Caveat, not in the source's own words but attached to this card by the ecomOS review**: Google Search does not use or require `llms.txt`; it is an emerging, not-universally-adopted convention. Treat any AI tool's actual support for it as unverified until documented by that engine. [web check: current llms.txt adoption by ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI]

### 3.11 Backlinks overview [2b4bd20978] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Link Building** > **Backlinks**. Enter the domain, click **Analyse**.
2. Read the top bar: referring domains, total links, monthly visits driven. Worked example: 2,600 referring sites, 28,000 links, 161,000 monthly visits.
3. Check **Authority Score** (Semrush's own metric combining backlink quantity/quality and site authenticity). Worked example: 47, described by the source as "pretty good," higher is better. View the trend panel alongside it.
4. Open the **Backlinks** tab; filter **Best** to see the links raising authority the most (from reputable sites).
5. Filter **Lost and Vital** to find links that used to point to the site and no longer do.
6. For a high-value, high-authority lost link, reach out to investigate — it may be a broken link needing an update, or a deliberate removal. Either way it's worth trying to restore (Principle 6/7 context).

### 3.12 Backlink Gap: outreach checklist [c5e862cbcad] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Competitive Analysis** > **Backlink Gap**.
2. Confirm the domain sits in the first field ("you"), add several competitors, click **Find Prospects**.
3. Read columns: referring domain, its authority score, **Matches** (how many of the input domains it links to — 0 means it doesn't link to that destination at all), and links per destination.
4. Filter by **Authority Score** with a lower threshold — the source uses **50** — and click **Apply**.
5. Scan for a domain that links to several competitors but not to you. Worked example: the Food Network links to 3 competitors but not to Brightland, has a high authority score and 22.5 million monthly visits — flagged as a strong outreach target.
6. Check the box next to a target referring domain and click **Start Outreach** to open the **Backlink Builder** tool for managing the campaign (not demoed in the source beyond noting it's straightforward to follow).

### 3.13 Position Tracking [db5cba249] [verify UI]
1. SEO toolkit > **Position Tracking**, select the project. Lands on the **Landscape** tab.
2. Read the headline metrics: visibility score (worked example: 65.72%, with a projected 66.02% over 3 months from small tweaks), estimated traffic, average position per keyword.
3. Scroll to **Rankings Overview** for the full keyword list. Check whether a keyword appears in the AI summary (worked example: "Agave versus honey" shown ranking first in the AI summary).
4. Click the magnifier/browser icon on a keyword to view a cached snapshot of the live search results page.
5. Expand any keyword to see its position history — more history accumulates the longer the account has run.
6. To add keywords from a saved list: **Keyword Strategy Builder** > select the list > tick the checkbox in the upper left > **Send Keywords** > **Position Tracking** > **Apply** > choose the project > confirm. Worked example: 19 keywords sent, 2 skipped as duplicates already tracked.
7. Filter by a specific keyword in the Overview tab to isolate its trend (example: "best olive oil").
8. Recommendation: track the **20–30 most important keywords**, check back **weekly** to see movement in both Google and AI search.

### 3.14 Domain Overview [996431d50] [verify UI]

2. Read the high-level standings for AI search visibility and traditional SEO visibility side by side.
3. Scroll to the traffic-by-type-over-time chart; toggle between AI search and Google search.

2. Set the region to the brand's primary market (worked example: switching from Worldwide to United States for a US-focused brand) — see Principle 11.
3. Read the AI visibility score out of 100 (worked example: 50 — described as "occasionally mentioned by LLM outputs" with room to improve).
4. Review monthly mentions (worked example: ~2,300) and the trend of mentions vs. citations vs. cited pages over time.

### 3.16 Brand Performance: how ChatGPT talks about you [37ca9b962] [verify UI]
1. AI Visibility toolkit > **Brand Performance**. Enter the domain, click **Analyse**, select a target location (worked example: United States), click **Get Started**. Allow time for the report to load.
2. Read the **Insights** panel: how ChatGPT discusses the brand across conversations. Worked example: strong sentiment at 78% but the lowest share of voice among named competitors, with a stated recommendation to push high-visibility campaigns to grow volume without losing positivity.

### 3.17 The four-step loop [e8139b1b3]
1. **Research**: Keyword Magic Tool (Google demand) + Prompt Research (AI demand). Same underlying intent, two platforms.
2. **Organize**: Keyword Strategy Builder to cluster research into topic clusters; pick the content/pages to create; take the first 5 clusters as the month's calendar.
3. **Fix the foundation**: Site Audit for technical errors and AI crawlability (robots.txt, llms.txt); Backlink Gap to close link gaps.
4. **Track**: Position Tracking + Domain Overview for Google rankings and AI visibility side by side. Then repeat from step 1.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **AI Overview presence check.** Before treating any keyword as a pure SEO target, open its mini Keyword Overview and check the SERP analysis panel for an AI Overview above the organic results — if present, it's also an AEO target. [1449fb3da4a7]
- **Keyword-list health check.** Before clustering, read the list's stats (keyword count, total volume, average KD). If the tool warns the list is too small for "profitable pages," add more keywords first for better cluster quality. [041545a9]
- **Brand-mention scan (Prompt Research).** For a commercially relevant prompt, open the full AI response and scan whether the brand is named and how prominently, versus competitors; absence or burial marks it as a content opportunity. [9e06293fa0bb]
- **Broken-link verification.** Don't trust the issue label alone — click through to the actual page and confirm the 404 before prioritizing the fix. [978afa7cb]
- **Site Health trend check.** Compare current health % against Semrush's stated top-10%-of-sites benchmark to gauge the gap, then re-check after each round of fixes — don't chase 100%. [978afa7cb]
- **AI-crawlability check.** In Site Audit's AI Search Health panel, look specifically for "Blocked from AI search" and the count of blocked pages per AI tool; re-run the audit after adding/editing llms.txt to confirm the count drops. [8f6ede777]
- **Lost-link check.** In Backlinks > Lost and Vital, look for any high-authority site that used to link and stopped; that is worth investigating (broken link vs. deliberate removal) before writing off. [2b4bd20978]
- **Position-tracking cadence check.** Once keywords are being tracked, check weekly whether they are moving in Google and whether they appear in AI summaries; a keyword with no history yet won't show a useful trend line. [db5cba249]
- **Region check before reading AI visibility.** Confirm the AI Visibility Overview region matches the brand's actual primary market before trusting the visibility score — a worldwide default can understate a geographically concentrated brand. [7a78cc534]
- **LLM-by-LLM gap check.** In AI Visibility Overview's distribution-by-LLM view, identify which specific LLM under-mentions the brand (example: ChatGPT rarely mentions Brightland while Google AI Overview does most of the work) and prioritize effort there. [7a78cc534]
- **Recommendation-priority check.** In Brand Performance's Strategy Recommendations, sort work by the stated timeframe tag (urgent/short/medium) rather than reading top-to-bottom by section order. [37ca9b962]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Chasing the single biggest seed keyword instead of easier, decent-volume "low hanging fruit" when starting out. [1449fb3da4a7]
- Ignoring the AI Overview shown in a keyword's SERP analysis and optimizing only for organic rank, missing that the AI answer is now often the first thing a searcher sees. [1449fb3da4a7]
- Writing one blog post on a topic and expecting authority; the source says single posts read as a "footnote" versus a multi-article cluster that builds topical expertise. [041545a9] [e82c9ec6e9]
- Clustering a keyword list that is too small — the tool itself warns results will be weaker, though it can still be forced through. [041545a9]
- Chasing a perfect Site Audit score or a perfect 10/10 writing score instead of steadily reducing issues — the source explicitly says perfection isn't realistic or necessary. [978afa7cb] [4fdf9241a]
- Working Site Audit issues out of order (e.g. warnings before critical errors). [978afa7cb]
- Leaving pages blocked from AI crawlers by not having a robots.txt/llms.txt reviewed, missing out on AI-answer visibility even if traditional SEO is fine. [8f6ede777]
- Chasing every single backlink on a list instead of prioritizing high-authority, high-relevance lost or missing links. [2b4bd20978] [c5e862cbcad]
- Reading a worldwide AI Visibility score without switching to the brand's actual primary region, which can make visibility look worse than it is (or hide where the real work is needed). [7a78cc534]
- Treating Semrush's Strategy Recommendations as a flat list instead of working urgent items first, then short-, then medium-timeframe items. [37ca9b962]
- Not tracking results at all after making changes — the source frames this as "just guessing." [e8139b1b3]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Vendor and affiliate bias, high.** This is not neutral SEO instruction: the presenter has partnered with Semrush, promotes a referral link with an extended trial throughout, and walks the viewer through entering payment details. Every tool and workflow shown is a Semrush feature; no alternative tool (Ahrefs, Moz, Google Search Console alone, etc.) is discussed or compared at any point. Read every recommendation as "how to do this inside Semrush," not "the only or best way to do this."
- **All numbers are one demo, not benchmarks.** Every score, percentage, and count in this playbook (Site Health 87%/92%, AI search health 75%/80%, Authority Score 47, AI visibility 50/100, sentiment 78%, all keyword volumes and KDs) comes from a single live pass on one real brand (Brightland) chosen because the presenter had no affiliation with it. None of these are stated as industry averages or targets, and the source itself frames several as "pretty good" or "room to improve" without giving what a bad or excellent score would look like.
- **"Authority Score" is a Semrush-proprietary metric.** It is explicitly not Google's own signal (the tool's own tooltip, quoted in the source, calls it Semrush's measure of reputation). Do not equate it with Domain Authority (Moz) or Domain Rating (Ahrefs) if those terms come up elsewhere; they are different vendors' different formulas.
- **Conflict with general technical-SEO understanding: llms.txt.** The source presents llms.txt as doing "the same job" as robots.txt but for AI tools, implying comparable importance and adoption. As of the source's own framing this is an emerging, not-yet-standardized convention — Google Search itself does not use or require llms.txt. Treat any specific AI tool's actual support for it as unverified until documented by that engine, and do not present it to a client as equivalent in maturity to robots.txt. [web check: current llms.txt adoption across ChatGPT, Perplexity, Gemini, Google AI Overviews/AI Mode]
- **Thin on "how" in several places.** The source shows the AI Article Generator and SEO Brief Generator only by name (Content toolkit, a separate paid add-on) and does not walk through using them. The Backlink Builder outreach tool is opened but not demoed — "pretty straightforward to use, just follow the prompts" is the entire guidance given. The exact mechanics behind Semrush's "resonance" ranking of headlines in Topic Research, or how the AI visibility/brand performance scores are computed, are not explained — they are presented as black-box outputs to read and act on.
- **No cross-source comparison possible.** This is the only source read for this file; compare its click paths, filter defaults and claimed AI-tool behavior against other 15-SEO-search and 16-AEO sources once they exist, since Semrush's own framing of AEO mechanics (e.g. what drives an LLM to cite a source) is asserted, not demonstrated with independent evidence.
- **Pricing and UI will drift fast.** The source itself is explicit that this is a point-in-time walkthrough ($199/month starter plan, specific 14-day trial terms, specific menu locations). Every `[verify UI]` and `[web check]` tag in this file should be re-checked before using this playbook to brief a client, especially given how quickly AI-search product features are changing across the industry.
- **No brand data used or needed for the workflow steps themselves** — all steps are generic tool navigation. Where a real brand would be worked, the domain, competitor list, seed keywords, and target region are brand-specific inputs. [needs brand data: brand's own domain, real competitor domains, actual seed keywords/topics, and primary market/region for AI Visibility and Brand Performance]
