## 1. What this source is for

It gives a Shopify/ecommerce operator a short priority list for SEO: stop obsessing over technical SEO and blog content, and put effort into two things instead — ranking transactional "money pages" and buying a modest number of quality backlinks (mainly link insertions). It also gives a minimal tool stack and a KPI to judge SEO by. It is a strategy and prioritization talk, not a step-by-step technical manual; procedures given are shallow (a short link-outreach sequence, a Screaming Frog crawl) with no settings depth.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 The anti-blog rule: rank money pages, not blog posts
Blog content that targets informational keywords (example given: "why should I use natural face wash") can rank easily but converts near 0%, because a reader has not shown buying intent. Transactional "money pages" — product and collection/category pages tied to buy-ready keywords (example: "natural face wash") — are what should get the SEO investment. Condition: this is a prioritization call for stores whose blog traffic is not converting; the source does not test whether some product categories (higher-consideration purchases) get real assisted value from informational content. [4ebd0ef27d6188d38deef845]

### 2.2 Technical SEO has exactly 4 requirements; everything else is noise
The only technical SEO items the source says matter: (1) key pages must be crawlable and indexed — "if Google cannot find key pages on your site... nothing else matters"; (2) no broken links (404s); (3) good mobile and desktop UX (fast enough, easy to navigate); (4) clear site hierarchy so Google and users know which pages matter most. Everything beyond these four is called a distraction agencies use to sell audits. Meta descriptions are named directly: "haven't even been a ranking factor since 2014." Site speed is downgraded: "it can help but it is absolutely not necessary." Condition: presented as the presenter's own filter, not an industry standard; Google's own guidance does treat page experience/speed as a ranking input, so "not necessary" is his opinion, worth a `[web check: Google's current page-experience/Core Web Vitals ranking guidance]`. ### 2.3 Organic search revenue is the only KPI that matters
Traffic growth, keyword rankings and blog impressions are explicitly downgraded as vanity metrics. "If it's not increasing, your SEO is simply not working." Basis: the $60K-$120K/year agency case (2.7) where traffic and blog output looked fine but revenue stayed flat. [7cc511272fe0dfd8c21cd41d]

### 2.4 Link insertions beat guest posts for cost and certainty
An "insertion" (adding a link into an already-published, already-traffic-getting page) is preferred over a guest post (a newly written, newly published page) because: (a) insertions run 20-30% cheaper than guest posts on average, reason given being it's less work for the site owner (tweak existing content vs. write a new piece); (b) an inserted link is placed on a page that already has proven traffic, while a new guest post has no guarantee of ever getting traffic. Condition: "on average," his own current spend; not a guaranteed ratio for every niche or site. [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]

### 2.5 You need 10-20 good backlinks, not thousands of cheap ones
Sites with thousands of backlinks still often fail to rank #1, so raw volume is not the goal. For "a lot of keywords" the source's heuristic is roughly 10-20 relevant, quality backlinks. Cheap SEO providers who flood a site with garbage links can actively hurt rankings rather than help. Condition: stated as a heuristic for "a lot of keywords," not a fixed number for every keyword or niche. [8901669ff766d3ba521e3094]

### 2.6 The 80/20 rule of SEO effort vs. revenue
Roughly 80% of the source's operating time (reporting, most technical SEO, blog content, internal linking) drives the minority of revenue. Roughly 20% of time (optimizing/building money pages, acquiring quality backlinks) drives roughly 80% of revenue growth. Rule: when capacity is limited, deliberately deprioritize the low-leverage 80% of tasks. Condition: presenter's own estimate of his agency's time allocation, not a measured study; he sells the outcome of following this ratio, so has an incentive to state it cleanly. [8df2a868c01740f78e884f85]

### 2.7 Digital PR and "earned" links are real but slow, expensive and mostly for high-authority brands
Two organic/earned link paths are named: (a) building relationships with industry bloggers and journalists over time (harder to start, can be cost-effective long-run); (b) creating genuinely link-worthy standout content ("easily the most difficult... least scalable" — expect to publish "hundreds of pieces of content" before earning the first organic link). Paying for digital PR services runs about $5,000/month per the source. Brands that earn links purely organically and at scale are typically already DR 70-80 (high domain authority). ### 2.8 A lean 3-tool stack is enough; knowing a tool is not the same as knowing SEO
The source explicitly argues against needing many paid tools: "knowing how to use a tool like Robin Hood does not make you a great stock trader... knowing how to use SEO tools does not make you an expert at ranking pages." His stack: Semrush (paid, all-in-one), Google Ads Keyword Planner (free), Screaming Frog (free up to 500 URLs). ### 2.9 Combine transactional keyword targeting with a small, deliberate link budget
The two prior levers (money pages + backlinks) are meant to run together, not separately: research transactional keywords, map them to new or existing collection pages (filling catalog gaps), then build 10-20 quality backlinks (mostly insertions) pointed at those same pages. ## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Decide where a keyword or page belongs (money page vs. blog)
1. List candidate keywords tied to your catalog.
2. Judge buying intent: would someone searching this term buy now, or are they researching (e.g. "natural face wash" vs. "why should I use natural face wash")? [4ebd0ef27d6188d38deef845]
3. Build/optimize a collection or product page for buy-ready keywords first.
4. Treat informational keywords as secondary; do not staff blog content ahead of money pages. [4ebd0ef27d6188d38deef845]
5. Measure conversion from any existing blog content directly, in your own store. `[needs brand data: blog-page conversion rate and assisted conversions]` [4ebd0ef27d6188d38deef845]

### 3.2 Run a lightweight technical SEO check (the source's only technical checklist)
1. Confirm key pages are indexed/crawlable. `[verify UI: Google Search Console coverage report]` [6eb7236440e64b474b1c365c]
2. Crawl the site for broken links (404s). Source names Screaming Frog for this (3.4). [6eb7236440e64b474b1c365c]
3. Check mobile and desktop UX by hand: is it fast enough, easy to navigate? No numeric thresholds given. [6eb7236440e64b474b1c365c]
4. Check that site/category hierarchy is clear (most important pages are easy for a user and for Google to identify). No specific method given beyond this. [6eb7236440e64b474b1c365c]
5. If an agency's audit goes past these four items and pushes many more "fixes" (e.g. meta description rewrites, minor speed tweaks), the source says treat that as a red flag, not a requirement. [6eb7236440e64b474b1c365c]

### 3.3 Acquire a link insertion (the source's outreach sequence)
1. Compile a list of sites in your niche. [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]
2. Find contact info using a scraping/contact tool, e.g. hunter.io. `[web check: current tool, current pricing]` [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]
3. Ask if the site accepts guest posts or link insertions.
4. If yes: evaluate the page on domain rating, domain traffic, page traffic and topical relevance before agreeing. [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]
5. Pay the fee ($100-$400 average per the source's current spend; can be more) and complete the insertion. [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]
6. Alternative: buy through a link marketplace instead of outreach. Expect marketplace prices roughly 20-60% higher than direct outreach (marketplaces have already done the outreach for you). If budget-constrained, do outreach yourself instead. [6b66d770867bdff19877f6fd]
Card addition, not stated by the presenter: qualify any paid placement with `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"` rather than buying unqualified links to manipulate rankings; this is a standard editorial-disclosure practice added at the card level, not something the transcript itself states. `[verify UI: current rel attribute guidance]`

2. Review status codes for 4xx/broken pages, missing H1s/H2s, page titles, meta descriptions, URLs and canonical tags — all in one report. [3dd3ec99006f014b772427fc]
3. If the site exceeds 500 URLs, a paid license is needed; source estimates "like 500 bucks for a one-year license" but flags this as possibly outdated ("last time I checked"). `[web check: current Screaming Frog pricing and free-tier limit]` [3dd3ec99006f014b772427fc]

2. Read search volume as a range, not an exact number. Small keywords: range is not a big problem. Larger keywords (10K-100K range): the gap inside that range is "quite substantial," so treat as directional only. [3dd3ec99006f014b772427fc]
