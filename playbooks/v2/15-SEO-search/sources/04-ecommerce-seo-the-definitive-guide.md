## 1. What this source is for

A worked, single-category, 90-day plan for making an ecommerce brand visible in both traditional search and AI answers (the source calls this AEO, answer engine optimization, alongside SEO). It walks one real audit end to end: pick one product category, run a scoped technical audit, build a five-stage content plan, find and blend the domains AI systems cite for that category, build competitor-comparison content, syndicate the same topics across owned channels (site, YouTube, LinkedIn, TikTok, Instagram), pursue earned coverage on the highest-cited third-party domains, and audit pages for both human search and AI/agent retrieval, including whether Common Crawl's bot can read the site. It is a strategy and workflow document, not a page-by-page editing guide: it shows what to build and in what order, using its own software to do it, more than it teaches transferable manual technique.

## 2. Principles

1. **Attack one category for a full quarter instead of the whole site.** The presenter says spreading effort across every category produces shallow progress everywhere ("a centimeter in each direction"), a mistake he says he made repeatedly early in his career. Pick one category from the site's main navigation (his example: creatine, inside a supplements brand) and commit roughly 90 days, sometimes one to two quarters, to dominating it before moving to the next category. [03e2459fa1f6]
2. **Scope every audit step to the chosen category, not the whole site.** A "technical audit" here means crawling the site and filtering to just that category's URLs (e.g. a crawl-CSV filter of "text contains: creatine"), not a full-site SEO audit. [03e2459fa1f6]
3. **Assign actions to existing URLs from data, not gut feel.** Where Search Console, GA4 and Bing Webmaster Tools are available, use their impressions, clicks, CTR and position data to decide whether an existing page should be consolidated, upgraded or deleted. The presenter states this is important on every audit, with or without any specific tool. [03e2459fa1f6]
4. **Build the content plan backward from the bottom of the funnel.** Using a five-stage customer-awareness template (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware), start with "most aware" (queries directly about your own product) because it converts fastest and is the easiest content to justify, then work upward. [f7f0469f3f8a]
5. **Ground AI content-planning prompts in your own real pages, not the model's general knowledge.** Paste your category/collection page plus several product pages (the source used five total) into the AI tool as sources before asking for topic ideas, so the plan reflects your actual catalog. Where available, also feed in Search Console performance data so the AI weighs current rankings, not just topic gaps. [a539906b58c4]
6. **Treat AI-generated topic lists as a draft, not a plan.** The presenter estimates roughly 20% of AI-suggested topics will be weak; a human still filters the list before committing content resources. [f7f0469f3f8a]
7. **AI answers are built from retrieval, and retrieval is built from citations.** When a model does not already "know" an answer from its training corpus, it performs web retrieval and grounds its answer in the pages it finds; citation pages, not the model's base knowledge, are what determines whether a brand gets mentioned. The practical conclusion: earning many citations across many query variants is the mechanism that makes a brand show up repeatedly in AI answers. [e8d5b62cbe8d]
8. **Model the platform's own "query fan-out" behavior.** After answering a head query, AI systems tend to run further related queries (alternatives, reviews, comparisons) on their own. The source's advice is to pre-build assets for the variants the AI is likely to generate, so the brand is already present when fan-out happens. [32d5cdf8c991]
9. **Blend traditional SERP exports with AI citation exports into one sheet, then look for repeat domains.** For each of at least five core queries, export three lists: which brands/competitors AI answers name, which URLs rank in traditional search across engines, and which URLs are used as AI citations. A domain that shows up across multiple queries and multiple surfaces (e.g. Brave, Google organic and Grok, in the source's example) is the strongest signal of where to invest outreach. [c65599bce8db] [cb73ca87f670]
10. **Prioritize the top-cited domains as core outreach channels, and treat paid and organic citation equally.** The presenter says sponsored and affiliate content commonly shows up as AI citations "just as much" as organic content, so a brand should be willing to pay for placement, give product, or build relationships on the domains AI cites most for its category (his example: Healthline as the single most-cited domain for creatine). [ccdeae1f4dff] [6ea0882a8403]
11. **Reddit threads already in AI retrieval outrank arbitrary Reddit threads.** Prioritize contributing to threads the citation export shows are already being retrieved, then keep a daily watch on relevant subreddits for new threads before they close or fill up. [e54f1c0e014f]
12. **Check whether cited marketplaces already carry your product; if not, that is a visibility gap.** If a marketplace such as Amazon is itself a cited or ranking entity for the category, and you sell there but withhold the specific product line, that is presented as a self-inflicted gap worth reversing. [1cdb6f4058e5] [6ea0882a8403]
13. **Build competitor-comparison content deliberately: reviews, alternatives, head-to-head, and "vs you."** Target the 5 to 10 competitors that recur most across your query set. In "alternatives" lists put your brand first, backed by claims you can support, and list 7 to 10 competitors. Keep reviews factual (real formulation, sourcing or serving differences) and explicitly avoid hostile or nasty comparisons. For head-to-head pages, use one shared category seed word rather than repeating it per brand (his example: "creatine battle: Legion vs Thorne vs First Form"). [5346127695b9]
14. **Silence on competitor comparisons cedes the decision to competitors and consumers.** The presenter argues that if you will not compare yourself to competitors, your competitors (or AI-summarized consumer opinion) will make that comparison for you; he recommends being willing to name competitors directly. [5346127695b9]
15. **Repeat the same topic set across every owned channel, not just the blog.** Publish the same core queries as site articles/FAQs, YouTube videos, LinkedIn company-page articles, and TikTok/Instagram micro-content. Choose the next channel by checking whether your existing posts there already index well; if they do, invest further there. [4b1cf178dc33]
16. **Use LinkedIn's domain authority as a parasite SEO surface.** Publish a variant (not a copy-paste duplicate) of the on-site article on the company page, then have employees, especially high-follower founders/execs, share, like or comment to drive crawl and ranking signals. Confirm indexation and ranking before moving to the next channel. [70d7dddf86e4]
17. **Focused, single-category competitors are hard to beat and may justify a micro-brand.** A competitor that only sells your target category, especially with a partial-match domain/brand name, has a structural advantage. The source raises (as an option, not a rule) launching a dedicated micro-brand or niche site to match that focus. [bb34ff423064]
18. **Reframe search work as full digital marketing: owned content plus earned placements, budgeted together.** A meaningful share of the effort is off-site, on domains you do not control; sequence fast on-site fixes first, then invest in earned placements on the domains that already dominate the top 10 for your category. [b1407de6644e]
19. **AI drafts are first drafts: fact-check numbers and cut hedged placeholders.** Generated listicles can include vague filler like "contact for pricing," which the source says is unacceptable in a listicle; verify every numeric or technical claim, and expect roughly three to five revision passes before a draft is production-ready. [17c5ce84a5ee]
20. **Page-level readiness for AI retrieval sits on top of ordinary technical SEO.** Crawlability, on-page structure and content freshness matter for traditional ranking, AI retrieval and model training inclusion alike. The source adds two AI-specific checks: literal file/code freshness (stale years visible in page source) and whether Common Crawl's bot (CCBot) can read the site, since that snapshot feeds future model training. [29ea2467bb38] [87b0e16b4d08]
21. **Agents read code, not design.** The presenter argues AI crawlers deconstruct a site to its structural fundamentals and do not care how the page looks visually; clean structure, real specs and prices (not "contact for pricing"), and a machine-readable version (e.g. markdown) matter more for agent audiences than visual polish. [856538db1893]
22. **Concentrate internal link equity on the category page as a hub.** Every modifier-query article (vegan, sensitive stomach, budget, etc.) should link back to the central category page and to at least one sibling article, so authority accumulates at the hub instead of spreading thin across many disconnected spokes. [91d2b64f095d]
23. **Resourcing, not strategy, is the actual bottleneck.** True topic domination in one category can require hundreds of assets (the source's own build-out reaches roughly 200 to 500 across five channels); the presenter states plainly that most brands do not do this because it is hard work, not because the approach is unknown. [c7abb031b4943] [c7abb031b494]

## 3. Procedures

### A. The 90-day single-category domination sequence (as demonstrated, in order)
1. Pick one category from the site's main menu or navigation (example: creatine, inside a supplements brand). Do not attempt the whole catalog at once. [03e2459fa1f6]
2. Run a technical crawl scoped to that category only. Filter the crawl export (e.g. "text contains: creatine") to see how many existing pages target it. `[verify UI: crawl-tool export format]`
3. Cross-check with a plain Google search for "[brand] [category seed term]" to eyeball existing asset volume (the source's example found about 7 pages in a partial crawl versus roughly 30 visible via a Google search — treated as small relative to the hundreds needed for real topic authority).
4. Where available, pull Search Console, GA4 and Bing Webmaster Tools data and assign each existing URL an action: consolidate, upgrade or delete, based on impressions/clicks/CTR/position, not intuition. `[verify UI]`
5. Build a five-column customer-awareness template (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware) and populate it in reverse order, starting with most aware.
6. Ground the AI ideation prompt: paste the category/collection page plus several (source used five) product pages into an AI tool or project as sources, then ask it to generate topic ideas across all five awareness stages. Filter out the roughly 20% weak ideas. [f7f0469f3f8a] [a539906b58c4]
7. Pick 5 core queries with different intent (e.g. a broad head term, a gendered variant, a format variant, a brand+category combination) and run each through a rank/citation tracker across traditional search, AI answers, AI citations, video search and TikTok. [88bfa4b9c21e]
8. For each of those 5 queries, export three lists: competitors named in AI answers, traditional SERP URLs across engines, and AI citation URLs (the source's example yielded roughly 53 citations per query, about 250 once multiplied across 5 queries, plus roughly 30 traditional results per query). [c65599bce8db]
9. Merge the exports into one sheet, keep a column for which surface each URL appeared on, sort by URL to find repeats across surfaces, and add a checkbox column to flag outreach targets; skip targets with no realistic chance. [c65599bce8db] [cb73ca87f670]
10. Build competitor-comparison content: reviews, alternatives (brand first, 7 to 10 competitors), head-to-head ("[A] vs [B] vs [you]"), and "[competitor] vs [you]." [5346127695b9]
11. Build the brief and draft for each asset with the AI tool (see Procedure D), fact-check it (Procedure covered under Principle 19), and publish, internally linking every asset back to the category hub. [478da8faf1d2] [91d2b64f095d]
12. Repeat the same query set on owned channels in this order: your own site first, then the next channel where your existing content already indexes well (commonly YouTube, then LinkedIn, then TikTok/Instagram). [4b1cf178dc33]
13. Run the LinkedIn parasite SEO play for high-value queries (Procedure C).
14. Pursue earned coverage on the highest-cited third-party domains from the blended sheet (Healthline-type publishers, marketplaces, Reddit, YouTube channels already covering the topic). [ccdeae1f4dff] [e54f1c0e014f] [1cdb6f4058e5]
15. Run the page-level and agent-readiness audit on your core category and product pages (Procedure E), and confirm CCBot access (Procedure F).
16. Re-benchmark visibility (SERP presence, AI answer mentions, AI citation appearances, video/TikTok mentions) at the end of the quarter before deciding whether to move to the next category. [c7abb031b494] [fd35fcb9c76d]

### B. Build the AI-citation-and-SERP blend sheet
1. Choose at least 5 core queries in the target category, covering different intents (broad, gendered, format, brand+category).
2. For each query, export: (a) the competitors/brands the AI answer names, (b) the traditional search results across multiple engines, (c) the AI citation URLs (the sources the answer draws from, not the answer text itself).
3. Combine the traditional and AI-citation exports into a single sheet, one row per URL, with a column recording which surface(s) it appeared on (Google, Bing, Brave, Grok, etc.).
4. Sort by URL and look for domains that repeat across surfaces; treat repetition as the strongest signal of where to focus outreach. [c65599bce8db]
5. Add a checkbox "opportunity" column. Do not flag URLs with no realistic path to inclusion (e.g. very restricted or niche sites).
6. Prioritize by number of citations and breadth of surfaces; identify owned-marketplace opportunities (e.g. Amazon) separately from earned-media opportunities (e.g. sponsored posts, partnerships). [cb73ca87f670]
7. Re-run and refresh the sheet periodically; citation sets drift over time. [cb73ca87f670]

### C. Publish and amplify on LinkedIn as a parasite SEO surface
1. Write a variant of the on-site article for the target query (not a direct copy-paste), titled with the query plus the current year for timeliness (source example: "supplements for vegans... 2026"). [70d7dddf86e4]
2. Publish on the company LinkedIn page.
3. Have employees, especially high-follower founders or executives (source example: a CEO with 5,000 followers), share, like or comment to drive crawl and ranking signals.
4. Embed links back to the website and to other related assets in the piece.
5. Verify the placement is indexed and ranking (check the query across multiple search engines) before starting the same process on the next channel.
6. Move to the next channel (commonly YouTube next, described as the second most popular site on the internet in the source's framing), repeating the same query set in that platform's native format. [70d7dddf86e4] [4b1cf178dc33]

### D. Build an AI-grounded, brand-first comparison listicle brief (as demonstrated in Rankability)
1. Enter the target query, set language/region (English/US by default), and add a custom instruction such as "rank [Brand]'s [product] as the number one [category] for [use case]." `[verify UI]` [478da8faf1d2]
2. Confirm the detected search intent (the source's example auto-detected "discover"/commercial-investigation intent).
3. Choose which search/research platforms to pull from; the presenter recommends including both Google and AI-citation sources for broader coverage rather than Google alone. `[verify UI]`
4. Generate differentiators with an AI assistant rather than writing them by hand: ask for 3 to 4 sentences on how the brand uniquely differs from competitors, then ask for 5 to 10 specific, unique differentiators as a simple list. Paste these into the brief with a note that they apply to the brand that should rank first. [478da8faf1d2]
5. Set up a brand voice profile in the tool's settings before running campaigns so drafts inherit it (the source auto-filled this for the demo rather than doing it properly). `[verify UI]`
6. Add brand terms and associated named entities (e.g. a founder's name) so the model links the brand correctly.
7. Run the brief generation. It researches the chosen platforms, extracts competitor entities/topics/content structure and word counts (source example: drawn from roughly 50 to 75 competitor pages/articles), and returns a title tag, meta description, suggested URL, an entity checklist, a People Also Ask list, and internal link suggestions. [3a1f01db06eb]
8. Review the brief: keep the suggested title/meta/URL if on-brand, capture the entity list as a coverage checklist, note any competitor content gaps the tool flags, and add the PAA questions to an FAQ section for later query-level fan-out measurement. Add at least one internal link to the category hub in addition to product links. [3a1f01db06eb] [91d2b64f095d]
9. Generate the draft from the approved brief.
10. Fact-check the draft: replace any hedged placeholder ("contact for pricing") with specific numbers, verify any AI-retrieved facts, split up dense/bulky sections for readability, and treat unused entities/topics as seeds for future supporting content rather than stuffing them in. Expect roughly 3 to 5 revision passes before publishing. [17c5ce84a5ee]
11. Repeat this whole process per query/modifier; the source states each run will surface a different competitor set, so the resulting assets are not duplicates of each other. [91d2b64f095d]

### E. Run a page-level audit for search and AI retrieval
1. Extract the target URL (category or product page) into the audit tool. `[verify UI]`
2. Review topic coverage against the core keyword; identify missing critical subtopics (the source's example category page was missing several).
3. Check crawling and indexing fundamentals and on-page structure.
4. Check content freshness signals.
5. Check literal file/code freshness: view the page source and look for stale dates (source example: spotting 2022/2023 timestamps in the code). The source argues AI platforms parse code, not visual design, so outdated markup, images and details all count against you. [29ea2467bb38]
6. Check UX and performance, specifically load speed; slow pages are penalized in both traditional search and AI retrieval, per the source.
7. Run the page through the tool's quality-score assessment (the source says this is modeled on Google's helpful-content guidelines) and act on its recommendations.
8. Check the agent-readiness section, including a Common Crawl-specific check (see Procedure F), and fix flagged issues.
9. Prioritize the single biggest gap first rather than working the list top to bottom. [29ea2467bb38]

### F. Open your site to CCBot without opening it to everything
1. Confirm robots.txt and any bot-specific rules are not blocking CCBot (Common Crawl's crawler), since its monthly snapshot is what AI model providers download and filter down for training. [87b0e16b4d08] `[web check: current CCBot user-agent string and robots.txt directive syntax]`
2. Recognize the two separate goals: being retrieved live during a query (requires being indexable/crawlable now) versus being "baked into" a future model's training data (requires being in a Common Crawl snapshot that a provider keeps after filtering). Both matter; neither guarantees the other. [87b0e16b4d08]
3. To fix agent-readiness issues quickly: download a markdown export of the page/site, hand it to an AI assistant, and ask it to identify and fix agent-friendliness gaps (clean structure, machine-readable content, working navigation for a bot). The source reports doing this in under an hour with no extra tooling. [87b0e16b4d08]
4. Re-run the audit afterward to confirm the fixes registered as agentic improvements. [87b0e16b4d08]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check: how much of a category is already covered?** How: filter a site crawl to the category term, and separately search "[brand] [seed term]" in Google to count visible assets. Good: hundreds of assets with strong Search Console signals across the sub-intents. Bad: a handful of pages, or moderate page count with no data behind the decisions. Next: run Procedure A steps 1-4. [88bfa4b9c21e]
- **Check: does a query variant already have a home page on your own site?** How: search your own domain for the modifier plus product (e.g. brand + "vegan"). Good: no close match, or a broader category page you should link into rather than duplicate. Bad: a near-duplicate page already targets the same intent (risk of cannibalization). Next: publish blog/listicle content instead of a new category page, and link it into the existing category page. [8a447e2a6302]
- **Check: are you visible across every search surface for a core query?** How: check traditional search, AI answers (are you named), AI citations (are you a retrieval source), video search and TikTok for the same query. Good: presence on most surfaces. Bad: absent everywhere despite a large owned audience (the source's First Form example). Next: prioritize citation-building (Procedure B) starting from that query. [88bfa4b9c21e]
- **Check: which off-site domains actually own your category's top 10?** How: pull top-10 results for core buy-intent queries and note recurring entities (marketplaces, publishers, forums). Good: you already have presence (owned listing, existing coverage, or a relationship) on most of the top 10. Bad: near-zero presence on domains that recur across every query. Next: prioritize marketplace listing and earned outreach for the domains you cannot outrank directly; re-run this each quarter since the domain mix shifts. [6ea0882a8403]
- **Check: is a given owned channel worth publishing to next?** How: search your brand/URL on Google to see if your existing posts on that channel are indexed and visible. Good: existing content already indexes well. Bad: no visible indexation. Next: fix indexation before investing more content there, or deprioritize that channel for now. [4b1cf178dc33] [70d7dddf86e4]
- **Check: is a page's code stale?** How: view page source on the live URL and scan for old dates (e.g. 2022/2023) in comments, metadata or asset filenames. Good: recent dates, no obviously stale markup. Bad: multiple old timestamps. Next: refresh the page's code and assets, not just its visible copy. [29ea2467bb38]
- **Check: can CCBot and other retrieval/indexing bots reach your key pages?** How: review robots.txt and any AI-specific bot rules. Good: indexing/retrieval bots explicitly allowed on commercial pages. Bad: bots blocked entirely, or no explicit policy at all. Next: run Procedure F. [87b0e16b4d08] `[web check: current bot names]`
- **Check: is an AI-generated draft safe to publish?** How: scan for hedged placeholders ("contact for pricing"), unverifiable claims, and bulky unreadable sections. Good: specific numbers, verified facts, readable structure. Bad: vague filler or unchecked "facts." Next: run the fact-check pass in Procedure D step 10 before publishing. [17c5ce84a5ee]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Trying to rank an entire site or every category at once instead of concentrating on one category for a full quarter; the presenter calls this one of the biggest mistakes and says he made it repeatedly early in his career. [03e2459fa1f6]
- Assigning actions to URLs (consolidate/upgrade/delete) based on gut feel instead of Search Console/GA4/Bing data. [03e2459fa1f6]
- Publishing near-duplicate category pages for narrow modifier queries when a broader category page already covers the topic; the source says to use blog content and internal links instead. [8a447e2a6302]
- Writing hostile or "nasty" competitor comparisons; the source explicitly says to keep differentiation factual and objective rather than disparaging. [5346127695b9]
- Trusting an AI-generated draft as final: leaving hedged placeholders like "contact for pricing," or failing to verify facts the model retrieved. [17c5ce84a5ee]
- Assuming a large owned social audience will translate into search or AI visibility on its own; the source's First Form example (hundreds of thousands to a million followers, near-zero category visibility) is used specifically to reject this assumption.
- Skipping off-site/earned work and treating SEO as an on-site-only exercise; the source frames modern search visibility as depending heavily on domains you do not control.
- Under-resourcing category domination and expecting quick wins; the presenter states most brands avoid this approach specifically because it requires producing hundreds of assets, not because it is unknown. [c7abb031b494]
- Ignoring literal code/file freshness (old dates left in page source) on the assumption that only visible copy matters to search or AI systems. [29ea2467bb38]
- Leaving AI crawlers such as CCBot blocked by default without a deliberate policy decision, which forfeits future model-training visibility. [87b0e16b4d08]
