## 2. Principles

1. **Prioritization, not execution, is the hard part of SEO.** You cannot and should not try to rank #1 for every keyword; pick a shortlist of business-driving terms. Condition: applies to keyword roadmap planning at the start of any SEO effort. Evidence: presenter's stated experience/opinion, no data cited. [41463bbdbca1]
2. **Set realistic expectations before spending resources.** Three reality checks the source gives: (a) ultra-competitive terms owned by enterprise brands for years/decades are usually not worth pursuing for smaller/mid-sized businesses; (b) holding a #1 ranking is an ongoing cost (refresh content, keep building authority), not a one-time win; (c) you don't need #1 for every relevant keyword. Evidence: opinion from presenter's decade of SEO work. [41463bbdbca1]
3. **Match your page format to the SERP's dominant format, not to what you feel like writing.** Condition: before building or rewriting any page for a target keyword. Evidence: presenter's method, no external citation. [8786029ccf04][bd25b3cabfdf]
4. **Where you lack domain authority to win a head-on homepage fight, use a roundup/comparison page instead.** Condition: competitive commercial keyword owned by bigger-brand homepages. Evidence: one case study (GetAccept). [be8eb0ae5390]
5. **Word count is not a ranking lever; topic completeness is.** Condition: applies whenever drafting page content. Evidence: presenter's flat assertion ("Google does not prefer or reward a particular word count"), no citation. [bd25b3cabfdf]

## 3. Procedures

**Step 1 — Identify keywords and search intent**
1. Set aside your site's existing rankings; start from a clean slate.
2. List your business-driving keywords in tiers: (1) brand name, (2) explicit product/service names or terms describing them, (3) for local businesses, geo-modified service terms (e.g., "Houston plumber").
3. Google each keyword (organic results, not just ads) and study the SERP.
4. Determine Google's "assumed search intent" from the dominant result format (homepages, listicles, videos, Reddit threads, AI overviews, People Also Ask).
5. If the SERP shows one dominant format, build that format.
6. If intent is mixed (multiple formats appear because Google itself is unsure), pick one of two approaches: (a) majority rule — if 5 or more of the top results share a format, target that format; or (b) strategic pick — choose the format your domain's current authority can realistically win, even if it's not the majority (this is the basis for the roundup backdoor in step 4 below).

**Step 2 — Build the best possible page for that keyword**
1. Match page format to the intent found in Step 1 (product page vs. long-form post vs. category page vs. video), while differentiating substance from competitors.
2. Do not target a word count. Ask instead: "what does someone searching this need to know, and have I covered it completely?"
3. Study competitors' outlines for the keyword to make sure your coverage is at least as thorough. [source uses Surfer's content editor for this — verify current feature set [verify UI]]
4. Optionally generate an AI outline for ideas; don't use it verbatim.
5. Check the "questions" tab / People Also Ask data and answer all of those questions in the content.
6. After drafting in your own words, review for the entities/subtopics top-ranking pages cover, for both traditional search and AI answer surfaces.
7. Layer in E-E-A-T signals (see section 4/5) and skimmability aids (table of contents, TL;DR).

**Step 3 — Power up the page (topical authority + backlinks + internal links)**
1. Pick the money page (product, category or service page) as the center of a content cluster.
2. Map every angle a buyer could search around that topic (source's example: best hydroponic garden systems for vegetables, outdoor growing, apartments, limited space, on a budget).
3. Use a tool that connects your domain and Google Search Console to see existing content coverage vs. the topic map and find gaps (source names Surfer's "sites dashboard" [verify UI]). [needs brand data: current content inventory and GSC data]
4. Publish supporting content in priority order of the gaps found.
5. Build backlinks to the supporting/educational posts (easier to earn than links to a sales page), pitching site owners with a concrete upgrade offer (e.g., "you link to a 6-year-old page on X; I just published a tested, current version") rather than a direct ask to link the money page.
6. Internally link each supporting post to the relevant money page (and to sibling posts in the cluster) with contextual anchor text, so backlink authority flows inward.
7. Repeat the full cycle (steps 1–6) for every revenue-driving page/money page in the catalog.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **SERP intent check.** Google the target keyword (incognito), read the organic results only (skip ads), note the format of the top ~5–10 results. Good: one format clearly dominates (5+ results) — build that format. Bad/ambiguous: formats are mixed — pick majority or the strategic backdoor. Caveat: SERPs vary by day, location and personalization ("depending on how Google is feeling that day"); re-check periodically since format decisions should follow SERP drift. [8786029ccf04]
- **E-E-A-T audit.** Check the page for: original photos, real screenshots (of actual product/software use), step-by-step documentation from doing the thing, case studies with specific numbers, personal anecdotes, a real author byline with photo, publish date, last-updated date, and a consistent review structure across compared items. Bad: none of these present, or content reads as generic/assembled. Fix: add the missing signals yourself, or interview/credit someone who has the first-hand experience (even someone found in product reviews) if you don't have it yourself. [1512b69d5402]
- **Topical coverage gap check.** Connect domain + Google Search Console to a coverage-mapping tool (source names Surfer's sites dashboard [verify UI]) to see which angles of the target topic are already covered vs. missing. [needs brand data: existing content inventory, GSC performance data] Fix: publish the highest-priority gaps first. [1e7d8c5e6402]
- **Backlink pitch check.** Before outreach, find a page the target site already links to that is outdated (source's example: six years old) or thin, and confirm you have a genuinely better, current replacement to offer instead of a bare link request. [1293de3e1c27]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Trusting anyone who claims they can guarantee a #1 Google ranking — presenter says this is beyond a marketer's control.
- Chasing ultra-competitive, enterprise-dominated terms with limited resources — usually not worth it for smaller/mid-sized businesses.
- Trying to rank #1 for every relevant keyword instead of a prioritized shortlist.
- Optimizing for word count instead of complete topic coverage.
- Publishing generic, "AI-spun fluff" content with no first-hand experience signals.
- Hunting for backlinks before building topical authority content — presenter's explicit stated preference is content-cluster first.
- Pitching link requests as "link to my sales page because I want money" instead of offering a genuinely better replacement resource.
- Assuming one well-optimized page is enough to rank, especially for a newer or lower-authority site — it needs the cluster and backlink/internal-link layer too.
- Forgetting that keeping a #1 ranking takes ongoing content refreshes and continued authority-building, since competitors will target the same spot.
