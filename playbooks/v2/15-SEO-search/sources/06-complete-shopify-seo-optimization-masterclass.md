## 1. What this source is for

Gives a beginner-level, repeatable monthly operating cycle for Shopify SEO: validate keywords commercially before touching a page, build keyword clusters instead of stuffing one term, push those keywords into Shopify's collection/product page fields and search engine listing, keep feeding Google with new content (blogs especially), and safely use ChatGPT to draft that content without triggering Google's AI-content penalty. It is a planning and cadence framework more than a deep technical SEO manual — no coverage of site speed, structured data, internal linking mechanics, or backlink acquisition tactics.

## 2. Principles

1. SEO is a long-term, ongoing maintenance activity, not a one-time setup task. Condition: applies to any store expecting organic growth. Evidence: presenter's seed/harvest analogy and a cited wine-industry client who did not see momentum until 12–13 months after starting. [f8b0ca8237ae]
2. Money-free traffic still costs time. Condition: when comparing SEO to paid acquisition. Evidence: presenter's framing that you trade time and effort instead of ad spend — staff the work accordingly. [f8b0ca8237ae]
3. Keyword clustering beats single-keyword stuffing because Google now reads whole-page context, not just keyword presence. Condition: applies to collection pages, product pages and blog posts alike. Evidence: presenter says Google detected old keyword-stuffing tactics and started ranking those sites down; no external citation given. [88385c2e69c2]
4. Off-page authority (backlinks) can outrank a better-optimized page. Condition: when a competitor with weaker on-page signals still outranks you. Evidence: presenter's illustrative example of a 300-rank site vs a 1-rank site linking to different pages, plus a Forbes-vs-small-store analogy. Note the 1–300 authority scale is the presenter's own teaching device, not Google's real domain-authority metric (which the source itself says runs 0–100). [21a9fd61d095]
5. Only target keywords with proven commercial intent and demand. Condition: before writing any page copy or blog content. Evidence: the C/Volume/KD/CPC filter (see section 4); presenter's own validation rule, not a documented Google algorithm requirement. [33805e3f1916]
6. Collection pages should usually be optimized before individual product pages for a given hero keyword. Condition: when choosing where to spend on-page effort for a commercial term like "natural soap." Evidence: presenter's reasoning that a collection page aggregates products, builds cluster relevance, and signals a "real store," versus a single product page. [e879133cad0d]
7. The Shopify Search Engine Listing (meta title/description) is a click-through asset, separate from ranking. Condition: once a page already ranks or is competing for a SERP slot. Evidence: presenter contrasts a bare keyword ("natural soap") against a CTA-driven listing ("buy natural soap online... fast delivery") as the difference between a weak and strong listing. [469605d28f73]
8. Google's index needs to be fed continuously to stay relevant; regular updates function as an active-site signal, citing news sites as the example of sites that rank well partly from update frequency. [f5fa218ed572]
9. AI-drafted content must be detected and partially rewritten before publishing, because Google penalizes AI-pattern content at scale. Condition: whenever ChatGPT (or similar) drafts a blog, description or article. Evidence: presenter's own case — his team published many AI blog posts, ranked well, then Google ran "a big sweep" and the AI content lost its ranking value (he says Google did not de-rank the sites, just stopped surfacing/using that content). [f6a17ed1177e]
10. Persona-rich prompting produces less generic AI content than a bare "write me a blog about X" prompt. Condition: any time ChatGPT is used to draft SEO content. Evidence: presenter's own before/after prompt example (see section 3). [d625e29f8820]

## 3. Procedures

### A. Monthly SEO cycle (the core operating rhythm)
1. Keywords: research and validate this month's hero keyword and its cluster using the C+Volume+KD+CPC filter (see section 4). [6c5f723270e6]
2. Update pages: push validated keywords into existing collection/product page titles, headings, SEO descriptions and search engine listings.
3. Feed the search engine: publish new content tied to this month's keyword theme (blog, new product, review, new collection, support/how-to article).
4. Get links: acquire links to the pages you are pushing this month. The source does not give a link-acquisition method — it names this stage but does not explain how, beyond the quality-over-quantity principle in section 2. Gap: how-to is not in this source.
5. Repeat monthly. Presenter's team runs this on a fixed calendar day each month inside an internal "GSD" (get stuff done) framework.
6. Every cycle, check position tracking to decide whether to reinforce a keyword or move to a new one. [d0068f9ba972]

### B. Validate a keyword in Semrush before using it
1. Get a Semrush account. Sign up via the presenter's link, complete email confirmation, then — per the presenter's workaround — click the Semrush logo at the top instead of following the onboarding workflow, which lands you in the account on the free plan. `[verify UI]` `[web check: does this bypass still work, and is it still 10 queries/day]` This is the presenter's own affiliate-style link (`bit.ly` shortlink shown in the transcript) and the free-tier bypass is presented as a "trick" — flag as presenter-promoted, not neutral advice. [7a9a3fea6b48]
2. Open the Keyword Magic Tool. Enter your hero keyword (source example: "natural soap"). Choose the country database you sell into (US, UK, Australia shown as options). Click search. `[verify UI]`
3. Read the results row for your hero keyword and check four things against the thresholds in section 4: the "C" (commercial) intent flag, monthly search volume, keyword difficulty (KD) percentage, and CPC.
4. Reject keywords that fail any filter. Source's worked example: "natural soap" = commercial, 5,400 searches/month, KD 34, CPC over $1 → accept. "How to make natural soap" = informational intent, KD 58, CPC $20.28 → reject (informational keywords don't convert unless you sell ingredients for that activity). [33805e3f1916]
5. From the same results list, pick 3 to 5 related commercial keywords to form the cluster (source examples: organic soap, handmade soap, natural products, natural bar soap, all natural soap), validating each against the same filter. [88385c2e69c2]
6. In Semrush, click "Projects" (top nav), then "Create project." Enter your store domain (source used a test store, ecohut.com). `[verify UI]`
7. Inside the project, open "Position Tracking," click "Set up," choose the country (e.g. United States), click continue, and enter your 3–5 chosen keywords. `[verify UI]`
8. Let Semrush send weekly ranking-update emails so you can track whether position moves. [7a9a3fea6b48]
9. Revisit tracked keywords after roughly 6 months: if rank isn't moving, consider another supporting blog post or a page update rather than abandoning the keyword. [d0068f9ba972]

### C. Optimize a Shopify collection page for a hero keyword
1. In Shopify admin, go to Products > Collections > Create collection (or edit an existing one). `[verify UI]`
2. Set the collection Title field to the main hero keyword (source example: "Natural Soap"). [e879133cad0d]
3. Write the collection Description as an SEO paragraph of roughly 400 words or more. It must contain the hero keyword plus supporting paragraphs covering each cluster keyword — the goal is a large, relevant text block Google can read alongside the title and headings. [e879133cad0d]
4. Structure that description using Shopify's built-in heading levels (H1, H2, H3, paragraph) rather than dumping plain text — assign the right format to the text you want Google to treat as primary vs supporting. `[verify UI]` [21a9fd61d095]
5. Scroll to the Search Engine Listing section at the bottom of the page. Edit the Title Tag and Meta Description: include the keyword, then add a CTA or differentiator (source examples: "buy natural soap online," "fast delivery," "only the best soaps"). Do not leave it as a bare keyword. `[verify UI]` [469605d28f73]
6. Before finalizing, check competitors' search engine listings for the same term and try to beat them on click appeal. [469605d28f73]
7. Apply the same title/description/search-engine-listing method to individual product pages and blog posts, per the source's note that "the same theory" carries across page types.

### D. Draft a blog post with ChatGPT (persona-prompt method)
1. Fill in a simple worksheet first: hero keyword (e.g. natural soap), cluster keywords (organic soap, handmade soap, etc.), target audience description (source example: "women 25–35 who are environmentally conscious"), and author persona (source example: "handmade soap maker with 15 years experience"). [d625e29f8820]
2. Prompt 1 (role-set, no output yet): "Act as a person who makes handmade natural and sustainable bathroom and cleaning products, writing a blog for an online store that sells natural soap. Do not write any blogs until I give you further instructions."
3. Prompt 2 (titles): "Write me five catchy blog post titles for [hero keyword], considering that the content will also include [cluster keywords]." Pick one title from the five returned.
4. Prompt 3 (voice/audience lock-in): "Everything you do from here should be written in [language/dialect, e.g. Australian English] and in a style that would appeal to [audience, e.g. environmentally conscious women under 30]."
5. Prompt 4 (write): "Great, write me the article."
6. Prompt 5 (image brief): "Summarize this blog into what photograph we could take to encapsulate the content of the blog." Feed that description into an image generator (source used DALL-E) to get a matching hero image. [d625e29f8820]
7. Before publishing, run the human-rewrite and AI-detection step (Procedure E) on the output.

### E. AI-detection and human rewrite pass (mandatory before publishing AI drafts)
1. Paste the AI-drafted article into an AI content detector — source names CopyLeaks' AI content detector. `[verify UI]` `[web check: is CopyLeaks still the tool of choice / free-tier limits]` Note: not disclosed as sponsored, but is a named third-party tool recommendation.
2. Treat the AI draft as roughly 80% done. Manually rewrite and adjust the last 10–20% yourself.
3. Do not substitute a spinning tool (source names Quillbot) for a genuine human rewrite — the source says nothing beats manually rewriting that last piece.
4. Publish only after the manual pass. [f6a17ed1177e]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- Diagnose a failing SEO effort by checking it against 4 patterns: expecting short-term results, betting on one keyword, trying to rank for everything at once (e.g. black t-shirt / black men's t-shirt / black women's t-shirt / black kids t-shirt all at once instead of picking one specific term), and treating SEO as a silver bullet. If any pattern matches, restructure toward the monthly cluster-and-cycle approach rather than abandoning organic search. [f8b0ca8237ae]
- Check keyword commercial fit monthly: does this keyword still match your products and buyer intent? Good: still relevant, keep tracking. Bad: no longer fits the business, swap it for a validated replacement. [6d1a4ae6d1e0]
- Check page signal count versus competitors: count how many of title, headings (H1/H2/H3), description paragraph, and search engine listing are properly filled and keyword-aligned on your page versus a competing page. More filled signals = better chance of ranking, not a guarantee. [e879133cad0d]
- Check search engine listing appeal: does it read as a bare keyword (weak) or keyword + CTA (strong, e.g. "buy natural soap online... fast delivery")? Benchmark against competitor listings for the same term. [469605d28f73]
- Check AI content before publishing: run it through an AI detector (CopyLeaks named). If flagged, hand-rewrite the last 10–20% before it goes live. [f6a17ed1177e]
- Check rank movement via Semrush Position Tracking weekly/monthly. No movement after ~6 months → add another blog post or update the page rather than dropping the keyword immediately. [d0068f9ba972]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Expecting short-term results from SEO (days/weeks) instead of treating it as a months-long compounding effort. [f8b0ca8237ae]
- Betting everything on a single keyword instead of a hero-plus-cluster structure. [f8b0ca8237ae], [88385c2e69c2]
- Trying to rank for too many broad variants at once (e.g. all t-shirt color/gender/age combinations) instead of a specific, validated term. [f8b0ca8237ae]
- Treating SEO as a "silver bullet" that alone fixes a struggling store, rather than one foundational piece of a holistic growth strategy. [f8b0ca8237ae]
- Keyword-stuffing a single term throughout a site — Google detects this pattern and ranks such sites down. [88385c2e69c2]
- Targeting keywords with strong search interest but no buying intent (informational queries) when the goal is sales. [33805e3f1916]
- Publishing raw AI-generated content with no human rewrite or AI-detector check — the presenter's own team lost ranking value on AI content this way after a Google update. [f6a17ed1177e]
- Relying on a spin tool (Quillbot named) instead of a genuine manual rewrite of AI drafts. [f6a17ed1177e]
- Leaving the Shopify Search Engine Listing as a bare keyword instead of adding a CTA, which the presenter says looks weak and gets fewer clicks even if it ranks. [469605d28f73]
- Dumping plain text into Shopify's content fields without assigning proper heading hierarchy (H1/H2/H3), which withholds a relevance signal from Google. [e879133cad0d]
