## 1. What this source is for

Gives a pre-sourcing, pre-manufacturing screen for a single physical product idea: 16 questions across market conditions and product-level economics/logistics, ending in a written go/no-go summary. It answers "should I invest time and money sourcing this specific product?" It does not cover active product scouting via ad libraries or spy tools (see 02-PRD Direct Response Product Scouting), supplier negotiation or manufacturing (06-OPS Supply Chain and Sourcing), or storefront build (05-WEB Storefront Architecture).

## 3. Procedures

**Procedure: Run the 16-question validation pass on one product idea**

Preconditions: you have a single specific product idea (not a category) to score.

Market-level questions (do these first):
1. Estimate potential market size or demand. Use BuzzSumo for daily social mentions and Google Keyword Planner for monthly search volume on words related to the product idea. [3b155508fd8a]
2. Research competitors. Search Google in every country you plan to sell in; identify all credible substitutes, not just branded competitors, including DIY/homemade and generic-commodity alternatives. [5101fbb610a7]
3. Classify the market direction: trend, fad, flat, or growing. Use Google Trends on the core keyword; consult trend-research tools (e.g. Trend Hunter) or your own judgment if the data is ambiguous. [2c09edad5b25]
4. Check local/offline availability. If customers can easily buy the product at a local store, treat that as a negative signal for online viability. [5101fbb610a7]
5. Profile target customers: who they are, where they live, whether they buy online. Use audience-estimation tools on competitor websites if a market leader exists; otherwise sample social media (e.g. Twitter) for keyword mentions and infer gender/age from profiles, and use Google Trends region data for geography. [adc4fca0bb7f]

Product-level questions (do these after market questions pass or show a fixable concern):
6. Set a potential selling price by pricing at least "about two dozen" comparable products in the category, then compare against the $50-$150 general sweet-spot band. [cbda8eff6638]
7. Estimate potential markup. Get an all-in cost per unit for an initial small production run (materials, packaging, assembly). Compute markup at both the low and high end of your realistic price range, and separately estimate how cost will fall with future bulk ordering. [952aae634b40]
8. Count SKUs required to launch. Default to exactly 1 SKU to minimize inventory capital, storage cost and shipping mix-ups; treat a line extension as a later-stage decision. [190344a3ce52]
9. Assess subscription fit. Check whether the product is consumable, then estimate the customer's real usage/replenishment rate before assuming any subscription cadence. [494c8db9f8ce]
10. Check size and weight. Estimate the packed shipping size/weight and price a rough shipment on your carrier's website to see the real cost before sourcing. [837eb78db394]
11. Assess durability of both packaging and the product itself; choose materials (e.g. plastic over glass) that survive parcel handling and reduce damage-driven returns. [c7268fae6d38]
12. Check seasonality. Plot core keywords in Google Trends across multiple years; look for recurring monthly spikes versus one-off noise. [52858475e05d]
13. Classify whether the product solves a pain or serves a passion (or both), versus being merely "nice to have." [ec2cf7075718]
14. Check whether the product is consumable or disposable, which supports repeat business. [480136acffdb]
15. Check perishability and shelf life; compare the shelf-life window against your realistic time to build a marketing plan and reach sell-through, and also account for how long the customer takes to use up a unit once delivered. [646fd0db3481]
16. Research regulations and restrictions for every country you plan to sell in, and identify what they mean for marketing claims, packaging copy and any testing/documentation obligations, especially for food, cosmetics or baby products. [5f471d64cfd2]

Close-out step:
17. Write an explicit highlights list and concerns list from the 16 answers. Judge whether each concern is fixable through branding, positioning or product improvement, versus dependent on the market itself changing. Proceed only when concerns have a plausible plan; revisit the summary post-launch to check which assumptions held. [090e016603b6]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Demand check**: what to check: monthly search volume (Keyword Planner) and daily social mentions (BuzzSumo) for the product's core keywords. Good: high volume plus steady chatter. Bad/weak: low volume, or chatter with no purchase-intent language. Next step: cross-check with Google Trends before concluding the demand is real and durable. [3b155508fd8a]
- **Trend-direction check**: what to check: multi-year Google Trends trajectory for the core and adjacent keywords. Good: consistent multi-year rise. Caution: rising but tied to a single fashionable ingredient/format that could rotate out. Bad: spike-and-collapse (fad) or outright decline. Next step: research why the trend is moving; use gut instinct or trend-hunting tools if the data is ambiguous. [2c09edad5b25]
- **Offline-availability check**: what to check: is the product easy to buy at a local/grocery store. Good (for online viability): scarce offline. Bad: freely available offline. [5101fbb610a7]
- **Price/markup check**: what to check: comparable pricing across ~2 dozen brands, plus your all-in unit cost at low and high price points. Good: healthy markup at the top of the realistic band. Bad: thin or negative markup even at the top. Next step: if markup only works at the top of the band, verify buyers will actually pay that price before sourcing. [952aae634b40]
- **Shipping-cost check**: what to check: packed size/weight priced on the actual carrier's website. Good: classified as a small, light package. Bad: shipping consumes a large share of the cost-to-price spread. Next step: raise price, shrink format, or reconsider the product. [837eb78db394]
- **Seasonality check**: what to check: recurring monthly search spikes across multiple years in Google Trends. Good: mild or no seasonality, or a repeatable pattern you can plan inventory/cash flow and marketing calendar around. Bad: undetected extreme seasonality (example given: Christmas trees, bought almost entirely in Nov-Dec) that concentrates revenue and risk. [52858475e05d]
- **Perishability check**: what to check: shelf life under actual storage/shipping conditions versus your realistic time to build a marketing plan and sell through. Good: shelf life comfortably exceeds your ramp timeline. Bad: shelf-life window shorter than your realistic launch-and-ramp timeline; leads to dead stock and write-offs if sales are slow. [646fd0db3481]
- **Regulatory check**: what to check: category-specific rules (food, cosmetics, baby products named explicitly) in every country you plan to sell in, covering claims, labelling, packaging copy and testing/documentation. Do this before investing, since a late-discovered blocker wastes time and money already spent. [5f471d64cfd2] [web check: current regulatory requirements per target country and category]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Skipping validation entirely, risking wasted time or money sunk into a product nobody will buy. [6d1e8d68fa75]
- Treating search volume or social mention counts as proof of buyers rather than a "vote of confidence." [3b155508fd8a]
- Missing that a market is declining ("missed the boat") by not checking trend direction before committing. [2c09edad5b25]
- Ignoring easy offline/local availability, which removes a reason for customers to buy online. [5101fbb610a7]
- Not confirming the target customer's country and shipping feasibility before committing to a product. [adc4fca0bb7f]
- Pricing so low that paid-acquisition margin cannot work, without recognizing it as a real red flag. [cbda8eff6638]
- Looking at markup only at one price point instead of across the realistic range. [952aae634b40]
- Launching with many SKUs, increasing inventory capital, storage cost and shipping mix-up risk. [190344a3ce52]
- Assuming any consumable product can support an aggressive subscription cadence without checking real usage frequency. [494c8db9f8ce]
- Choosing an oversized or heavy product without pricing real carrier costs first. [837eb78db394]
- Using fragile packaging (e.g. glass) that increases breakage and returns. [c7268fae6d38]
- Not checking seasonality and getting caught off guard on cash flow. [52858475e05d]
- Choosing a perishable product without checking shelf life against realistic sell-through time, risking dead stock. [646fd0db3481]
- Investing in a regulated category (food, cosmetics, baby products) before researching restrictions. [5f471d64cfd2]
- Treating the 16-question pass as a strict pass/fail instead of scoring pass/caution/red flag and accepting some flags. [6d1e8d68fa75]
