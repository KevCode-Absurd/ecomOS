## 1. What this source is for

Gives a beginner-to-intermediate dropshipper a repeatable weekly routine for finding products to test: a 4-point pass/fail screen, three sourcing methods (2 free, 1 paid), and a positioning technique ("Blue Ocean" angle pivoting) for reviving products that look too competitive to touch. It is a sourcing-and-screening playbook, not a full marketing or store-build guide, though it name-checks marketing psychology as "the other 50%" of the game.

## 2. Principles

1. **Margin over trend.** A product must clear a hard gross-margin floor before anything else is considered. Condition: applies to every candidate from every method (ad library, Reels, Winning Hunter). Evidence: presenter's own examples (hand massager $25→$80, Pilates set $40→$120, ab-routine equipment $80→$160). `[9651e08d5907]` `[9ffdbd6051d3]`
2. **Problem-solving beats "cool."** The product must relieve a real pain point, insecurity or friction (pain, hygiene, home fitness, convenience) rather than ride pure novelty. Evidence: contrast of "junk" (music projector, wall-mount gym bag) versus "winners" (hand massager, Pilates set). `[9651e08d5907]` `[9ffdbd6051d3]`
3. **Content availability is a hard gate, not a nice-to-have.** If there is no existing TikTok/social content for a product, the source says skip it, because you need raw footage to build ads and product-page assets. Condition: applies before you commit ad spend; does not apply to sourcing decisions once you already have your own content plan. `[9651e08d5907]`
4. **Avoid heavy/oversized items.** Framed as a logistics rule specific to dropshipping fulfillment (shipping cost and damage risk), not a product-quality rule. `[9651e08d5907]`
5. **Selling what you personally like is not a research method.** The source's own counter-example: "if selling what you like worked, I'd be selling golf gear or fish pics." This is presenter opinion, not tested against data. `[4255b2303ee8]`
6. **Copying a competitor's product/store as a beginner "simply doesn't work."** Stated as an absolute by the source with no supporting data; framed as a beginner-specific rule, not a claim about experienced operators (the source later builds an entire method — Blue Ocean pivoting — on deliberately entering already-crowded products). `[4255b2303ee8]`
7. **Saturation signals demand, not failure.** Many competitors selling a product proves people already want it, which the source treats as valuable because it removes "teach the market from scratch" risk. Condition: only actionable if you have a concrete plan to reach an audience segment competitors are not addressing. `[4255b2303ee8]` `[2ad4276c7787]`
8. **Random, un-niched products lower the bar to entry for beginners.** Dropshipping assumption: this is presented specifically as a way to dodge high CPMs in crowded, brand-driven verticals (beauty aimed at women, dog products) where an unbranded new seller cannot compete on ad cost. Explicitly framed as optional ("by no means a must-do"), not a permanent rule. `[c97ea08c9bfc]`
9. **50/50 split between finding a product and thinking about how to market it.** The source attributes half of the "product research" outcome to audience/positioning strategy (Blue Ocean), not to the sourcing method used. This is an opinion/estimate, not a measured breakdown. `[2ad4276c7787]`
10. **Angle pivoting keeps the product and offer fixed; only the buyer avatar and message change.** This keeps the pivot cheap (no new sourcing) and fast to test. `[c4dc84a5fa7e]`
11. **A viable pivot needs a real, visible gap in the competitive set** — audited from actual running ads — not a hypothetical audience. The source's own guardrail: an angle should be a small share of current spend (example: ~10%), not literally zero, since zero-share angles carry more unproven-demand risk. `[c4dc84a5fa7e]` `[c840da78d857]`

## 3. Procedures

**A. Baseline screen (run on every candidate, from any method)**
1. Check gross margin: sale price minus supplier cost must clear the source's $30–$40 floor (see thresholds table).
2. Confirm the product solves a real problem (pain, hygiene, fitness, convenience, insecurity) rather than only riding a visual/novelty trend.
3. Search the product name on TikTok; confirm usable existing video content exists.
4. Check physical weight/size; reject heavy or oversized items for shipping-logistics reasons.
5. If all four pass, log the candidate in the research sheet (see Procedure D) before spending further time or money on it.
`[9651e08d5907]`

**B. Method 1 — Meta Ad Library (free)**
1. Open Meta Ad Library. `[verify UI]`
2. Search a generic dropshipping-style offer keyword: "free shipping" or "50% off" (these attract dropshipping-style advertisers rather than established brands). Vertical keywords like "pain relief" work too, for problem-solving products specifically.
3. Filter to Active ads only. `[verify UI]`
4. Optionally restrict the date filter to the last one to two weeks, to bias toward newly launched tests. `[verify UI]`
5. Scroll results; screen each candidate product against the baseline checklist (Procedure A).
6. For a candidate that passes, search that exact product name again in Ad Library and count distinct stores currently running ads for it.
7. Apply the competitor threshold (see table): proceed if under 5 stores in the last 7 days; under 3 is a stronger signal.
8. Repeat with different generic and vertical keywords to keep sourcing new candidates.
`[0cdc9e5914ee]`

**C. Method 1b — TikTok cross-check (paired with B)**
1. Search the exact product term on TikTok.
2. Assess volume of usable product videos.
3. Download clips (source mentions "a Facebook video downloader" or direct save) and mix/remix into your own edits — do not repost competitor video untouched.
4. Do not disqualify a product for being saturated on TikTok alone; weight what you see on your actual ad platform (Meta, per this source) far more heavily.
5. Note in the research sheet when a product is TikTok-heavy but Meta-light — the source treats this gap as an untapped signal worth recording.
`[cf56089fcd7c]`

**D. Method 2 — Instagram Reels feed training (free)**
1. Create a brand-new Instagram account (so existing feed history does not pollute recommendations).
2. Use in-app search for the hashtag #winningproduct.
3. Like 5 to 10 videos that clearly show physical products (including obvious dropshipping ads). Source notes this works "even better on mobile."
4. Switch to the Reels tab; the algorithm should begin serving a stream of trending dropshipping product ads.
5. Continue double-tapping/liking every video that clears your criteria (high margin, solves a problem, usable visuals) to reinforce the signal; expect a mix of low-value "junk" items mixed in.
6. For each promising product, check AliExpress for the real sourcing cost.
7. Log qualifying products in the research sheet.
8. Repeat in short, frequent sessions rather than one long session — the source frames this as continuous, idle-time research.
`[ab2f47bf79d4]` `[1bd4e5736ad6]`

**E. Method 3 — Winning Hunter (paid ad-spy tool)**
1. In the tool, select the drop-ship ads filter so results show only dropshipping-style creatives. `[verify UI]`
2. Scroll results, skipping irrelevant categories (the presenter personally skips kitchen).
3. Screen candidates against the baseline checklist (Procedure A).
4. Save qualifying ads to a saved/favorites list inside the tool.
5. Scroll for 10–20 minutes per day to build the saved list, then periodically pick favorites from it.
6. Open the competitor's store from a saved ad; judge store quality (a poorly built store is "beatable").
7. Use the tool's AI competitor-search feature (named "Magic AI" in this source) with a query like the product name (example: "toothbrush cleaner") to list every store currently running that product and related products in the category. `[verify UI]`
8. Apply the competitor thresholds (see table): proceed if under ~10 total stores are selling it and no more than 3 are "serious" (well-built, branded) competitors. A store that has stopped running the product does not count against the total.
9. Pull video/photo assets and layout inspiration from weaker competitor sites to build your own creative and page.
`[2ee82e58c7a1]`

**F. Daily research log and selection funnel**
1. Maintain a Google Sheet of candidates with columns: product name, buy price, intended sell price, competitor pages to revisit, free-text notes (example note given: "seen little on Facebook but heavy on TikTok — untapped").
2. Add roughly 3 products per day that pass the baseline screen.
3. Narrow the day's 3 down to the strongest 3 overall favorites (by margin, clarity of problem solved, and weakest competition).
4. Pick 1 of those 3 to actually run as that day's live test.
5. Use the sheet as the artifact for reviewing decisions later (the presenter says he reviews students' sheets live on coaching calls).
`[9b3d44f6bf5b]`

**G. Blue Ocean angle-pivot audit (for a saturated but otherwise-qualifying product)**
1. Confirm the product clears the baseline screen (margin, problem, content, weight) — do this before pivoting, not instead of it.
2. Search the product on TikTok (or your ad platform) and catalogue which audiences current advertisers are targeting, based on the creative and copy you see.
3. Write down explicitly what none, or very few, of the current ads are saying: which customer type, life stage or outcome is absent or rare.
4. List concrete alternative avatars the product genuinely serves (not any avatar — one it actually fits). Source examples: a shoe dryer sold generically as a "shoe dryer" could instead target parents of kids with muddy shoes, campers/outdoor enthusiasts, or fitness players with smelly shoes; a home-fitness product sold generically to young women doing quick home workouts could instead target older adults with stiff joints who won't commit to a gym, or people who are moderately overweight and want to "take back control."
5. Check that your chosen angle is a minority share of current ad volume (source's own example: ~10% of ads used the weight-loss framing) rather than a literal zero — some existing volume on the angle is treated as validation, total absence is riskier.
6. Rebuild the hook, landing copy, imagery and proof entirely around the new avatar's specific situation — do not bolt the new avatar on as a secondary message.
7. Keep the product/offer itself unchanged; this is a positioning and creative change only, not a new sourcing project.
`[c4dc84a5fa7e]` `[c840da78d857]` `[2ad4276c7787]`

**H. Geo-arbitrage (a specific Blue Ocean variant)**
1. Identify a product with strong existing US/UK ad volume (used here as proof of demand, not as a market to enter).
2. Choose a target market where that same product has not yet been advertised (source example: an "untapped" European country).
3. Localize creative and copy into the target market's language and context before launch.
4. Verify local shipping cost, delivery time and import duty/VAT rules before committing — the source flags these can erase the margin advantage. `[web check: destination-country import duty, VAT and shipping-time rules before launch]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is this candidate worth researching further?** Check margin math first (fastest disqualifier), then problem-solved, then content availability, then weight/shipping. Good: passes all four. Bad: fails any one — drop it immediately rather than researching competitors first. `[9651e08d5907]`
- **Is a product too saturated on Meta to test?** Check active-ad count for the exact product name in the last 7 days. Good: under 5 (better: under 3). Bad: 5+ active distinct stores with no differentiation angle in hand — either find a Blue Ocean angle (Procedure G) or drop it. `[0cdc9e5914ee]`
- **Is TikTok saturation disqualifying?** Check: is your ad platform Meta? If yes, TikTok saturation alone is not disqualifying — treat it "with a grain of salt" because it is a different platform from where you will advertise. If your ad platform is TikTok itself, the source's guidance does not directly transfer; weight TikTok's own competitive density instead. `[cf56089fcd7c]`
- **Is a Winning Hunter candidate viable?** Check total stores selling it (good: <10) and count of well-built/branded storefronts among them (good: ≤3 "serious"). Bad: 10+ total or 4+ serious competitors with no differentiation plan — pass or find a pivot angle first. `[2ee82e58c7a1]`
- **Is a competitor a real threat or not?** Open their store. Good (low threat): the site looks unfinished/generic/"awful," or the store has stopped running the product. Bad (real threat): a polished, clearly branded storefront actively running the ad. `[2ee82e58c7a1]`
- **Is a Blue Ocean angle worth pursuing?** Check the share of current ad volume using that angle. Good: a small but nonzero share (example: ~10%) — some validation exists. Caution: a literal 0% share on TikTok/Meta search is treated by the source as opportunity but carries more unproven-demand risk since no one has tested it yet (source does not explicitly resolve this trade-off). `[c4dc84a5fa7e]`
- **Is this genuinely a "random"/beginner-safe product or a hidden competitive niche?** Check whether it falls into an established, brand-heavy vertical (dog products, mainstream beauty for women). Good: no clear niche home, low expected CPC. Bad: fits an established vertical — expect higher ad costs and more competition even if this specific SKU looks unclaimed today. `[c97ea08c9bfc]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Selling what you personally like instead of validating demand. `[4255b2303ee8]`
- Copying a competitor's product/store as a beginner and trying to make it "a bit better." `[4255b2303ee8]`
- Choosing a product with no existing online content, leaving you with no raw material for ads or product pages. `[4255b2303ee8]` `[9651e08d5907]`
- Selling heavy/oversized items without understanding the shipping-logistics burden. `[9651e08d5907]`
- Treating a product's trend/virality as validation instead of checking real margin and problem-solved fit ("junk" examples: music projector, wall-mount gym bag). `[9ffdbd6051d3]`
- Reposting a competitor's ad video untouched instead of re-editing/remixing it — flagged as an ethics/rights issue, not just a strategy note. `[cf56089fcd7c]`
- Letting a fresh Instagram Reels feed drift without continued curation — the source says feed training keeps drifting, so you must keep liking to stay on-topic. `[ab2f47bf79d4]`
- "Robotic," non-thinking dropshipping — running products on autopilot without doing the audience/competitor-gap audit — is called out by name as how "you lose your money."
- Forcing an ill-fitting avatar onto a product purely to escape saturation, which the source says wastes ad budget. `[2ad4276c7787]`
- Skipping the logistics check (import duty, VAT, shipping time) when doing geo-arbitrage, which can erase the margin advantage the pivot was supposed to create. `[533302cea515]`

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Self-reported, unverified numbers.** Every revenue figure, the "3–5 winners a week" and "1 in 2 products succeed" claims are the presenter's own unaudited statements. Treat all as marketing claims for the source's paid program, not measured benchmarks. `[web check: presenter identity and any independent corroboration]`
- **Internal tension on competition.** The source says (a) "copying a competitor as a beginner... simply doesn't work" but also (b) recommends directly entering saturated markets via Blue Ocean angle pivoting, and (c) treats geo-arbitrage — copying a proven US/UK product into a new market — as "pretty much 100% success" with no differentiation required beyond geography. The source does not reconcile why copying-with-a-new-audience/geography is fine while copying-with-the-same-audience is not; the operative distinguishing factor it implies is "does something in your execution differ from what current sellers do" (audience, geography, or angle), not the product itself.
- **Margin figure is dropshipping-specific and stated as dollars, not percentage or margin rate.** A $30–$40 floor makes little sense for a $15 item versus a $300 item; the source never addresses margin as a percentage or how the floor should scale with price point. Flag as thin: `no percentage-margin guidance given`.
- **No stated sample size or win-rate methodology** behind the "under 5 stores" / "under 10 stores, max 3 serious" thresholds — presented as personal heuristics only, not backed by a described test.
- **TikTok/Meta platform-weighting advice is stated for a Meta-first advertiser.** The source explicitly says the guidance changes if you advertise elsewhere; this playbook should not be read as recommending Meta as the only or best channel — that is unaddressed here. `[cf56089fcd7c]`
- **Winning Hunter recommendation carries a disclosed affiliate discount code.** The presenter states no sponsorship, but a financial incentive (referral code) exists; treat competitor/tool comparisons in this source as non-neutral. `[web check: whether Winning Hunter is still the presenter's recommended/affiliated tool, and current market alternatives]`
- **No guidance on legal/IP risk of "downloading and mixing" competitor or influencer video** beyond "never steal a video... if you want to have some morals, maybe mix the videos into your own" — this is a soft ethical aside, not a rights-clearance procedure. Flag as thin.
- **No guidance on supplier vetting, shipping-time SLAs, or return/refund handling** — the source is entirely about finding and screening products, not fulfilling or operating them. That is out of scope for this document; point to a fulfillment/operations playbook if one exists in this library.
- **"Blue Ocean" as used here is a loose borrowing of the general strategy term**, applied narrowly to audience/geo pivoting on an already-sourced product; it is not the source's own coinage and the source does not cite the original strategy literature.
