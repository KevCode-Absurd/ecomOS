# Ecommerce specificity review: 10-MCR-paid-social-creative

Scope: PLAYBOOK.md, checked against `sources/`. Does not edit PLAYBOOK.md.

## 1. Score: 3 out of 5

This playbook is a solid, well-sourced creative-craft manual, but most of its 178 rules are generic direct-response advertising knowledge that would read the same for a course seller or a lead-gen funnel. The ecommerce edge is real but a minority: offer and bundle economics tied to AOV, inventory and cash pacing for winners, contribution margin, product-photo accuracy, and destination match to a PDP or educational page. The playbook correctly hands account structure, offers and landing pages to neighbor folders, so what remains is mostly hook, script and static craft, and that craft is not store-specific by nature.

### Section 3 rule count by type (178 rules, R1 to R178)

- (a) Store-specific: 18 rules (10%). Example: R8 (bundles/AOV/subscription), R133 (spend paced by inventory and cash), R142 (add-to-cart, checkout, shipping in the read chain), R166 (real product photo), R177 (offer must match live checkout).
- (b) Generic but correctly applied to a store: 28 rules (16%). Example: R1 (concept levers, offer tied to store), R24 (gross profit per ad), R128 (volume from account economics), R145 (incremental ROAS at campaign level).
- (c) Generic with no store framing: 132 rules (74%). Example: hook grading (R76), video script skeleton (R52), founder ad structure (R99), most of compliance (R169 to R176). These would read the same for a software brand.
- (d) Out of scope for a store: 0 rules. No B2B, SaaS or service-only rule was found. The 2 live-selling sources (06, 14) are isolated in Appendix A already.

These counts are a judgment call. A rule counted as (a) only when it named a store mechanic (PDP, checkout, AOV, SKU, inventory, subscription, real product photo). A rule counted as (b) when the idea is generic but tied to an ad-account or store number (ROAS, CPA, offer). Everything else is (c).

## 2. Prioritisation check: no priority block exists

Section 0's task table is a router, not a ranked list of what matters most for a store, and carries no conditions by store type. There is no answer anywhere to "for this brand, right now, what matters most." Draft block below.

### 3.0 Ecommerce priority order (draft, up to 12 levers)

1. Concept before format: persona x angle x offer (R1, R3, R108). Hero-SKU stores gain most from angle testing; catalog stores from persona testing across SKUs. `[general practice]` rank.
2. Offer as a store lever, not a discount: bundles, tiered quantity, subscription (R8, R72). Low-AOV consumables need 3/5/6 packs; subscription needs first-box vs renewal framing.
3. Weight new assets toward cold stages, 70%+ (R20, R25). Across the catalog, or the 1 SKU for a hero-SKU store.
4. Match destination to the ad: educational page for curiosity, PDP for a clear offer (R30, R34). Marketplace-heavy sellers decide this first, since there may be no owned PDP. `[general practice]` rank.
5. Pull objections from the store's own data: reviews, tickets, checkout abandoner calls (R13, R26, R59, R178). Apparel stores should add fit and sizing even though no source names it (section 4).
6. Watch hero-SKU concentration risk (R126, R131). A single-SKU store cannot shift spend when its top ad fatigues; a catalog store can.
7. Size monthly volume and budget to the store's own spend tier and hit rate (R128, R129), not the fallback.
8. Judge winners on contribution margin and portfolio profit, not top ROAS (R146, R147). Matters most for thin-margin goods.
9. Pace spend by inventory and cash position, not ROAS alone (R133, R155). Ranks below the growth levers since it constrains them. `[general practice]` rank.
10. Use repeat-purchase and LTV economics only where repeat rate is real (R123). A one-off or gift store holds to first-order ROAS. `[general practice]` rank.
11. Keep product images accurate to the real item (R166, R42). Ranks above compliance since a wrong color or texture drives returns, not just policy risk.
12. Run the compliance gate last, sized to category risk (P19, 3.16). Health and beauty carry the most risk; home goods and apparel the least.

## 3. Rule rewrites (15 of the clearest generic-to-store cases)

Condition, sources and card ids stay as written in PLAYBOOK.md; only the rule text changes.

- **R3.** Before opening a design tool or booking a shoot, decide what the ad must say about this product, price and offer. Format comes after that.
- **R11.** Build personas from what customers say in reviews, tickets and post-purchase surveys, not from age or gender labels.
- **R19.** Tag every creative with the awareness stage it serves and the shopper-journey point it lands at: before cart, at cart, at checkout, or post-purchase.
- **R30.** State on every brief whether the click lands on an educational page, the PDP or the cart, and confirm that page is live and matches the ad's offer.
- **R44.** Do not send a plain white-background product shot to cold shoppers who have never seen the PDP; it reads as a catalog thumbnail, not a reason to click through.
- **R65.** Edit so a shopper scrolling on their phone keeps watching past the first product frame; a still talking head reads as skippable next to a store's other content.
- **R84.** A shopper scrolling past other product ads decides to stop in well under 3 seconds; the first frame must beat the next ad in the feed.
- **R95.** Read hook rate and hold rate against this store's own account average, not an industry number, before blaming the hook or the body.
- **R120.** Build a VSL only where a shopper cannot understand the product from a PDP and 1 ad: a new ingredient, an unfamiliar mechanism, or a category the store's own data shows converts poorly cold.
- **R127.** Set the store's own 7-out-of-10 bar from its past winning ads before any new creative counts toward the monthly target.
- **R135.** Keep 1 ad set per persona-and-angle pair so the store can see which segment and message drives purchases, not just which ad set spent the most.
- **R138.** Do not judge a new pack before day 7; a store's checkout and shipping promise takes time to show up in purchase data.
- **R151.** Before touching a fatigued ad, check whether the persona pool is small for this product, or the ad has simply run its course.
- **R156.** Keep the portfolio diverse (persona, angle, offer, format) so the platform serves each ad to a different shopper, instead of merging near-identical ads and starving the account of fresh reach.
- **R169.** Before launch, run every health, weight, body, fertility or appearance claim past what the product can prove and past platform and local rules for the store's market.

## 6. Deliverables check (section 9): mostly pass

## 7. The single highest-value change

Add section 3.0, the ecommerce priority order, and point every other section back to it by store type. Right now an agent loading this playbook gets 178 rules of mostly generic creative craft, with the store-specific rules (offer economics, inventory pacing, contribution margin, destination match) at the same weight as hook-grading theory. A short, ranked block, "for a single-SKU subscription brand do this first, for a multi-SKU catalog brand do this first," lets the agent act like an ecommerce specialist from the first read, instead of reading 38,000 words hoping the store-specific parts stand out. Everything needed already exists in the playbook's own rules; it only needs pulling forward and ranking.
