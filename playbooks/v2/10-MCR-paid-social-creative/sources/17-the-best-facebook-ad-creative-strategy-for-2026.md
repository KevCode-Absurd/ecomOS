## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator a repeatable loop for Meta creative, which the presenter calls a "creative flywheel": source concepts (mainly from competitors' longest-running ads), build many on-brand versions, launch each batch as its own ad set ("pack") in 1 prospecting CBO campaign, force early spend with a 7-day ad set minimum equal to 1x target CPA, analyze with incremental attribution sorted by spend, then iterate on the winners and repeat. It also argues for judging concepts by total profit across a portfolio rather than by the single highest ROAS. It is strong on the loop and the test-launch settings. It is thin on how to turn a competitor concept into your own ad, what a concept, iteration and variation each are, copy, and how to set a threshold.

## 2. Principles

1. **Build a system that raises the win rate, not the ad count.** He contrasts the "old school" workflow (think of an idea, make ads that look like past ones, upload a single image or video, write primary text, headlines and descriptions by hand) with a system that "increases the likelihood of finding winning ads". Pumping out 1,000 ads in an hour, making "fake UGC", or hiring many creators gives a "very low" win rate. Condition: he gives no win-rate numbers for either approach. [a15bb69c9b3a]
2. **Work in new concepts, iterations and variations, not duplicates of your best ad.** He says these are "all very different things" under Andromeda and GEM. He does not define them. Conflict: later in the talk he tells you to create "duplicates, variants, iterations" of winning ads (see section 9). [a15bb69c9b3a] [cba1f889934b] [be42d3427124]
3. **New ads come from 2 places, and competitors are the most important one.** He names competitors as "the single most important place". He never names the second place out loud; the loop later makes it clear that the second source is your own winning ads. [5790ccbc3904] [be42d3427124]
4. **Longest-running or highest-spend competitor ads are the signal.** Look for ads that "have either been live the longest or spent the most". An ad marked "low impression count" is not a high performer. Condition: run time and spend are proxies, not proof of profit (card caveat). He has no data on the competitors' results. [5790ccbc3904]
5. **Study brands 1 or 2 steps above you, not category giants.** If you do $1 million in revenue, look at a brand doing $10 million. Do not compare a small athleisure brand to Lululemon or Nike ("I'm never going to take inspiration from Nike"). Also avoid brands so small they are "not driving any sales". Condition: he says this right after using Lululemon as his demo, so the demo brand breaks his own rule for a small brand. [5790ccbc3904] `[needs brand data: the brand's revenue tier and a list of competitors 1 to 2 steps above it]`
6. **Look for real concepts and common threads across competitors.** Collect videos, statics, GIFs and carousels. Each concept "can be heavily expanded on". Do this for every competitor, find the "common threads of creative that generally work in your category", then build those for yourself. [5790ccbc3904] [cba1f889934b]
7. **Expand 1 proven concept into dozens of versions across your catalog.** "You should extrapolate out dozens of different iterations of this." If you know a concept wins, you can make it "for every single product". Condition: he says "if I know it's a winning concept". Conflict with ecomOS rules on how close to copy (section 9). [cba1f889934b]
8. **New ads should compete inside your own account.** Launch all new ads in a prospecting CBO campaign because "we want competition within our own ad account". [08ed4f654c83]
9. **New ads starve unless you force spend.** Older ads "hog all the spend", even for new brands. Without a push, new ads take too long to get enough spend to be judged, which keeps the account in a "non-stop waiting cycle" and slows growth. The ad set minimum shortens the wait: a winner scales sooner, a loser stays at the floor spend. [663713e886d2]
10. **Waiting is a step, but only with the right setup.** "You wait." He says patience is only possible when the new ads are set up to get spend. [663713e886d2] [a15bb69c9b3a]
11. **Winners are high-spend and above threshold, with outsized results on that spend.** Not the highest ROAS on low spend. Leave ads that sit just at the threshold alone for now. [2497f72f8deb]
12. **Set the threshold from your own economics, even if it looks low.** His example brand needs incremental ROAS above about 1.1 on new customers. He says this "sounds crazy to many people" but "that is just what this brand functions at". He does not explain why that brand can run at 1.1. [2497f72f8deb] `[needs brand data: breakeven incremental ROAS for new customers, from contribution margin and repeat value]`
13. **Care about total profit, not the single best ROAS.** "Old school Facebook" says run only the 10x ad. "Andromeda Facebook" wants you to run every profitable concept, because each adds profit. "Don't care as much about an ad that runs a 5x versus a 2x. As long as 2x is profitable, we want to run both." [1defb5a33424]
14. **Concepts get matched to audiences and each performs at its own rate.** He presents this as what happens when you run many concepts, not as a manual targeting step. The card turns it into an operator action ("match each concept to the audiences"); the transcript does not give a how. [1defb5a33424]
15. **The flywheel is for growth, not maintenance.** As concepts stack, raise spend. "It doesn't mean you have to increase your spend. It just means that you should not be afraid of things like the learning phase or increasing spend in general." [be42d3427124]
16. **Repetition builds core concepts.** Doing the same loop "over and over" solidifies core concepts in the account, and you "stack concepts". A creative machine is "one of the most important things" for fueling and scaling a Meta account. [be42d3427124] [a15bb69c9b3a]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 The flywheel loop (overview)

1. Source new concepts: competitors first, then your own winners. [a15bb69c9b3a]
2. Build them for your brand and products as many versions. [cba1f889934b]
3. Launch each batch as a new ad set in the prospecting CBO campaign, with a 7-day ad set minimum of 1x target CPA. [08ed4f654c83] [663713e886d2]
4. Wait up to 7 days, then remove the minimum. [663713e886d2]
5. Analyze with incremental attribution, sorted by spend. [2497f72f8deb]
6. Take the high-spend, above-threshold ads back to step 2 as concepts to iterate. [be42d3427124]
7. Repeat. Raise spend as the account grows. [be42d3427124]

### 3.2 Source concepts from the Meta Ad Library (free route) `[verify UI]`

1. Open the Facebook Ad Library. Click "All ads". [5790ccbc3904]
2. Search each relevant competitor one by one and select their page. [5790ccbc3904]
3. Check whether the brand runs ads from more than 1 page. Search each page. [5790ccbc3904]
4. Change the active status to all available ads, because some ads may be paused after a period of time. [5790ccbc3904]
5. Skip ads marked "low impression count". [5790ccbc3904]
6. Scroll to the bottom to find the ads that have run the longest. He warns this "might take a minute" on a big brand. [5790ccbc3904]
7. Save the real concepts you find (videos, statics, GIFs, carousels) into a collection. Note each as its own concept. [5790ccbc3904] [cba1f889934b]
8. Repeat for every competitor. Look for threads that repeat across brands. [5790ccbc3904]

### 3.3 Source concepts with Magic Brief (paid route) `[verify UI]`

1. Search the competitor. Magic Brief shows active ads across the brand's pages in 1 view (he saw 2,382 active Lululemon ads, against "hundreds" on 1 Ad Library page). [5790ccbc3904]
2. Scroll the overview and download the imagery if wanted. [5790ccbc3904]
3. Pick the ads that have run the longest. He says the tool is not a sponsor. [5790ccbc3904] `[web check: Magic Brief current features and price]`

### 3.4 Turn a competitor concept into your ads

The source is thin here. What it gives:
1. Take the long-running concept (his example: a Lululemon carousel live for 469 days) as inspiration. [cba1f889934b]
2. Build it for your own brand. He says "we can recreate this very easily" and "you can make this exact ad ... for every single product". The card adds: re-shoot or re-design with your own product and assets, do not reuse their files. [cba1f889934b]
3. Make dozens of iterations of it. [cba1f889934b]
4. Card adds (not in transcript): spread versions across the formats the competitor used, and keep a running concept library so each ad maps to a known concept. [cba1f889934b]

No steps are given for briefing, copy, hooks, shooting, editing or design.

### 3.5 Launch a pack in the prospecting CBO campaign `[verify UI]`

1. Use 1 prospecting campaign with campaign budget optimization (CBO). [08ed4f654c83]
2. Every time you have new creatives, create a new ad set for them. Do not add them to an existing ad set. [08ed4f654c83]
3. Put 4 to 8 creatives in the ad set. He says anywhere from 1, 2, 10 or 15 happens; 4 to 8 is "generally ... the sweet spot". "Don't over complicate it." [08ed4f654c83]
4. Name ad sets as numbered packs. His example account showed "Broadpack 76", meaning over 76 ad sets launched and tested for 1 brand. [08ed4f654c83]

### 3.6 Set the 7-day ad set minimum `[verify UI]`

1. Open the new ad set. Go to the "Budget and schedule" section. [663713e886d2]
2. Click "Ad set spending limits". [663713e886d2]
3. Click "Set a minimum or maximum". [663713e886d2]
4. Change the percent value to a dollar value. [663713e886d2]
5. Enter an average daily minimum equal to 1x the target CPA. Example: target CPA $50, minimum $50. [663713e886d2] `[needs brand data: target CPA]`
6. Set a calendar invite or alarm for 7 days out. "These never stay on for more than seven days at a time." [663713e886d2]

### 3.7 Review at day 7

1. Look back within the 7-day window and decide whether you are willing to spend more on this ad set. [663713e886d2]
2. Remove the minimum. He describes this as "a win-win": a winner is usually spending above the floor and keeps spending above it after removal; a loser drops to lower spend. [663713e886d2]
3. Do not leave the minimum on by default. Keeping it keeps spend at the same level, "which is most likely what you do not want to do". [663713e886d2]
4. Card adds (not in transcript): kill the ad set if it only spent at the floor; reapply the minimum on the next pack. [663713e886d2]

### 3.8 Analyze with incremental attribution `[verify UI]`

1. Open the prospecting campaign. [2497f72f8deb]
2. Open the column set, go to "Compare attribution settings" and select "incremental attribution". [2497f72f8deb]
3. Sort all ads from highest to lowest spend. He picks a date range; his example was the last 14 days. [2497f72f8deb]
4. Default: look at all ads at once. Also break them into groups by product, category or whatever else matters in the business. "There are nuances to everything I am talking about." [2497f72f8deb]
5. Flag ads that are high spend, above your incremental ROAS threshold, and show outsized results on that spend. [2497f72f8deb]
6. Leave ads that sit just at the threshold untouched for now. [2497f72f8deb]
7. Send the flagged winners to iteration. [2497f72f8deb] [be42d3427124]

### 3.9 Iterate on your own winners

1. Treat each winner the way you treated competitor concepts: make duplicates, variants and iterations. [be42d3427124]
2. Make variant families across collections: same concept, swap products and model shots. His Talentless example keeps a simple headline and groups different products and model shots with "semi in between shots, like these trio shots". [be42d3427124]
3. Merge 2 proven concepts into a third. His example: concept 1 plus concept 2 gives concept 3. [be42d3427124]
4. Launch the new versions as a new pack (3.5) and repeat. [be42d3427124]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **New ads get no spend.** Check: spend on packs launched in the last 7 days against older ad sets in the same CBO. Bad: new packs near $0 while old ads take most of the budget. Fix (source): add the 1x-CPA ad set minimum for up to 7 days. [663713e886d2]
- **Minimum left on too long.** Check: any ad set with a spending minimum older than 7 days. Bad: minimum still on, ad set spending only at the floor. Fix: remove the minimum. [663713e886d2]
- **Budget can cover the floors (not in source).** The source does not say what happens when many packs run minimums at once. Check that the number of packs with a minimum times target CPA per day fits inside the CBO daily budget `[verify UI: how Meta handles ad set minimums that exceed or crowd the campaign budget]` `[needs brand data: campaign daily budget and number of live packs]`.
- **Is it a winner?** Check: incremental attribution view, sorted by spend. Good: high spend, well above threshold (example: $26,000 spend, over 25% of campaign spend, at 1.46 against a 1.1 bar). Hold: at the line (1.13, 1.16). Next: iterate the good ones. [2497f72f8deb]
- **Reported vs incremental ROAS.** His example showed 1.42 reported and 1.23 incremental on the same $97,000. Check both and judge against a threshold set in the same terms. [2497f72f8deb]
- **Is a competitor ad worth studying?** Good: long run time (his example: 469 days), not "low impression count", from a brand 1 to 2 steps above you. Bad: category giant, or a tiny brand with no sales. Caveat: long run does not prove profit. [5790ccbc3904]
- **Is a competitor search complete?** Check: does the brand run from several pages? Is the status set to all ads, not only active? A paid tool may show far more ads (2,382 vs "hundreds" for Lululemon). [5790ccbc3904]
- **Are you chasing a single high ROAS?** Check: rank concepts by profit contribution at their spend, not by ROAS alone. Bad: cutting a 2x concept that spends $50,000 profitably to feed a 10x concept that can only spend $500. [1defb5a33424] `[needs brand data: contribution margin per order]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Duplicating your best ad instead of making new concepts, iterations and variations. [a15bb69c9b3a]
- Chasing volume (1,000 ads an hour, fake UGC, many creators) with a low win rate. [a15bb69c9b3a]
- Making new ads that just look like old ones, and writing copy by hand for each single upload. [a15bb69c9b3a]
- Studying giants like Nike or Lululemon as a small brand, or brands too small to be selling. [5790ccbc3904]
- Taking inspiration from "low impression count" ads. [5790ccbc3904]
- Searching only 1 page or only active ads in the Ad Library. [5790ccbc3904]
- Adding new ads to old ad sets, where older ads take the spend. [08ed4f654c83] [663713e886d2]
- Waiting passively for new ads to get spend. [663713e886d2]
- Forgetting to return after 7 days, or leaving the minimum on so a loser keeps spending. [663713e886d2]
- Touching ads that sit just at the threshold. [2497f72f8deb]
- Running only the highest-ROAS ad and cutting profitable lower-ROAS concepts. [1defb5a33424]
- Fearing the learning phase or higher spend; treating the system as maintenance. [be42d3427124]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Self-contradiction on duplicates.** Early: "instead of duplicating your best performing ads" focus on concepts, iterations and variations. Later: create "duplicates, variants, iterations of these winning ads". Read the second as "make new versions of the winning concept", but the source does not resolve it. [a15bb69c9b3a] [be42d3427124]
- **"7 days" vs "a few days".** He says minimums "never stay on for more than seven days" and also that the minimum "only forces the spend for a few days". Treat 7 days as the maximum. [663713e886d2]
- **Concept, iteration, variation are never defined.** He says Andromeda treats them as different things but gives no definitions or examples of each. Pass 2 should take definitions from other sources in this folder, not from this one.
- **Copying competitors conflicts with ecomOS rules.** He says "we can recreate this very easily" and "you can make this exact ad ... for every single product". ecomOS treats competitor ads as signals for layout and mood only and never copies text, logos, products or faces. The card also warns that literal copying risks brand and legal issues. Apply the ecomOS rule. [cba1f889934b]
- **Longevity is a proxy.** A long-running competitor ad may be a brand habit, not a profit driver. He has no data on competitor results. [5790ccbc3904]
- **His demo breaks his own rule.** He uses Lululemon as the demo, then says small brands should not study Lululemon.
- **Threshold setting is missing.** He says the 1.1 bar fits that brand but not how to compute one. It likely rests on margin and repeat value, which he does not state. `[needs brand data: breakeven incremental ROAS, contribution margin, repeat purchase value]` [2497f72f8deb]
- **"High spend" and "outsized" are undefined.** No share-of-spend or dollar cut-off is given beyond the $26,000 (over 25%) example.
- **Budget interaction is missing.** No guidance on how many packs can run minimums at once, how the CBO budget is set, or what happens to older winners when floors pull budget. `[verify UI]` `[needs brand data: daily budget, target CPA]`
- **The 1x CPA daily floor is his heuristic.** It is not a Meta rule. Over 7 days it can force up to 7x CPA per pack. Many operators instead judge a test after a spend of a set multiple of CPA, or use separate ABO test campaigns; the source does not compare. `[web check: current common practice on Meta creative testing structure]`
- **Many ad sets vs consolidation.** 76+ ad sets in 1 CBO may conflict with Andromeda-era advice to consolidate ad sets. The source does not address it. `[web check: Meta guidance on ad set consolidation under Andromeda]`
- **Incremental attribution caveats.** It is a Meta-modeled view, not a holdout test, and differs from reported ROAS (1.23 vs 1.42 in his example). The source does not say how it is computed or whether to cross-check with blended numbers. `[web check: Meta incremental attribution method]`
- **Profit framing is in ROAS only.** He says care about "your bank account" but all numbers are ROAS and spend. Real profit needs product cost, shipping, fees and returns. `[needs brand data: contribution margin]` [1defb5a33424]
- **"Matched to audiences" has no how.** He means the algorithm matches concepts to people; the card turns it into a manual step. Do not build a targeting procedure from this source. [1defb5a33424]
- **Card adds.** Several card lines are not in the transcript: kill the ad set after 7 days if it only spent at the floor [663713e886d2]; spread variations across formats and keep a concept library [cba1f889934b]; use creator, in-house or AI production and do not fake UGC [a15bb69c9b3a]; iterating on 1 concept can saturate [be42d3427124].
