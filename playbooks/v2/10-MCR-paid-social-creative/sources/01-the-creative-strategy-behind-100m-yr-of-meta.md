## 2. Principles

1. **Creative is the lever; media buying is compressed.** Condition: Meta in 2026 to 2027. Targeting is automated, consolidation wins, and old hacks (duplicating adsets, bidding tricks) no longer give an edge. Source's basis: an arbitrage exists only while a skill gap exists; most brands are "terrible at creative", so the gap is there now. [c7538e182b53] [7b353cb56615]
2. **The creative is the targeting.** Condition: broad targeting on Andromeda. Meta reads the ad transcript (it auto-transcribes every ad), on-screen words, copy and past conversion data, then finds buyers. Identical creative targets the same people. An ad that calls out "everyone" confuses delivery. Source recommends broad for "99% of people" (his estimate). [c7538e182b53] [b4b918f8b794] [7b353cb56615] [d8253f20d2a9]
3. **Similar creatives get bundled and cost you reach.** Condition: ads that are slight variants (same shoot with the model in a different pose, UGC with a small script change) or different formats speaking to the same audience. Andromeda serves them to one pool, so frequency rises, reach falls and ROAS declines. Diverse creatives reach separate audiences with little overlap and can scale into colder audiences. The Creative Similarity Score shows this; higher is worse; most accounts cannot see it, so ask a Meta rep. [1e5ab3cbe70b] [web check: Creative Similarity Score availability and scale]
4. **Portfolio beats hero ads.** Condition: always. Every creative fatigues and has a maximum spend threshold. Source prefers 3 ads holding $1,000/day at a blended 4x ROAS over 1 ad holding $1,000/day at 4x (illustrative). He says 8 to 10 clients came to him 40% down year on year in new customer acquisition after building an 8-figure business on 3 creatives that held $1M in spend; he calls that "too late" and says it needed fixing 6 to 8 months earlier. [5db051a99754] [07ea7f1c6fd7] [db5e1836d487]
5. **Novel creative keeps purchase probability up; repeated identical creative does not.** Condition: cold prospecting. Paraphrasing a Meta article from "4 or 5 years ago": after 2 impressions of the identical ad, purchase probability falls sharply toward 0; the 2nd view may be equal or slightly better than the 1st. Serving a different ad as the 3rd and 4th impression keeps probability high. [70e1681410fa] [web check: the Meta article on identical-creative frequency]
6. **Meta optimizes a sequence of ads; reporting is last click.** Condition: several ads in an adset. Meta optimizes on multi-click and view-through, but ad-level ROAS credits the last clicked ad. An opener can show 1.4x while the closer shows 6x (illustrative). Ads Manager gives no visibility into the sequence, so "trust the system". A skilled strategist can read the sequence from the creatives: unaware opener, product-aware middle, most-aware closer. [d304b260b888] [d875376938d0]
7. **ROAS reliability rises as you move up the structure.** Ad level: close to meaningless for decisions. Adset level: more reliable, but cross-adset serving exists (not prioritized), so one adset can look better by picking up bottom-funnel sales another adset created. Campaign level: "super reliable" and close to the P&L only on 7-day click with existing customers excluded. [9d506de16067] [b7fe20be2d09]
8. **4 practical implications of Andromeda:** concept diversity (1 concept per adset), format variety (Meta shows people the formats they engage with; the presenter says he never sees UGC or DPAs), hook quality (any persona call-out steers delivery to that persona), and volume (mean spend per ad is about $700 to $1,500 depending on account, so you need many ads). Volume without strategy does not work. [1d9173fc3207] [d8253f20d2a9] [db5e1836d487]
9. **A concept is persona + angle + offer, with format as a 4th, weaker lever.** Condition: planning diversity. Change at least 1 of the 3 to get a genuinely new concept. Offer change = different product, value proposition or packaging. Angle change = a different problem agitated (skincare: wrinkles versus another problem). Persona change = who you talk to and who is in the ad (a 60-year-old woman versus a 20-year-old woman). Format was added "recently" because Andromeda serves formats to different people; same persona, angle and offer in a new format gives some novel reach but a lot of overlap. [939b4100ac4f] [f18d8afedb58]
10. **Personas are archetypes defined by problems, desires and buying triggers, not demographics.** Condition: always, below mid 8 figures. Broad personas ("women 25 to 45", "busy professional", "health-conscious parent") make forgettable creative. Specific ones ("a consultant who flies twice a week and needs a carry-on wardrobe that works for boardrooms and bars", "a mom who's tried 6 different supplements because her toddler won't eat vegetables") tell you the script, the talent, the language and the proof. Better 5 ads for a specific persona than 5 for a broad one. Broad unaware ads that work for a whole population need very high skill; source says try them at "mid 8 figure" scale in one place and "$50 million plus" in another. [53ee81550f45] [939b4100ac4f] [e671f82f98b0]
11. **An angle is the argument, built from components, not product features.** Components: problem agitation (lead with pain; skip if the viewer is not problem aware), contrarian truth, social proof (stack toward the back), domain authority (a marathon runner for a running product, not a lab coat everywhere), curiosity gap (open early, close late), comparison or objection handling ("I tried this... it didn't work"), transformation (before and after). The angle is in effect the full script; a static states it in a few sentences. Proof must fit the persona (studies and case studies that match their age and gender). [087eee279056]
12. **Testing priority: angle, then offer, then persona, then format.** Condition: creative not working. Angle first, because past buyers prove the persona buys; if a specific persona stops buying it is an angle or offer problem. Offer may be the single most important variable, but you have little room to change it (single product; value-proposition tests run out). Persona gives breadth and volume once the offer is fixed. Format is last. Most teams run this order backwards (format, persona, offer, angle). "UGC as a concept doesn't work"; the script, persona, angle and offer inside it do. [4a3ce680ae23]
13. **A hook is a promise of relevance, not a trick.** Condition: video. Bait (memes) raises hook rate but not conversions. 3 layers: visual, audio, copy (overlay text, and also the primary text above the ad). Source says 80% of viewers never watch past the hook. [6ce954d6297e]
14. **Put production time into hooks over bodies, if body scripts are good.** In the same hour, 2 bodies x 15 hooks usually beats 2 hooks x 5 bodies, because the performance gap is larger in the hook. If scripting is weak, one lucky body can distort what you learn. [dd35cdd74a75]
15. **Relevance comes from the problem, not a persona call-out.** Source says persona call-outs are "super overrated"; people skip "agency owners who need X, listen up" but watch a hook that agitates their exact problem. This is his experience, not a measured result. [f994209714f5] [d8253f20d2a9]
16. **Novelty (white space) is what makes an ad hold very large spend.** Source uses "purple ocean": between red ocean (same as everyone, priced to zero) and blue ocean (truly new, no proven demand). Stay adjacent to proven demand but look different. Standard hook types can build an 8-figure brand; an ad that holds $500,000 of spend usually needs something novel up front, and competitors will then copy you. [f994209714f5] [10686d689660]
17. **Blend the bridge; introduce the product as late as possible.** Condition: top-of-funnel unaware video. A sudden jump from hook to product ("creatine from [brand] is your solution") ruins an otherwise good ad. Good TOF ads walk the viewer through unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, then aware. Do not introduce the product early just because the retention curve shows 80% gone by 90 seconds; later introduction gives fewer but more qualified viewers and, per source, more total purchases. [c218585e29e4] [3ae52427e9c8]
18. **Hook testing still works after Andromeda.** A new hook gets its own creative ID, is not bundled with the original, and reaches some new audience (with overlap, since the rest of the ad is the same concept). It is nearly free: record 6 hooks at the shoot, or put new hooks on old winners. [2f44cbccd498]
19. **Pick format last, after the concept.** Statics are cheap and can hold top-of-funnel spend; "images are bottom of funnel, video is top of funnel" is "a skill issue" per source. [bc432cb2ccf7] [aa8f85929d1f]
20. **DPAs look better than they are.** Attributed ROAS drops a lot once existing customers are removed and incremental attribution is applied. They can run on cold at about $1,000/day in some accounts but not $50,000/day. Moving budget from TOF to DPAs starves the funnel and causes a "death spiral" (seen often in fashion). [fe7572d0a82a] [d2be5dc4e5fa]
21. **Budget follows creative volume now, not campaign splits.** Condition: consolidated accounts. Source's rough split: 70% to 90% TOF, about 20% MOF, 5% to 10% BOF. Because campaigns are consolidated, apply it to the mix of assets you make: 70% to 90% for the less-aware stages, about 20% for middle to bottom. [a23b0e4bcf1c]
22. **Partnership ads are underused (in 2026) and use both audiences.** They run through the creator's handle; delivery uses the audience data of both handles to find cold buyers, and they feel less like ads. Source says the arbitrage is "probably coming towards its end". [646aacb0f2a9] [web check: current partnership ads performance and settings]
23. **Account complexity should match business complexity.** A $10,000/month single-product startup needs a very simple account; an $800M fashion retailer with 50 new SKUs a week needs more. The structure in section 3 is for small to midsize brands. [044de7406ebb]
24. **Do not touch what is hitting KPI.** Adding ads to a winning adset resets learning in part and breaks sequencing; turning off low-ROAS ads in a winning adset breaks it too. If the adset misses KPI, change anything. [d875376938d0] [c66780271b6f] [0519aa2b0cc3]
25. **ABO versus CBO is a risk choice.** Pareto compounds: 20% of ads hold 80%, and 20% of that 20% (4% of ads) hold about 64% of spend and revenue in every account the source has analysed. CBO leans into that: likely higher ROI, higher risk, and it also skews spend to 20% of products (sell-through risk). ABO: lower risk, slightly lower ROI. Source runs ABO as the steady state and switches to CBO when he needs a quick efficiency spike. [1493b95923ed] [a02bc677cd84]
26. **Shift large retailers from desire-led to pain-led creative.** Brands built on campaign shoots run about 90/10 desire to pain. Source says move toward 60/40, and the best brands are nearer 70/30, with pain-led (performance) as the larger share. [fb43b02e6f51]
27. **Retargeting creative is objection handling.** At a 5% site conversion rate, 95% did not buy; map why and make BOF ads for each objection. [0cd3c4f726de]
28. **Volume matters most, but only above a quality bar.** Hierarchy: volume, concept quality, hook strength, editing quality, UGC talent, storytelling. No one can pick the winner among 5 good ads (source says 10 top strategists would all guess wrong). Define what a 7 out of 10 ad is and maximise volume above it. Below the bar it is "junk volume". [fa5fa8da1288] [ca0232a9ea6e] [689d4746686a]
29. **Creative is a revenue driver, not a cost centre.** The platforms are distribution; the creative drives revenue, and organic posting gives it free distribution too. [54ba7f2f938e] [ca0232a9ea6e]
30. **Every ad has a total spend capacity you can shape but not escape.** Squeeze it (high budget, faster fatigue) or let it breathe (for example $100/day for 6 months rather than 6 weeks). Choose by inventory and cash, not by ROAS alone. [2856ec36e1a9]
31. **Fatigue has 2 causes:** the ad goes stale (reached capacity, or looks and speaks like the other ads so it fatigues faster), or the audience is too small for the spend. [e671f82f98b0]
32. **Above about $50,000/month, media buying is portfolio and inventory management.** Balance spend across ads and products, and align with the balance sheet, not only the P&L. [6255e96b9dce]
33. **Most production effort goes to replicating winners.** Replication has the highest impact in paid and organic; iteration second; net new third but never 0%. [9ebeaefa9a83] [250bd27148f4]
34. **Brief quality sets creator output quality.** [f94a2801c0ad]
35. **Use AI in the middle of the pipeline, not the ends.** Ideation and analysis stay human; briefing, scripting and production use AI heavily. LLMs "output the mean". [c17baa93a884] [bf65df9beea1]
36. **Organic first builds creative skill.** Source calls content-first brands "the approach for 26 and 27", not because organic beats paid but because it trains you to make content people watch. [d5563b66d646]
37. **Use native-accent creators for each market.** Same script, local creator. AU accent into the US does poorly; UK to AU translates "decently"; native always wins (his observation). [f94a2801c0ad]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build concepts (persona, angle, offer, format)
1. Spend about 20 minutes on avatars: who buys, what problems the product solves for them, and for each: pain points, language, and the proof they need. [53ee81550f45]
2. List 2 to 4 core personas, then drill into problem variants. Example: acne in women 20 to 25, 30 to 35 (likely hormonal), 40 to 45 are 3 personas. Fashion example: a mom aged 30 to 35 with young kids who needs an affordable dress that goes from work to a Friday night out. Aim for 30 to 40 good personas by hand (source also says 20 to 30). [53ee81550f45] [bf65df9beea1]
3. Give the full list plus wide business and customer context to an LLM (source names Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini). Ask for 20 to 30 more. Keep only 4 to 5 good ones, feed those back as "these were good", ask for 10 to 20 more. Goal: roughly double the list. Do not start from 3 personas and a website URL; you will get the average answer everyone else gets. [bf65df9beea1]
4. For each persona write the angle in 1 sentence (persona, product, why it solves their problem, proof), then pick angle components (section 2, principle 11) and write the script. [087eee279056] [f18d8afedb58]
5. Set the offer: product plus value framing. Value-proposition options named: bundles, gift with purchase, tiered discount (percent off when buying 2 or 3), subscription. Avoid deep discounting (source: do not eat 40% of margin). [4a3ce680ae23]
6. Only then pick formats (section 3.4).
7. Make variants by changing 1 variable at a time: same angle for a different persona, or a tweaked angle for a new persona. [939b4100ac4f]

Worked example (illustrative, no result claimed) [f18d8afedb58]:
- Persona: male casual runner training for a sub-4-hour marathon, gym goer, follows named fitness influencers, earns about 70K a year.
- Angle: science-backed (Andrew Huberman style); creatine improves recovery for the long run, enabling a 5-minute improvement in marathon time.
- Offer: creatine gummies, buy 2 get 1 free (chosen to raise perceived value at that income, as creatine is expensive).
- Formats: UGC ("great"), founder talking head, educational hi-fi, carousel; VSL left out because the persona is too young. About 10 ads.
- Variants: women training for a marathon (persona swap); beginners working up to a 5K (angle tweak).

### 3.2 Write and grade hooks
1. Decide the hook's job: tell the right viewer this is relevant. Work all 3 layers: visual, audio, copy overlay (and primary text). [6ce954d6297e]
2. Grade on 5 criteria; a couple done well is enough [f994209714f5]:
   - Clarity: a stranger understands it within 3 seconds.
   - Relevance: agitate the viewer's problem rather than calling out their demographic.
   - Novelty: ask "has this hook been done a million times?" If yes, change it.
   - Specificity: numbers, names, outcomes; a famous relevant name drop; "67% of people got this result".
   - Credibility: usually visual; a famous face, a doctor for supplements, a runner for running supplements.
3. Pick from the 9 hook types (about 7 fit most brands) [10686d689660]:
   1. Problem agitation: "Are you struggling with back pain from desk work?" Only problem-aware viewers; does not scale above that stage.
   2. Contrarian truth: "Everything you've been told about protein timing is wrong." Works across awareness levels, organic and paid.
   3. Specific proof: "I lost 12 kilos in 90 days without giving up pasta."
   4. Curiosity gap: "What the top 1% of brands know about Meta ads that you don't." Source says his average view time on this hook style is over 45 seconds (organic).
   5. Truth bomb: lead with an uncomfortable truth on price, ingredients or industry. "This product costs $120. Yes, it's expensive, but it's our best seller. Here's why." For premium or high-AOV products where price is the main objection; cuts through cold.
   6. Psychological confrontation: a friend calling you out. "You know that drawer full of products you never use? That's the problem." / "Stop buying things that make you feel guilty for not using them."
   7. Sensory or texture (ASMR): 2 to 3 seconds, no words, close-up of fabric handled, product squeezed, liquid poured, package opened. "Unbelievably well in fashion right now."
   8. Founder's letter: origin plus real stakes. "I remortgaged my house and put $300,000 down to order my first batch." Must be true.
   9. Social proof: "90% of customers saw results in 30 days." [needs brand data: real claim substantiation]
4. At the shoot, record each hook about 6 times (variants) so hook swaps are nearly free. [2f44cbccd498]

### 3.3 Script the bridge and product reveal (TOF video)
1. Map awareness on a line through the ad: unaware (hook, often a curiosity gap), problem aware, solution aware, product aware. [c218585e29e4]
2. Introduce the product only after the viewer is problem and solution aware. Blend it in; no hard jump. [3ae52427e9c8]
3. Ignore the urge to reveal before a retention drop-off point. Source examples: a low-testosterone ad reveals the product at about 1.5 minutes and "scales so well"; in an IM8 gut VSL he would add about 15 seconds of mechanism education (prebiotics, probiotics, postbiotics together) before naming the product. [3ae52427e9c8]
4. Check the body-to-CTA bridge the same way. [c218585e29e4]

### 3.4 Choose a format (5 questions) [bc432cb2ccf7]
1. What is the concept? Define persona, angle, offer first.
2. Does it need education? If yes, a longer form (VSL, long video, long-copy static).
3. Can the value proposition fit in 1 frame? If yes, lean to images or carousels. Produce statics whenever you can.
4. Does the product need a demo?
5. What awareness stage? Less aware viewers need longer educational formats (usually video); product aware and most aware can take DPAs and images.
Two uses of statics: test angles and concepts fast, then turn winners into video; or go heavy on images because production economics favour them (section 3.5). [aa8f85929d1f]

### 3.5 Production cost versus contribution per ad [aa8f85929d1f] [0c485f7f58f5]
1. Compute average revenue per ad (source says most accounts are about $2,000). [needs brand data: ads launched, spend, revenue per ad]
2. Convert to contribution margin per ad: subtract cost of goods and the ad spend. Example: $350 per ad.
3. Compare with production cost per ad. Example: $400, so each ad loses money.
4. Split by format. Example: images cost $50 and return $300; videos cost $500 and return $400, so double down on images even though videos "perform better".
5. At $100K+/month, keep a model of cost per asset by source (each creator, designer, agency) and profit contribution per ad, and track ROI per ad as spend-driven return against production cost. [needs brand data: production costs tagged to assets]

### 3.6 Testing cycle and account structure (small to midsize brands) [70512842dc08] [c66780271b6f] [044de7406ebb]
1. Hypothesis: name what you test, usually a concept and 1 of its 4 variables.
2. Build 10+ ads per concept. At a 5% hit rate you need about 20 ads to expect 1 winner. [needs brand data: account hit rate]
3. Launch into a testing campaign (ABO or CBO, see 3.8). 1 adset = 1 concept. Minimum 3 ads per adset; upper limit roughly 50 to 100 or more, set by budget.
4. Name adsets so the concept is filterable (for example "concept 1 / shoot 2"). [verify UI: naming only]
5. Analyse against the hypothesis: expected spend, expected conversions, expected ROI versus actual.
6. If it works: scale (3.7). Then iterate. Iteration gets faster as spend grows.
7. New ads for a winning concept go into a new adset of the same concept, never into the winning adset. Into a losing adset, add or cut anything. [0519aa2b0cc3]
8. Campaign set: (a) testing campaign; (b) optional scaling campaign fed by post IDs of winners (keeps engagement); drop it if it does not work; (c) retargeting campaign on existing customers, with existing customers excluded from testing and scaling so those stay purely cold. Optional adset on 90-day website visitors as mid-funnel, only if you need more frequency on engaged people. [verify UI] [044de7406ebb]

### 3.7 Scale a winning concept [c66780271b6f]
1. Increase the budget. Never turn the winner off to relaunch it elsewhere ("rookie mistake").
2. Optionally copy its post ID into a scaling campaign.
3. Make more ads for the concept and launch them as a new adset.
4. Keep portfolio limits in mind (3.8, 3.12).

### 3.8 Choose ABO or CBO [a02bc677cd84] [1493b95923ed]
1. Steady state: ABO (spreads spend, lower risk, protects product sell-through).
2. Short-term need for efficiency, testing and de-risking not a priority: switch to CBO.
3. Watch concentration under CBO: expect about 4% of ads to take about 64% of spend. [needs brand data: spend share by ad and by product]

### 3.9 Check DPA truth with incremental attribution [d2be5dc4e5fa] [fe7572d0a82a]
1. In Ads Manager: Columns > Compare attribution settings > Incremental attribution > Apply. [verify UI]
2. Exclude existing customers from the read.
3. Read incremental ROAS next to default ROAS. Source's explanation: Meta holds out about 10% of users; if 1% of the holdout and 2% of the exposed buy, the ads caused 1 point, so a reported 5x becomes about 2.5x.
4. If DPAs take budget from TOF because their reported ROAS looks good, move it back. Keep DPAs on cold modest (source: $1,000/day possible, $50,000/day not).

### 3.10 Build retargeting (BOF) creative by objection [0cd3c4f726de]
1. Estimate why non-buyers did not buy. Source's illustrative split of the 95% who did not buy at 5% CVR: 20% price, 20% trust, 20% quality doubt, 20% not ready. [needs brand data: objection mix; source does not say how to measure it]
2. Price: discounts, value anchoring, cost-per-use (a supplement at "half the price of a coffee"). Source says cost-per-use "crushes" at BOF.
3. Trust: social proof (customer count, 5-star review count, UGC testimonials).
4. Quality doubt: UGC of the product in use, before and afters, demos.
5. Not ready: educational or lifestyle content to stay present.
6. Mix all 4 in the BOF set.

### 3.11 Size monthly creative volume [43ade564407b]
Simple rule: 1 new ad per $1,000 of monthly spend (AUD; roughly fine for USD). $30K/month = 30 ads; $50K/month = 50 ads.
Back-propagation:
1. Mean spend per ad = total spend / ads launched (example: $500,000 / 500 = $1,000). [needs brand data: last 12 months of ads and spend]
2. Average return per ad: aMER (new customer revenue / ad spend) or ROAS on 7-day click (example: 3).
3. Expected revenue per ad = mean spend x return (example: $3,000).
4. Ads needed = monthly new customer revenue target / expected revenue per ad (example: $300,000 / $3,000 = 100 ads/month).
5. Flex the number with the revenue forecast through the year.
Limits the source names: ignores how long an ad takes to spend (revenue may land over 4 months, which raises the volume needed) and does not split winners from losers (forecast them separately). His full model is offered via a form; not in the source.

### 3.12 Handle a fatiguing ad [764735dc658b] [2856ec36e1a9]
1. Iterate: put new hooks on it.
2. Move it to a cost cap campaign for more spend (short-term; fatigues it faster).
3. Move its post ID into a scaling campaign (sometimes works; "won't save the account").
4. Rotate format: video to image, VSL to UGC, image to an unaware long-copy static.
5. Not recommended: duplicating adsets ("too tacky", used to work).
6. Accept that a fatigued ad cannot be saved for long; the fix is having other working ads (portfolio).

### 3.13 Validate creative with trial reels (as of April 2026) [514bd1dc3fea] [4346adb6eca1]
1. Cut the CTA off the ad.
2. Post it as a trial reel on the business or personal page the ad will run through. [verify UI]
3. Read reach (how viral it went, compared across posts) and the retention graph (is the hook, bridge or body weak?).
4. Use V1 before it is polished; feed back into the V2 edit within 24 hours.
5. For hooks: post about 20 hook variants, keep the best, and launch only about 5 as ads.

### 3.14 Brief and vet creators [f94a2801c0ad]
1. Ask for past work and the brief behind each piece.
2. Compare output to brief: a great result from a thin brief means broad strokes are fine; a detailed brief behind it means you must match that depth.
3. Default to the most detailed brief you can write.
4. Allow 2 takes: one held to the script, one more natural. Pick or run both.
5. Use creators native to the target market's accent.

### 3.15 Use AI in the pipeline [c17baa93a884]
1. Ideation: human (creative strategist). Source says LLM ideation reverts to the mean even with rich context or Reddit scraping.
2. Briefing and scripting: build a brand-specific Claude skill that asks about 20 questions (persona, concept, offer, long form or not, awareness stage) and outputs a script or brief "99% of the way there".
3. Production: heavy AI; VSLs and statics can be about 90% AI. His agency makes AI VSLs end to end; some hold hundreds of thousands in spend.
4. Analysis: use with care. AI misses the metric hierarchy, account structure and commercial context and "a lot of the time" misleads.
Rule of thumb: "skip the outsides, maximise throughput on the middle".

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Cold frequency check.** Ads Manager, ad level, last 30 and 60 days, cold campaigns, frequency column. Good: every cold ad under 2. Bad: any above 2 means creative fatigue and too little diversity and volume. Fix: more distinct concepts and more ads. [70e1681410fa] [verify UI]
- **Similarity check.** Ask your Meta rep for the Creative Similarity Score. Higher is worse. Signs without the score: frequency rising, reach falling, ROAS falling together. Fix: change persona, angle or offer, not just the shot or the format. [1e5ab3cbe70b]
- **Before cutting an ad.** Is the adset hitting KPI? If yes, do not turn off low-ROAS ads or add new ads. If no, change anything. [d875376938d0]
- **Ranking ads.** Use amount spent first (an ad at 1.7x with double the spend of an ad at 2.2x is the stronger one, likely a TOF opener causing sales elsewhere), then ROAS or CPA on 7-day click or incremental, then CPC and CTR as leading signals, then hook and hold rate as diagnostics. [8d71a067a46c] [57ca8c633830] [2af6392d4a31]
- **Hook rate low vs account average:** swap the hook and see if the body works. **Hold rate low:** viewers drop at the bridge; redo that section. Hook and hold rates do not correlate with conversions. [8d71a067a46c]
- **Adset that looks better than it is.** Cross-adset serving can hand one adset BOF sales created by another. Check that each adset holds 1 concept. [9d506de16067]
- **Mixed-concept adset.** If an adset holds several concepts you cannot learn which one worked. Split by concept. [044de7406ebb] [68bf58e8538f]
- **DPA check.** Compare DPA ROAS with existing customers excluded and incremental attribution on. If much lower and TOF budget has been moving to DPAs, reverse it. [d2be5dc4e5fa]
- **Bottleneck diagnosis before adding volume.** Candidates the source lists: creative quality, format diversity, too few TOF assets for the awareness stages you need, no partnership ads, other unused strategies. The 2 accounts launching 2,000+ ads/month on about $4M/month were unprofitable because ads were below the quality bar. [ca0232a9ea6e]
- **Hero-ad dependence.** Share of spend in the top 1 to 3 ads, by month. Bad: a few ads holding most spend with no new winners behind them. Fix: production now, 6 to 8 months before the decline. [5db051a99754] [needs brand data: spend share by ad]
- **Production underinvestment.** Compare production spend with media spend against the tier (section 4). Compare live ad count with spend (the case: 15 live ads on $350K/month). [54ba7f2f938e]
- **Ad economics.** Contribution margin per ad vs production cost per ad, by format and source. [aa8f85929d1f]
- **Fatigue cause.** Is spend pushed through a small persona (frequency up) or has the ad hit its lifetime capacity? Small persona: hold spend at its capacity and rotate creative, because people enter and leave the audience (marathon trainees churn). Capacity reached: iterate or replace. [e671f82f98b0]
- **Inventory check before squeezing a winner.** Lots of stock to clear this month: squeeze. Low stock, awaiting stock, or cash conversion cycle cannot keep up: run at efficiency and let it breathe. [2856ec36e1a9] [needs brand data: inventory, cash conversion cycle]
- **Balance sheet check.** Example: P&L shows $2M profit, balance sheet holds $3M unsold stock, cash is effectively -$1M. Fix: restructure the account and make creative for the slow stock, accepting lower efficiency. [6255e96b9dce] [needs brand data: stock by SKU]
- **Desire/pain audit.** Tag live creative as desire-led or pain-led. Bad: about 90/10 desire. Target 60/40 to 70/30 pain-led. [fb43b02e6f51]
- **AI script tells.** Remove "contrast negation" ("it's not X, it's Y" and "not a single soldier, a complete military operation"); the source flags these as AI script patterns in a VSL breakdown. [156808a7c843]

## 7. Examples and case results

All are examples, not promises.
- **Account turnaround, no new creative** [54ba7f2f938e]: brand at $350K/month on Meta, CAC worsening for 2 years, 15 live ads, about 25 campaigns with bid caps and cost caps. Agency relaunched and reworked old winners to about 60 ads. Result: spend up about $40K, CAC down about 20% to 30%, new customer revenue up about 28% (from memory). Founder refused production spend; the 60 ads fatigued and the account slid back. Agency's unaccepted proposal: move $10K from media to production for about 40 assets (some hook swaps, some statics), nearly doubling live ads, for a likely 5%+ efficiency gain: 5% of $350K = $17,500 saved, or at the 3x return then, $40K to $50K more revenue a month. The agency proposed and reported this, so it is not independent.
- **Hook refresh** [2f44cbccd498]: ads that had spent $50K to $100K and fatigued got 50 new hooks (about 1 hour of shooting) and spent another $100K at the same efficiency.
- **Hero-ad collapse** [5db051a99754]: 8 to 10 clients, 40% down year on year in new customer acquisition, built on 3 creatives holding $1M.
- **Junk volume** [ca0232a9ea6e]: 2 accounts launching 2,000+ ads/month, about $4M/month spend, unprofitable.
- **AI images at scale** [aa8f85929d1f]: source knows people doing $200,000/day in revenue on AI images alone; others doing hundreds of thousands a day on statics only; he has seen statics reach $500,000 lifetime spend.
- **IM8 tweet-style static** [bc432cb2ccf7]: 4,700 likes; a repost of a tweet, run from a doctor's profile as whitelisting without the partnership label so it feels organic; lists many symptoms of vitamin deficiency (dry eyes, poor vision, low energy, bad skin, mouth cracks, fatigue, bleeding gums, painful muscles, bruising) for a very wide market; copy then names the product with 10% off.
- **Advertorial static funnel** [95a5ffb79c86]: "The best hair growth products of 2025 and the ones to avoid", run from a dermatologist page; Learn more goes to a brand-owned "Dermatologist Reviews" site that ranks products, only the brand's entry is clickable to a custom landing page, then a quiz funnel, then checkout; rivals are listed with ticks and crosses and Amazon affiliate links. Source: advertorials only work with an education-based landing page or heavy education in primary text.
- **Long-copy statics for 50+** [4101de6af476]: static image plus long story copy on Facebook feeds walks unaware readers to conversion; needs real copywriting skill.
- **VSLs** [156808a7c843]: gut-health VSL (IBS, leaky gut, bloating call-out; "40% of people have it" to raise self-identification; 12-week clinical trial with 85% proof); a weight-loss VSL with objection-handling UGC and split screen at the front, 25 million views, running 3 to 4 years, first 28 seconds all contrarian takes; a newer ad with 2,000 likes in 5 days; a low-testosterone ad on an influencer's profile, faceless educational format, "global testosterone levels have dropped over 30% in the last 50 years", product at about 1.5 minutes, fast cuts and changing formats.
- **Audience size** [e671f82f98b0]: "ecommerce CFOs in Australia at $100M+" is about 1,000 to 2,000 people; $10,000/day there would hit each "600 times a day". "Ecommerce founders earning over 100K" is much larger. The marathon persona cannot hold $10,000/day in Australia.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Building the business on 1 to 3 hero ads and stopping production. [5db051a99754]
2. Confusing activity with strategy: thousands of ads with no quality bar. [ca0232a9ea6e]
3. Misdiagnosing the bottleneck (adding volume when quality or funnel mix is the problem). [ca0232a9ea6e]
4. Treating creative as a cost centre. [54ba7f2f938e]
5. Still running interest targeting. [7b353cb56615]
6. Loading near-identical variants and calling it diversity. [1e5ab3cbe70b]
7. Calling out "everyone", or mixing personas (men and women, old and young) in one adset. [b4b918f8b794] [68bf58e8538f]
8. Segmenting adsets by arbitrary traits such as launch date (from card-builder header). [68bf58e8538f]
9. Turning off low-ROAS ads in an adset that is hitting KPI. [d875376938d0]
10. Judging creative on ad-level last-click ROAS. [57ca8c633830]
11. Testing in the wrong order: format first, angle last. Believing "UGC works" as a format. [4a3ce680ae23]
12. Broad personas ("busy professional"). [53ee81550f45]
13. Asking an LLM for personas from 3 examples and a URL. [bf65df9beea1]
14. Leading with product features instead of an angle. [087eee279056]
15. Bait hooks (memes) that win hook rate and lose sales. [6ce954d6297e]
16. Persona call-out hooks without problem agitation. [d8253f20d2a9]
17. Abrupt bridge from hook to product; revealing the product early because of the retention curve. [c218585e29e4] [3ae52427e9c8]
18. Believing statics are only BOF. [bc432cb2ccf7]
19. Over-funding DPAs because their ROAS looks high, starving TOF. [fe7572d0a82a]
20. Launching only 1 to 3 ads per concept. [70512842dc08]
21. Injecting new ads into a winning adset. [0519aa2b0cc3]
22. Turning a winner off to relaunch it in a scaling campaign. [c66780271b6f]
23. Letting CBO concentrate all spend without portfolio control. [a02bc677cd84]
24. Desire-heavy (campaign shoot) creative mix. [fb43b02e6f51]
25. Agencies squeezing winners for a 30 to 90 day case study and ROAS report, causing stockouts and short account life. [2856ec36e1a9]
26. Optimizing the P&L while stock piles up on the balance sheet; media buyers not knowing inventory. [6255e96b9dce]
27. Doing 0% net new concepts, or overweighting net new (the source admits he does 80% to 90% net new in his own organic). [9ebeaefa9a83]
28. Deep discounting as the only offer lever (eating 40% of margin). [4a3ce680ae23]
29. Duplicating adsets as a fatigue fix. [764735dc658b]
30. Automating ideation and analysis with AI. [c17baa93a884]
31. Using non-native accents for a market. [f94a2801c0ad]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **One presenter, one agency.** All thresholds are his working rules. He sells audits, so case stories favour his view. [54ba7f2f938e]
- **Compliance conflict.** He calls before and afters the best-performing angle component and says to "sneak" them past Meta's review. That is a policy risk; do not follow it. Check Meta's advertising standards for your category, especially health and weight loss claims. [087eee279056] [web check: Meta ad policy on before and after images and health claims]
- **Whitelisting without disclosure.** The IM8 example is praised because "you don't know that this is an ad". Paid partnerships may need disclosure rules. [web check: branded content and disclosure rules in the target market]
- **Advertorial funnel.** A brand-owned "Dermatologist Reviews" site that ranks its own product first may breach consumer protection or platform rules on misleading reviews. The source praises it and only guesses it is "a little bit compliant". [web check: advertorial and review-site rules]
- **Andromeda claims are unverified.** Transcript reading, bundling, the similarity score and 10% holdout mechanics are his descriptions. [web check: Meta documentation]
- **Meta article on 2 impressions** is paraphrased from memory ("4 or 5 years ago"); he says he will link it but the link is not in the text. [web check]
- **Internal inconsistencies.** Broad unaware ads are for "mid 8 figure" brands in one place and "$50 million plus" in another. The funnel split (70 to 90 / 20 / 5 to 10) does not sum to 100. He promises "a better way to calculate bottom of funnel budgeting later in the video" and never gives it. He opens the static breakdown naming "a creatine gummies ad" and "a GLP-1 hair loss ad", but the ads shown are an IM8 tweet, a hair-growth advertorial and a 50+ long-copy static. He says 10% of media at 25% MER equals 2.5% of revenue "going towards media budget"; he means production budget. "Very high stage of awareness" in the format questions means far from purchase (less aware).
- **ABO dropped in transcript.** "Whether this is an [ABO] or a CBO": the word ABO is lost in the captions; meaning is clear from context.
- **Hit rate, mean spend and revenue per ad** do not line up exactly across sections ($700 to $1,500 mean spend; $2,000 revenue per ad; $1,000 per ad rule). Use your own data. [needs brand data: hit rate, mean spend per ad, revenue per ad]
- **Offer vs angle priority.** He says the offer may be the most important variable (and cites "offer is king" from Hormozi) yet ranks it second to angle because it is harder to change. This conflicts with common advice to fix the offer first; his ranking is about what to test next, not what matters most.
- **Volume rule vs "activity is not strategy".** Both are stated; the reconciling rule is the quality bar.
- **Persona call-outs.** He says call-outs are overrated in hooks, yet praises a VSL that opens with one ("You have IBS, leaky gut, or bloating?"), which is really a problem call-out.
- **What is thin on how:** how to measure the objection mix; how to judge the 7 out of 10 bar; exact trial reel steps; how to build the financial model with time-to-spend and winner/loser split; how to "blend" a bridge (he calls it intuitive).
- **Card conflicts with the text.** [ca0232a9ea6e] says the source gives no numeric ads-per-month target; the text gives 1 new ad per $1,000 monthly spend. [4346adb6eca1] adds steps (routing winners out of a trial/partnership "layer", keeping a validation layer running) that the source does not state. [764735dc658b] lists duplicating adsets as a response; the source says he does not recommend it. [8d71a067a46c] writes "roROAS", a caption artifact for ROAS. [1e5ab3cbe70b] says the score cannot be read in Ads Manager; the source says it can be obtained from a Meta rep.
- **Market.** Figures are AUD and the accent advice is from the Australian market. [needs brand data: market, currency]
- **Freshness.** Trial reels "as of April 2026"; partnership ads arbitrage "coming towards its end". Re-check both before use.
