## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist review a Meta video ad before or while it spends, and decide what to fix. It gives a structure to check (hook, bridge, education or problem agitation, objection handling, feature-to-benefit links, proof, cut pace, CTA) and 6 worked teardowns across ecom, service, influencer, subscription, low and high order value products. Its main use is fixing scripts: rebuild the first half, delay the pitch, lead with benefits, show proof. It does not cover metrics, budgets or scaling (those live in 11-ADC and 24-DEP).

## 2. Principles

1. **A hook must grab the right people for the right reason, not just attention.** Condition: every cold paid video hook. Confusion earns attention from the wrong viewers and loses the right ones. Evidence: the roofing ad opens with "They can crack very easily. Okay, guys. Imagine this is your old tile roof", and the viewer only learns it is about roofs later; the presenter says it creates "more confusion than clarity". The library header and cards add a second example (a hammer hitting a table, relying on audio confusion) that is not in the transcript text. [b3dd2d5c796e] [f4aee117fef5] [66dd24ac26be]
2. **Good hooks for this presenter are high-stakes warnings or curiosity questions that open a gap and then answer it.** Condition: cold audiences. Examples: "Your sauna sessions are burning off your hair because you haven't found out about this yet" (high stakes, then a gap: why, and how do I fix it); "How did I just add 23 g of protein to my favorite cereals?" (curiosity, then the answer). A contrarian negative hook ("our food system is quite toxic") also counts, but only with proof later. [b3dd2d5c796e] [ef1959f490cb]
3. **If you cannot name the hook type, there is no hook.** Condition: auditing any ad. The Vitable ad opens "These are my vital packs"; the presenter, who says he is "pretty good at categorizing hooks", could not classify it. Exception the source gives: a product-first opener "would have been okay" if it ran as a partnership ad through the influencer's own page. It did not. [2a76fe7e97e3] [b3dd2d5c796e]
4. **The bridge is where most ads break: do not name and pitch the product straight after the hook.** Condition: the section right after the hook (the card puts it at about 5 to 15 seconds). Weak pattern: "The solution is our product, the Halo hat, and here's all the benefits." Strong pattern (sauna ad): "The solution to this is simple yet highly effective", a brief glimpse of the hat, then education on why the problem happens. Note: the sauna ad does show the product briefly at the bridge; the rule is against the hard pitch, not against any product on screen. [09fddf695faa] [66dd24ac26be]
5. **Educate and problem-agitate before selling.** Condition: all 3 "good" ads. The sauna ad explains that sauna sessions dry the scalp, split ends and weaken hair until it breaks. The chair ad opens with poor posture from working at home. The presenter's summary: good ads "index towards education and the consumer experience rather than selling"; bad ads "sell very hard" and "lack education or problem agitation". [09fddf695faa] [2a76fe7e97e3]
6. **Handle objections inside the script, before the CTA.** Condition: whenever buyers hold a known doubt. Sauna ad: "It's not a hair mask. It's not a magic pill." Protein ad: reason 3 is taste, since people think plant protein is worse than whey ("protein isn't meant to be nasty"). Chair ad: "I've been avoiding ergonomic chairs because I don't want to look like a gamer boy." Vitable: "so much easier than dealing with powders or random bottles." Roofing ad: "there's no visible cracks anywhere", then explains roofs crack from expanding and contracting. [09fddf695faa] [cac9faff0225] [dc0bcc2c4ae1] [ef1959f490cb]
7. **Sell the category when the category has a strong story.** Condition: category-defining or unfamiliar product. The sauna ad sells the whole sauna-hat category ("invented thousands of years ago in central Europe") instead of the brand's differences from rivals. Card caveat: this does little for a commodity where rivals look the same. [09fddf695faa] [e17eff7e3b5c]
8. **Borrow authority when the brand has no famous face.** Condition: cold audience, no known founder. Sources of borrowed authority in the examples: licensed big brands (Kellogg's plus a named creator in the protein collab), design origin ("intentionally designed and developed this way in Japan" in the chair ad), and category history (sauna hat). The presenter notes the Japan-as-premium link would not have worked about 50 years ago; it is tied to today's view of Japanese manufacturing. [e17eff7e3b5c]
9. **Every feature must be tied to a benefit, and the benefit should come first.** Condition: any script that names features. The question the viewer asks is "why do I care that it's velvet smooth? Why do I care that the handles adjust?" Tie each to a user-level outcome. Then flip the order: "I can even lie down in the middle of the day and have a 30-minute nap because the armrests go down and the material is velvet smooth." Reason: the retention curve only goes down, so more people hear what comes first. The presenter says you "will likely get better performance"; he gives no test. [cac9faff0225]
10. **Move fast through table-stakes features.** Condition: features every buyer in the category already expects. The protein ad says dairy-free, gluten-free, plant-based quickly; the presenter says most people would spend the whole ad on it, and that is wrong. Card caveat: do not drop compliance-required statements. [cac9faff0225] [ef1959f490cb]
11. **Name benefit pillars broad enough to keep the market wide.** Condition: products with many possible use cases. Vitable's "energy, focus, recovery" is praised; "helps me at the gym, helps me when I'm running, helps me with energy" would narrow to 1 avatar and "not scale very well". [ef1959f490cb]
12. **The first half decides the ad.** Condition: every video ad. The presenter's estimate: "probably only 15% of people even get to the second half". A good first half can carry an average second half; the reverse does not work. So fix the opening first; the second half is often reusable. [66dd24ac26be] [2a76fe7e97e3] [09fddf695faa] [needs brand data: actual retention curve per ad]
13. **Cut at least every 3 seconds.** Condition: the source applies it to all ads, stressing Instagram. The sauna ad cuts "every 3 seconds at a maximum". The roofing ad's second half is a talking head with no cuts and no social proof on screen, and reads "very salesy" and "not very engaging". [66dd24ac26be] [2a76fe7e97e3]
14. **Match the CTA to the tone and the work already done.** Condition: soft CTA after heavy education and objection handling, or in creator and partnership ads. Sauna: "Your halo is waiting for you" (the buyer "should already be sold"). Chair: "you should definitely invest in a comfortable and luxurious working experience", which fits a product used about 10 hours a day. A sudden hard pitch in a soft creator ad "would be very odd". Card caveat: on a weak script a soft CTA just adds friction. [129eda6e1e3a]
15. **Point the CTA at the offer you want to push, and use scarcity only if real.** Condition: bundles and limited offers. Protein ad: "Two legends, one mega box ... grab this pack before stock runs out", tied to the end of the licensing deal. The presenter still calls it "a good finish" although the ad had run for 20 weeks; the card adds that scarcity must be true. [129eda6e1e3a]
16. **An ad should reach past organic reach, not repeat it.** Condition: founder or influencer-led creative for cold audiences. The Blueprint ad relies on trust in Bryan Johnson. It will sell to people who already trust him, but "if you change the avatar to someone else, this ad would 100% not work at all", and a damaging article would stop it working. If it only converts existing fans, "just post this organically". [d3ac75d0d660]
17. **Claims need visible proof.** Condition: any claim of testing, superiority or competitor weakness. Blueprint says it spent "more than $50,000 last month" testing, and a rival protein came back with "seven times" the toxins stated, but shows no test results. The fix the presenter names: overlay the actual test results. "Cleanest best tasting protein on market" and "legit the best protein in the world" are called completely subjective. [d3ac75d0d660]
18. **The destination must keep the ad's promise.** Condition: every live ad. Blueprint says third-party lab results are on the website; the presenter clicked and got a 404. A broken or mismatched page wastes the creative. [8cce5e9474f5]
19. **Opening with an obvious sales line causes drop-off.** Condition: cold audiences. "I'm proud to announce..." in the Blueprint ad "would have caused a massive drop off from the beginning as people knew they were being sold to." [2a76fe7e97e3]
20. **Format should fit the profile the ad runs from.** Condition: partnership ads. An unboxing video "makes a lot of contextual sense on an influencer profile"; a soft CTA suits a partnership ad. A POV visual hook text ("POV, you finally find a chair that meets all your sitting needs") was praised; the other 2 good ads lacked a visual hook. [129eda6e1e3a] [dc0bcc2c4ae1]

## 3. Procedures

The source shows a method by example. The cards turn it into steps. Where a step comes from a card and not the transcript, it says so.

### 3.1 Frame-by-frame teardown of a video ad
1. Watch the ad through once, then step through it frame by frame. The card suggests 3-second chunks. [66dd24ac26be]
2. Hook (first 3 to 5 seconds): name the hook type. Ask what the viewer knows after the hook: the problem? the product category? If they do not know what the ad is about, relevance is missing. Check if there is a visual hook (on-screen text such as a POV line) as well as a spoken one. [b3dd2d5c796e] [f4aee117fef5]
3. Bridge: find where the hook hands off to the body. Pass if it states a solution exists and moves into education or agitation. Fail if it names the product and lists benefits straight away. [09fddf695faa]
4. Body: is there education or problem agitation? When does the value proposition first appear? (Vitable: 7 to 10 seconds in, too late.) [2a76fe7e97e3]
5. Features: list each feature named. Is each tied to a benefit? Is the benefit said first? Are table-stakes features passed quickly? [cac9faff0225]
6. Objections: list the doubts the buyer likely holds. Does the script answer at least 1 before the CTA? [cac9faff0225] [dc0bcc2c4ae1]
7. Proof and authority: is every claim backed by something on screen (tests, licensed brands, origin, category history)? Or is it all subjective? [d3ac75d0d660] [e17eff7e3b5c]
8. Pace: count cuts. Flag any stretch over 3 seconds with no visual change, and any talking-head back half with no overlays or social proof. [66dd24ac26be]
9. CTA: soft or hard? Does it match the tone and the format? Does it point at the specific offer or bundle? Is any scarcity true? [129eda6e1e3a]
10. Creator swap test (creator or founder ads): would it still work with a different face? [d3ac75d0d660]
11. Click the ad and check the destination loads and holds what the ad promised. [8cce5e9474f5]
12. Judge. The presenter's only scale: the protein ad is "above a 7 out of 10. It should go straight in the account." He gives no scoring rubric; the 7 structural elements in the card are a card-level synthesis. Card caveat: use the review to rank fixes, not to predict ROAS. [66dd24ac26be]

### 3.2 Rewrite a weak ad (keep the back half)
1. Confirm the second half has usable benefits, objection handling and a CTA. (Vitable: second half "not bad ... maybe even good".) [2a76fe7e97e3]
2. Write a new hook that names the problem or the stakes, so the right buyer self-selects. [b3dd2d5c796e]
3. Write a bridge that says a solution exists, then teach why the problem happens. [09fddf695faa]
4. Pull the strongest value line forward. Vitable's "you just take a quick quiz and Vitable sorts it all out for you" should be "pulled forward", per the presenter. [2a76fe7e97e3]
5. Reattach the existing second half. Card: the back half is often reusable. [09fddf695faa]

### 3.3 Quick hook test with existing footage
1. Take a strong line from the middle of an existing ad and move it to the start. The presenter says this is "completely fine" as a cheap test. [f4aee117fef5]
2. Check it still makes clear what the ad is about. The roofing ad did this and, per the presenter, it failed because the moved line caused confusion. [f4aee117fef5]
3. Card step: compare the 3-second hold and the retention curve after the hook to see if the body delivers what the hook promised [verify UI: Meta video metric names] [needs brand data: 3-second views, hold and retention per ad]. [f4aee117fef5]

### 3.4 Feature-to-benefit rewrite
1. List the features the script names (chair example: fold-out leg rest, adjustable pillows, armrests that "can go anywhere", velvet suede). [cac9faff0225]
2. For each, write the outcome at the user level ("I can relax for a couple hours, then get back to work"). [cac9faff0225]
3. Flip each line so the benefit comes first and the feature follows as the reason. [cac9faff0225]
4. Cut table-stakes features to 1 quick line. [cac9faff0225]

### 3.5 Self-image objection reposition
1. Say the identity barrier out loud in the creator's voice ("I don't want to look like a gamer boy"). [dc0bcc2c4ae1]
2. Reposition against it ("giving luxury without losing its functionality"). [dc0bcc2c4ae1]
3. Back the new frame with a reason (design origin in Japan). [dc0bcc2c4ae1] [e17eff7e3b5c]
4. Close with a CTA that fits the new frame ("invest in a comfortable working experience"). [dc0bcc2c4ae1]

### 3.6 Numbered-reasons body
1. Curiosity hook that promises an answer; give the answer within seconds. [ef1959f490cb]
2. "Here are 3 reasons why you need it." Reason 1 a benefit (the protein ad says "look how creamy this is", a benefit, not the feature causing it). Reason 2 the table-stakes value props, fast. Reason 3 the main objection. [ef1959f490cb] [cac9faff0225]
3. CTA to the bundle, with scarcity only if true. [129eda6e1e3a]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Hooks that confuse or grab the wrong viewers (roofing ad). [f4aee117fef5]
2. Product-first hooks with no reason to care (Vitable). [b3dd2d5c796e]
3. Naming and pitching the product at the bridge ("the solution is our product"). [09fddf695faa]
4. Listing features without saying why they matter; putting features before benefits. [cac9faff0225]
5. Spending the whole ad on table-stakes features. [cac9faff0225]
6. Hyper-specific pillars that shrink the audience to 1 avatar. [ef1959f490cb]
7. Claims with no visible proof; subjective superlatives. [d3ac75d0d660]
8. Ads that only work because of the founder's face. [d3ac75d0d660]
9. Announcing a sale up front ("I'm proud to announce"). [2a76fe7e97e3]
10. Long uncut talking-head sections with no overlays or social proof. [66dd24ac26be]
11. A hard pitch at the end of a soft creator ad. [129eda6e1e3a]
12. Sending traffic to a broken page, or a page missing what the ad promised. [8cce5e9474f5]
13. Trying to save a weak opening with a strong second half. [66dd24ac26be]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **All judgements are opinion.** No ad's spend, CTR, retention or ROAS is shown. "The data shows" (roofing) is assertion. The 15% second-half figure is an estimate. Benefit-first is argued from the retention curve, not tested. Treat these as hypotheses to test [needs brand data: per-ad retention, hold rate, CTR and purchase data].
- **Conflict with common advice on early product and brand.** Meta's own creative guidance and many practitioners say show the product or brand in the first seconds [web check: current Meta creative best practices on early branding]. This source says a hard pitch that early causes drop-off. It is not absolute: the sauna ad glimpses the hat at the bridge, and the presenter allows a product-first open in partnership ads. Card 2a76fe7e97e3 adds that urgency-driven commodity offers may tolerate product-first hooks. Resolve with own tests.
- **Conflict with common advice on founder-led ads.** Many sources treat founder-led creative as a strong cold asset. This source says founder-reliant ads cap at the founder's organic reach. Card d3ac75d0d660 flags this as strong opinion with no measurement.
- **Conflict with common advice on CTAs.** Direct response advice often favours a clear, direct CTA. This source prefers soft CTAs after heavy education and in creator ads. Card 129eda6e1e3a adds that a soft CTA on a weak script is just friction.
- **Cut pace vs native lo-fi.** The "cut every 3 seconds" rule is stated as general. Lo-fi native UGC often uses fewer cuts; the source does not address this. Test per placement.
- **Card content not in the transcript.** The 5 hook criteria (clarity, relevance, novelty, specificity, credibility) and the "hammer hitting a table" example come from the library header and cards, not from the spoken text. The 7-element scoring list and 3-second viewing chunks are card synthesis.
- **Category selling limit.** Selling the category (sauna hat) helps a category-defining product, not a commodity [09fddf695faa]. Origin-as-premium (Japan) depends on culture and era [e17eff7e3b5c]. Repositioning can alienate existing buyers [dc0bcc2c4ae1].
- **Scarcity.** The protein ad's "before stock runs out" ran for 20 weeks. The presenter calls it a good finish; the card warns scarcity must be true. Keep scarcity truthful.
- **Missing how.** No hook-writing method beyond examples. No script templates, no testing design, no metric thresholds, no guidance on static ads, lengths or aspect ratios. The presenter points to a separate hook video on his channel, not in this library.
- **Brand fit for ecomOS.** The source covers video only. For a brand with product-accuracy needs (for example jewellery), the "brief glimpse of the product" still needs the real product shot; this source does not discuss it.
