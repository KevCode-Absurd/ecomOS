## 1. What this source is for

It tells a creative strategist which ads to brief for Meta prospecting: only extreme top-of-funnel problem/solution ads and extreme bottom-of-funnel objection ads, nothing built for the middle. It gives a way to generate top-of-funnel angles (avatar x problem), a way to find bottom-of-funnel objections (write them down, call checkout abandoners), a quality metric (creative hit rate), and a full Ads Manager setup for 1 CBO prospecting campaign where each ad set is a "pack" named by avatar and angle. It argues against flooding an account with thousands of low-quality ads.

## 2. Principles

1. **Winning creative matters; creative volume by itself does not.** Condition: always, when deciding how many ads to make. The total number of ads "literally means nothing"; only the number of high-performing ads matters. Make more ads only if you believe each one is high quality and "could be a home run". The path to winners is usually high-quality volume, so volume is not written off. Evidence: presenter's opinion from agency experience. [681541ae0981] [a932cc43b8da]
2. **Low-quality volume mis-trains the pixel (source's model, not a Meta spec).** Condition: when an account is flooded with many sloppy ads (the source names AI creative, UGC and influencer content bought as "test budget"). His model: the pixel fires, sends events to Meta, and delivery learns from the purchase event to find similar buyers at a lower CPA. With too many ads, Meta "doesn't know where to shift or guide that budget", unqualified visitors bounce, and these "negative events" still train the pixel, even if a few of them buy at a very low rate. Result: the pixel "doesn't know what direction to properly go in". "Whatever you feed the pixel, this is what comes out of the pixel." No threshold for how many negative events cause harm. [a932cc43b8da] [web check: Meta documentation on how delivery learns from non-converting traffic]
3. **Consistency of purchase signal is the thing to protect.** Condition: prospecting on a purchase event. The 19,000 server-side events example is shown for the consistency of daily purchases, not the volume. [a932cc43b8da]
4. **The classic top/middle/bottom funnel is "great in theory" but "not how Facebook ads work".** Condition: Meta prospecting, per the source. Building separate ads for top, middle and bottom spreads production thin. [7d806738414a]
5. **Build only for the extremes: "tippy top" and "very bottom".** Condition: creative planning for prospecting. Build nothing for the middle, nothing for the top-middle bridge, nothing for the middle-bottom bridge. Reason: producers make mistakes, so some ads meant for the top land nearer the middle and some meant for the bottom land nearer the middle-bottom. This "natural osmosis" covers the middle, lowers production volume and lets the team focus on quality. The card adds: build middle creative only if you have a specific reason; the source does not state that exception. [7d806738414a]
6. **Funnel stages are defined by awareness.** Top: not aware of the product, the problem or the solution; truly net new. Middle: problem aware or solution aware, but not product aware. Bottom: aware of problem, solution and your product. The job is to convert all stages, but the ads must change by stage. [7d806738414a]
7. **Top of funnel is where the business gains most value; use problem/solution.** Condition: new-customer ads. Address the avatar's core problem, then position the product as the unique solution. Problem/solution works because it answers the main objections that top-of-funnel people have. [7d806738414a]
8. **Every product solves a problem, even when it looks like it does not.** Condition: brands that think they do not solve a problem (fashion, lifestyle). A problem can be functional ("my product solves back pain") or emotional or identity ("a clothing brand makes people feel good about themselves, feel cool, feel part of something"). [7d806738414a]
9. **Avatar x angle = concept.** The avatar is the person you sell to (your ICP, ideal client profile). Every problem the avatar has is an ad angle. Mixing 1 avatar with 1 angle creates the concept, "package", which is the ad. Each ad calls out the avatar, calls out the problem, and presents the product as the solution. [72f6438afa3a]
10. **Whoever the ad talks about is who sees it (source's claim about Andromeda).** Condition: current Meta delivery, per the source. An ad that says "if you're [X person]" is more likely shown to X people. So explicit avatar callouts do the targeting. Unverified; may not persist. [72f6438afa3a] [web check: Meta statements on creative-led targeting under Andromeda]
11. **One product can sell to several avatars for different purposes.** Condition: products with more than 1 use. Grüns example in section 7. [72f6438afa3a]
12. **Format does not matter for this system.** Image ads and video ads both follow the same problem/solution rule. [7d806738414a]
13. **Bottom of funnel only handles objections.** Condition: ads for people who already know the product. 2 layers: turn on catalog ads to retarget visitors of similar products, and make all bottom creative objection-based. [7d806738414a] [8e3f775ece26]
14. **1 objection, 1 proof point per bottom ad.** Condition: every bottom ad. Do not give away every piece of information; call out the one thing the product solves. These ads do nothing at the top but "crush" at the bottom because the viewer has already seen the earlier ads. [8e3f775ece26] [7d806738414a]
15. **Separate funnel positions into separate ad sets ("packs") in 1 CBO campaign.** Condition: prospecting setup. Ad set count does not matter to him (2, 4, or 30 to 100). Many ad sets give control through value rules, minimums and maximums instead of pausing ads. [a8e061b0c56f]
16. **Top packs and bottom packs should behave differently, and that is desired.** Top-of-funnel ads spend more, scale further, and drive lower ROAS. Bottom-of-funnel ads convert better but scale less. "This is exactly what you want." [a8e061b0c56f] [needs brand data: spend, ROAS and conversion rate by pack]
17. **Do not use budget to push spend.** Condition: setting the campaign budget. Start where you are already spending and comfortable. [a8e061b0c56f]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build top-of-funnel concepts with the avatar framework
1. Pick 1 avatar (ICP): the specific person you sell to. [72f6438afa3a] [needs brand data: customer profiles, survey answers, reviews]
2. List every problem that avatar has. Include problems that look unrelated to the product; the source says "it literally doesn't matter". [72f6438afa3a]
3. Treat each problem as an ad angle. [72f6438afa3a]
4. Combine 1 avatar + 1 angle into 1 concept. [72f6438afa3a]
5. Write the ad: call out the avatar, call out the problem, position the product as the unique solution. Use image or video. [72f6438afa3a] [7d806738414a]
6. Repeat for other avatars who could buy the same product for a different purpose. [72f6438afa3a]
7. The source does not say how to pick which angles to produce from the list. The card adds a selection step ("select the strongest angles"); that is the card's addition, not the transcript's. [72f6438afa3a]

### 3.2 Build bottom-of-funnel objection ads
1. Turn on catalog ads that retarget anyone who visited similar products. The source says "you should do that right now". Settings not given. [7d806738414a] [verify UI: Advantage+ catalog ads setup]
2. Sit with a piece of paper and write every objection to your product you already know. [8e3f775ece26]
3. Call customers who abandoned checkout and ask why they did not buy. The source says this "sounds wild" but shows "very clearly" why people do not purchase. No script, sample size or response rate given. [8e3f775ece26] [needs brand data: abandoned checkout list with phone consent]
4. The header and card also name post-purchase surveys as an objection source; the transcript does not describe them. [8e3f775ece26]
5. Make 1 ad per objection, with 1 proof point. [8e3f775ece26]
6. Match proof to objection:
   - Price (named as one of the most common; the shopper sees a cheaper product elsewhere): show your product against a competitor if you are very competitive, or call out why your product is better and show its value. [8e3f775ece26]
   - Warranty or product lifespan: show the guarantee against competitors' shorter ones (HexClad example). [8e3f775ece26]
   - Efficacy: 1 clinical claim (haircare example). [8e3f775ece26]
   - Delivery speed: named in the header and cards as an objection; the transcript gives no example. [8e3f775ece26]
7. Load these ads into a separate bottom-of-funnel pack (3.4). [a8e061b0c56f]

### 3.5 Measure creative hit rate
1. Before launching, define a high-performing ad. His definition: spent more than 5% of the total campaign budget AND hit target CPA. [681541ae0981] [needs brand data: target CPA]
2. Count high-performing ads. Divide by total ads launched. Multiply by 100. [681541ae0981]
3. Compare with his benchmark: 5% is "really solid, fairly normal". [681541ae0981]
4. Project winners from volume only if quality holds: at 5%, 1,000 ads implies about 50 winners "if all things remain equal". [681541ae0981]
5. The source gives no time window for the count and does not say whether "total campaign budget" means daily budget or lifetime spend over the period. [681541ae0981]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Hit rate check.** How: run 3.5 on ads launched in a period. Good: around 5% or higher by his benchmark. Bad: hit rate falling as volume rises. Next: the card says a falling rate with rising volume means you are diluting quality, not scaling; cut volume and raise the quality bar (card's reading; the transcript implies but does not state it). [681541ae0981] [needs brand data: ad-level spend and CPA]
2. **Volume sanity check.** How: before adding ads, ask of each one "could this be a home run?" and "who is it for?". Bad: ads made because "creative volume and diversity is critical". Next: do not launch ads you do not believe in. [681541ae0981] [a932cc43b8da]
3. **Negative event check.** The source gives no metric or threshold. The card suggests vetting each ad for whether it will attract the right avatar and rejecting ads likely to pull unqualified clicks. Possible signals the source names: people who bounce around the site and do not convert, abandoned carts. How to measure them is not given. [a932cc43b8da] [needs brand data: bounce and cart abandonment by ad]
4. **Funnel position check.** How: compare packs on spend share, ROAS and conversion rate. Expected: top packs spend more with lower ROAS; bottom packs convert better and spend less. The source says you "might be wrong on some of them" and pack results show where each ad really sits. The source gives no rule for what to do when a pack is misplaced. [a8e061b0c56f] [needs brand data: pack-level spend, ROAS, CVR]
5. **Top ad check.** Does every top ad name the avatar and the problem, and show the product as the solution? If not, rewrite. [72f6438afa3a]
6. **Bottom ad check.** Does each bottom ad carry exactly 1 objection and 1 proof point? If it lists many benefits, split it. [8e3f775ece26]
7. **Setup check.** Existing customers (180-day) excluded; dataset correct; event is Purchase; attribution 7-1-1 as above; dynamic creative and live video off; placements open; new batch in a new pack. [a8e061b0c56f]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Spending thousands on "test budget" for AI creative, UGC and influencers in the belief that more ads alone will scale the account. [a932cc43b8da]
2. Flooding the account with thousands of sloppy ads, which scatters budget and feeds the pixel negative events. [a932cc43b8da]
3. Treating total ads launched as progress. Only winners count. [681541ae0981]
4. Building separate creative for top, middle and bottom funnel, including bridge stages. [7d806738414a]
5. Thinking your product does not solve a problem. [7d806738414a]
6. Packing every benefit into a bottom-of-funnel ad. [8e3f775ece26]
7. Using the budget field as a lever to increase spend. [a8e061b0c56f]
8. Using all-time purchasers as existing customers instead of 180-day purchasers. [a8e061b0c56f]
9. Choosing any conversion event other than Purchase. [a8e061b0c56f]
10. Hitting existing customers (retention) from the prospecting campaign. [a8e061b0c56f]
11. Pausing ads as the main control, instead of value rules and ad set limits across many packs. [a8e061b0c56f]
12. Skipping catalog retargeting at the bottom of the funnel. [7d806738414a]
