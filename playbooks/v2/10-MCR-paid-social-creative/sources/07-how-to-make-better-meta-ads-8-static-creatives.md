## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist or designer judge and fix single-image Meta ads before and after launch. It gives 4 lenses: the path the eye takes through the ad, clarity versus curiosity in the copy, image and headline as 1 unit, and matching the ad to cold or warm traffic. It shows each lens on 8 real statics (5 judged good or great, 3 judged bad or weak) and gives concrete rewrites. It is strong on layout and headline craft. It is thin on testing, numbers and landing pages.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Choose clarity or curiosity. Never sit in the middle
A static's copy must do 1 of 2 jobs. Either it is fully clear about what the product is and does, so the reader is primed to buy before the landing page. Or it is vague on purpose, so it opens a curiosity gap that pushes a click or pushes the eye further into the ad. What fails is confusion: copy that is neither clear nor a gap anyone wants closed. His examples of the failure: "You don't have to feel ready. You just have to begin." (productivity timer) and "We don't stop improving. Neither should you." (AG1). His example of a clear angle: "Fasting window hack by 2026" (Ketone IQ). His example of a useful gap: "Symptoms of vitamin deficiency" (IM8). Condition: applies to cold-audience statics; the card calls it a copy lens, not a tested rule. [17bfe69f2476] [1f32e05c1042]

### 2.2 A curiosity gap only works if the reader cares about the answer
AG1's "We don't stop improving" technically opens a gap ("what does that mean?"), but most people think "I don't care" and scroll on. Nobody has a stake in a brand's own improvement. Test: would the target buyer want the gap closed for their own benefit? [17bfe69f2476] [1f32e05c1042]

### 2.3 Design for the eye's reading path
Plan where the eye lands first, second and third. The first element must earn attention and give a reason to keep reading. If the reader does not keep moving through the static, "you've lost them". The best static in the set (protein mocha drink) flows: striking background photo and big headline (either order) open a question, a bold centre line answers it, short facts add detail, then a testimonial and a real product shot give trust. Condition: design craft from 8 teardowns, not measured. [08f49b8491b0] [1f32e05c1042]

### 2.4 Treat image and headline like a YouTube thumbnail and title
The image and the headline must add context to each other so together they earn the click. Neither should carry the message alone. Example: "This one simple switch made taking creatine 10x more enjoyable" works only because the photo shows a woman holding creatine gummies, so the "switch" is clear: gummies instead of powder. [2022d1907c7a]

### 2.5 A static can do the full job of a video on cold traffic
Many think only video can convert cold audiences at high spend, because only video can show what is unique, explain how it works and add proof. The presenter says the protein mocha static does all 3 in 1 image and has run for 2.5 years on cold audiences. Condition: his inference of spend and cold targeting from run time only. [08f49b8491b0]

### 2.6 Plain benefit first, science second
Lead with what the buyer feels. Put the mechanism after, in brackets or smaller type. This opens the ad to a larger market, because "you don't need a biology degree" to get it. He recommends reordering, not deleting the science. Example: Ketone IQ's "lowers ghrelin, improves mitochondrial efficiency, increases leptin" should become "feel less hungry (lowers ghrelin)" and "more energy (mitochondrial efficiency)". [9f262dc7beaa] [1f32e05c1042]

### 2.7 Specific numbers read as authority
A precise figure ("96% of users are more productive") makes a line look backed by science. The presenter adds that this works "despite it might not even being true and you've just made it up". This playbook does not accept that part: use only numbers you can prove. `[needs brand data: the study, survey or order data behind any % or number claim]` [385b3e7e17c3] [17bfe69f2476]

### 2.8 Name a persona and a problem in as few words as possible
Like a hook, a short line that names who and what ("GLP1 hair loss hits hard") gets the right people to read and self-select. He says it also reaches people in the group who do not have the problem yet, because it raises a fear they did not know about ("I'm on GLP-1s. Is this a problem?"). Condition: his reasoning, no audience data; fear-based health agitation carries claim risk (card caveat). [6afdcae5d6b5]

### 2.9 Build a broad cold ad from a list of common problems
The IM8 static lists 9 common symptoms and labels each with a vitamin in the product. Because most people have at least 1 of these problems at some point in a year, it reads as relevant to a large share of people while still only pulling in those who care about vitamins. He guesses the list covers "like 70%" of the general population; this is an impression, not a stat. [37c10bda4253]

### 2.10 Early use of a native format gives a short-lived edge
A format people do not see as an ad (here a tweet screenshot) stands out because "people aren't used to it on their feeds". "There's always an arbitrage opportunity when you're first to a new format." Once people get used to it, the edge fades. He gives no time frame. [dbda4aba2928] [37c10bda4253]

### 2.11 Partnership ads work as statics, not only as video
The IM8 static runs from another person's handle as a partnership ad. He says most brands only run partnership ads as video. `[verify UI: current partnership ad setup and eligibility for image ads in Ads Manager]` [37c10bda4253] [7c1f89cbddee]

### 2.12 A convincing ad does not need a call to action
He tells people who want to run organic posts as ads to "just take it and launch it on ads", with no CTA added. "People will naturally try to take the next step if the ad is convincing enough." His evidence is 1 ad (IM8) that he says is "scaling really well" with "a decent amount of spend". The card treats this as a hypothesis to test. Note: Meta still shows its own CTA button and "Paid partnership" label on the ad unit `[verify UI]`. [4e47f314df0c] [37c10bda4253]

### 2.13 Leaving the founder or influencer out can open a new demographic
The creatine gummy static belongs to a brand tied to a male influencer. He is not in the ad. The presenter says ads with him in them likely skew male, so an ad without him opens the product to women. The ad shows a woman holding the product. Condition: his reasoning, no audience split shown. [7c1f89cbddee]

### 2.14 Poor graphic design costs trust
Awkward spacing, a switch from lowercase to uppercase mid-line, an unneeded logo, a confusing graph and text too small to read on an iPhone each make the ad look amateur and cut authority. "You lose authority and you lose trust because just the graphic design isn't good." [1f32e05c1042] [08f49b8491b0]

### 2.15 Proof and a real product shot close the sale in a static
The last element in the best static is a testimonial plus a photo of the actual product as it looks when made, so the buyer trusts it will not look worse at home (his "McDonald's photo shoot" comparison). [08f49b8491b0]

### 2.16 Clickbait on the ad is fine if the landing page and retargeting do the teaching
A static can carry a little clickbait ("10x more enjoyable" exaggerates vs powder) to drive the click, as long as the landing page educates and later retargeting ads continue. He does not describe the landing page or the retargeting ads. [2022d1907c7a] [369b559b9fb2]

### 2.17 Use illustration for problems that are hard to show
For GLP-1 hair loss, a cartoon shows the problem cleanly. A real photo would be hard to make and could look graphic. He guesses a real image "might trigger the platform to either crank CPMs up on it or not push that ad as hard because it's leaning into health conditions". This is his guess about delivery, not Meta policy. Card 5bfda2bed53d reframes it as "test an illustration against a product or lifestyle image" and warns illustration does not guarantee approval. `[web check: Meta ad policy on health conditions, personal attributes and before-after imagery]` `[web check: Meta rules on AI-generated image disclosure]` [5bfda2bed53d] [6afdcae5d6b5]

### 2.18 Build a template once, then swap only the words and the picture
Two statics are praised as templates to scale: the energy gum layout (same clean product shot, new 5 to 6 word headline per angle) and the Grüns layout (swap the headline and the cartoon for each problem the product solves). [4f26d77793fa] [6afdcae5d6b5]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Audit a static (the presenter's order of checks)
1. Look at the ad as a viewer and note where your eye lands first. Usually the biggest headline or the most striking image. [08f49b8491b0] [1f32e05c1042]
2. Read that element and ask: does it give the target buyer a reason to care? If the honest answer is "I don't care", the ad fails here. [1f32e05c1042]
3. Place the copy on the clarity or curiosity line. If it is clear, check a buyer instantly knows the product and the benefit. If it is curiosity, check the gap is one the buyer wants closed. If it is neither, rewrite. [17bfe69f2476]
4. Check the image and headline pair with the substitution test (3.3). [2022d1907c7a]
5. Follow the eye to the next element. Ask whether each step answers the question the last one raised. [08f49b8491b0]
6. Read every supporting line on a phone screen. Flag anything too small, any jargon, any graph a reader cannot decode at a glance. [9f262dc7beaa] [1f32e05c1042]
7. Check craft: spacing, capital letters, logo placement, whether the brand logo adds anything. [1f32e05c1042]
8. Check trust: is there proof (testimonial, specific number you can back) and a true-to-life product shot? [08f49b8491b0] [385b3e7e17c3]
9. Ask "why this instead of what they use now?" If the ad does not answer it, the headline needs a new angle (3.4). [4f26d77793fa]
10. For announcement ads (new formula, new version), ask "what is new, and why should I care?" for both new and past buyers. [1f32e05c1042]
11. Decide: fix copy, fix layout, or cut. Do not add spend to a confusing ad. [1f32e05c1042]

### 3.2 Rewrite science-heavy benefit lines (Ketone IQ fix)
1. List every technical claim in the ad. [9f262dc7beaa]
2. Write the plain outcome next to each ("ghrelin" is a hunger hormone, so "feel less hungry"). [9f262dc7beaa]
3. Flip the order: plain outcome first and larger, technical term after in brackets or smaller. [9f262dc7beaa]
4. Make the supporting text about 30% bigger and move it up. [9f262dc7beaa] [08f49b8491b0]
5. If space is short, keep the product the same size, shift it left and put the benefit text in a right-hand panel. [9f262dc7beaa]
6. Keep what already works: the familiar icon style and the large product. [9f262dc7beaa]

### 3.3 Image and headline substitution test (creatine gummy method)
1. Write the headline and choose the image as 1 unit. Ask what the image tells the reader that the headline does not. [2022d1907c7a]
2. Swap in a generic image in your head (a plain tub of powder; a person holding a bag that just says the product name). [2022d1907c7a]
3. Re-read the headline with that image. If the reader can no longer tell what the headline means ("wait, what's the simple switch?"), the pair is too loose. [2022d1907c7a]
4. Avoid generic category shots when the headline depends on something new. A plain creatine powder photo fails on 2 counts: saturated market and no link to the headline. [2022d1907c7a]

### 3.4 Rewrite a weak benefit headline (energy gum fix)
1. Name what the product replaces. For energy gum it is coffee, which already gives "guaranteed energy". [4f26d77793fa]
2. Write new headlines of 5 to 6 words that answer "why this instead?". His angle types: a specific moment ("Avoid the 1pm crash", "Avoid the 11:30am crash"), money ("Save $6 per day"), or a specific persona plus problem. [4f26d77793fa]
3. Pick 1 route per ad and commit:
   - Curiosity route: e.g. "Save $6 per day" with the product blurred, so the reader must click to learn what it is.
   - Clarity route: educational copy so the reader is primed to buy before landing.
   [4f26d77793fa] [17bfe69f2476]
4. Send the curiosity route to a page that explains the angle. His example: a listicle showing gum at about 50 cents each versus $6 coffee a day. [4f26d77793fa] [369b559b9fb2]
5. Reuse the same clean product-shot layout for each angle; change only the headline. [4f26d77793fa]
Brand inputs before use: `[needs brand data: real unit price, real competitor or substitute price, customer language for the problem moment]`.

### 3.5 Fix a confusing static (productivity timer fix)
1. Keep what draws the eye: the specific number headline and the large-then-small font sizing. [385b3e7e17c3] [1f32e05c1042]
2. Fix spacing and the lowercase-to-uppercase jump. His fix: move the product slightly to the right. [1f32e05c1042]
3. Cut the slogan that says nothing. Replace it with either a clear line on what the product is and does, or a deliberate gap. [17bfe69f2476]
4. Remove the brand logo if the buyer will not know the brand; it is "not relevant". [1f32e05c1042]

### 3.6 Build a problem-list native static (IM8 method)
1. Pick a native format readers do not see as an ad (his example: a tweet). [37c10bda4253] [dbda4aba2928]
2. Write an educational headline that also filters the audience ("Symptoms of vitamin deficiency"). [37c10bda4253]
3. List common problems and tie each to an ingredient the product has. [37c10bda4253]
4. Put the product name and offer in the primary text, not the image (IM8: "The best multivitamin is IM8" plus a 10% off code). [37c10bda4253]
5. No CTA in the image. [4e47f314df0c]
6. Consider running it from a partner handle as a partnership ad `[verify UI]`. [37c10bda4253]
Brand inputs: `[needs brand data: ingredient list and which problems each can lawfully be linked to]`.

### 3.7 Build a persona-problem static (Grüns method)
1. Headline: persona plus problem in as few words as possible ("GLP1 hair loss hits hard"). [6afdcae5d6b5]
2. Visual: a clear illustration of the problem (he guesses an AI-generated image) with the product laid over it. [6afdcae5d6b5] [5bfda2bed53d]
3. Solution line naming how the product helps ("Support with essential nutrients like these") and "Click to find out more". [6afdcae5d6b5]
4. Keep it free of ambiguity and do not go "over the top". [6afdcae5d6b5]
5. Repeat for each core problem: swap the headline and the illustration. [6afdcae5d6b5]
Checks before launch: `[web check: Meta health and personal-attribute policy]`, `[needs brand data: substantiation for the product's link to the problem]`.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Copy that confuses instead of being clear or curious (slogans like "You just have to begin"). [17bfe69f2476]
2. Brand-centred headlines that give the buyer no reason to care ("We don't stop improving"). [1f32e05c1042]
