## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist or media buyer run a creative round that teaches something. The job: pick 1 customer problem from research, write 1 angle in 1 sentence, build the ad so every line proves that sentence with visuals, test a small batch that fits the budget, judge winners on the full metric chain (not spend share, CPA or CTR alone), and turn the result into the next brief. It is thin on account structure, budgets per ad and platform settings; those belong to 11-ADC.

## 2. Principles

1. **Start from what the ad must say, not from the format.** Condition: every new creative round. UGC, static and carousel are formats, "how you deliver the message". Decide the message first, then pick the format that carries it. Source calls starting from "should we make UGC or a static?" backwards. Evidence: presenter's agency practice. [e36ec3e6809d]
2. **Research the customer before anyone opens Canva or a camera.** Condition: before every round, and before a new product's first ads. Source's research menu: existing reviews, comments on your ads and social posts, support tickets, sales-call objections (for SaaS or info products), post-purchase surveys sent by email, Reddit threads, competitor reviews, and comments under competitor ads. The card treats this as a menu, not a mandatory checklist. [e36ec3e6809d] [needs brand data: reviews, ad comments, support tickets, post-purchase survey answers]
3. **Extract 3 things from research.** Condition: always. (a) What does the customer want? (b) What is stopping them from getting it? (c) What have they already tried? Each strong, repeated statement is an ad idea. Walking pad example: "I sit at my desk for 8 hours and realize I barely moved", "I want a treadmill but I don't really have space", "I'd use one during work but I can't have something super loud while I'm on calls". Source: "Those are our ads." [e36ec3e6809d]
4. **Let the value decide the format.** Condition: after the angle is set. If the product is easy to understand and the value is plain, a static image can work (source says it does for some clients). If the value needs to be seen (something folding open, real-world use), a static cannot show it; use video. [e36ec3e6809d]
5. **1 ad, 1 argument, 1 persona.** Condition: cold traffic. Packing every selling point (dimensions, 30-day returns, guarantee, quiet, compact, easy to store, motor, speed, warranty) turns the ad into "an infomercial when someone was just watching a cat video the reel before". Pick 1 argument for the persona you target. Everything in the ad must support it. [13fa07bab746]
6. **Leave logistics to the landing page.** Condition: cold-traffic creative. Source says the 30-day return, warranty and guarantee should not be in the ad; "they'll learn all of that later on the landing page". This is 1 source's view; see section 9. [13fa07bab746]
7. **Paint the value, not the object, for cold traffic.** Condition: cold audiences. A white-background product shot with "at home treadmill" shows "the metal rectangle", not the reason to buy. Exception the source gives: that plain product image may work for high-intent people who are already sold and only choose who to buy from and when. Cold traffic "need the picture painted" and need to feel excited about the value. [13fa07bab746]
8. **1 product has many reasons to buy, so many angles.** Condition: when budget allows. Source: 1 product can have 10 reasons to buy, which can become 10 creative angles, not 1 ad explaining all 10. Build 1 angle to profitability first, then use "side budget" to test the others. [13fa07bab746] [320d499e601c] [64b12f085516]
9. **Aim for self-recognition, not cleverness.** Condition: hooks and scripts for cold traffic. "We're not trying to be super clever." If the viewer thinks "this solves my exact problem", that beats "the world's most cinematic ad that appeals to everybody". The trade-off (narrow resonance over broad appeal) is intended. [3921589ed44c]
10. **Every claim needs visual proof.** Condition: every claim in voiceover, copy or overlay. Ask "how can I prove this visually?" for each. Reason given: anyone can say "this product changed my life", and with AI copy everywhere people no longer trust it. The creative should do as much of the selling as possible visually. [306f74067bdf]
11. **Show numbers only when the brand can back them.** Condition: a quantifiable claim. If the brand has data, show the actual number rather than an adjective. If it does not, it is an unsupported claim; do not show it. [306f74067bdf] [needs brand data: test results or specs that support any number shown]
12. **Repeated review themes are new angles.** Condition: a brand with 1 generic creative that wants more angles. Look for the same use case repeated across reviews, not one-off mentions. Build a separate creative per cluster. [320d499e601c]
13. **Make variations of an angle, not random new ads.** Condition: once an angle looks promising. Keep the angle, product and argument; change 1 meaningful element (the source's example is format: UGC video versus carousel). If one "massively outperforms", you learned something. [1c4808b43997]
14. **Match testing volume to budget.** Condition: every test launch. At $30, $50 or $100 a day you cannot feed an unlimited number of ads enough spend to learn. Launch a focused batch and give the platform room to spread spend and return a signal. [d13b3e03307b]
15. **The ad with the most spend is not automatically the winner.** Condition: any test where 1 ad took most of the spend. It drives the most sales because it was "out in the market more than anything else". The platform snowballs spend onto ads with strong CTR, good link CTR and below-average CPM; that is the platform reacting to engagement, not proof of buying intent. Watch the ad and understand why it works. [cae81d0649c]
16. **High CTR can be empty attention.** Condition: reading test results. A visually stimulating ad can win clicks while viewers never learn what is sold or whether they are even a customer. Judge by the whole metric chain: CTR, the drop from CTR to link CTR, and post-click buying behaviour. [03cf444bb483]
17. **Do not judge on CPA or ROAS alone.** Condition: all winner calls. "There's a lot more to the story than just return on ad spend and CPA." A good CPA may mean the ad only works at low spend, or would be a better retargeting ad. The card marks this as the source's stated position, not a platform rule. [cae81d0649c] [64b12f085516]
18. **Change 1 variable at a time.** Condition: all tests after a winner is found. If hook, angle, audience, landing page, offer, campaign and creative all change at once, "what did we test? Nothing, really." Keep enough stable to isolate what you learn. [1c4808b43997]
19. **Growth is a 2-piece puzzle: cost to get attention and conversion rate.** Condition: diagnosing profit. Illustration: sending 1 million people to a site that converts at 0.5% will "never be profitable". The 0.5% is an arithmetic illustration, not a threshold. [f6fbbab529e7]
20. **Place each ad where its metrics say it belongs in the funnel.** Condition: after a test produces several usable ads. Combined metrics (CTR, link CTR, CPA, post-click behaviour, ability to hold spend at scale) tell you top, middle or bottom of funnel. No single metric assigns the role. The source gives no cutoffs. [64b12f085516] [cae81d0649c]
21. **Every test is the next brief.** Condition: after each round. Read the result as a production instruction, not a score. "Let the result tell you what to make next." [01183c908919]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Research to angle (before production)
1. Collect customer language from the research menu in principle 2. Include competitor reviews and comments under competitor ads. [e36ec3e6809d] [needs brand data: review exports, ad comments, tickets, survey answers] [web check: competitor reviews and competitor ad comments]
2. Sort statements into 3 buckets: wants, blockers, already tried. [e36ec3e6809d]
3. Look for clusters: the same use case or complaint repeated. Prioritize by how often it appears; the card adds "how well it lines up with the audience you can reach affordably". [320d499e601c] [needs brand data: review theme counts]
4. From competitor reviews, note what rivals do badly. That becomes an objection to counter in your ad and a way to differ beyond "we have this and it's good". [e36ec3e6809d] [01183c908919]
5. Pick 1 customer problem. Write the angle in 1 sentence for 1 persona. Source example: "Get more steps while you work without needing a giant treadmill taking precious space." [13fa07bab746] [01183c908919]
6. Choose the format that shows the value (principle 4). [e36ec3e6809d]

### 3.2 Build the ad from the angle
1. Hook: turn the problem into a direct, qualifying first line. Source example: "If you work from home and somehow finish the day with 800 steps when your goal was 10,000, keep listening." Card adds: reuse the customer's own words from reviews and tickets where possible. [3921589ed44c]
2. Solution: state it plainly. Example: the pad fits under your standing desk, lets you get steps in while you work, and can be put away when you are done. [3921589ed44c]
3. Show the whole process on screen. Example sequence: person working at the desk, pad underneath, person on a call, person putting it away. Source says this sequence can carry the ad with almost no text overlay. [3921589ed44c]
4. List every claim in the script. For each, write the shot that proves it. [306f74067bdf]
   - "compact": show how small it is; show the room still has space.
   - "fits under a desk": film it under a desk.
   - "stores easily": show someone putting it in an apartment closet or moving it; source suggests even showing a 55+ person doing it.
   - "quiet enough for calls": film someone on an important call or interview, then zoom out to reveal them walking. Only if the product is actually that quiet.
   - any quantified claim: show the real number, only if the brand has data. [13fa07bab746] [306f74067bdf]
5. Cut everything that does not support the angle sentence, including returns, warranty and guarantee. [13fa07bab746]
6. Pre-launch check: does every line prove the angle sentence? Is every claim shown, not just said? [01183c908919] [306f74067bdf]

### 3.3 Set up a creative test
1. Check daily spend before deciding batch size. [d13b3e03307b] [needs brand data: daily spend, account CPM]
2. Make variations of 1 angle, not unrelated ads. Example: version 1 a UGC video, version 2 a carousel whose slides tell the story; same angle, same product, same argument. [1c4808b43997]
3. Run them "apples-to-apples" so each gets equal visibility. The source's example account: 13 ads at $30 a day, with a per-ad impression limit first set to 1,000, then lowered to 800 because CPMs averaged $50 to $70 (a newer account whose pixel had little data). [d13b3e03307b] [1c4808b43997] [verify UI: how to cap or equalize impressions per ad in Meta; the source does not say which setting, rule or tool he used]
4. Aim to find winners within the first 1 to 2 weeks. [d13b3e03307b]
5. Do not let the platform alone pick the winner. In the example, 1 ad took the most spend on day 1; left alone, source says the platform would have spent $150, $200 or $400 on it. [cae81d0649c]
6. If most ads in a big batch never got spend, record the test as inconclusive, not as failures, and re-run fewer creatives with enough delivery (card instruction). [d13b3e03307b] [cae81d0649c]

### 3.4 Read the result (attention-to-purchase chain)
1. Did we get attention? If nobody stops: the hook may be weak or the visual not interesting. [f6fbbab529e7]
2. Did they click to the site? Compare CTR with link CTR. If people watch or engage but do not click through: the ad may entertain but not explain the product or why they need it. [f6fbbab529e7] [03cf444bb483]
3. What happened after the click? Check conversion rate, add to carts, checkouts, offer, pricing, shipping and site experience. If an ad sends many quality clicks and nobody buys, do not blame the creative first; the landing page may be slow or weak. [f6fbbab529e7] [needs brand data: post-click funnel by ad, landing page speed]
4. Watch the ad to explain the numbers. Ask whether the reason it performs relates to the product and its value. [cae81d0649c] [03cf444bb483]
5. Ask whether the ad can hold spend at larger budget. An ad that looks great at $30 a day can fall apart when scaled. [cae81d0649c] [64b12f085516]
6. Assign a funnel role from the combined pattern. A strong CPA that may only hold at low spend could be a retargeting ad. [64b12f085516]
7. Cut the weakest ad. Keep what won. [1c4808b43997]

### 3.5 Turn the result into the next brief
1. Angle won: make more creatives on that angle. [01183c908919]
2. 1 hook "crushed the others": keep that hook and write 3 different video bodies after it. [01183c908919]
3. A format lost and another won: follow the evidence. Example: review-style UGC underperformed but a simple product demonstration worked, so make more demonstrations. [01183c908919]
4. A concept came up in last week's winners: brief UGC about that concept or selling angle. [01183c908919]
5. Competitor reviews show a weakness: brief an ad that counters that objection. [01183c908919]
6. In the source's live test, the next step was more before-and-after and routine footage. [1c4808b43997]
7. Replace the weekly question "can we get AI to make more static images?" with "what did customers respond to last week, and what do we make from it?" Source says the first question gave no results over 2 weeks for teams he saw. [01183c908919]
8. Repeat the round formula: 1 customer problem, 1-sentence angle, every line proves it, change 1 meaningful thing at a time. [01183c908919]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Starting from format ("UGC or static?") instead of the message. [e36ec3e6809d]
2. Copying a UGC ad one week, a static the next, adding 15 concepts, and still not knowing what the winners proved. (source text only, intro)
3. Cramming every selling point into 1 ad (the infomercial). [13fa07bab746]
4. Using a plain white-background product shot for cold traffic. [13fa07bab746]
5. Trying to be clever or cinematic instead of clear. [3921589ed44c]
6. Claims without visual proof; generic praise ("changed my life", "best walking pad ever"). [306f74067bdf]
7. Launching 30 ads on a small budget and calling them failures after 2 days when they never got spend. [cae81d0649c] [d13b3e03307b]
8. Crowning the ad that got the most spend. [cae81d0649c]
9. Trusting high CTR as proof of a winner. [03cf444bb483]
10. Judging on CPA or ROAS alone. [cae81d0649c] [64b12f085516]
11. Blaming the creative when quality clicks do not convert; the landing page may be the problem. [f6fbbab529e7]
12. Changing many variables at once. [1c4808b43997]
13. Assuming an ad that works at $30 a day will hold at scale. [cae81d0649c]
14. Making "random new ads" instead of variations of a working angle. [1c4808b43997]
15. Weekly "make more AI statics" production with no link to last week's results. [01183c908919]
16. Keeping ads that drag results down "even if it's 5 cent cost per click" worse; source says leave them behind. (source text only)

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single practitioner, agency pitch.** All rules come from 1 agency owner's experience; the video sells his service. Evidence is 1 account and 1 pair of ads. [cae81d0649c] [01183c908919]
- **Forced equal delivery versus letting Meta choose.** The source wants apples-to-apples tests with equal visibility and distrusts the platform's spend allocation. Much common Meta advice says to let the algorithm allocate spend across many creatives and trust its choice. Both sides exist; the source does not address how its approach interacts with Meta's delivery system. [web check: current Meta guidance on creative testing and its A/B test tool] [cae81d0649c] [1c4808b43997]
- **Offers and guarantees out of the ad.** The source says leave returns, warranty and guarantee to the landing page. Common ecommerce advice often puts an offer or risk reversal in the ad, and other sources in this library may disagree. Treat as this source's view for cold, value-first ads, not a rule. [13fa07bab746]
- **CTR drop is unclear.** "Over 3%" and "less than 2%" drop from CTR to link CTR could mean percentage points or relative change; the source does not say. It also does not name which CTR (all clicks or link clicks) he means. [03cf444bb483] [verify UI: Meta CTR column definitions]
- **Timeline does not add up.** The example test's own numbers imply about 17 to 24 days at $30 a day, beyond the stated 1 to 2 weeks (see section 4). The source does not explain.
- **UGC versus carousel is not a clean single-variable test.** The source admits video versus carousel is a big difference and says to "use this in your own scenarios". Card: control as much as possible. [1c4808b43997]
- **What is missing:** how many ads per dollar of budget; what spend or impressions make a result significant; which campaign type or structure (ABO, CBO, Advantage+) to test in; how the impression limit is set; attention metrics (hook rate, thumb-stop) for stage 1; funnel-placement cutoffs (the card says none are given); how to build the "side budget". Look to 11-ADC for these. [64b12f085516] [d13b3e03307b]
- **Scope overlap.** Test setup, metrics and funnel placement lean into 11-ADC (performance, scaling). Landing page checks lean into 06-MEA and 09-CRO. They are kept here because the source ties them to creative decisions.
- **Card additions beyond the transcript:** "reuse the customer's own words" [3921589ed44c], "prioritize segments by reachable audience" [320d499e601c], and "record the test as inconclusive" [d13b3e03307b] are card-writer inferences, consistent with but not stated in the transcript.
- **"CT, LT" and "stairway to paid ads heaven"** are named but not explained; do not build rules from them.
- Brand fit: before using any angle, claim or number, confirm it with the brand's own reviews and data. [needs brand data: review themes, product specs, proof assets such as real product footage]
