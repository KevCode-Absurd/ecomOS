## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist or founder decide what a single Meta ad must do before it is made, and how to prepare for it. It gives: 4 psychological pillars (attention, curiosity, emotion, connection), how to pick which pillars to lean on based on the product and the funnel behind the ad, a manual research routine, a 6-field static ad brief, and a 4-point "made for me" review. It is deliberately basic. It skips account structure, KPIs, testing setup, awareness stages and video structure. It is strong on the "why does this ad feel good" judgment. It is thin on execution detail and has no measured results.

## 2. Principles

1. **A good ad does 4 things: gets attention, sparks curiosity, invokes emotion, and connects with the buyer.** He calls these the psychology that "boils down to these four things". The card frames them as a checklist, not a strict order. Condition: his framework, no data. [84a55aede410]
2. **You have about 0.5 seconds to get attention, not 3.** "A lot of the data says you need 3 seconds. I would 100% disagree with that." His basis: watching people scroll, they move on "in less than a second" if the ad is not interesting. Condition: opinion from observation, not a measured threshold. Card caveat agrees: treat it as a push toward faster hooks. [84a55aede410] `[web check: current Meta guidance on hook timing and thumb-stop metrics]` `[needs brand data: 3-second view rate and hold rate by ad, to see where the brand's viewers drop]`
3. **An ad does not always have to close the sale. Sometimes it only needs to earn the click.** A long prospecting VSL that takes a cold scroller all the way to buyer "is an example of where you're basically selling all in that original ad". But if the bottom of the funnel (a listicle, a quiz funnel, or "a really good landing page") explains the product and why to buy, "all you need is curiosity in some ads". Condition: only when the page behind the ad does the selling. [84a55aede410] [8bf081bed714] `[needs brand data: which landing page, quiz or listicle the ad routes to, and its conversion rate]`
4. **Pick the emotion that fits who the buyer is and where they are.** Emotions range from positive (humor, laughter, excitement) to "really dark negative emotions, fear, insecurity". "This is extremely important that you understand who you're selling to and where they are so you can figure out what emotion is most likely to influence them to make a buying decision." Condition: the choice must come from research on the ICP, not taste. [84a55aede410] [8bf081bed714]
5. **Connection means using the ICP's own words and in-group signals.** Example: the looksmaxing ad uses "ascend", a niche term, "because it's giving us that connection with our ICP". The golf ads speak to the Gen Z and millennial golfer, not "the old guard of golfers". [84a55aede410] [9d1bbe5069da]
6. **Not every ad leans on the same pillars.** Products that solve a big emotional problem lean on curiosity and emotion, and often route to a quiz or "consumption funnel". Light, low-emotion products (golf driver and putter covers, a dip cup) do not need dark curiosity. They lean on fast attention and connection, often through humor and meme formats. "Not every ad is built the same, but those four key psychological principles are what's going to allow you to actually build ads that are going to connect." [8bf081bed714] [84a55aede410]
7. **An ad built for one ICP should fail with others, and that is fine.** The fertility or looksmaxing ICP would scroll past the golf ads "in 2 seconds and vice versa". Each ad is judged only by whether its own ICP stops, clicks, buys or shares. The cards turn this into a pre-spend fit test. [84a55aede410] [9d1bbe5069da]
8. **Shareable, funny ads can spread the product and, he says, raise AOV.** On the golf ad: "This is a sharable ad." "We're also increasing the average order value by making an ad like this because this is an ad that they would share to their friends." He does not explain the mechanism (for example, friends buying together or buying for the group) and gives no AOV number. Condition: his claim, untested here. Card caveat: test it, do not assume it. [9d1bbe5069da] `[needs brand data: AOV and units per order for orders attributed to meme or humor ads vs other ads]`
9. **Product specs alone do not make a good ad.** "These are not just products with, you know, the ingredients that the product has. Nobody cares." "Product pictures with, you know, how heavy the t-shirt weighs or what GSM it is. News flash, nobody cares." What works instead: funny, emotionally hitting, curiosity-driven content "that people actually like to see". Condition: stated as a blanket rule. See section 9 for where this conflicts with his own "USP call out" creative type and with common advice. [220538bd0247]
10. **Research is the first step before any ad, and it must be manual.** "Before you make any ad, any ad, you should be doing tons of deep research." Most people at his Miami talk had not done much manual research. He has "really good prompts" and says AI deep research in 5 minutes is possible, but he still reads Reddit comments himself "because that's where you can actually understand your customer". [d59ecd84945b]
11. **AI will not write niche humor for you.** "ChatGPT is not going to write you a golf headline about a foursome." It will not write "spitters and quitters" either. "You have to actually think, be funny, try to write some decent copy." The research document is "not that you're going to paste into ChatGPT". Condition: his opinion on AI copy. The card caveat (not the transcript) softens this: AI can help pick candidates if a human reviews them. [d59ecd84945b]
12. **Name the creative type on every brief so the set stays diverse.** "Always write down your creative type because you don't want to make a hundred of the exact same ad. You do need creative diversity." [377393f355c6]
13. **A good ad makes the viewer feel "this is made for me".** His closing recap lists 4 traits: it speaks directly to the ICP, creates a curiosity gap, triggers an emotional response, and provides a clear path to a solution. Note this recap drops "attention" and adds "clear path to a solution" compared with the 4 pillars in principle 1. The way to get there: "not making generic ads that look like everybody else's", good research, speaking to the ICP, and testing systematically. [220538bd0247]
14. **A strong offer can close the sale on its own.** He bought Skims for his girlfriend at Christmas from an ad with "an offer so good I just could not refuse". "If I ever see an ad with a really good offer, that's what makes me buy." Condition: a personal anecdote, said as an aside. The card turns it into "pair good ad psychology with a compelling offer". ## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Manual research sprint (before any ad)

1. Block 3 to 4 days in a row for research on the niche and the product. Card caveat: scale this to how unfamiliar the niche is. [d59ecd84945b]
2. Read, in the customer's own words: Reddit threads; competitors' reviews on their websites; Amazon reviews; comments on TikTok videos; ad comments. [d59ecd84945b]
3. Do competitor analysis. He names it but gives no method. [d59ecd84945b]
4. Build one research document by hand. Copy and paste Reddit comments, reviews from competitor websites and Amazon, and ad comments. Take screenshots. Write your own notes. Keep raw wording. [d59ecd84945b]
5. Do not paste the document into ChatGPT to write the ads. Use it yourself to find the angle, the emotion, the in-group words and the jokes. [d59ecd84945b]
6. Use the document as the source for every brief (3.2). Card adds this as an explicit step. [d59ecd84945b]

Thin on how: he does not say how to search Reddit, how many sources to read, how to tag or organize the document, or how to turn quotes into angles.

### 3.2 Write a brief per static ad

He gives this as his agency's brief framework and calls it "the framework of how you should be laying out your planning for static ads". Fields, in his order: [377393f355c6]

1. **Concept.** Not defined further.
2. **Creative type.** His examples: curiosity-based static, us versus them, USP call out, social proof and review-based ad. Always fill this in, to keep diversity.
3. **Angle.** "What are you actually doing? Who are you actually selling to?" So in his framing the angle field also holds the target buyer.
4. **Headline.**
5. **CTA.**
6. **Visual direction.**

Then either hand the brief to a designer, or review your own brief and build the ad yourself in Canva `[verify UI]`. [377393f355c6]

Thin on how: no example of a filled brief, no guidance on how many briefs per concept, and no field for emotion, pillar, funnel destination or offer, even though the talk says those matter. An operator may want to add them; that is an adaptation, not the source.

### 3.3 Choose the ad's job and pillar mix

Pieced together from the 4 examples. He does not give it as numbered steps. [8bf081bed714] [84a55aede410]

1. Decide where the click goes. Quiz funnel, consumption funnel, listicle or strong landing page: the ad only needs curiosity and emotion. No such page: the ad has to do more of the selling (his example is a long prospecting VSL).
2. Decide how heavy the problem is for the buyer. Big private problem (fertility, appearance): lead with the private fear or self-blame and offer a next step such as a quiz. Light product (golf accessories, dip cup): lead with fast attention and connection through humor or a meme.
3. Pick one core emotion from research: fear, insecurity, excitement, humor. [8bf081bed714]
4. Write in the ICP's language, including niche terms and jokes they would repeat.
5. Check the ad against the 4 pillars (3.4).

### 3.4 Pre-launch review

Two checks from the talk. The cards turn them into a scorecard.

1. Pillar check: Did we get their attention? Are we sparking curiosity? Are we hitting an emotional pain point? Are we connecting with our ICP? He runs this out loud on the fertility ad and answers yes to all 4. [84a55aede410]
2. "Made for me" check: Does it speak directly to the ICP? Does it create a curiosity gap? Does it trigger an emotional response? Does it provide a clear path to a solution? [220538bd0247]
3. ICP fit check: picture the exact buyer seeing it in their real context (the woman trying to get pregnant, the 16 to 22 year old doom scrolling at night, the Gen Z golfer with his friends, the man whose girlfriend complains about his dip cup). Would they stop, click, share or buy? An off-target viewer scrolling in 2 seconds is expected. [84a55aede410] [9d1bbe5069da]
4. Card adds: rework any ad that looks like everyone else's or leans on specs like shirt weight or GSM; check the offer too. [220538bd0247]
5. Proofread the live copy. His own fertility ad example has a typo ("text" instead of "test"). He says the version that ran was correct, but the saved file was not. This check is implied by the example, not stated as a step.

## 7. Examples and case results

All 4 are ads his agency made for clients. He gives no results for any of them. Treat them as examples of reasoning, not proof. [8bf081bed714] [9d1bbe5069da]

1. **Fertility product (sent to a quiz funnel).** Buyer: women trying to get pregnant, "in a very emotional time", maybe thinking "What's wrong with me?" Copy: "Stop blaming yourself for not seeing that positive test. Take the quiz and see what's stopping you." He says it hits all 4 pillars. The saved image had a typo ("text"). Little selling in the ad; the quiz does the work.
2. **Looksmaxing tool (sent to a quiz or consumption funnel).** Visual: a dark image with a face "looking back at them". Imagined viewer: 16 to 22, doom scrolling at night. Copy: "What if one tool gave you everything you need to ascend?" "Ascend" is niche slang, used for connection. Darkness supplies the emotion. "There's a lot more consumption in the funnel."
3. **Golf driver and putter covers.** Meme-style copy: "The foursome experience you dream about." Buyer: Gen Z or millennial golfer, weekend round with friends and beer. Goal: feel like "one of the boys", funny, shareable. He claims it raises AOV because people share it. No number.
4. **Dip cup ("a cup that's cleaner for dipping").** Copy: "Made for spitters, not quitters." Buyer: a man who dips and whose girlfriend complains about his dip cup. He does not dip (he uses nicotine pouches) but says he would find it funny and share it.
5. **Offer anecdote.** He bought Skims for his girlfriend at Christmas because the ad's offer was too good to refuse. One personal purchase, not data. [220538bd0247]
6. **Brands named as doing ads well**: AG1, Comfrt (transcribed "Comfort"), Young LA, Skims, LMNT (transcribed "Element"). He says they "have spent millions to crack customer psychology". No ads from them are shown or analysed.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Believing you have 3 seconds to hook. He says 0.5. [84a55aede410]
2. Trying to sell fully inside every ad when a quiz or landing page could finish the job. [84a55aede410] [8bf081bed714]
3. Using dark curiosity and emotion for products that do not need it, or ignoring humor and connection for light products. [8bf081bed714]
4. Making ads about specs (ingredients, t-shirt weight, GSM) that "nobody cares" about. [220538bd0247]
5. Skipping manual research, or replacing it with a 5-minute ChatGPT deep research prompt. [d59ecd84945b]
6. Expecting ChatGPT to write niche jokes and headlines. [d59ecd84945b]
7. Pasting Reddit into ChatGPT and taking its headlines as the ad. [d59ecd84945b]
8. Not naming the creative type, which leads to "a hundred of the exact same ad". [377393f355c6]
9. Generic ads "that look like everybody else's". [220538bd0247]
10. Not testing systematically. He says "test properly" but gives no method in this video. [220538bd0247]
11. Shipping copy with typos (from his own example, not stated as a rule).
