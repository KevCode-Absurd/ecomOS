## 1. What this source is for

It gives a creative strategist a menu of 7 native short-form video formats for TikTok (organic, affiliate and paid), each with 1 live example and its numbers, plus 1 shared structure: hook first, raw story or demo in the middle, product and CTA at the back. Use it to pick which formats to brief first for a product and to write briefs for creators or in-house shoots. It also has 1 side note on bundling low-price products to lift AOV. It does not cover targeting, bids, budgets or how to read results (those live in 11-ADC and 24-DEP).

## 3. Procedures

The source gives formats by example, not step-by-step. Steps below keep his words. Where a step comes from a card and not from the transcript, it says so.

### 3.1 "Don't do this if you like this" hook
1. Pick something the target buyer already loves that the product restricts or changes. Example: "don't drink this if you like eating" for an appetite-curbing mate drink. [e897b3068f2a]
2. Put that line up front as the headline or opening line. His reason: it "naturally gets people curious". [e897b3068f2a]
3. Have the creator (or you) explain the product through personal story. Example lines: "I'm a foodie", "if I do not eat before I drink this I will forget to for the rest of the day". [e897b3068f2a]
4. Post organically, give to affiliates, run as an ad. [e897b3068f2a]
- Card addition, not in the transcript: cast a creator who visibly embodies the tension. [e897b3068f2a]
- Compliance: appetite suppression is a health-style claim [web check: TikTok ad policy on weight-loss and appetite claims in the target market].

### 3.2 Problem/solution with competitor comparison
1. Call out the problem at the start in the viewer's words ("if you see a lot of dust"). [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
2. Show competing products failing. Example: 2 rival screen tools that "broke", scratched, or had "no alignment". [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
3. Show your product doing the same job: removes all the dust at once, does not scratch, does not break, easy to apply ("simply slap it on"). [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
4. List several reasons a buyer might stay with a rival and several of your benefits, so the difference is clear. [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
5. Film "in a setting comparing other products". Use conditional lines: "if you're experiencing X problem you need this", "if you're using X product and you're not seeing X result you should use this". [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
- Card additions, not in the transcript: "one continuous shot", "one take", and "rather than cutting between clips". [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
- He notes the example is "definitely over exaggerated" (far more dust than a real phone). He presents that as part of why it got views; the card flags it as a trust risk. ecomOS rule: no invented claims, so do not stage a problem that is not real.

### 3.3 Transformation teaser
1. Use a sound overlay or voiceover that teases a future state without showing it. Example: "see you in 3 months on the beach in a bikini with an attitude and abs", "good luck finding my waist". [91a9091b2a20]
2. Show the product and call out key ingredients. Example: 20 g of protein. The card suggests 2 to 3 ingredients or specs; the transcript names only protein. [91a9091b2a20]
3. Keep it short. Example was "only a few seconds long". [91a9091b2a20]
4. Post on the brand's own page. No influencer or affiliate is needed. [91a9091b2a20]
5. Variant: before/after, where you do show the before and after. [91a9091b2a20]
- Body-image claims ("abs", "finding my waist") can breach ad policy [web check: TikTok ad policy on body image and weight-loss claims].

### 3.4 Reaction-style video
1. Check the product gives a strong sensation or visible change on first use. If not, skip this format. [01058f4f2412]
2. Set up a first try on camera. Ideas he gives: street interviews where people try the product; a gym taste test for a gym supplement; a clothing swap ("what you're normally wearing versus what you should be wearing", "what you're wearing now versus what you're going to wear going out this evening"). [01058f4f2412]
3. Capture the reaction. Example opener: "two, one, take a deep breath ... what is this". [01058f4f2412]
4. Let the viewer's not knowing (what it smells like, what it does) drive questions in the comments. He says this "is going to drive up sales volume". [01058f4f2412]
- Card additions, not in the transcript: "one take", place product explanation and CTA after the reaction, and "use real first-time participants". [01058f4f2412]

### 3.5 Misconception video
1. Name a belief people hold that is wrong. Example theme: mouth breathing at night. [de0b0ea997a3]
2. Correct it with a credible source on camera. Example: a speaker cites a book, "Jaws", by his "colleagues at Stanford" (captioned "Paul erck and Sandra KH") and twins, 1 mouth breather and 1 nose breather, with different facial structure [web check: book title, authors and what it actually claims]. The card calls it "a Stanford study"; the transcript says a book. [de0b0ea997a3]
3. Present the product at the end as the solution. Example: mouth tape worn during sleep. [de0b0ea997a3]
4. Expect questions ("how does this even work"). Answer them with comment-reply videos that link to the product. [de0b0ea997a3] [52eb399debbc]
5. Take the organic post into an ad to scale. [de0b0ea997a3]
- Framing to reuse: "if you've always believed you should be doing this you're actually wrong". [de0b0ea997a3]
- The example claims mouth breathing affects the jawline and that taping "can actually bring back your jawline". That is a health claim. ecomOS rule: no invented claims; any study cited in brand creative must be checked first [web check: the specific study or book before citing it].

### 3.6 Comment-reply video
1. Find a comment with a question or disbelief. Example comment: "what is this sorcery". He notes many people liked that comment. [52eb399debbc]
2. Reply to it with a video that demonstrates how the product works in a visual way. Example: an adhesive bra alternative, "grippy not sticky so they grip to your natural body heat", with a visible before and after. [52eb399debbc]
3. Link directly to the product. [52eb399debbc]
4. Run the reply video as a TikTok ad to scale. [52eb399debbc]
- He says some replies "go viral for the wrong reasons", but the algorithm "is really good at getting in front of the right audience". The card adds: watch who converts, not just views [needs brand data: purchases by video, not views]. [52eb399debbc]
- Card addition, not in the transcript: pick comments with "visible likes and replies" as the demand signal. [52eb399debbc]

### 3.7 Free tip / secret narrative
1. Open with the problem state in a peer's voice. Example: "I no longer wake up multiple times at night to go to the bathroom". [bea60e1a6e4a]
2. Frame the answer as a tip or secret others keep asking about ("everyone keeps asking me what am I doing"; "I actually just listened to my wife, she was spot on"). [bea60e1a6e4a]
3. Reveal the tip is a product. Example: 2 products, 1 a nitric oxide booster "helps with my blood flow circulation a healthy BP". [bea60e1a6e4a]
4. Keep it looking like raw storytelling; pitch in the middle and back; clear CTA. [bea60e1a6e4a] [03820985a569]
- Blood pressure and circulation are health claims [web check: TikTok and local ad rules on supplement health claims]. The source says nothing about compliance.

### 3.8 Pick formats for a product (card synthesis)
The source does not give a selection guide. Card [2eae62f9a017] maps each format to a product type from the examples. Use as a starting slate, not a rule:

| Product trait | Format to try first |
|---|---|
| Strong sensation or visual change on first use | Reaction (3.4) |
| Buyers use a flawed rival product | Problem/solution comparison (3.2) |
| Aspirational result that takes time (fitness, body, skin) | Transformation teaser (3.3) |
| Category full of false beliefs | Misconception (3.5) |
| Consumable tied to a recurring annoyance | Free tip / secret (3.7) |
| Looks surprising in use | Comment-reply demo (3.6) |
| Restricts a desire the buyer has | "Don't do this if you like this" (3.1) |

Then shoot each with hook, value, CTA and post to organic, affiliate and paid. [2eae62f9a017] [needs brand data: which formats this brand has already tested and how they did]

### 3.9 Bundle a low-ticket product
1. Check whether the single-unit price can carry ad cost and margin [needs brand data: unit cost, margin, CAC]. [5aaabe53c66f]
2. If not, offer 3, 5 and 6 packs. Source example: about $4.99 per unit, AOV lifted to $25 to $30. [5aaabe53c66f]
- Card additions, not in the transcript: make the bundle the default path, check it fits the replenishment cycle, and show a real per-unit saving versus single units. [5aaabe53c66f]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Assuming views mean sales. 34.5 million views did not scale sales in line. [c7c5b8ef2ed7]
- Showing the end result straight away in a transformation video. He says it kills the curiosity. [91a9091b2a20]
- Making videos that look like ads instead of raw stories. [03820985a569]
- Pitching before the hook and story have earned attention. [03820985a569]
- Using a reaction format on a product without a strong sensation. [01058f4f2412]
- Raising unit price when bundling would lift AOV instead. [5aaabe53c66f]
- Ignoring comments. Questions and disbelief are raw material for the next video. [52eb399debbc]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Copying.** The title says "STEAL THESE". ecomOS treats these ads as format, layout and mood signals only. Never copy their text, products, claims or faces.
- **Competitor callouts.** He shows rival products failing and says to call out "multiple reasons why somebody might go with a competitor". Common advice and platform rules are stricter: naming or showing a rival brand can breach ad policy and comparative-advertising law, and staged failures can mislead [web check: TikTok ad policy on comparative claims in the target market]. Safer default: show a generic "old way" and your product doing better, with true claims only.
- **Exaggeration.** He praises the "over exaggerated" dust. This conflicts with ecomOS writing rules (no invented claims) and with common advice that staged problems hurt trust in comments.
- **Health and body claims.** 4 of 7 examples carry health or body claims (appetite, jawline, abs and waist, blood pressure). He never mentions compliance. The cards flag it for 2. Check every claim before paid use.
- **"Curiosity always wins."** Stated as a law, from examples only. Common testing advice is to test hooks against the brand's audience. The cards say the same.
- **Withhold vs show results.** He says withholding the result drives more views, then says the same format "also works for before and afters where you actually show the before and after". He does not say which to prefer.
- **1 video in 3 channels.** He does not cover usage rights, affiliate terms or ad approvals. The card flags it.
- **Survivor bias.** All 7 are winners he picked. No failed videos, no spend, no run time, no ROAS. We cannot tell if the sales came from organic, affiliate or paid.
- **Missing how.** No scripts beyond the quoted lines, no length guidance beyond "a few seconds" for 1 format, no editing, captions, sound or casting guidance, no testing plan, no metrics for judging a format.
- **Platform.** All examples are TikTok Shop. The card applies them to Reels; the source does not.
- **Card additions.** Several card bullets go past the transcript: "one take" and "continuous shot" (c7c5b8ef2ed7, 01058f4f2412), "real first-time participants", "2 to 3 ingredients", "visible likes and replies", the format selection guide (2eae62f9a017), and the bundle default path, replenishment and per-unit saving points (5aaabe53c66f). They are sensible but not the presenter's words.
- **Brand data needed.** Views, orders and revenue per video; creator rights; margin and CAC for bundle decisions; which formats the brand has already tested.
