## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator write, grade and rewrite the first 3 seconds of a paid social video ad for ecommerce, then carry the viewer from the hook into the body without a sudden sales pitch. It covers the purpose of a hook (filter for buyers, then retain), the 3 hook elements (audio, copy, visual), 1 plug-and-play template, 7 hook types with ecommerce fit notes, a 5-criteria grading order, how to multiply 1 hook into 20 to 30 variants, and the "product awareness curve" for bridging. It is strongest on hook scripting and critique. It is thin on measurement: it never defines hook rate, gives no thresholds, and has no test results.

## 2. Principles

1. **The hook is most of the ad.** Condition: video ads in paid social. The presenter says "the hook is 80% of performance" because "80% of people never make it past the hook". Evidence: his framing, not a measured figure. Use it as a reason to spend effort on hooks, not as a benchmark. [aaacbb3da60f]

2. **A hook has 2 jobs, in this order: attract the right audience, then keep them watching.** Condition: paid ads, not organic. Most people think the hook's job is only to stop the scroll. He says the first job is that the right people stop. "Whoever's in your hook needs to be the correct target demographic, and it needs to be relevant to what you're actually about to talk to." The second job is to get people past the first 3 seconds so the ad can sell. [aaacbb3da60f]

3. **Irrelevant hooks train Meta toward the wrong people.** Condition: paid delivery on Meta. His reasoning: Meta optimizes toward soft metrics "to a degree". If a group holds through the first 50% of the ad, Meta serves more people like them. If the hook is not relevant to the product, Meta finds the wrong people and "it destroys the performance of an ad". He says that after about 50 conversions in a campaign or ad set Meta "should stop doing that supposedly, but I think it still does". Evidence: his belief plus 1 anecdote (section 7.1). [aaacbb3da60f] [web check: how Meta weights engagement signals vs conversion events after the learning phase]

4. **Paid and organic have different economics.** Condition: when borrowing an organic viral format for ads. A broad, off-topic reel can be fine organically because reach is free: 10 leads from millions of free views is still a gain. Buying the same views costs money (his estimate: 2 to 10 million views through ads is "like half a million dollars"), so 10 leads would be a very bad result. Meme or off-topic openers work in paid only as rare exceptions. [aaacbb3da60f]

5. **Every rule has a small exception.** Condition: applies to all of his advice. He says nearly everything he teaches has "a 1 or 2% exception" in specific niches. His example: meme openers may make sense for gaming ads. [aaacbb3da60f] [20656fe8bb82]

6. **A hook has 3 elements: audio, copy, visual.** Condition: every hook, video or static. Audio is what is said (a function of the script), and sound alone can hook. Copy is the text on the creative itself: on a static, the text on the image; on a video, the headline or text overlay. It is not the ad's primary text or headline field, though it can match them. Visual is the scroll stopper: the footage or image. Most people only think about the visual. Credited to Mark Builds Brands. [98385b907b9b]

7. **A good hook can become a high performer by fixing 1 weak element.** Condition: a hook that is "doing okay". His example: adding exaggerated on-screen copy ("this transformed my life") to an existing hook doubled hook rate and doubled the creative's performance. Evidence: 1 anecdote, no numbers beyond "double". [98385b907b9b]

8. **A hook script is only part of the hook.** Condition: when handing written hooks to production. He warns that if a brand pastes his written hooks into an account without changing visuals, they may fail and the brand will wrongly blame the hook. "You can actually just change the visual in the background and get completely different performance." (He says "one of the four elements" here, though he defined 3; treat it as a slip.) [a535c8449dc1] [227f69979a61]

9. **Frameworks are a start, not the end state.** Condition: skill level. If you cannot yet tell a good hook from a bad one, templates are "a really good way to start getting one foot in front of the other". Later, move away from them because they become generic and everyone uses them. The goal is to write hooks from scratch and innovate on format to find white space against competitors. [20656fe8bb82]

10. **Common does not mean best, but do not ignore what competitors run.** Condition: choosing hook types. If all competitors use the same hook types, the market fills with similar-looking ads, so look for white space. He also says "you also as well do want to double down on what competitors are doing". He holds both; he does not say how to weigh them. [9197d49f4af4] [20656fe8bb82]

11. **Grade hooks in a fixed order: clarity, relevance, novelty, then specificity and credibility.** Condition: judging any hook. Clarity, relevance and novelty are first tier. Specificity and credibility are "second tier". Details in section 5.1. [6d6308f42500]

12. **Specific beats vague in hook copy.** Condition: when a claim can carry a concrete number or constraint that is true. "Found the same 2 problems" beats "found the same problems": the number feels short enough to sit through and raises the question of which 2. Evidence: his reasoning ("I bet there would probably be a worse hook rate"), no test. [0ac3b2659983]

13. **Do not sell in the hook.** Condition: video ads. The rewritten hooks do not mention the product and do not signal an ad. They open an educational piece (for example, how to check a protein snack label). [a535c8449dc1] [46035a697541]

14. **The speaker must match the persona called out.** Condition: any persona-led hook. "You can't have a 50-year-old mom saying, 'If you're a 20-year-old guy and you're struggling with X'". A mismatch removes relevance and trust. [21dee8ebaffe]

15. **Almost any product can be reframed toward a new audience pool.** Condition: when writing overlay copy for different pools, and only within claim rules. His electrolyte example pushes a strength angle ("how I doubled my strength") although he admits electrolytes have "very little, if not nothing at all, to do with increasing strength". The logic: dehydration made the person tired, fixing it got him back to the gym, the gym made him stronger. He then says "obviously probably don't go that far". See section 9 on claims. [46035a697541] [227f69979a61]

16. **Product awareness should rise slowly across a video.** Condition: video assets. He says statics are "a little bit different". Plot time on the x-axis and "product awareness" (the viewer knowing they are being sold to and that you have a product to buy) on the y-axis. Target: at 0 through the hook, then a slow creep up, peaking at the call to action. Do not "stepstone" (jump up at each section change). "Slowly bleed it into the ad" so the viewer moves from unaware of being sold to convinced without noticing the shift. Evidence: his mental model, not a measured metric. [0efe34c9febc]

17. **The hook means nothing if you cannot bridge into the body.** Condition: all video ads. He calls the bridge "one of the most common mistakes in people that are actually good at making ads": they know good hooks, bodies and CTAs but not how to join them without a large drop-off. Bridge smoothly into education, not into the product. [b7e6d1e3ad02]

18. **The last bridge, into the call to action, can sink a strong ad.** Condition: ads with a good hook and body. He says strong creative "flop[s] a lot of the time" because the ad jumps from education into buying with no reason given. [b7e6d1e3ad02]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Write a first hook with the 4-part template (Persona + Problem + Pattern Interrupt + Promise)
1. Persona: open by naming who the ad is for. This filters out everyone else. [21dee8ebaffe]
2. Problem: agitate the pain quickly, "within the first literally 2 seconds". [21dee8ebaffe]
3. Pattern interrupt: add something unexpected here. Once you name a persona and a problem, many viewers sense an ad is coming and leave; the interrupt keeps them. He admits his own example interrupt was weak; the source gives no concrete list of interrupt devices. [21dee8ebaffe]
4. Promise: give a reason to keep watching. [21dee8ebaffe]
5. Fill the sentence: "If you're [persona] and you have [pain point], you're probably doing [common approach] right now. That's why you're getting [bad outcome]. Here's the better method." He says "I can almost guarantee if you take this, put in a bunch of ads, it'll probably perform well." Treat as his confidence, not a result. [21dee8ebaffe]
6. Cast a speaker who plausibly is the persona. [21dee8ebaffe] [needs brand data: who the core buyer personas are, and which creators match them]
7. To make volume: paste the template sentence into an LLM (he names ChatGPT and Claude) with the website, the product and some context, and ask for many versions. [21dee8ebaffe]
8. Then plan the visual and on-screen copy separately (section 3.3). [a535c8449dc1]

The same structure is common in organic YouTube openers. His own example: "If you're an e-commerce brand and you're currently struggling with creative content, I'm going to give you the best method possible for creating hooks. This will be a 20-minute video where I break down all the frameworks." [21dee8ebaffe]

### 3.2 Audit and rewrite a live hook (the order he follows on screen)
1. Watch the hook. Name which of the 3 elements is doing the work (audio, copy or visual). [a535c8449dc1] [98385b907b9b]
2. Ask the first-job question: does this hook attract the right audience, or anyone who is curious? [a535c8449dc1]
3. Score it on clarity, relevance, novelty, specificity, credibility, in that order (section 5.1). [6d6308f42500]
4. Watch a few more seconds of the body for context: what does the product claim, and is there any emotion or only value propositions? [a535c8449dc1]
5. Pick a hook type from section 3.4 that suits the product. He picks high-stakes warning for jerky and the 4-part template for electrolytes. [a535c8449dc1] [46035a697541]
6. Rewrite the audio or script. Point it at an educational payoff, not at the product. [a535c8449dc1]
7. Write 2 to 3 versions. For high-stakes warning he suggests swapping the warning to reach different pools of people. [9197d49f4af4]
8. Plan the visual for each version (section 3.3). [227f69979a61]
9. Plan the on-screen copy for each version: plain captions of what is said, or a bold separate headline. [98385b907b9b] [227f69979a61]
10. Plan the bridge from hook to body and from body to CTA (section 3.5). [b7e6d1e3ad02]

Card [20656fe8bb82] summarizes this as the model workflow: score, pick a type, rewrite audio, then plan visual and overlay copy separately.

### 3.3 Turn 1 hook into 20 to 30 variants
1. Start from a strong audio line (his example: a UGC creator saying "We taste tested the bestselling jerkys and found the same 2 problems in almost all of them"). [227f69979a61]
2. Vary only the visual. Version A: plain talking head. Version B: the creator says "We taste tested", then at about the 1 second mark cut to a pan shot of someone in a supermarket grabbing all the jerky off the shelves and taking it through checkout, then cut back to the creator saying "found 2 problems in almost all of them" and pulling out a whiteboard with "1" and "2" blacked out, about to write. He expects B to beat A: "probably going to perform worse" for the plain version. Test to confirm. [227f69979a61] [0ac3b2659983]
3. Keep visuals fast, dynamic and tied to what is being said. Options he lists: camera moving fast, dynamic cuts; a 1 to 2 second AI video of jerky cracking like cardboard (if it fits the brand positioning); a pan through competitor ingredient lists; throwing competitor products in the bin; showing 10 to 20 jerky packs on screen for the taste-test claim. [98385b907b9b] [227f69979a61] [0ac3b2659983]
4. Vary the on-screen copy separately. Try bold static headlines over the top at different levels of severity so each speaks to a different pool. Check health-claim rules first where they apply. [227f69979a61] [46035a697541] [web check: Meta advertising policy on health and personal attribute claims]
5. Vary the audio separately. Combining changes in all 3 elements gives "20 to 30 different hooks" from 1 concept. [227f69979a61] [98385b907b9b]
6. When a hook is flat, find its weakest element and change that one (card's reading of his "one of these three elements just needs to get tweaked"). [98385b907b9b]

The source does not say how to structure the test (ad set layout, spend per variant, how long to run, what metric picks the winner). [needs brand data: hook rate and hold rate per variant from the ad account]

### 3.4 Choose a hook type
He says there are infinite hook types; these are the ones he uses most "both for our agency ads, but also for e-commerce brands". His recap says "about 8", but the source gives 7. [9197d49f4af4]

| Hook type | His example line | Ecommerce fit per source | Card |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contrarian truth | "The advice that everyone follows is backwards because..." then "you've tried this, but it doesn't work because of this", then education | Works in paid and "really well on organic" | [9197d49f4af4] |
| Specific proof | "We did X and got Y in Z days. Here's what actually caused it." | "A great high performer" | [9197d49f4af4] |
| Common mistakes | "The number one mistake I see brands making when they try to scale." | He still uses it but is "not a huge fan": overused, "lost its weighting"; viewers know it is a hook and doubt it is the number 1 mistake | [9197d49f4af4] [20656fe8bb82] |
| High-stakes warning | "If you're using protein powder, you're slowly destroying your gut. Here's why." | He uses it "a lot in ecom". Persona is called out in the first words, then a warning. Write 2 to 3 versions with different warnings to reach different pools | [9197d49f4af4] |
| Curiosity gap | "Meta now treats these two ads as the same. Most people don't realize the consequence of it." | Key in organic, translates to paid. Put the gap in the hook, thumbnail and copy. For statics, see principle notes below | [9197d49f4af4] |
| Tactical promise | "In 90 seconds, I'll give you the exact checklist I use to write hooks that hold attention." | "Doesn't really translate into ecom very well"; better for service businesses | [9197d49f4af4] |
| Mythbusting objection | "If you think agencies can't be trusted, you're not wrong. Here's how to tell if yours is the exception." | "Works quite well in ecom". Bust myths about your product or category | [9197d49f4af4] |

Curiosity-gap statics: he says the best statics "by far" on click-through rate are infomercial-style images that explain nothing, for example "The number 1 issue as to why you have gut pain" with an arrow to a man holding his stomach. The downside is "a lot of junk click-through rates" from people not ready to buy. So send that traffic to a listicle or a long educational landing page that does not mention the product until the end, then pushes to another page that sells. He calls it "very old school" but says it "still works incredibly well". [9197d49f4af4] [needs brand data: whether the brand has an advertorial or listicle page to send this traffic to]

### 3.5 Script the bridge and the awareness curve
1. Keep product awareness at 0 through the hook: no product name, no product on screen, no sign of an ad. [0efe34c9febc] [46035a697541]
2. After the hook, move into education the viewer values, not into the product or offer. [b7e6d1e3ad02]
3. If you introduce the product in the body, do it briefly and go straight back to education. A small bump followed by more teaching is acceptable (his read of the Hyro sponge ad). [0efe34c9febc] [b7e6d1e3ad02]
4. Keep the bridge visually engaging. His example: the speaker keeps moving a sponge "for no reason" as a visual cue to hold attention. [b7e6d1e3ad02]
5. Bring the product in late, after the viewer has seen alternatives fail. [46035a697541]
6. Plan the bridge from body to CTA on purpose: give the viewer a reason to move from learning to buying. Do not jump from education straight to the offer. [b7e6d1e3ad02]
7. Check the script second by second: "does the viewer know yet that this is an ad for a specific product?" If yes before the CTA, pull back and teach. (Card's check, built on his model.) [0efe34c9febc]
8. After launch, look for drop-offs on the retention curve at the bridges. He says the bridge "is where you can lose a lot of people, particularly on your retention curves". The card adds: a drop right after the hook or right before the CTA points to the bridge. [b7e6d1e3ad02] [needs brand data: video retention curves per ad]

### 3.6 Full script flow (his electrolyte rewrite, as a model)
He says this is "what the flow should look like from hook through to call to action". [46035a697541]
1. Persona and problem: "If you love to go to the gym and your workouts are getting shorter and shorter..."
2. Failed common approaches: tried natural testosterone boosters, tried eating more before the gym, "tried all of the common approaches", and that is why you are "slowly getting less and less motivated".
3. Discovery, as education: "I was dehydrated." Muscle force was lower because of a sodium imbalance. Explain the chemical imbalance behind the pains the viewer shares; do not stop at "I was dehydrated, here is the product".
4. Alternatives rejected: tried many electrolyte powders, "Hydrolyte didn't work", they lacked enough sodium and the right magnesium balance.
5. Product: "when I finally found Hyro, I actually felt better."
6. Outcome: back in the gym 1.5 to 2 hours per session, 3 to 4 times a week.
7. Offer: "I reached out to the Hyro team", they partnered, "you can get 20% off".
Visual: no product in the hook. Either an authentic iPhone talking head ("I've been feeling really lethargic recently") or dynamic B-roll of the person feeling bad. He admits he does not know exactly what that B-roll would look like. Overlay options: "thought I had a testosterone issue", "how I doubled my strength", tiered by severity and checked against health-claim rules. [46035a697541] [227f69979a61]

All claims in this script are invented for illustration. See section 9.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

### 5.1 Hook grading rubric (in order of importance)
| Order | Criterion | Question | Tier | Card |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Clarity | Can a stranger understand what the product is and what is going on in the creative? | First | [6d6308f42500] |
| 2 | Relevance | Is the pain point obvious? Can the viewer relate the product to themselves? | First | [6d6308f42500] |
| 3 | Novelty | Is there a twist, a new angle, or a format no one has done (white space)? | First | [6d6308f42500] |
| 4 | Specificity | Is the hook concrete about what it calls out? "The least important of all of them." | Second | [6d6308f42500] |
| 5 | Credibility | Is there proof or a reason to believe? Hard to fit in 3 to 4 seconds. Get it free by having an influencer or a famous person deliver a normal hook. | Second | [6d6308f42500] |

Bad: low clarity and low relevance. That hook will not filter for buyers however entertaining it is. The source gives no scores or pass marks; the card adds that a clear, relevant hook with low novelty may still run. [6d6308f42500]

## 7. Examples and case results

All are examples, not promises. None has test numbers except where stated.

7.1 **Snow-removal organic reel (bad for paid).** An educational reel about how sailors escape a failing submarine cuts into an ad for Abraham Landscape Group, a commercial snow and ice management company. He thinks it got "a few million views in a very short period". He was served it in Australia, where he says it does not snow and he needs no ice removal, because he watches that kind of educational content. Lesson: free organic reach can tolerate it; paid spend on it would buy views from non-buyers. [aaacbb3da60f]

7.2 **Exaggerated overlay.** An okay hook got "this transformed my life" as on-screen copy; hook rate and performance doubled. Unnamed brand. [98385b907b9b]

7.3 **Jerky ad (brand "Nothing", per the transcript).** Opens with a table being hit and the product eaten, a sound-led curiosity gap. Scores: clarity low, relevance low, novelty possible ("I haven't really seen many people do a hook like this"), specificity no, credibility no. Everyone watches to learn what it is, including vegetarians and people who do not like jerky, then most leave. Body: "It's called Nothing cuz it's got no processed ingredients in it. It's grass-fed beef, couple herbs and spices, and that's it." Value propositions, no emotion. [a535c8449dc1]
   Rewrites given:
   - Reuse the protein powder warning: eat jerky instead of protein powder because protein powder harms the gut, then a point on bioavailability (he hedges: "or something"). [a535c8449dc1]
   - "If most jerky is either dry as cardboard or loaded with rubbish ingredients, we fix both, and I'll show you how." [a535c8449dc1]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Treating the hook as a scroll stopper only, not as a buyer filter. [aaacbb3da60f]
2. Meme or off-topic viral openers in paid ads (outside rare niches like gaming). [aaacbb3da60f]
3. Copying organic viral formats into paid without the relevance filter. [aaacbb3da60f]
4. Thinking only about the visual and ignoring audio and on-screen copy. [98385b907b9b]
5. On-screen copy that only captions the speech when a headline could pull people in. [98385b907b9b] [46035a697541]
6. A speaker who does not match the persona called out. [21dee8ebaffe]
7. Staying on frameworks forever, so hooks look like everyone else's. [20656fe8bb82]
8. Leaning on overused types like "the number 1 mistake". [9197d49f4af4]
9. Curiosity gaps that attract anyone curious (the jerky hook). [a535c8449dc1]
10. Curiosity-gap statics sent to a product page: junk clicks, few buyers. [9197d49f4af4]
11. Vague quantities ("some problems") where a true number fits. [0ac3b2659983]
12. Naming or showing the product in the hook. [46035a697541] [0efe34c9febc]
13. Bodies that list value propositions with no emotion. [a535c8449dc1]
14. Testing a written hook with no visual rework, then blaming the hook. [a535c8449dc1]
15. Stopping after writing the hook and skipping the bridge ("this is where people stop"). [b7e6d1e3ad02]
16. Going from hook straight into the pitch. "This is the biggest mistake that people make." [b7e6d1e3ad02]
17. Stepstoning product awareness up at each section change. [0efe34c9febc]
18. A good hook and body with a bad bridge into the CTA. [b7e6d1e3ad02]
19. Pushing overlay claims past what health rules allow. [46035a697541] [227f69979a61]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Numbers are framing, not data.** "80% of performance", "80% never pass the hook", the 2x overlay lift and "20 to 30 variants" have no study or account data behind them. [aaacbb3da60f] [98385b907b9b]
- **Meta mechanics are hearsay.** He says Meta expands to people who hold through 50% of the ad, and doubts the about-50-conversions cutoff himself. [web check: Meta learning phase and optimization signals]
- **Internal tension on curiosity.** He says hooks must filter for buyers and faults the jerky curiosity hook, yet lists curiosity gap as a key type and calls info-gap statics the best CTR statics "by far". His resolution for statics is the landing page (listicle or long educational page), which accepts junk clicks. For video he gives no rule on when a curiosity gap is filtered enough. Keep both views.
- **Internal tension on competitors.** Find white space, but also "double down on what competitors are doing". No rule for which wins. [9197d49f4af4] [20656fe8bb82]
- **Conflict with common ecommerce advice.** Much creative guidance, including Meta's own, says to show the product or brand in the first seconds [web check: current Meta creative best practices on early branding]. This source says keep product awareness at 0 through the hook and bring the product in late. The source's reason is audience quality and retention; it gives no test proving it. Test both. [needs brand data: hook rate, hold rate and purchase rate for product-first vs product-late hooks]
- **Claims risk.** His electrolyte script invents claims (sodium imbalance lowering muscle force, 1.5 to 2 hour sessions) and a strength overlay he admits is unrelated. Card [46035a697541] calls the strength framing a messaging angle, not a causal claim. Under ecomOS rules no invented claims are allowed, so use only claims the brand can back. [needs brand data: substantiated claims and regulatory limits per product] [web check: Meta health and personal attributes policy; local ad rules for supplements, for example ASCI in India]
- **Specificity must be true.** He does not address substantiation. "2 problems" works only if there really are 2. [0ac3b2659983]
- **Statics.** He says the awareness curve and bridge apply to video; statics are "a little bit different" and he does not say how. [0efe34c9febc] [b7e6d1e3ad02]
- **Thin on how:** no list of pattern interrupt devices (his own example was weak by his admission); no definition of hook rate or hold rate; no testing setup, budgets, sample sizes or winner criteria; no guidance on hook length beyond "first 3 seconds"; no guidance on sound-off viewing; no platform differences (TikTok, YouTube) beyond Meta.
- **Card additions beyond the transcript.** [6d6308f42500] adds "fix the lowest-scoring criterion" and "a clear, relevant, low-novelty hook may still run". [b7e6d1e3ad02] adds "a drop right after the hook or right before the CTA means the bridge is the problem". [0efe34c9febc] adds the second-by-second self-check. [a535c8449dc1] labels the "dry as cardboard" rewrite "problem + promise". These are reasonable readings, not the presenter's words.
- **Organic transfer.** He says principles move between organic and ads in both directions, but also that organic tolerates broad hooks that paid does not. Apply organic ideas with the buyer filter.
