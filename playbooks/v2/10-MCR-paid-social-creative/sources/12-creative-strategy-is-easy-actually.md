## 1. What this source is for

It gives a creative strategist a shared vocabulary to break any paid social ad into parts: 7 messaging parts (segment, concept, angle, mechanism, copy blocks, hook, format) and, for video, 5 creative parts (CAST: conceit, actor, style, terrain, setup). With that vocabulary the operator can (1) diagnose why an ad works or fails, (2) rotate single parts to make iterations, (3) spot gaps against competitors, (4) pull ideas from 7 sources (STORMING), and (5) choose what to test first under a fixed production and spend limit. It also gives a 4-step hook method and a short production workflow. It is a framework talk; it is thin on how to measure, how many ads to run, and how to read results. All worked examples are health and supplement direct-response ads, so transfer to other categories (for example jewelry) is untested here.

## 2. Principles

1. **The job is to make winning ads and funnels, with enough diversity that the algorithm serves them separately.** Condition: paid social acquisition. Source lists the parts of the job: persuasive and fun ads aimed at the right people; deep market knowledge; staying on top of winning formats and styles; watching patterns, winners and gaps; making strategic bets; writing strong hooks with direct-response basics. Evidence: opinion. The "algorithm serves them separately" claim is not explained [web check: how Meta's current delivery system treats similar vs distinct creatives]. [1b2a2554a0c8] [d3000970b47e]
2. **Every ad has 2 layers: messaging (what you say) and creative (what it looks like).** Messaging splits into WHO (segment), WHAT (concept, angle, mechanism, copy blocks) and HOW (hook, format). Creative is the video or image. Source says these are "the most important building blocks", not all of them; other parts exist. [1b2a2554a0c8] [2f0b793e3ea7]
3. **For video, describe the creative with CAST.** Conceit (the media format the ad pretends to be), Actor (who or what appears, including personified objects), Style (visual gloss: realistic, claymation, felt, Pixar), Terrain (environment and setting), Setup (big production elements: captions, B-roll, inserts, cutaways, split screen, hook stacking). Condition: video only. A conceit is optional; if the ad does not imitate any media format, leave it blank. Source says the grammar "comes pretty dang close" to describing an ad but not all of it. [765ed4cbcc9c] [10b56ec55756]
4. **Shared grammar makes pattern detection possible, for you and for AI.** Condition: when you tag ads the same way across your account, competitor accounts and the outside world (organic content, forums, comments, reviews). Then you can see whether a format, style, concept or mechanism is the thing that is working. Evidence: opinion. [b6e4c6832826]
5. **A segment is a slice of a market, built from 3 dimensions: outcome x demographic x facet.** Outcome: the core thing they want or do not want. Demographic: gender, race, age, buyer. Facet: a specific state, event or condition (smoker, on finasteride, considering TRT, vegetarian, vegan, runner, GLP-1 user, diabetic). Multiply as few or as many as you want; go broad or narrow. The source does not say which granularity is better. [0682f4fbff61]
6. **Most of the biggest gains come from new outcomes and sub-outcomes, and from multiplying facets.** Source calls this "horizontal scaling": target those people and write ads specifically for them. Evidence: opinion, no numbers. [0682f4fbff61] [d3000970b47e]
7. **Concept, angle and mechanism are different things; get each clear before writing hooks.** Concept is the core idea (example: too much vitamin D is bad for you). Angle is how you present it (a conspiracy about the vitamin D industry, a whistleblower, or 2 people with and without vitamin D). Mechanism is how the product works. Source: knowing the concept is "really important" to write hooks and to see which ideas resonate. [8cfd832f085a]
8. **A mechanism has 2 parts: the unique mechanism of the problem (why do I have this problem?) and of the solution (why is your product right for it?).** Best mechanisms are simple, intuitive and new. Typically 1 problem and 1 solution, but not always (the ED example uses "5 science-backed ingredients"). The mechanism gets stronger the more it forms one story with the founder, the product creation story, unique features and ingredients, unique characterizations and unique moments in time. [8cfd832f085a] [2f0b793e3ea7]
9. **Copy blocks are a persuasion checklist: pain, promise, proof, curiosity, constraints, conditions.** Source calls them Lego pieces. "The more that you have in your ads and the stronger that these are, typically the better your ads will be." Condition: "typically", not always. The source does not define constraints or conditions here; it points to another video. [bacba7a80164]
10. **The hook is "probably 90% of the messaging".** Source's opinion, no data. It drives the production order: hook first, then the rest. [85ea7dc45ab8] [3fb8f0a02cc8]
11. **Format is the structure you use to deliver the idea, and formats trend.** Named formats: whistleblower, timeline ad, personification (a body part or object that talks), Wall Street Journal style (compare 2 people and their paths). A winning concept or angle can be put into a winning format and a winning style to make ads fast. [10b56ec55756]
12. **Creatives come in 3 types: branded, template and native.** Native ads look like realistic images; templates follow pre-set template types. These "can be broken down into" bottom, middle and top of funnel. The source gives no mapping of type to funnel stage and says it has no examples to show. [6f2084b2f993]
13. **Find gaps by comparing your ad grammar to competitors and to customer language.** A gap is something others run that you do not (for example a competitor runs many timeline ads and you run none; a competitor uses a new style), or a segment buried in your ad comments or Amazon reviews. You only see gaps if you can break ads into parts. Look at patterns in winners and in losers. [1daabb35866f] [b6e4c6832826]
14. **Taste and instinct are trainable and are the most important skill, "especially in the age of AI".** Source says it builds from understanding ads deeply and making tests and hypotheses; it works "beneath language". Analogy: chicken sexers, the book Blink, and a Shopify CEO interview. These are analogies, not evidence. Source insists it is "not a mystical process" but "mechanical". [29b3a1123b39]
15. **Diversify idea sources to raise hit rate.** If ideas come only from competitors, your own head or proven templates, the idea pool narrows and hit rate drops. Use STORMING (section 3.4). Gambits (wild cards) have a low hit rate "but they can be bangers if you execute them correctly". [36945e14679a]
16. **The hard part is choosing what to test first, not finding ideas.** Condition: production and spend are always limited (editor capacity, and "how many ads can you actually test"), even when AI raises output. Source says there is no one-size-fits-all answer. [d3000970b47e]
17. **Default high-leverage bets build on winning ideas.** Source's list: new conceits or formats on winning ideas; new facets and sub-outcomes on winning ideas; new styles on winning ideas; winning styles applied across past winning ideas. "The simpler and easier you make it for yourself, the better. Normally, the easiest answer is the right one." Stated as "usually", "in general". [d3000970b47e]
18. **Most people drop ideas earlier than the market does.** Condition: when an idea has worked. Keep building on it longer. Opinion, no fatigue data given. [d3000970b47e]
19. **Most ads fail; keep going.** Source gives no win rate. Refine decisions with data and judgment several times per week. [6d03d8961586]
20. **Enjoy the work.** Source believes strategists who enjoy the work get the best outcomes. Opinion, not a finding. [6d03d8961586]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Break down an ad (the "DNA" sheet)
Goal: describe an ad well enough to say which parts might be driving results.
1. Write the segment (who). Use outcome x demographic x facet. [1b2a2554a0c8] [0682f4fbff61]
2. Write the concept in 1 plain sentence. [8cfd832f085a]
3. Name the angle (how the concept is presented). [8cfd832f085a]
4. Write the mechanism: problem cause and solution cause. [8cfd832f085a]
5. Mark each copy block (pain, promise, proof, curiosity, constraints, conditions) as present and strong, present but weak, or missing. [bacba7a80164]
6. Quote the hook. [85ea7dc45ab8]
7. Name the format (whistleblower, timeline, personification, WSJ style, confessional, or other). [10b56ec55756]
8. For video, fill CAST: conceit (or "none"), actor(s), style, terrain, setup elements. [765ed4cbcc9c]
9. Ask: if this ad works, is it 1 element or the combination of elements? Write the hypothesis. [1b2a2554a0c8] [b6e4c6832826]
10. Card addition, not in the source: run this on your top 5 and bottom 5 ads to start. [1b2a2554a0c8] [needs brand data: ad-level results to pick top and bottom ads]

### 3.2 Build segments (horizontal scaling)
1. List outcomes the product serves: wants and do-not-wants, then sub-outcomes. Source example for a testosterone product: better sex, getting rid of man boobs, losing weight, better erections. [0682f4fbff61]
2. List demographics: gender, race, age, buyer. [0682f4fbff61]
3. List facets: states, events, conditions (smoker, on finasteride, considering TRT, vegetarian, vegan, runner, GLP-1, diabetic). [0682f4fbff61]
4. Multiply them into segments at the granularity you want. Source examples: "all men who just want to have better sex", "all men who want to get rid of man boobs", "all black men struggling with erections", "all men over 50 on finasteride who want to lose weight". [0682f4fbff61]
5. Write ads specifically for each segment. Source says this is where most of the biggest gains are. [0682f4fbff61]
6. Mine new segments from ad comments and Amazon reviews. [1daabb35866f] [needs brand data: ad comments, product reviews, customer survey answers]
Note: race and health conditions in ad copy can break platform personal-attribute and health policies. Source does not mention this. [web check: Meta personal attributes and health ad policy]

### 3.3 Write a hook (isolate, commit, deep fry, make it bang)
1. Isolate the idea. Start from the product and its mechanism. Example: a magnesium cream that is better than capsules for pain. Ask why it is better: it absorbs through the skin. [85ea7dc45ab8]
2. Commit to that idea. [85ea7dc45ab8]
3. Deep fry it: add contrast, surprise, novelty, nuance and stakes. Look for what you know about the product that can be stretched. Example: the magnesium has no attached molecule, so it is "bare", so "naked magnesium". [85ea7dc45ab8]
4. Make it bang (the wording): emotional trigger words, simple language, best information first, open loops. Example: "My grandma used this weird naked magnesium trick that fixed her back pain in 2 weeks." Contrast: grandma vs naked. Emotional words: weird, naked. Open loop: "trick". [85ea7dc45ab8]
5. Source: this example is not claimed to work; it shows the process only. [85ea7dc45ab8]
6. Check the claim inside the hook ("fixed her back pain in 2 weeks") against what the brand can prove. Source does not cover this. [needs brand data: substantiated claims]

### 3.4 Generate ideas with STORMING
Use all 7 sources so the pipeline is not built from 1 place. [36945e14679a]
1. **S**wipes: ideas from competitor ads.
2. **T**emplates: proven templates or formats.
3. **O**rganic research: organic content, comments, Amazon reviews, Facebook comments, forums, new research and papers.
4. **M**atrix: list all possible segments and all possible formats; find the missing combinations (gaps).
5. **I**nternal vectors: patterns in your own account.
6. **N**ew styles: trending looks (Pixar, claymation).
7. **G**ambit: a guess, shower thought or wild card. Low hit rate.
Card addition, not in the source: record which source each test idea came from so you can see whether sourcing is really diverse. [36945e14679a]

### 3.5 Find gaps (segment x format matrix)
1. List your possible segments and possible formats and styles. [1daabb35866f]
2. Mark which cells you have run and which competitors run. [1daabb35866f] [needs brand data: own ad history tagged by segment, format, style]
3. Flag gaps: formats, styles or conceits competitors use and you do not; segments in comments and reviews you have not written for. [1daabb35866f]
4. Feed gaps into the bet choice (3.6). Source gives no number of gap tests per batch. [1daabb35866f]

### 3.6 Choose what to test (strategic bets)
1. State your limit: ads per week the editors can make and how many the spend can test. Source uses 10 and 50 per week as illustration, not benchmarks. [d3000970b47e] [needs brand data: weekly production capacity and test budget]
2. Answer the batch questions: iterate a winner or make net new? Which idea sources? Which ideas first? More proven or more wild card? New segments or deepen a segment? New styles, formats or conceits? [d3000970b47e]
3. Default toward: new conceits or formats on winning ideas; new facets and sub-outcomes on winning ideas; new styles on winning ideas; winning styles across past winning ideas. [d3000970b47e]
4. Use taste and instinct at this step; source says this is where they matter most. [29b3a1123b39]
5. Do not drop an idea that works too early. [d3000970b47e]

### 3.7 Rotate elements to iterate a winner
1. List the winner's messaging and CAST parts (3.1). [765ed4cbcc9c] [b7618be5460c]
2. Guess the active part: concept, actor, or the combination. [b7618be5460c]
3. If it is the actor (source example: the high-end escort), make more ads with that actor and rotate other parts. [765ed4cbcc9c]
4. If it is the concept ("men cheat because their wife stops glowing"), keep it and rotate the rest. [765ed4cbcc9c]
5. If it is concept plus actor together, keep both and change angle, mechanism, hook, or the conceit and format: a podcast, the actor speaking on stage, a street interview, a slice-of-life film. [b7618be5460c] [10b56ec55756]
6. Brief the editor with the winning reference plus the changed part. [765ed4cbcc9c] [3fb8f0a02cc8]
Note: 2 cards say change exactly 1 element per variant and label it; the source's own example changes several at once. See section 9. [b7618be5460c]

### 3.8 Produce the ad
1. Pick the idea from the bet list. [3fb8f0a02cc8]
2. Write the hook or hooks first. [3fb8f0a02cc8]
3. Draft the rest with AI (source uses its own Genesis bots and Exodus). [3fb8f0a02cc8]
4. Edit by hand the major points, mainly the mechanism and copy blocks. Source: "if your hook is right and you have a good AI writing process, the rest of the ad writes itself". Opinion. [3fb8f0a02cc8]
5. Make statics in the AI tool; send video to a human editor with a winning reference. [3fb8f0a02cc8]
6. Review AI drafts for claims and brand voice before launch (card caveat, not in source). [3fb8f0a02cc8]

### 3.9 Refinement loop
1. Several times per week, look at your account, competitor accounts and the market. [6d03d8961586]
2. For each new ad ask: why is it working, kind of working, or not working? Form a hypothesis and test it. [b6e4c6832826]
3. Let data and judgment reset priorities. Expect most ads to fail. [6d03d8961586]
Source does not say which metrics decide "working", how long to wait, or how much spend is enough to judge. [needs brand data: account's win definition, e.g. spend and ROAS or CPA threshold]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Pulling ideas only from competitors, your own head or proven templates. It narrows ideas and lowers hit rate. [36945e14679a]
2. Moving away from a winning idea earlier than the market does. [d3000970b47e]
3. Overcomplicating bet choice; "the easiest answer is the right one" normally. [d3000970b47e]
4. Not being able to break ads into parts, so gaps and patterns stay invisible. [1daabb35866f] [b6e4c6832826]
5. Treating most ads failing as a reason to stop. [6d03d8961586]
6. Treating taste as mystical instead of building it through repeated analysis and hypotheses. [29b3a1123b39]
7. Weak or missing copy blocks, especially proof (both dissected ads had weak blocks). [bacba7a80164]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Sales pitch.** The video is a funnel into Genesis, Exodus and an email list. Earnings and win claims are unaudited. The document header tells readers to skip these parts.
- **No performance evidence.** The 2 dissected ads are never shown to be winners. Card 2f0b793e3ea7 calls them "example ads from the presenter's accounts"; the source does not say that.
- **Cards add advice the source did not give.** Treat these as card suggestions: top 5 and bottom 5 audit [1b2a2554a0c8]; native for top of funnel, branded for bottom [6f2084b2f993]; fix weak proof with testimonials, demo splices or ingredient data [bacba7a80164]; "change the format first" [10b56ec55756]; mix proven cells into gap batches [1daabb35866f]; track idea source per test [36945e14679a]; exactly 1 element per variant [b7618be5460c].
- **1 element vs many.** Card b7618be5460c says vary exactly 1 element per test for clean attribution. The source's own rotation example keeps concept and actor and changes angle, mechanism, hook and format together. The source never states a 1-variable rule. This also differs from common one-variable A/B testing advice; the source is about creative iteration speed, not clean attribution.
- **Algorithm diversity claim** is stated, not explained. [web check: current Meta guidance on creative diversity and similar-ad grouping]
- **Policy risk the source ignores.** Segments by race and health condition ("all black men struggling with erections", "diabetic men with ED") and copy that implies personal attributes can break Meta's personal attributes and health policies. Pretend formats (fake interview, WSJ style, whistleblower) and actor roles (escort) raise authenticity, disclosure and brand-safety issues. Health claims like "fixed her back pain in 2 weeks" need substantiation. [web check: Meta advertising standards on personal attributes, health claims and misleading formats]
- **Category fit.** All examples are supplements and health. For ecomOS brands (Gehna jewelry, Peach Bucket childproofing) the grammar transfers, but mechanism, whistleblower and facet-heavy DR styles may not fit the brand voice. [needs brand data: brand voice and category norms]
- **Missing how.** No definitions for constraints and conditions blocks; no metrics or thresholds for "winning"; no test budget, test length or sample size; no ads-per-week target; no platform setup; no funnel mapping for the 3 creative types; no static ad guidance beyond "make statics in Exodus".
- **Taste.** Source ranks taste as the most important skill, supported only by analogies. Taste can encode bias; keep testing. [29b3a1123b39]
- **Hook 90%** is opinion. Common advice agrees hooks matter most, but no source here measures it.
