## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist plan, brief, review and judge static image ads for Meta, mainly for cold (top of funnel) traffic. It gives: why statics are high leverage (cost, gross profit per ad, testing ground for video), the 3 jobs of a static, the clarity vs curiosity spectrum, visual hierarchy levers, the concept definition (persona, angle, offer, optional format), what counts as a real variation under Andromeda, the Schwartz stages of awareness applied to statics, a format-by-funnel map, a 6-step production pipeline with AI options, 2 creative volume forecasting models, and why last-click attribution undervalues top of funnel statics. It closes with many live ad breakdowns. It is strong on judgment and review. It is thin on design execution steps, copy templates, testing setup in Ads Manager, and measured results.

## 2. Principles

1. **Statics are the most undervalued, highest leverage creative type in a Meta account.** Reasons he gives: lowest CPMs of any creative type, cheapest and fastest to make, fastest way to test new angles, concepts and offers. He says statics "pretty much always beat videos unless there's a compliance issue". Condition: this is his account-level claim with no data shown. Card caveat: may vary by account and platform. [00c2e103130f] [b0132ad73a68] `[needs brand data: CPM by creative type in the brand's own account]`
2. **Production is about 4 times cheaper than video.** In the time and cost of 1 good 60 to 90 second video you could make about 4 statics, or 3 great statics if you spend a bit more time on quality. AI amplifies this: AI can help build, concept and ideate, so statics can cost "like a dollar a static or less". Condition: his estimate, not a benchmark. [00c2e103130f] [b0132ad73a68]
3. **Compare creative types by expected gross profit per ad, not ROAS alone.** Expected gross profit per ad = average revenue per ad minus average ad spend per ad minus production cost per ad. Because statics cost less to make, they return more gross profit per ad when revenue and return are equal, and the gap widens as return drops. Worked numbers in section 7. [602ee9ba7290] [b0132ad73a68]
4. **Statics are a testing ground for video.** Test new angles, concepts, personas and offers in a static first. If one performs, roll it into a higher production shoot. This is "particularly helpful in offer testing": split test offers on warm audiences directly with statics, find which converts better and what gross profit it returns. Condition: offer testing on warm audiences is fine even if your statics sit at the bottom or middle of the funnel. [00c2e103130f] [b0132ad73a68]
5. **Clarity beats design under Andromeda.** "With the introduction of Andromeda, where creative does the targeting, you want clarity over design. The design of a static ad really doesn't matter." What matters is quick clarity on the message, the problem being agitated, and the solution. He says some of his best examples look ugly but have spent a quarter million dollars. Conflict: he later says design matters for communicating the message and can destroy authority (principle 12). See section 9. [f8a4e5a25281] [3db510f74584]
6. **3 reasons everyone fails with statics.** (a) They only run bottom of funnel statics, because it is hard to move someone through all stages of awareness in 1 1080x1080 image with few words, so people take the easy path of retargeting. (b) Most statics in most accounts are the same concept with design variations; a new design is not a new concept and does not reach new audiences. (c) They judge statics by last-click attribution and do not use landing pages for top of funnel statics. [2e9ac19f8685] [8c915d03411c] [b3058b60c97e]
7. **Formats matter least.** "Formats fundamentally don't matter. It's the least important of the components as to what creates a good static ad." He refuses to teach format lists as the core lesson. Condition: he still gives a format map (section 3.5) and says format innovation gives temporary arbitrage (principle 11). [1190d2930cfd] [65acf3feb958]
8. **A static has 3 jobs: stop the scroll, communicate the message, drive action.** Each job resolves into 1 of 2 routes. [a0ce2d2f7363]
   - Stop the scroll: either feel native to the platform so people do not realise it is an ad, or be clickbaity and non-native so it is a pattern interrupt.
   - Communicate the message: normally the angle, the offer and who it is for, with few words and little room. "This is where creativity needs to get leveraged the most", and where design elements "actually become important". If the image stays native and light on information, use the primary text instead.
   - Drive action: a clickbaity or advertorial static drives action by hinting at an article or blog behind the click. A native static drives action through long-form copy that pulls the reader through the stages of awareness.
9. **Familiarity is a hook.** Familiar organic formats, such as the notepad format or the text message format, stop the scroll and give a vehicle for the message. This links to native statics. [a0ce2d2f7363]
10. **Copying formats has little edge unless you are first.** You can copy formats from competitors and other industries all day, but it "won't have a huge edge unless you're first to market". [a0ce2d2f7363]
11. **Format innovation gives a temporary arbitrage.** If you create a new format that works, you get a temporary advantage. [a0ce2d2f7363] [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
12. **Design can lose authority.** In the creatine ad, a bold specific percentage at the top built clinical-sounding authority, then poor spacing and an off-left product made it feel clunky and "any authority that was built up the top here using specificity is now lost just through design". "You can lose framing, you can lose authority through bad design." [3db510f74584]
13. **Primary text and headline are underrated on statics.** On video, he "would imagine" people read the headline and primary text much less. On a static, people are much more likely to read both. So give them equal weight to the image. Native statics and advertorials give little away in the image and "force people to read the primary text", which is "where we sell". Condition: his intuition, stated as such. Card caveat: truncation varies by placement `[verify UI]`. [541bb0ca2a17] [9a2fc128c78d]
14. **Pair image and text like a YouTube thumbnail and title.** The thumbnail makes you question what is going on; the title adds context. Example: title "miracle drug", thumbnail of someone pouring a powder into a drink, blurred. The gap makes you click the ad or tap "learn more" to read the primary text. Use the same pairing between the static and the primary text, or the static and the headline. [541bb0ca2a17]
15. **Pick one end of the clarity vs curiosity spectrum; the middle fails.** Left end: maximum clarity, where looking for 3 to 4 seconds tells you what is sold, the angle, who it is for and the offer. Right end: a deliberate curiosity gap, such as a native static, advertorial or listicle, where you do not know you are being sold to and are pulled into clicking or reading the primary text. "People die always in the middle." You cannot be half curious and half clear; the middle produces "slop creative". A middle ad sits as a middle or bottom of funnel ad, "scoop[s] up attribution", never gets turned off, and never drives growth. He says this spectrum idea applies to "literally everything" in business. [102cc3ec8112] [033a793e7d07] [60f8424e2b37] [8eb2a2b5a787]
16. **Visual hierarchy decides the stage of awareness.** The eye snaps to a first spot, then a second, third and fourth, along a path. If the first thing the eye hits is a huge "protein powder" headline, the ad reads as bottom of funnel ("I'm getting sold protein powder"). Put the highest-awareness message where the eye lands first, so it resonates with a larger pool. Example: replace "protein powder" with "new technology" for a clear protein water; the image of clear protein mixing into a glass does not make sense, so the eye moves on and the viewer is pulled through the stages quickly, then clicks. [f8a4e5a25281] [19abb79c0e64]
17. **3 levers control the hierarchy: size, contrast, whitespace.** Size: the brain goes to the biggest element. Contrast: the eye goes to whatever is different. Whitespace does the opposite: it directs the eye away from empty space onto the elements that matter. [f8a4e5a25281]
18. **A concept is persona, angle, offer, and optionally format.** Before building any static, define: the exact persona, the angle, the offer, and optionally the format ("the mechanism for delivery of the concept"). He drops format for statics because the talk is inside the static format. [ab5dac71c1dc] [8c915d03411c]
19. **New design is not a new concept.** 3 new statics are variations unless persona, angle or offer changes. A different offer can be a different product, or a different representation of value of the 1 product you sell. A different angle: buy for longevity instead of weight loss. If you talk to the same people with the same angle and offer, "it is the same ad". Design "has nothing to do with broadening out and reaching new audiences". [8c915d03411c] [ab5dac71c1dc]
20. **Do variations, but make them big enough to measure.** Variations are high leverage: they put more volume into the account by spinning more creative under a working concept. But a background colour change or a slightly different product or model pose is "not worth it". The effect might be 1% or 2%, which is almost impossible to measure without enormous A/B budgets, and spending that budget to learn a background colour is a poor use of money. [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
21. **Only run tests that pass 3 checks.** (1) Measurable. (2) Worth putting spend behind. (3) Would materially change your creative approach and feed a creative flywheel. Feedback to the creative team should be about which offers, angles and personas work, not "use blue backgrounds rather than red". You can read into why a variation won only if the variable change was big enough. [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
22. **Under Andromeda, this is now mandatory, not optional.** Andromeda "will natively group similar ads", count them "as the same ad ID", and serve them to the same audience, so slight variants give no uplift. His relayed reason (from Meta reps): when AI image models arrived, ad volume skyrocketed; Meta prioritises accounts with higher creative velocity, so floods of near-identical AI ads were gaming that; grouping similar ads counters it. Condition: secondhand, from reps. The card header calls it "Entity ID"; the transcript says "ad ID". `[web check: Meta documentation on Andromeda, creative similarity grouping and Entity IDs]` [d6f4dfaaf4c4] [ab5dac71c1dc]
23. **Most statics fail to scale because they sit too low in the stages of awareness.** Eugene Schwartz stages as he applies them: [2e9ac19f8685] [1a178c0c2400]
   - Most aware: knows your brand, probably visited the site. A white background product shot "will hit no one" except these people.
   - Product aware: knows the product but has not clicked or bought. He says this is "90% of static ads that are created by most people".
   - Solution aware: knows the problem and the general solution, not your product. "This is effectively where we start getting cold audiences." Most people never make statics here.
   - Problem aware: understands the problem, does not know a solution exists. "Going very cold."
   - Unaware: does not know a problem exists.
24. **Unaware statics need primary text plus a landing page.** A static consumed in about 4 seconds cannot make someone aware of a problem, then a solution, then your product, then convince them to buy. It can work if the static redirects attention into the primary text or onto a landing page "where we can sell all day long". [2e9ac19f8685] [9a2fc128c78d]
25. **Image and copy must sit at the same stage of awareness.** A product-aware image with solution-aware copy fails. Example: VPA creatine static shows the product (product aware) while the copy explains what creatine does (solution aware). This applies to video too: "the actual ad, the video, the static, whatever it is, matches the copy". [5ca4fa6b39ad] [541bb0ca2a17]
26. **Curiosity clicks must go to an educational page, not a PDP.** Top of funnel curiosity, advertorial and native statics need an educational lander (advertorial, listicle, quiz, long-form page). For the curiosity direction, "you don't even want to sell them on the landing page that you drive them to". Sending these clicks to a product page is where "most people fall apart" and conclude statics do not scale. [4a4cc4147dbc] [f5fe1d2b059b] [60f8424e2b37] [f4d743d1a127]
27. **The education lane can go warmer.** If the static educates hard on what is new and what changed, clickers arrive "really warm and they're likely to convert". Card adds: these can survive a shorter path, and clear offer statics for product or most aware audiences can go to a PDP. [8eb2a2b5a787] [f5fe1d2b059b] [102cc3ec8112]
28. **Do not judge top of funnel statics by last-click.** Top of funnel statics drive cheap CPCs and a lot of traffic, which gets retargeted elsewhere in the account, and the retargeting ad takes the attribution. So the static may show ROAS below target while the halo effect on the account is "enormous". Bottom of funnel statics may show 7x ROAS; proper top of funnel statics will not, and people wrongly conclude they "don't work". "Measure success and scale against financial KPIs, not just in platform metrics." He does not name which financial KPIs. Card adds: blended revenue, gross profit, new-customer volume. [b3058b60c97e] `[needs brand data: blended revenue, new customer count and MER around top of funnel static launches]`
29. **Research is the most important step.** Order of priority is the pipeline order: research and concepting first, then build (and format), then variations (one of the least important, "just to squeeze a little bit more spend through the concept"), then feedback and launch. "The build means nothing without a good concept. The variations mean nothing without a good build. The feedback means nothing without everything above it." People skip research because they think they understand the consumer's pain points, then write long-form landing pages and primary text to someone they do not understand, so the copy does not resonate. [d797d2ae775b] [789c10faf325]
30. **Do not start in Canva.** Opening Canva and browsing templates starts at the least important step. Start "in a word doc or in some kind of research buildout tool". About 80% of the time should go to research and concepting; production should then be "relatively quick". [789c10faf325] [d797d2ae775b]
31. **Concepting is the key human step when using AI.** If everyone concepts with an LLM, everyone gets the same answers. Loading research files into the context window gives a bit more uniqueness, but once everyone does that the edge is gone. AI does not cross-translate peripheral ideas from other industries well; creative people do, and that is where differentiation and arbitrage come from. [7a115c9bb3e2] [d797d2ae775b]
32. **Scale concepts, not individual ads.** One of his 5 takeaways. When a format works, keep the format and swap one concept element (persona, offer or angle). [6480746607a0] [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
33. **A new, unsaturated product category can win with mediocre ads, for a while.** Protein lollies/gummies example: the product sits in an existing, unmet desire, so "people can get very quick wins with very mediocre ads". Expect this to stop working as competitors enter and to need better ads to differentiate. No card covers this.
34. **Brand equity can prop up weak creative.** On AG1 he says the logo "props up the performance of the ad", and that AG1 sits in the grey middle because it must stay compliant. A more aggressive performance marketer would go more curiosity-driven; he thinks AG1 should instead treat it as a branding and education piece. [0e29070e9b20] [8eb2a2b5a787]
35. **Drop the logo from the image when the page already shows the brand.** On the creatine ad: "We don't need the logo in the ad. The logo was in the page at the top of the ad." He says he does not hate the corner logo, but would remove it. Conflict: in the Venroy native static, a light logo is part of how the viewer discovers it is an ad. [3db510f74584] [784187eac526]
36. **Social proof must be recognisable as proof.** An unattributed review line can read as confusing; the second voice says you almost need 5 stars to read it as a review. The presenter says the mocha protein ad would "probably win" if a famous person's face and name carried the review. [f520a0111397] [19abb79c0e64]
37. **A plain product shot can add useful friction.** On the mocha protein ad, a big tub of whey "probably pulls the quality of the ad down" against the premium background, but he thinks it also "adds friction" so buyers are not shocked on the site that the product is a tub of whey. Condition: his guess on 1 ad. [ae9948825d17]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Static ad production pipeline
Source order, with his priority weighting. [d797d2ae775b] [789c10faf325] [7a115c9bb3e2]
1. Research. Understand the consumer and their real pain points. Start in a document or research tool, not Canva. About 80% of total time goes to research plus concepting. The source does not list research inputs. Card adds: reviews, support tickets, customer language, competitor comments, community posts. [789c10faf325] `[needs brand data: reviews, support tickets, post-purchase surveys, customer language]`
2. Concept. Write persona, angle, offer and optional format for each idea. Card adds: hand the designer a written brief with persona, angle, offer, format and key message. [ab5dac71c1dc] [789c10faf325]
3. Build. Make the static. Decide clarity or curiosity end (3.3) and design the eye path (3.4). "Production should be relatively quick."
4. Variations. Either make variations of a working concept, or vary 1 of the 3 concept elements (persona, angle, offer) to make more concepts. See 3.6.
5. Internal feedback. A human review before launch is recommended (see 3.2).
6. Push live.

### 3.2 Where to put AI in the pipeline
AI can be used at every step. Do not automate everything, or you get "a lot of slop". Choose where the human intervenes. His 3 patterns, which he says real clients use: [7a115c9bb3e2]
1. Fully automate research, concepting, build and variations, with human feedback before launch. (He also mentions fully AI feedback, "which I don't know, I don't think that's a great idea".)
2. Human build, as the check that research and concepting are good; AI variations; then feedback.
3. AI-assisted research (scraping large databases to understand the consumer, which he calls "super helpful"), human concepting, AI-assisted build (AI image generation to speed it up), then feedback.
He calls concepting "one of the most important interventions by the human". Card 7a115c9bb3e2 frames option A as "keep research and concepting human"; the transcript's first option automates those and keeps human feedback. Use the transcript version.

### 3.3 Choose the clarity or curiosity end before building
1. Decide which end the static sits at. [102cc3ec8112] [033a793e7d07]
2. Clarity end: someone looking for 3 to 4 seconds understands what is sold, the angle, who it is for and the offer. Use the whole static to educate. Make the problem, solution and offer explicit.
3. Curiosity end: the viewer cannot tell what is sold or that it is an ad. Withhold or blur the product. Keep one open loop. Put the selling in long-form primary text and/or an educational landing page.
4. Review the draft with one question: "Is this clear enough to sell, or curious enough to pull a click?" If neither, push it to one end. [033a793e7d07] [60f8424e2b37]
5. Match the destination (3.8).

### 3.4 Design the eye path
1. Find where the eye lands first: the biggest and highest-contrast element. [f8a4e5a25281] [19abb79c0e64]
2. Make that element the highest-awareness message (problem, persona call-out, "new technology"), not the product category name.
3. Use size to make the key element biggest, contrast to pull the eye to what differs, and whitespace to steer the eye off empty areas onto the elements that matter.
4. Plan 3 to 4 eye stops that pull the viewer through the stages of awareness. In the mocha protein read: product image creates desire, then the bold middle claim ("two in one mocha protein") confirms, then top call-outs add detail ("grass-fed Aussie whey", "100% real coffee and chocolate"), then a review at the bottom adds trust. [19abb79c0e64]
5. Cut call-outs that repeat what the headline already said. He says the mocha top call-outs "could have done a little bit of a better job" because the headline already gave that context. [19abb79c0e64]
6. Fix basic layout faults that cost authority: poor spacing, product pushed off to one side. [3db510f74584]
7. Card adds: show the draft to a colleague; if they cannot tell what the product is or that a review is a review, the read order is broken. [19abb79c0e64]

### 3.5 Pick formats by funnel stage
His map, and formats he names elsewhere. [1190d2930cfd] [65acf3feb958]
1. Top of funnel: problem agitation statics, native statics, PR and advertorial statics, founder statics (a photo of the founder doing something, either very curious or very clear, with a founder story in the primary text).
2. Middle of funnel: comparison ads, before and afters, listicles, feature call-outs. "This is where honestly most people just make all their statics."
3. Bottom of funnel: offer call-outs, reviews, hero product shots.
4. Other formats named: notepad, text message, tweet repost (IM8), paparazzi-style photo (Venroy), "read this before buying" negative hook, listicle-style advertorial.
5. Test across stages, find the format that works, then scale it with 3.6.
Note: he also calls listicles a curiosity-end format in the spectrum section, and card 65acf3feb958 places feature call-outs at the clarity end. The map above is the more specific statement.

### 3.6 Scale a working format by rotating 1 concept element
1. Confirm a format works (example: problem agitation, native static). [6480746607a0] [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
2. Keep the format and the messaging.
3. Swap 1 element: the product or offer (greens powder static rerun for protein powder), or the persona (women 30 to 40 to men, same format, offer and angle), or the angle (weight loss to longevity).
4. Do not keep making slight iterations on the format itself.
Card adds: rotate only 1 element at a time so you can attribute the change. [6480746607a0]

### 3.7 Forecast creative volume
He uses both models. Do not just "test as much as possible": each ad needs enough budget for statistical relevance. [a667b506d058] [5482737fbbe4]

Churn model:
1. Count current winning ads (example: 10). `[needs brand data: current winners and the account's definition of a winner]`
2. Count winners that churned out this month (example: 2). Churn rate = 2 / 10 = 20%.
3. Winners to replace per month = churned winners (2).
4. Find the hit rate (example: 5%, which he calls "pretty normal across most large ad accounts", range 5 to 15%). 5% means 20 ads per winner. `[needs brand data: historical hit rate]`
5. Required ads per month = winners to replace / hit rate = 2 / 0.05 = 40 ads, "assuming quality maintains consistent with historical quality within the account".

Average revenue per ad model:
1. Find average spend per ad, average ROI (return), average revenue per ad, and average ad duration (how long ads stay on). `[needs brand data: spend, revenue and live days per ad]`
2. Set the revenue target (example: $1 million this month).
3. Back into how many ads you need, given revenue per ad and how long each runs.
The source gives no formula or worked numbers for this model. Do the arithmetic in code with the brand's own data.

### 3.8 Match the static to its destination
1. Classify the static: curiosity or native, advertorial, education-heavy, or product-forward. [f5fe1d2b059b]
2. Curiosity, native, advertorial and broad-solution statics: send to a highly educational long-form lander, listicle, advertorial or quiz. Do not sell hard on that page. [4a4cc4147dbc] [60f8424e2b37] [f4d743d1a127]
3. Education-heavy statics that already explain what is new: clickers arrive warm. [8eb2a2b5a787]
4. Match the landing page offer to the ad. GLP-1 hair loss example: the lander's offer "less shedding in 26 days or less" is "directly congruent" with the ad. Two examples used custom landers per angle.
5. If a curiosity static underperforms on a PDP, card adds: test a deeper landing page before deciding statics do not work. [f5fe1d2b059b]

### 3.9 Write the primary text for a native or curiosity static
From his example reads. He gives no template; these are the recurring moves. [541bb0ca2a17] [9a2fc128c78d] [e10005e1b904]
1. Open with a persona or problem call-out ("Read this if you're shaving your face daily", "Read this if you're always bloated by 3 p.m.").
2. Tell a personal story in first person ("This was my fourth doctor in 18 months", "I was diagnosed with IBS at 19").
3. Move through the stages of awareness: why the problem happens, how it happened to the narrator, then the solution.
4. Add social proof and timelines.
5. Reveal the product late ("here's where I actually got it from").
6. Send to a custom lander that matches the angle.
Card adds for the story version: problem that hurts a relationship, sceptical first try, a guarantee that lowers the barrier, positive outcome. [e10005e1b904]

### 3.10 Build an advertorial roundup funnel (observed, hair growth example)
He describes this as a funnel he saw and liked, not one he ran. [1620b9b6a2e3]
1. Static: headline "The best hair growth products of 2025 and the ones to avoid", sub-line "Here's what the big brands are not telling you", a grid of products.
2. Run from a doctor's page (a dermatologist) for authority. Short primary text: "As a dermatologist, I set out on a mission to find the most effective hair growth products."
3. Land on a site named "Dermatologist Reviews", owned by the brand. Post opens with who the author is and why they have authority.
4. Rank products: the hero first with reasons, then others with reasons not to choose them.
5. Only the hero is clickable in the ranking. It goes to a custom landing page, then a quiz funnel, then conversion.
6. Lower down, a comparison table with many green ticks for the hero and red marks for others; the other products link to an Amazon affiliate link so non-buyers still earn something.
Compliance: this presents a brand-owned site as independent review. See section 9 before using. `[web check: FTC and ASA rules on native advertising, endorsements and brand-owned review sites; Meta advertising standards]`

## 7. Examples and case results

All are the presenter's reads of other brands' live ads. None include results data except likes. Treat as examples, never as promises.

- **Expected gross profit (worked example).** Each ad returns $1,000 revenue. At 10x: spend $100; static costs $20 to make, GP $880; video $200, GP $700. At 2x: spend $500; static GP $480, video GP $300. He assumes both are top of funnel and return the same. [602ee9ba7290]
- **Churn forecast (worked example).** 10 winners, 2 churn (20%), 5% hit rate, 40 ads per month needed. [a667b506d058]
- **GLP-1 hair loss native static.** Headline "GLP1s gave you your body back. Then it took your hair." Image: an injection pen, with the woman losing hair in the mirror behind. Persona clear, angle clear, offer hidden, so a curiosity gap. Long-form primary text walks through why it happens via a personal story to pumpkin seed oil. Lander offer: "less shedding in 26 days or less". He says it "would be crushing it" and can "spend hundreds of thousands". He guesses the image is AI. [f4d743d1a127]
- **Before and after, face shaving.** Primary text "Read this if you're shaving your face daily" validates the image (acne or shaving). Runs through a page that looks like a person, not a brand (whitelisting). Opens "This was my fourth doctor in 18 months", then story, social proof, timelines. Custom lander for the angle.
- **Bloated by 3 p.m.** Clickbaity image, unclear what it is. Primary text "Read this if you're always bloated by 3 p.m." as persona and problem call-out. Story: "I was diagnosed with IBS at 19", 14 to 15 years of managing it. Resolves into detoxing for worms. Health claim risk, see section 9.
- **Thick hair before and after.** "Get your thick hair back. Limited stock left." Runs via a company page, so less native. He says "limited stock left" gives away that it is an ad and pushes it down the stages of awareness. Still uses long-form copy.
- **"Read this before buying" negative hook static.** Large curiosity gap, then a list of reasons "you shouldn't buy this product because it's great". Basic copy, primary text not used. He places it far down the funnel and doubts it cuts through cold, but calls it an interesting new format to test.
- **Protein lollies/gummies.** "Imagine these, but 20+ g of protein and 6.4 g of sugar" beside a famous Australian lolly brand. He clicked, landed on a protein gummy page, then got retargeted. He calls it middle to bottom of funnel that works because the category is new. He questions legality and would blur heavier and remove the brand name, showing only the packaging style.
- **Caffeine gum.** Great shot, good spacing. Headline "Guaranteed energy in just 5 minutes" is the only selling. Not strong enough to sell cold, not vague enough for a real gap. Sends to a product page, "that's when this ad stops working". Should go to an educational listicle. [60f8424e2b37]
- **VPA creatine.** Good: a bold, specific percentage at the top suggests a study "even if there wasn't". Bad: poor spacing, product too far left, logo bottom right (he would drop it), slogan with no meaning, copy at solution aware while the image is product aware. "This ad won't work." ## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Making only bottom of funnel statics (product shots, retargeting) and calling that a static strategy. [2e9ac19f8685] [1190d2930cfd]
2. Serving a white background product shot to cold audiences. [1a178c0c2400]
3. Treating a new design as a new concept; filling the account with variations of 1 concept. [8c915d03411c]
4. Testing background colours or slight pose changes; tiny effects are unmeasurable and Andromeda groups them anyway. [d6f4dfaaf4c4]
5. Sitting in the middle of the clarity vs curiosity spectrum. [033a793e7d07] [60f8424e2b37]
6. Opening with curiosity, then dumping jargon that educates nobody. [8eb2a2b5a787]
7. Driving curiosity or native statics to a standard PDP. [4a4cc4147dbc] [f4d743d1a127]
8. Trying to do too much in the creative instead of building curiosity and letting the lander educate. [f4d743d1a127]
9. Mismatching image stage and copy stage. [5ca4fa6b39ad]
10. Putting the product category in the biggest text so the ad reads as a sales pitch at first glance. [f8a4e5a25281]
11. Judging top of funnel statics by last-click in-platform ROAS. [b3058b60c97e]
12. Starting in Canva and templates; skipping research because you think you know the customer. [789c10faf325] [d797d2ae775b]
13. Automating the whole pipeline with AI, including feedback; relying on LLM concepting alone. [7a115c9bb3e2]
14. Copying competitor formats and expecting an edge without being first. [a0ce2d2f7363]
15. Poor spacing and placement that wipes out authority; meaningless slogans; unnecessary logos. [3db510f74584]
16. Unattributed review lines that do not read as reviews. [f520a0111397]
17. Signals that give the ad away too early ("Limited stock left") on a native static meant for cold traffic.
18. Testing "as much as possible" without enough budget per ad for statistical relevance. [a667b506d058]
19. Assuming a big brand's weak static works because it runs; the logo may carry it. [0e29070e9b20]
20. Mistaking early wins in a new, unsaturated category for creative skill.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Design matters or not.** He opens with "the design of a static ad really doesn't matter", then says design elements "actually become important" for communicating the message and bad design loses authority. Workable reading: design is not the performance lever, but layout, hierarchy and spacing must not break clarity or trust. [f8a4e5a25281] [3db510f74584]
- **Format matters least vs format innovation and format maps.** He says formats "fundamentally don't matter", yet gives a format map, praises new formats (tweet, negative hook) and says innovation gives arbitrage. Reading: format is secondary to persona, angle and offer, but still a lever for novelty and scroll-stop.
- **Concept elements count.** He lists 4 elements (format optional) and says "change one of these three things". Card ab5dac71c1dc counts format as a concept change; card 8c915d03411c says format swaps are only variations. The transcript, when discussing variation, names persona, angle and offer only.
- **Listicles.** Listed at the curiosity end in the spectrum section and at middle of funnel in the format map.
- **Gross profit percentages.** His "50% or 30% less profit" does not match his own numbers (20.5% and 37.5% lower). See section 4.
- **Statics beat video on CPM.** Unsupported claim with no data. Common advice is that the best creative mix depends on account and placement. `[needs brand data: CPM, CTR and cost per purchase by creative type]`
- **Andromeda grouping and "velocity" claim.** Secondhand from Meta reps; no documentation cited. `[web check: Meta statements on Andromeda, creative similarity and account creative volume]`
- **Last-click and halo.** The halo claim is asserted, not measured. He does not say how to measure it (no holdout, lift test or MER method). The card's KPI list is card adds. `[needs brand data: blended revenue, new customers and MER around top of funnel launches]`
- **Revenue-per-ad model is incomplete.** Inputs named, no formula or example given.
- **No testing setup.** No guidance on campaign structure, budget per test, how long to run, or what defines a "winner". Those belong to 11-ADC and 24-DEP.
- **Research step is thin.** He says research is 80% of the value but names no inputs, methods or tools except AI scraping of "large databases".
- **Compliance and ethics risks in his examples.** Several praised ads rely on disguise: whitelisting from a doctor's profile without a partnership label, a brand-owned "Dermatologist Reviews" site presented as independent, "a bold percentage... even if there wasn't" a study, story posts from what look like real individuals, health claims (GLP-1 side effects, worms detox, vitamin deficiency from symptoms), and a competitor's trademarked product. He hedges only on the lolly trademark. For ecomOS, the house rules apply: no invented claims, no impersonation, no fake studies; competitor ads are signals for layout and mood only. `[web check: FTC endorsement and native advertising guides, ASA/ASCI rules for the brand's market, Meta advertising standards on health and misleading formats]`
- **Category bias.** Most examples are health, supplements and beauty (CPG). He says native statics are "usually only used in CPG". Transfer to fashion, jewellery or high-ticket goods is untested in the source. `[needs brand data: which awareness stages and formats have worked in the brand's own account]`
- **Card drift.** Card 789c10faf325 adds research sources. Card c23189aedfc7 adds consent and disclosure steps. Card f8a4e5a25281 calls the protein example "a folded powder product"; the transcript says clear protein mixing into a glass. Card 7a115c9bb3e2 reverses the first AI option. Card 2e9ac19f8685 says "build solution-aware statics for cold audiences"; the transcript says solution aware is where cold audiences start and problem aware and unaware are colder. Card 5482737fbbe4 defines churn as ad fatigue across live ads; the transcript defines it as winners churning out.
- **Common ecommerce advice conflicts.** (1) Many guides tell brands to send paid social to the PDP or a collection page; this source says cold curiosity statics must go to an educational page instead. (2) Many guides push high-volume small-variation testing; this source says small variations are wasted under Andromeda. (3) Many guides stress polished, on-brand design; this source puts clarity first.
