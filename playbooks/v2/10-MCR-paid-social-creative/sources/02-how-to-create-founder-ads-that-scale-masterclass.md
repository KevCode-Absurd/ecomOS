## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist plan, script, shoot and refresh founder-led video ads for Meta cold acquisition. It gives: what a founder ad must contain, 1 data-backed body script structure, a hook-by-body batch model for volume, 3 ways to beat founder-face fatigue (format, founder involvement level, founder script role), a concept matrix, a monthly collection pipeline, production rules, a partnership-ad decision, and an awareness-stage check for founder creative. It says little about hook writing (deferred to other videos), metrics, or budgets.

## 2. Principles

1. **A founder ad presents the founder's argument. It is not a video of the founder.** Shooting raw iPhone footage of the founder talking is the common failure. The reframe changes how you script, film and edit. Basis: presenter's experience. [bd02cb579cd7] [59b1c68249c2]
2. **The format earns 3 things: authenticity, authority and trust.** Authenticity: a real person, which "pulls away from AI UGC or AI-driven creative". Authority: built without credibility markers such as celebrities or many reviews; the bigger the founder's personal brand, the bigger the effect. Trust: follows from the other 2. But stopping there fails: "If this is all you take away from the video, it's over." The script does the work. [59b1c68249c2]
3. **Founder ads are optional.** Source says a $200M fashion brand does not need the founder on camera to reach $300M; it is "probably not the bottleneck". But when done well, founder content "will perform better" ("categorically true if the founder content is good"). This is his generalisation, not tested data. [b27572e198f4]
4. **The only stated downside is exit risk.** If founder ads hold a large share of spend, it becomes "a marker within the term sheet". Source raises it, then says put it aside and run founder content anyway. [b27572e198f4]
5. **Business case 1: low cost.** The production cost is founder time, which helps small brands that would otherwise pay agencies, freelancers or creators. [b27572e198f4]
6. **Business case 2: differentiation for commodity products.** In a saturated category (fashion, electrolytes, creatine) where everyone sells the same mechanism, reposition through the founder's own story aimed at a narrow persona and problem. Example (hypothetical): a pro marathon runner hit a dehydration problem, tried every electrolyte on the market, found them lacking "because of XYZ", made his own. The presenter admits the story is differentiation, "not a unique mechanism". [b27bfdea1a9c] [b27572e198f4]
7. **It is not personal branding.** Some of the best founder ads he has seen had founders with zero following. The ad is "a vehicle for paid distribution" to cold audiences. Script so the product is not sold because of the person; the founder carries trust and tells the brand story. Basis: text only, no card covers this point directly.
8. **A founder story has 3 elements only the founder (or an expert) can own.** (a) Origin: why the business started, a claim only the founder can make credibly (example line: "I built this because the X industry was lying about this"). (b) Mechanism: why the solution works, credible only from the founder or a credentialed expert, not a UGC creator. (c) Adversarial angle: "we bought the competitors, tested them, and they were terrible for XYZ reason". Others avoid (c) for compliance and legal reasons; founders of small brands are often willing, which gives an edge "even though bridging on compliant". [bd02cb579cd7] [b27bfdea1a9c]
9. **Product experience is not a founder moat.** Anyone can speak to the experience, and the founder is biased, so it has less credibility from him or her. Carry experience with customers who look like the viewer and share the viewer's problem (diverse persona callouts). [b27bfdea1a9c]
10. **Nobody cares about the founder.** Called "the most important 1 to 2 minutes" of the video. Tell the story through the viewer's eyes and their existing desires. A story the viewer has lived ("makeup your whole life caused acne") holds attention because they think "this is me". Then problem-agitate, open a curiosity gap that you solved it, walk through the mechanisms, and close the gap at the end. Founder-only stories "sometimes work, but majority of the time people don't care." [59b1c68249c2] [6db074412846]
11. **99% of performance and work is in scripting, copywriting and ideation.** "The second you start recording, all the work is done." Filming is 4 to 5 minutes for 30 ads. [29d159fbe711]
12. **The founder's face is "the strongest sameness signal in the account".** A talking head in the same scene fatigues fast and serves to the same people. Fix = true concept diversification plus format variation. [73f8f845b93b]
13. **Format variation is easy; concept variation is hard.** Concept = persona, angle (the core unique mechanism, why buy) or offer (which product). Difficulty order per source: offer easiest (tell the founder story about another product), angle hard (there may be only 1 true mechanism), persona hardest (viewers resonate with the people in the ad; example: a 65-year-old male founder of a fashion brand for women 20 to 40). [73f8f845b93b]
14. **Persona can still move if the story is true.** Example (hypothetical): a 30-year-old male founder of creatine gummies re-angles to "my mum would not take chalky creatine powder, so I gave her the gummies, and we launched strawberry and orange because they were her favourite flavours", which opens older women as a persona. [73f8f845b93b]
15. **Never invent a founder story.** He calls it "black hat". The story must be true; the founder's presence lets you say things others cannot say with authority. Invented stories also hurt exit value. [59b1c68249c2] [fb523fb2f4cd]
16. **Use portfolio theory across all creative.** VSLs and founder ads are the 2 buckets of top single-ad revenue he has seen (by total lifetime spend and ROI); animation and claymation ads are similar to VSLs in structure. Founder ads with all variants and hook swaps have driven $20M to $30M in total revenue for DTC brands. The risk: brands ride 1 founder ad, it fatigues, everyone in the target region has seen it, they keep making variants of a fatigued concept, and the business decays. Keep sales, statics, animation and every other format live. When a founder ad wins, lean in, but do not overweight. [684258aed19c]
17. **Founder involvement is a spectrum, not on or off.** Protagonist (face and voice, "top of the pyramid", ideal) > voice only > evidence > proxy > absent. Effect drops as you move down; proxy and absent stretch away from a true founder ad. Use the whole spectrum with the same script to get format diversity. [51c9b7fece1b] [713723220d75]
18. **The founder's script role is a concept lever.** About 99% of founder ads use the protagonist role. Witness, interviewer and reader roles give different scripts from the same story. High leverage for 6 to 7 figure brands that do not want to pay creators; less needed for 8 to 10 figure brands, where founder time is scarce and these ads can run "just as well" on non-founders. [289897a2c363] [4098dd29a9fa]
19. **Founder story material is finite; some inputs renew.** There is 1 origin story told 100 ways. Static parts must stay consistent or you lose credibility. Renewable parts (reviews, emails, supplier news, complaints) change weekly and feed new scripts. [fb523fb2f4cd]
20. **Think in pipelines, not single ads.** Plan a monthly founder-ad target inside the wider mix, collect raw material all month, script in bulk, shoot rarely. [d0b9623db467]
21. **Hooks still matter under Andromeda.** Most people do not watch past the hook, so reshooting or changing the hook is high leverage: a loser can become a winner, and a winner can be squeezed further. [d0761aaedfd8]
22. **Story-based ads "always" perform better** ("I've never seen this not be the case"). Founder stories are the easiest story, but a product-origin story also works. Opinion, no data. [c75046cd85fd]
23. **Low-fi footage does not damage premium positioning if the product is not in it.** Low-fi founder or problem footage up front, then high-end product shots later, creates contrast instead of a cheap association. Presenter's reasoning, untested. [a9237fc18657] [417270a12d37]
24. **Most creative should sit in awareness stages 1 to 3, about 20% in stages 4 and 5.** Of 5 stages. A product-aware ad (testimonials, "how good my product is") cannot educate an unaware viewer. Heuristic, no data. [af4c74b18af1] [322374ef1b12]
25. **Human presence is a counter to AI creative.** A real founder "building connection and a human presence" is "particularly missing with all of the AI creative that's flooding" accounts. No brand is too big to add a founder ad. [ec40d606be63]
26. **Founder-led underdog positioning builds trust.** The founders put their reputation on the line; "almost any small business" can tell a small-versus-giants story if they put it into the creative. [7c2ba984d570]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Define the founder story before any script
1. Write the origin: the founder's personal problem and why the business started. Must be true. [bd02cb579cd7] [fb523fb2f4cd]
2. Write the mechanism: why the product works. Decide who can say it: founder or a credentialed expert. [bd02cb579cd7]
3. Write the adversarial angle: what the category or competitors do wrong, and what proof exists (tests you ran, labels, studies). `[needs brand data: real competitor test results, lab reports, certificates]` [bd02cb579cd7]
4. Check the story through the viewer's eyes: which problem or desire in it does the target buyer already have? If none, rework. [59b1c68249c2]
5. Split elements into static (origin, why started, personal problem, co-founder story) and renewable (reviews, emails, support inbox, supplier news, 1-star reviews, conversations, complaints). [fb523fb2f4cd]
6. Legal check on every adversarial or health claim. The source flags compliance risk but gives no substantiation steps. `[web check: comparative advertising and health-claim rules in the brand's market]` [acb7fe437e41] [f970fc3788a8]

### 3.2 Write the body with the top-performing founder structure
Source: most common structure in the agency's top 15 founder ads by spend and KPIs. [c57792ab090f] [acb7fe437e41]
1. **Claim.** Usually adversarial. It doubles as the hook. Examples: "The big supermarkets are trying to sell you this, but it actually is full of plastic." "Creatine gummies don't actually have creatine. It's a lie."
2. **Proof of claim, immediately.** None of the top founder ads worked with a claim alone. Example framing: "XYZ did a study and found all these competitors only have 1/100 of the creatine they said."
3. **Unique mechanism, tied to the founder story.** "The industry does this bad thing, here is proof, so I did this instead."
4. **Proof of mechanism.** More founder story as evidence. Beef jerky example: all jerky has chemicals (show labels, packaging), so I made jerky with no chemicals, flew to the factories, ran third-party testing, and the ingredient label shows only meat.
5. **CTA plus social proof.** How to buy, the offer, scarcity, urgency, then more proof from real customers.
6. Put hooks in front of this body and test them; do not rewrite the body per hook. [c57792ab090f]

### 3.3 Viewer-first story arc (general founder script logic)
1. Open on the viewer's lived problem or desire, not the founder's biography. [59b1c68249c2] [6db074412846]
2. Problem-agitate so the viewer keeps watching.
3. Open a curiosity gap: the founder solved it.
4. Walk through the mechanism(s).
5. Close the curiosity gap before the end. Do not open new loops you never close (see MacroMike in 7). [69e259507ec7]

### 3.4 Batch-shoot a hook by body matrix
1. Write 3 bodies that tell "materially different" stories, not rewordings. [29d159fbe711] [254ef036ab38]
2. Write 10 hooks. Each must bridge naturally into the body it will sit on. [29d159fbe711] [bb2386f4df37]
3. Combine: 10 hooks x 3 bodies = 30 ads.
4. Optional: add 2 CTA and social-proof endings to reach 60 ads. Source does not like testing CTAs because average watch time means almost no one reaches the CTA; do it only to hit a volume target.
5. Record: about 4 to 5 minutes of camera time for this set, off a teleprompter or a memorised script while walking.
6. Assemble in edit. In large brands, a script team or agency writes the hooks and bodies and does post, so founder time is "very minimal to none": hundreds of ads in a 1 to 2 hour block, enough for 3 to 4 months. [d0b9623db467]
Note: the intro promises 50 to 60 ads from "one afternoon". The card detail "shoot all hooks against the same lighting" [254ef036ab38] is not in the source; the source actually says change scenes during the shoot (3.6).

### 3.5 Build a founder concept matrix
Variables: persona, stage of awareness, angle, offer, proof type, founder role. [e52028da9a2d]
The source's 3 base ads:

| Base ad | Persona | Awareness | Angle | Offer | Proof type | Founder role |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 (source's "number 1 highest-performing approach") | Skeptical first-time category buyer, has not bought alternatives | Problem aware | "The entire category is lying to you", so the founder made this | Single SKU | Lab certificate ("these end up doing really well") | Protagonist |
| 2 | Existing category buyer (e.g. already buys makeup) | Solution aware | Dosage: the category does not dose the key ingredient correctly (only for CPG with doses) | Bundle, justified in script because the second product makes the first work (his example: concealer needs foundation; he says he does not know makeup well) | Competitor teardown on dose | Protagonist, or interview with a team member, or several founders together |
| 3 | Gift buyer | Product aware, deciding whether to gift | Taste: better than all competitors (consumables) | Gifting pack, optional | Email read: founder reads a customer email saying it is the best they have had | Witness/reader |

Steps:
1. Fill the 3 rows for your category. `[needs brand data: personas, SKUs, bundles, available proof]`
2. Change 1 variable at a time to make a new concept. Swapping role alone gives 4 versions of each ad. Changing the offer across a couple of offers triples the set. Moving the awareness stage also changes the proof and angle needed. Expect 20 to 30 concepts. [e52028da9a2d] [289897a2c363]
3. When the awareness stage changes, also change the proof and angle; do not just relabel the persona. [e52028da9a2d]
4. Add a couple of hook options per base ad, and possibly a couple of bodies.

### 3.6 Refresh a fatigued founder ad
1. Decide if the fatigue is format (same face, same scene, same talking head to the same people) or concept (everyone in the target has seen the idea). [73f8f845b93b] [684258aed19c]
2. Format fix, cheap: reshoot the same script in a new location, with the same editing and B-roll. Case: gym to outside the gym gave another $40,000 of spend. Reusing scripts is "totally fine, even under Andromeda" if editing or scenes differ enough. `[web check: current Meta guidance on Andromeda creative similarity]` [73f8f845b93b]
3. As volume grows, stretch further: a truly new set, or a new format (tier-list video, founder walking a store aisle handling competitor products, other organic-style formats). [73f8f845b93b]
4. Move down the involvement spectrum with the same script (voice, evidence, proxy, absent). [713723220d75]
5. Reshoot only the hook, in different places, with small changes. [d0761aaedfd8]
6. Concept fix: change offer first (easiest), then angle, then persona (hardest), using true stories only. [73f8f845b93b]
7. If the concept is fatigued, stop making variants of it and move budget to other formats. [684258aed19c]

### 3.7 Pick the founder involvement level
[51c9b7fece1b] [713723220d75]
1. Protagonist: founder on camera, speaking. Ideal and most fatigue-prone.
2. Voice: founder narrates over B-roll, animation or UGC creator footage. Use when the founder is not used to camera.
3. Evidence: founder not in the video; use founder photos in the lab, with certificates, or walking through the factory facing away from camera.
4. Proxy: an employee, a high-credibility source such as a dietitian, or the supplier tells the founder story. Less effective; use when the founder wants no face, voice or evidence.
5. Absent: no founder at all; tell the same story end to end in animation or claymation AI. Strength: can show the problem visually (for example, weight gain in real time) and show the mechanism (cells changing).
6. Try all 5 with the same script and concept for format diversity.

### 3.8 Change the founder's script role
[289897a2c363] [4098dd29a9fa]
1. Protagonist: "I built this."
2. Witness: "A customer emailed me last week and said..."
3. Interviewer: "I asked our lab why." Then cut to the lab, manufacturing, design or buying team explaining a choice that benefits the customer.
4. Reader: "I just got a 1-star review, let me read it out." The review must walk the viewer through the stages of awareness and still sell. See caveat in 9 on scripted reviews.
Card [4098dd29a9fa] adds "match reader to skeptical audiences, interviewer to mechanism-seekers"; the source does not say this.

### 3.9 Run a monthly collection pipeline
[d0b9623db467] [fb523fb2f4cd]
1. Set a monthly founder-ad target inside the wider creative mix. Source illustration: 40 founder ads a month. `[needs brand data: current creative volume and mix]`
2. Pipe reviews into a database.
3. Pipe the support inbox into "some kind of AI model" and pull ad-relevant items daily or weekly.
4. Record or note every supplier call and supplier comm. Ask of each: could this be content?
5. Capture 1-star reviews, day-to-day founder conversations and in-person complaints ("too expensive", "I don't like this new flavour").
6. Script each month from the stash; mix iterations of winners with new concepts.
7. Shoot general content about once a month and the founder about once a quarter, in about 1 hour of recording.
The source does not name tools, owners or formats for this pipeline.

### 3.10 Shoot-day and edit rules
[bb2386f4df37]
1. Do not run an unedited talking head. Use fast, dynamic editing, cuts, overlays and motion; treat it as an ad that must retain like good organic content. Exception: founders who are famous.
2. For talking-head stories, use 2 cameras and some motion.
3. Check every hook makes contextual sense going into its body. A hook like "10 years ago this happened to me" that cuts to an unrelated body is "a big issue" in founder ads.
4. If the founder is weak on a teleprompter, write the sentence-by-sentence structure and let them talk off script.
5. Expect a new founder to sound robotic for the first 10 or so hours on camera. Do not conclude "founder ads don't work for us" from robotic early takes.
6. Move around during the shoot. Do not shoot 1 hour in 1 spot.
7. Low-fi opening footage is fine for a premium brand if the product is not shown in it; show the product later in high-end shots. [a9237fc18657]

### 3.11 Decide page identity: company page or founder partnership ad
[7c9fa26551de] [6560e7ff8a96] `[verify UI]` partnership ad setup and eligibility in Meta Ads Manager.
1. Check the founder's personal Instagram: does it post regularly, and is the content relevant to the founder story (not "photos of you at the park with your dog")? `[needs brand data: founder handle content volume and relevance]`
2. If yes, and the founder does not mind ads through the personal page, run as a partnership ad through the founder's handle. Expected lift per source: 5% to 30%, not 10x. He has "almost never seen" the company page beat the founder partnership ad.
3. If no, keep it on the company page: it builds brand association and avoids customers "buying you instead".
4. Source's default for "a lot of people": keep it on the same (company) page.
5. Measure both if you can. The source gives no test method. `[needs brand data: side-by-side results]`

### 3.12 Hook count per body
[d0761aaedfd8] [6db074412846]
1. Write only as many hooks as can bridge naturally into the body.
2. Launch 2 to 4 hooks per body; up to 8 at most.
3. Do not go to 10, 20, 30 or 40 hooks per body: the budget spreads too thin for the ad set to exit learning and gather conversions. His example: $1,000 a day across 3 hook variants tests better than across 20 to 40.
4. When an ad works, swap or reshoot hooks.
Note: 3.4 shoots 10 hooks per body, while 3.12 launches 2 to 4. The source does not reconcile these. One reading: shoot many, launch in small waves. That reading is not stated.

## 6. Tools, settings and named features

- **Meta MCP plus Claude.** Used to pull every founder ad in the business manager, capture screenshots, pull scripts, rank by total spend, performance over time and other KPIs, then find common script structures. `[verify UI]` (For ecomOS: Meta is read-only; read tools only.)
- **Meta Andromeda.** Named as current Meta delivery context; source says reused scripts with varied edits or scenes and hook variations still work under it. `[web check: Andromeda creative diversity guidance]`
- **Partnership ads through the founder's handle.** vs running from the company page. `[verify UI]`

## 7. Examples and case results

All are examples. Numbers are the brand's or the presenter's, not benchmarks.

1. **Gym scene change.** Same script, edit and B-roll shot outside the gym instead of inside revived a fatigued ad for another $40,000 of spend. [73f8f845b93b]
2. **$20M to $30M founder ads.** Brands rode 1 founder ad plus hook swaps to $20M to $30M in a year, then decayed after fatigue. [684258aed19c]
3. **Dishwashing sheets (2 brothers from Queensland).** Hook: "I need to publicly apologize to anyone who's ever bought these dishwashing sheets before because I lied." Why it worked per presenter: negative hook plus fast pacing (does not feel like an ad); product shown working in the first 3 seconds; clearly founder-led; underdog story against giants "poisoning you through bleaches". [7c2ba984d570]
4. **Natural deodorant ("I went to the lab in Texas").** 634 likes. Novel format: story told to a crowd in a store, professionally shot, few shots on the speaker; story-based. Improvements he would test: do not show the product in the first 2 to 3 seconds; hold longer on the speaker; cut B-roll (some out of lip sync) or delay it to at least 20 seconds. [c75046cd85fd]
5. **MacroMike pea protein.** Credibility hook ("took over 2 years of product development"), curiosity gap ("here's why"), founder walks the warehouse as social proof, objection handling (every pea protein was "gluggy, gritty, grainy and gluey"), benefits over features (smooth, creamy, under 1g sugar, budget friendly, Canadian golden peas). Weak points: reopens a curiosity gap without explaining it; CTA "forget everything you know about pea protein" opens a loop that bridges nowhere. [69e259507ec7]
6. **Gym Rat Jerky.** Contrarian hook "Protein industry is broken. I'm fixing it" with fast graphics; persona agitation (want muscle, protein snacks are ultra-processed); agitation of alternatives (jerky made of soy, sugar, low-quality mince, not high protein, not made for lifters); founder story (steak, salt, spice; 45g protein a bag; quit job; posted daily for a year; 100,000+ followers; shipped across Australia); scarcity after the story ("I sell out all the time, but right now I've got stock"); CTA. "Such a good ad end to end." [d6540c83db2f]
7. **Step One underwear.** Authority hook: "I sold over 27 million pairs of underwear in under 7 years." Then the founder's physical problem (hiking, chafing, big legs, bike shorts, sweating), garage self-solutioning (panels only between the legs; elastic pouch that "lifted, separated and supported"; bamboo fabric), risk reversal (wear 30 days, free if you do not love them), "join over 2 million customers", URL CTA. Shot on iPhone walking in a park, "probably scripted in 5 to 10 minutes". Presenter believes it is a $100M business running its first founder ad; unverified. [ec40d606be63]
8. **Hemp and rosehip face oil.** Founder says she gets compliments and daily photos of healed skin, rolls through video testimonials. Verdict: a product-aware (stage 4) ad; fine at bottom of funnel; "not a good ad for spending a quarter million dollars behind" in cold top of funnel. [af4c74b18af1]
9. **Sunday Market colostrum gummies.** Introduced as "generated about half a million dollars in revenue to date". Hook about buying $33,000 worth of colostrum (transcript garbled) with a matching strange visual; "let me explain"; mother's gut issues, hair and skin improved; scientific studies paired with the subjective claim; friction reason (powdered colostrum is hydrophobic, a "chalky mess"); 8 months of work, Wisconsin farm with first-day milking whole colostrum, gummy maker, first 5,000 bags; CTA "drop a follow" for first access. [f970fc3788a8]
10. **Miro Shine toothbrush.** Authority ("2 years in dental labs"), big claim ("one thing the big brands hope you never notice"), stakes (bought every top-rated brush, thousands of dollars, $300 price tags, used for months), agitation (porous plastic, mouldy charging bases, Bluetooth apps to justify markup), product only after agitation (aircraft-grade aluminium, 14 motor frequencies tested, 32,000 micro-vibrations a minute, wall mount, 60-day battery), close "Upgrade to the standard you actually deserve." Low-fi start, high-end product shots in the last 20 seconds. [a9237fc18657]
11. **Blueprint (Bryan Johnson) drink.** Called "pretty bad overall": relies on his personal brand; lists ingredients (taurine, glycine, creatine, glutathione, L-theanine); pitches taste and "cheaper than the pills separately", which are bottom-funnel objection handling. Whole library sits low in awareness, so he expects it will not scale on cold audiences. No account data. [cf1973e97542]
12. **Creatine gummies re-angle (hypothetical).** Mum persona and flavour line. [73f8f845b93b]
13. **Electrolytes (hypothetical).** Marathon runner origin for a commodity product. [b27bfdea1a9c]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Shooting raw videos of the founder instead of scripting the founder's argument. [bd02cb579cd7]
2. Stopping at "authenticity, authority, trust" and skipping the script. [59b1c68249c2]
3. Telling the founder's story for its own sake; nobody cares about the founder. [59b1c68249c2]
4. Making a claim without proof. [acb7fe437e41]
5. Letting 1 founder ad carry the business, then making variants of a fatigued concept instead of other formats. [684258aed19c]
6. Shooting in the same spot every time; only running talking heads as volume grows. [73f8f845b93b] [bb2386f4df37]
7. Running an unedited talking head. [bb2386f4df37]
8. Hooks that do not fit the body. [bb2386f4df37]
9. Forcing a founder who is weak on a teleprompter to read it word for word. [bb2386f4df37]
10. Deciding "founder ads don't work for us" after robotic first takes. [bb2386f4df37]
11. 10 to 40 hooks per body on a modest budget. [d0761aaedfd8]
12. Testing CTAs, since few viewers reach them. [29d159fbe711]
13. Inventing a founder story. [59b1c68249c2]
14. Changing static story facts over time. [fb523fb2f4cd]
15. Treating founder ads as personal branding for followers. (text only)
16. Relying on the founder to praise the product experience; use customers who match the viewer. [b27bfdea1a9c]
17. Showing the product early and cutting to B-roll so often the viewer keeps being reminded it is an ad (story formats). [c75046cd85fd]
18. Opening curiosity loops in the CTA that never close. [69e259507ec7]
19. Putting big cold budget behind product-aware ads; building a whole library at the bottom of the funnel. [af4c74b18af1] [cf1973e97542]
20. Running from the founder's page when it has no relevant content. [6560e7ff8a96]
21. Scripting and shooting ad by ad, day by day, instead of a pipeline; not collecting reviews, inbox and supplier material. [d0b9623db467]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Scripted reviews and emails.** For the reader role the source says "we're scripting the one-star review in a way, or it's a real review". A scripted review presented as real conflicts with his own "black hat" rule and with fake-review law. Use only real reviews and emails, with permission. `[web check: fake review and testimonial rules in the brand's market, e.g. FTC rule in the US, ASCI guidance in India]` [289897a2c363] [4098dd29a9fa]
- **Adversarial and health claims.** He admits founders take compliance risks agencies avoid. Colostrum ad makes gut, hair and skin claims. No substantiation steps given. `[web check: comparative and health claim rules]` [acb7fe437e41] [f970fc3788a8]
- **Product timing conflict inside the source.** Dishwashing ad: product working in the first 3 seconds is a strength. Deodorant ad: product in the first 2 to 3 seconds is a weakness. Toothbrush ad: product held back is praised. Likely depends on format (demo vs story) and awareness stage, but the source does not say so. Keep as a test variable. [7c2ba984d570] [c75046cd85fd] [a9237fc18657]
- **Hook count tension.** 10 hooks per body in the batch example vs 2 to 4 (up to 8) for launch. Not reconciled. [29d159fbe711] [d0761aaedfd8]
- **Evidence is thin.** Top 15 founder ads from 1 agency's accounts, KPIs not defined, no control. The $40,000, $20M to $30M, 5% to 30% and 20% figures are anecdotes or heuristics. Many ad breakdowns are opinions from the public ad library with no spend data.
- **Card error.** [af4c74b18af1] says the face-oil ad made about $500,000. In the transcript, "about half a million dollars in revenue to date" introduces the next ad (colostrum gummies). Treat the $500,000 as the colostrum ad's figure.
- **Card extrapolations not in the source:** "shoot all hooks against the same lighting" [254ef036ab38]; "match reader role to skeptical audiences, interviewer to mechanism-seekers" [4098dd29a9fa]; "a single session can yield protagonist, voice and evidence assets" [51c9b7fece1b]; "track and rotate angle, scenery and edit combinations" [417270a12d37]; most of the stage-by-stage emphasis in [322374ef1b12]. Treat these as card-writer ideas, not source claims.
- **Missing how:** no hook writing method (deferred to other videos); no fatigue metrics (frequency, hook rate, CPA trend); no campaign or testing structure beyond the $1,000 a day example; no video length, aspect ratio or caption specs; no data on how much each step down the involvement spectrum costs; no tooling for the collection pipeline; no persona research method.
- **"Story-based ads always perform better"** and "founder content categorically performs better when good" are opinions.
- **Exit-risk** claim is his view of term sheets; varies by buyer.
- **Persona limits.** A founder who does not match the buyer (example: 65-year-old man, women's fashion 20 to 40) limits persona reach; fix only with true stories from the founder's life.
- **Possible conflicts with common ecommerce advice:** (a) many teams test lots of hooks and CTAs; this source caps hooks at 2 to 4 (up to 8) per body and says CTA tests are rarely worth it; (b) the common "show the product in the first 3 seconds" rule is not upheld here for story formats; (c) partnership ads through the founder are often sold as a big win; he puts it at 5% to 30% and defaults many brands to the company page; (d) he says founder ads are optional for large brands, against advice that every DTC brand needs founder content.
- **Brand fit.** Whether a brand's founder can appear, has a true origin story, has lab or supplier proof, or has an active personal handle all need checking. `[needs brand data: founder availability, origin story facts, proof assets, founder handle stats]`
