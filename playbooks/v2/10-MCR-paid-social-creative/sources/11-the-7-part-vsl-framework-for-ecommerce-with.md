## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist decide whether a brand needs a VSL (video sales letter) ad on Meta, then script one in 7 parts, test it cheaply, and pair it with the right landing page. It is most useful for complex products, low-trust categories (the example is supplements) and new categories where buyers need education before they buy. It also gives a review checklist for a VSL that is not performing.

## 2. Principles

1. **A VSL is a sales argument, not a long video.** It removes doubt in a set order. Length is not the point. Source says good VSLs "can be as long as 90 seconds" (meaning as short as), and the same structure can run 2 minutes or 20 minutes. Condition: applies to any VSL length. Evidence: presenter's definition. [583ab7df2aaf]
2. **Only use a VSL when the buyer needs education to convert.** The 3 qualifying cases: (a) a complicated product that is hard to explain, (b) a low-trust category ("a scammy industry") where the ad must build rapport, (c) a new category or new ingredient nobody has heard of. A 4th signal: top-of-funnel conversion rate is capped because customers do not understand the product. Condition: if the product does not need heavy education, a VSL is not the format. Evidence: presenter's opinion plus the supplement account. [1567d2668c63]
3. **Education has to happen in the ad or on the landing page. Best is both.** If the landing page does not educate, the ad must. Source says VSLs are the best option for educating in the ad, and "even better when you pair the two". Evidence: the supplement account drives every VSL to a different landing page. [1567d2668c63] [9d1aae2f4dcb]
4. **The 7-part order is a starting point, not a law.** Hook, Problem (in the customer's words), Mechanism, Objection handling, Proof, Risk reversal, CTA. Source: "You don't always have to script VSLs like this, but this is a good starting point." Evidence: the tonsil-stone example ran a different order and still performed "decently". [583ab7df2aaf] [053dc07009fa]
5. **The hook is the biggest lever.** Source states 80% of people will not watch past the hook. So build the body and CTA once, then test 3 to 6 hooks on it. Condition: the 80% figure is the presenter's statement, not a measured benchmark from this account. [556ea817b608]
6. **Do not bridge to the product right after the hook.** Source says this is "where most people mess up VSLs". Stay in education (problem, mechanism) so the ad reads as "an educational piece", not an ad. Bring the product in as the answer once the education has landed. [2370029af17b] [556ea817b608]
7. **Explain why, not just what.** Every ingredient or feature gets a reason it matters ("does X in your mouth, which causes Z"). Claims with no mechanism are a listed mistake. Condition: the mechanism in CPG and supplements is "the actual ingredient". [2370029af17b] [dda332d2d5b2]
8. **Objection handling should come from real customer data, not guesses.** Use reviews, support tickets, post-purchase surveys and call recordings about why people almost did not buy, and map those reasons into the script. [4fb56bc1019f] [needs brand data: reviews, support tickets, post-purchase survey answers, sales call notes on why people almost did not buy]
9. **Scripting matters more than length.** Source: "I don't think lengths have a massive impact on the actual performance." Length cuts are still useful as extra variations. Condition: presenter's opinion. [c5a32b550f4d]
10. **Start short, then go longer.** Longer VSLs cost more to produce. Source warns less-skilled writers against starting with a 60-minute video, though "people have had major success running 20-minute Facebook ads". [1567d2668c63] [583ab7df2aaf]
11. **Keep the visual style consistent.** VSLs mix AI UGC, real UGC and animation to fill a long ad without a shoot. But too much variety "comes across a little bit scammy". Source prefers "one or the other": more UGC and less animation, or go all in on animation. Condition: subjective view on 1 ad. [8e86c45ad8e2]
12. **Include a CTA and a risk reversal.** Source links the weaker example's lower engagement partly to missing both. For a first VSL, keep the CTA on the asset instead of splitting the close into a retargeting ad. [b8868a83a786] [dda332d2d5b2]
13. **Judge VSL economics on retention, not first-order ROAS.** The supplement account runs at about 2.5 to 3 ROAS on new-customer acquisition but "we make pretty much all of the money on second, third purchase". Condition: only holds for brands with strong repeat purchase. [9d1aae2f4dcb] [23116e553dcf] [needs brand data: repeat purchase rate and 12-month customer value] (ROAS and scaling rules sit in 11-ADC.)
14. **VSLs skew to older viewers, so text must be large.** Source says VSLs "perform better with older age demographics". Condition: presenter's opinion. [dda332d2d5b2] [needs brand data: age split of buyers and of video viewers]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Decide if the product needs a VSL [1567d2668c63]
1. Check the product against the 3 cases: complicated, low-trust category, or new category/ingredient.
2. Check if top-of-funnel conversion is capped because people do not understand the product. [needs brand data: cold-traffic conversion rate, customer questions before purchase]
3. If none apply, do not make a VSL just because it worked for a supplement brand.
4. If yes, decide where education lives: ad, landing page, or both (source prefers both).
5. Plan a short first version. Do not start with a 60-minute script.

### 3.2 Script the VSL in 7 parts [583ab7df2aaf]
1. **Hook.** Something relevant to the viewer's problem. Not a brand story. Call out who has the problem so the right people stay and others leave (example: "The big problem people with tonsil stones are having..."). An engaging animation plus an ingredient claim also works (dental example). [556ea817b608]
2. **Problem.** In the customer's own words, so they recognise themselves. Use educational problem-agitation, such as the biological cause of the problem. Do not name the brand or product yet. [2370029af17b]
3. **Mechanism.** Why the solution works. In supplements, the ingredient. For each ingredient, say what it is and why it matters.
4. **Objection handling.** Name what they already tried and why it failed (example: squeezing stones out "is just removing the symptom"; salt water or apple cider vinegar "only rinse the surface and can't reach the deep biofilm"). Pull these from customer data (see 3.3).
5. **Bridge to product.** Present the product as the thing that combines the mechanism (example: "you don't have to look for all these ingredients individually because [brand] already combined everything into one piece of gum").
6. **Proof.** UGC, influencers, testimonials, customer count (example: "80,000 plus customers"). Proof can be on the product or still on the mechanism.
7. **Risk reversal.** Example: "Skeptical? Good. With every single order, you get 90 days to test it yourself."
8. **CTA.** Simple and clear. Example: "Get yours in the link below." Make the offer clear and convincing.

### 3.3 Build the objection-handling section from customer data [4fb56bc1019f]
1. Collect reviews, support tickets, post-purchase surveys and call recordings. [needs brand data: these 4 sources]
2. Pull out the reasons people almost did not buy, and what they tried before.
3. For each, write: the thing they tried, then why it did not work.
4. Place it so the viewer feels understood before, or around, the mechanism. Source does not give a fixed spot; its 2 examples differ.

### 3.4 Test plan for a finished VSL [556ea817b608] [c5a32b550f4d]
1. Finish the body and CTA first.
2. Write and produce 3 to 6 hooks. Run them on the same body. Hooks are the main test variable.
3. Make modular edits: move whole sections. Tests the source names: mechanism before problem; objection handling earlier; problem agitation pulled forward (dental example, where it arrives at about 15 to 20 seconds); a faster mechanism section (tonsil example, about 30 seconds of ingredient education). Source says "we've actually seen good success moving stuff around."
4. Cut lengths. From a 6 to 10 minute VSL, an editor can usually cut a 2 to 3 minute version and a 45-second version. Treat these as extra variations, not the main lever.
5. Source does not say how to read the test (no metric, spend or time threshold). Use 11-ADC for that.

### 3.5 Produce visuals without a shoot [8e86c45ad8e2]
1. Mix AI UGC, real UGC and animation to carry the story.
2. For hard-to-film topics, take a still image (source: something "found somewhere on Google images") and animate it with an AI video model to make motion graphics. [web check: image rights before using found images; the source does not address this]
3. Keep the look consistent: lean UGC or lean animation, not a scramble of everything.
4. Brief the editor on pacing and large, readable text.

### 3.6 Set the destination [9d1aae2f4dcb]
1. Build a custom landing page (advertorial or dedicated page) for each VSL message and angle.
2. Continue the ad's mechanism and story on the page. Do not send to a standard PDP with no link to what the ad said.
3. Make the page itself strong. Source: "The VSL won't work if you're just dropping to a product page that has no continuity."

### 3.7 If you choose to run a VSL without a CTA (not advised for a first VSL) [b8868a83a786]
1. Option A: send people off-platform to search for the product on Amazon (source says this is common in the US now, via TikTok ads or organic posts, because Amazon "has a much higher conversion rate than an actual website landing page"). [web check: Amazon vs DTC site conversion rates]
2. Option B: run the VSL as pure top of funnel and retarget anyone who watched past 25% of the video with a hard-close ad, plus the platform's natural retargeting. [verify UI: Meta video-view custom audience options and the 25% threshold]
3. Source's advice for a first VSL: do not do either. Include the CTA and keep the retargeting setup simple.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

Review checklist for a VSL draft or an underperforming VSL [dda332d2d5b2]:

| check | how | good | bad | next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Watch the first seconds with sound off and on | Speaks to the viewer's problem; filters in the right people | Brand story or founder intro as the hook | Rewrite as a problem-led hook. Move the founder story into proof or objection handling (the founder "might have tried all of these different things") |
| Bridge | Find the moment the product first appears | After problem and mechanism have landed | Product pitch right after the intro | Push the product later; add problem education first |
| Mechanism | For each claim, ask "why does it work?" | Each ingredient or feature has a reason | Only claims ("does X, Y and Z, and Sarah says it works") | Add the why for each claim |
| Objection handling | Is there a "what you tried and why it failed" section? | Present, from real customer reasons | Missing (the dental example skipped it) | Add it from reviews, tickets, surveys, calls |
| Proof | Is proof visible and early enough? | UGC, testimonials, customer count clearly shown | Weak or buried | Source calls this "a really common and easy way to fix an underperforming VSL" |
| Risk reversal | Look for a guarantee or trial window | Present (example: 90 days to test) | Missing (tonsil example) | Add one |
| Offer and CTA | Last seconds of the ad | Clear, convincing offer and a simple ask | Vague offer or no CTA | Add a CTA unless running a deliberate no-CTA setup (3.7) |
| Pacing and text | Watch at normal speed on a phone | Moves quickly; large, readable text | Slow; small text | Editor fix. Matters more because VSLs skew older |
| Visual consistency | Scan the footage mix | One main style | Everything the team could find; feels scammy | Pick UGC-led or animation-led |
| Landing page | Click through | Page continues the ad's story and mechanism | Generic PDP | Build a matching advertorial or dedicated page |

## 7. Examples and case results

1. **Australian CPG supplement account (presenter's).** Many VSLs, "millions of dollars in revenue aggregated together". 1 VSL spent $360,000. ROAS about 2.5 to 3 on pure new-customer acquisition. About 150% profit lift after the first year from repeat purchases. Very low frequency at that spend in Australia. New ingredient, so heavy top-of-funnel education. 2. **Dental gum VSL (public ad; brand name garbled in transcript, "Lorine").** About 25,000 likes, launched about 2 months earlier, estimated at least $100,000 spend. ## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Making the hook a brand story. A VSL is not a brand-story ad. [dda332d2d5b2]
2. Bridging to the product or CTA right after the intro ("where most people mess up VSLs"). [2370029af17b]
3. Too much "what it is", not enough "why it matters"; claims with no mechanism. [dda332d2d5b2]
4. Weak or buried proof. [dda332d2d5b2]
5. A vague offer, or no CTA. [dda332d2d5b2] [b8868a83a786]
6. Slow pacing or unreadable text, worse because viewers skew older. [dda332d2d5b2]
7. Skipping objection handling or risk reversal (flagged in both examples). [053dc07009fa] [dda332d2d5b2]
8. Sending VSL traffic to a generic PDP with no continuity. [9d1aae2f4dcb]
9. Making a long video for its own sake. [583ab7df2aaf]
10. Starting with a very long (60-minute) VSL before you have the skill. [1567d2668c63]
11. Using every piece of footage you can find, so the ad looks inconsistent and scammy. [8e86c45ad8e2]
12. Splitting the CTA into a separate retargeting setup on your first VSL. [b8868a83a786]
13. Making a VSL when the product does not need education. [1567d2668c63]
14. Reading a 2.5 to 3 ROAS as "not very good" without looking at repeat purchase. [23116e553dcf]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Evidence is thin.** 1 own account and 2 public ads. The 80% hook drop-off, "older demographics", "length does not matter much" and the Amazon conversion claim are all stated without data.
- **The order is flexible, and the source says so.** Neither example follows the 7-part order exactly. The dental ad puts mechanism before problem. The tonsil ad puts objection handling before mechanism. Do not enforce the order as a rule. [053dc07009fa]
- **Card errors to note.** Card [2370029af17b] says the roughly 30 seconds of ingredient education was in the "dental example"; in the transcript it was the tonsil-stone example. Card [dda332d2d5b2] is titled "six common mistakes", but the source lists 5 numbered mistakes; the 6th (missing risk reversal) comes from the example critiques. Card [053dc07009fa] summary says "moves problem before hook education", which does not match the transcript; the step list in that card is correct.
- **Weak spend proxy.** Likes plus launch age as a spend estimate is a guess. The "50x" population figure looks wrong. [web check: US vs Australia population]
- **No test reading rules.** The source says what to test (hooks, section order, length) but not how long, how much spend, or which metric decides. See 11-ADC.
- **No landing page how-to.** It insists on a matched advertorial but gives no page structure. Look in the landing page folder.
- **No script templates or word counts** beyond the 7 part names and the 2 examples.
- **Rights and compliance gaps.** Animating images "found on Google images" and making strong health claims ("completely get rid of your cavities forever", "the stones stop forming") are shown without comment. Health and supplement claims can break ad policy and local law. [web check: Meta health and supplement ad policy; claim rules in the brand's market] Do not copy these claims.
- **Conflict with common ecommerce advice.** Common Meta advice favours short video with the product or brand shown in the first few seconds. This source delays the product until after problem and mechanism, and supports ads from 45 seconds to 20 minutes. It also rejects sending cold VSL traffic to the PDP, where many brands default to the PDP. Both views can be right: this source limits VSLs to products that need education. For simple, known products, short product-first ads are still the default.
- **Retention dependency.** The case economics rely on strong repeat purchase. A 1-purchase product may not survive a 2.5 ROAS. [needs brand data: margin, repeat rate, break-even ROAS]
