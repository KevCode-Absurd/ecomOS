## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator write, review and fix a direct-response video ad script for Meta. It gives a 4-part script frame (hook, bridge, body education, product plus proof), a way to check that the hook and the body serve the same awareness stage, a segment-by-segment diagnosis for a weak ad, and a short list of editing and setting choices that make a talking-head ad watchable. It teaches through 5 example ads graded bad, bad, okay, okay, great. It is thin on shot-level production steps, exact lengths, and any measured result.

## 2. Principles

1. **A hook's job is to pull in relevant viewers, not everyone.** Condition: applies to any hook meant to start education at the problem-aware or solution-aware stage. The presenter: "The objective of a hook isn't to just hook everyone and do something weird. The objective of a hook is to pull in relevant users so that you can start to educate them at the problem aware or solution aware stage." Evidence: his reading of the Magic Mind knife hook and the Devoted shaker hook. The card adds "feeds bad optimization data to the Meta targeting algorithm" (header note, not in transcript). [8e0335ccf918]
2. **Curiosity without qualification is the failure, not curiosity itself.** A weird visual (a knife stabbed into the product) makes everyone ask "what's going on?" but does not make the right person think "this is about me." Condition: curiosity that also names a relevant problem can work; the FU2 hook ("the heat used in manufacturing destroys the creatine inside") and the NAD+ hook (famous actor plus the words "NAD+" and "peptides") both open curiosity gaps and he rates both hooks good. [8e0335ccf918] [2ef96388e6ab]
3. **The hook and the body must serve the same awareness stage.** A top-of-funnel attention hook followed by a bottom-of-funnel feature and ingredient tour "makes no sense." Condition: a product-feature body can work on product-aware, bottom-of-funnel viewers if the hook matches it. Evidence: Magic Mind example. The presenter says he is "bored by this stage unless I'm product aware." [cb664cb20762]
4. **Do not show or say the product in the first seconds of a top-of-funnel ad.** The Magic Mind ad shows the product on screen, says its name around "the third word" and puts it in the primary text at the top. He counts this as reason 2 the hook is bad. Condition: he frames this against a top-of-funnel hook; for a product-aware ad he does not say early product mention is wrong. [cb664cb20762] [8e0335ccf918]
5. **The bridge (hook to body to product) must not hard-sell too early.** The presenter's word "bridge" covers the move into the product. In the FU2 ad he loved "everything before it" and the "first seven eight seconds", then "it introduces the product too early, too aggressively." In IM8 he calls the product entry "a very aggressive bridge." Condition: his view from 2 examples; he calls the FU2 cause a guess ("I'd probably say"). [94e95729b482] [89a475819a6d]
6. **Explain the mechanism, the "why," before naming the product.** Problem-aware viewers need to know what the mechanism is, why it declines and why that causes the problem. The NAD+ ad never explains what NAD is or why it drops, so he places it at solution aware, not problem aware. Condition: solution-aware viewers already know the mechanism and need reinforcement, difference and offer. [4e1cb65d14b8] [4f6f2e7601e9] [a48f56ddf075]
7. **Match the depth of education to the stage you want to serve, and place the ad by that stage.** Classify an ad by how much it explains. Use the stage to decide where it runs rather than force 1 ad to serve every stage (card wording; the transcript only makes the stage calls). [4e1cb65d14b8]
8. **Widen the call-out across related personas.** "You have IBS, leaky gut, or bloating" names 3 personas and "increases the TAM slightly." "If he just said leaky gut, it would lose most people." Condition: stay inside the product's true market; card caveat says broadening too far weakens relevance. [51a88883a763]
9. **After naming a condition, prove the viewer likely has it.** IM8 names "dysbiosis" then says "40% of people have it. Most of them don't even know." That line does 2 jobs: grows the perceived market and makes the viewer self-identify. The mistake is to say the clinical word and move on; viewers think "what did that word mean" and "I definitely don't have that." [2656d0241771] [51a88883a763]
10. **Do not narrow positioning below the real market.** For supplements "the main market is sedentary people ... majority of the population." "It's not for the everyday sedentary crowd. This is more for your serious athlete" cuts the TAM so much "you can almost guarantee that this will like never be a hundred million business." Elite athletes are also hard to sell: coaches oversee what they take and compliance is strict. Condition: his opinion, stated about 1 Australian creatine brand. [df555261a040]
11. **Sell benefits that apply without the core activity.** For creatine he would sell sleep deprivation, brain health "regardless of whether you go to the gym or not." He adds "Alzheimer's. I'm not sure if that's a compliant claim." Treat that one as unverified and likely non-compliant. [df555261a040] [10c228fa0ec1]
12. **Features must become benefits.** The transcript shows this through criticism of Magic Mind's "fruiting body mushroom extracts", "ceremonial grade matcha", "nanoencapsulation" list. The card example "velvet suede material" to "taking a nap at work" is from the header note, not in transcript. [10c228fa0ec1]
13. **Agitate against an enemy and handle the real objection.** The NAD+ ad makes injections the enemy: "a little bit painful ... uncomfortable and it's expensive." The Devoted shaker ad handles "you go online and buy a metal one only to realize that it is still made out of plastic." He likes both. 14. **Match word precision to the audience.** "Three simple ingredients. Electrolytes, magnesium, and creatine" fails with elite athletes because magnesium is an electrolyte. 15. **A good script can still fail with no motion.** Devoted: "the scripting there isn't actually that bad", but a static, unmoving talking-head shot through a long script will "drop people due to the lack of movement." 16. **Native settings read as organic; studio setups read as ads.** The NAD+ founder films in his car. 17. **Borrowed fame plus a trending word makes a strong hook.** The NAD+ ad opens on "the new Transformers actor" with "NAD+" and "peptides" as on-screen text. 18. **Remove AI-script tells, above all contrast negation.** "Your gut isn't broken, it's starving" is "a huge giveaway that this is an AI scripted ad, which is it is not X, it is Y." Condition: his style view; the IM8 ad he calls "incredible" still contains both lines. [ba72533dddbc]
19. **Stack proof after the product.** IM8 closes with 12-week trial percentages and "92 nutrients", "using as much social proofing and credibility stacking as possible." [a48f56ddf075] [10c228fa0ec1]
20. **Fix the weak segment, keep the strong one.** Diagnose hook, bridge and body apart; he blames FU2's bridge while praising its hook and early education, and guesses IM8 already runs "a variant" with a longer education section. [89a475819a6d]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Script structure (the IM8 VSL pattern, the source's model ad)

The presenter reviews only the first 30 to 40 seconds of a VSL he says "normally go for 3 to 4 minutes." The order below is the IM8 script as heard, plus his 1 suggested change. [a48f56ddf075] [94e95729b482] [2656d0241771]

1. Persona call-out on 2 to 3 related conditions. Example: "You have IBS, leaky gut, or bloating."
2. Curiosity line. Example: "You're probably missing this one thing."
3. Problem agitation through past failed fixes. Example: "Your doctor probably told you to just manage it with a course of pills. That's because they're treating the symptom, not the cause."
4. Reframe the cause (mechanism). Example: trillions of bacteria; good bacteria die, bad take over, inflammation, leaky gut, poor nutrient absorption. Do not write this as "isn't X, it's Y."
5. Name the condition, then prove prevalence and invisibility. Example: "This is dysbiosis and it's not rare. 40% of people have it. Most of them don't even know." Then "You might be among them."
6. Attack every past solution. Example: "every gut supplement you've tried ... just throw probiotics at it."
7. His suggested change, before the product: 4 to 6 more seconds of education on the full mechanism (prebiotics and postbiotics are needed for probiotics to work). Then bridge with a convenience benefit: 1 product instead of many supplements "before, during, after you eat," "one sachet."
8. Product and mechanism steps. Example: "first digestive enzymes ... second prebiotics ... third ... probiotics ... fourth postbiotics."
9. Proof stack. Example: 12-week trial, 85% better digestion and less bloating; then "92 nutrients"; 95% more energy, 80% slept better, 75% sharper (cut off in transcript).
10. Close. The transcript does not include the IM8 close or offer.

### 3.2 Script structure for a shorter problem-solution ad (from the FU2 and NAD+ critiques)

Source is thin here; this is his critique turned into order, not a template he wrote. [94e95729b482] [89a475819a6d] [4e1cb65d14b8]

1. Hook that names a known problem or industry narrative. Example: "The problem with creatine gummies is that the heat used in the manufacturing actually destroys the creatine inside making it useless." He likes it because it matches a narrative buyers have already heard ("most creatine gummies don't actually have creatine").
2. Proof of the problem. Example: "The studies done on creatine gummies in 2025 showed many major brands had almost zero creatine." `[web check: that 2025 study exists and says this before reusing the claim]`
3. Bridge with a story, not the product. His rewrite: add a line on the founder doing his own research, then a line on him "reaching directly out to suppliers internationally" because local vendors failed him. Only then name the product.
4. Explain why the product works, at the depth the target stage needs (see 3.4).
5. Benefits and objection handling.
6. Offer. Do not end abruptly: the FU2 ad "bridged very aggressively and then just ended ... That ad should have definitely kept coming."

### 3.3 Hook writing and review

[8e0335ccf918] [cb664cb20762] [51a88883a763] [2ef96388e6ab]

1. Pick the stage the whole ad serves (top, middle, bottom). `[needs brand data: which stage the target ad set or audience serves]`
2. Write a hook that names the viewer's problem or persona, or a known industry narrative.
3. If the market is broad, list 2 to 3 related personas or symptoms instead of 1.
4. If you use a visual pattern-break, tie it to the problem. Reject props with no link to the viewer's problem (the knife).
5. For top-of-funnel hooks, keep the product off screen, out of the first words and out of the top of the primary text.
6. Optional authority hook: a known face, on-screen trending terms, shot in a native setting. Clear the rights first. `[web check: right-of-publicity and Meta ad policy on using a public figure's likeness in the ad's market]`
7. Test the hook with 1 question: does a relevant viewer think "this is about me," or does everyone think "what is going on?"
8. Check the body stage matches the hook stage.

Card 8e0335ccf918 says "opening 3-5 seconds" for the hook; the transcript gives no hook length. The only timing in the transcript is FU2's good opening "first seven eight seconds."

### 3.4 Awareness-stage check for the body

[4e1cb65d14b8] [cb664cb20762]

1. List what the body explains: problem, mechanism, why the mechanism fails, product, features, proof, offer.
2. Mostly features, ingredients, absorption and trial stats = product aware, bottom of funnel (Magic Mind).
3. Asserts a cause ("NAD levels dropping") without explaining what it is or why = solution aware (NAD+ ad).
4. Explains the mechanism and why the viewer has the problem before the product = problem aware (IM8).
5. If the target is problem aware and the "why" is missing, add it before the pitch or expect weak results with that group.
6. Placement by stage (deployment, neighbour folder 24-DEP): the card suggests running each ad at the stage it fits. The source gives no targeting or campaign steps. `[verify UI]`

### 3.5 Shot list and edit pass for a talking-head ad

The source gives edit principles, not a shot list. Everything below is from his comments. [82b6f200aecd] [2ef96388e6ab]

1. Do not run a static, unmoving talking head through a long script.
2. Add "a few overlays through there."
3. Add "a few different cuts."
4. At minimum, add handheld motion: the speaker holding the camera "pulling out, pulling in."
5. Add B-roll (named in the header note and card, not in the spoken critique).
6. Cut rate: the header note says "cutting at least every 3 seconds" (header note, not in transcript). He himself gives no number.
7. Setting: film in a native place such as a car, not a studio. Avoid a visible "three cameras" and "lighting rig" look.
8. On-screen text: put the trending or curiosity terms in large text at the top (NAD+ ad shows "NAD+" and "peptides").
9. Format: interview or conversation framing ("What is your stack of peptides that you like?") reads as content, not a commercial.

### 3.6 Diagnose an underperforming video ad

[89a475819a6d] [cb664cb20762]

1. Split the ad into hook, bridge, body. Watch each alone.
2. Hook: qualifies the persona, or only grabs attention? Matches the body's stage?
3. Bridge: does the product arrive before the mechanism is clear? Is the sell "too early, too aggressively"?
4. Body: does it educate, handle objections, turn features into benefits, explain terms it names?
5. Edit: is there motion (overlays, cuts, camera moves)?
6. Name 1 weakest segment. Keep the strong segments. Build a variant that changes only that segment.
7. Pull real metrics before blaming a segment. He used "three likes" over "a couple weeks," which is weak. `[needs brand data: hook rate, hold rate, CTR, CPA, spend per ad from the ad account]`

### 3.7 Script QA before production

[ba72533dddbc] [4f6f2e7601e9] [ee8baa9c8041] [df555261a040]

1. Search for "isn't ... it's", "not X, it's Y". Rewrite as a plain, specific claim.
2. Search for stock analogies ("single soldier into a war zone") and generic science tone. Rewrite.
3. Check every term against the audience's expertise (magnesium is an electrolyte).
4. Every named condition or mechanism is followed by why the viewer has it and what it does.
5. Every number has a defensible source (card rule; the transcript only reads numbers out). `[needs brand data: study, test or order data behind each claim]`
6. Every offer ("up to 16% off today") matches live price, dates and checkout. `[needs brand data: current offer and dates]`
7. Remove disease claims unless cleared for the market. `[web check: health claim rules for the sell market, for example FTC, TGA or ASCI]`
8. Persona is not narrower than the real market. `[needs brand data: customer mix by use case]`
9. When using an AI writer, add the standing line "Do not use contrast negation."

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Weird visual hook with no link to the viewer's problem (knife in product). [8e0335ccf918]
2. Top-funnel hook in front of a bottom-funnel feature tour. [cb664cb20762]
3. Product shown and named in the first words of a top-funnel ad. [cb664cb20762]
4. Bridge that sells too early or too hard ("how do you know who to trust? That's why I started ..."). [94e95729b482]
5. Ending the ad right after the bridge. [89a475819a6d]
6. Listing features and ingredients instead of benefits. [10c228fa0ec1]
7. Static talking head through a long script, no overlays, cuts or motion. [82b6f200aecd]
8. Studio look (3 cameras, lighting rig) that screams "ad." [2ef96388e6ab]
9. Naming a clinical term ("dysbiosis") and moving on. [2656d0241771]
10. Claiming a cause (NAD decline) without saying what it is or why. [4e1cb65d14b8]
11. Wrong or overlapping terms for an expert audience (magnesium as separate from electrolytes). [4f6f2e7601e9]
12. Positioning a mass-market product to a narrow elite group ("serious athlete"). [df555261a040]
13. Single-condition call-out that most viewers self-exclude from ("leaky gut" alone). [51a88883a763]
14. AI tells: contrast negation, stock war analogies. [ba72533dddbc]
15. Unverified or non-compliant claims (he floats Alzheimer's himself, then doubts it). [df555261a040] [ee8baa9c8041]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- Evidence is 1 presenter's opinion on 5 ads. No spend, reach, CTR, hold rate or sales. The only number used as performance is a like count. The "20 times more engaging" is an impression.
- He admits guesses: FU2's bridge "I'd probably say" caused underperformance; IM8 "probably already" has a restructured variant.
- Self-tension: he calls the IM8 ad "incredible" end to end while it breaks 2 of his rules (contrast negation, aggressive bridge). He calls the NAD+ script "sloppy" yet says it performs well. So neither rule is a hard kill rule by his own grading.
