## 1. What this source is for

It helps a seller start live auction selling on Whatnot from zero followers. It covers how small the first show should be, how often to go live, how to host an empty room, what to sell and at what price, when to sell to resellers instead of end buyers, how to add entertainment so viewers stay, when to use the buyer appreciation giveaway, and how the first sale ships. It is strongest on cold-start mindset, show size and inventory choice. It is thin on platform mechanics (the presenter sends viewers to Whatnot's Seller Academy for those), fees, analytics and follower growth measurement. It says nothing about paid ads. See section 9 for folder fit.

## 2. Principles

1. **Start small: 1 item, 1 photo, same-day show, about 15 minutes.** Condition: first shows on an account with no or few followers. The presenter says new sellers who schedule a show 2 months out, promote it daily, and load 100 items with 3 or 4 photos each often sell 5 or 10 things and quit. Evidence: posts he has seen for about 1.5 years in the Facebook group "Whatnot for Resellers", plus his own 1-item test (section 7). [026fb6c3c7e0] [0a79429bbe38]

2. **Your first show will probably be bad, and that is expected.** Condition: any account without followers. The presenter says a weak first show is caused by having no followers, not by the platform. He does not say how many shows it takes. [026fb6c3c7e0] [fb5bc410481b]

3. **More short shows beat fewer long shows.** Condition: cold start. Source says: "it's way better to do four 15 minutes shows for 4 days then one a 1 hour show for one day". The card adds the reason that each show is a data point and a chance to add followers; the presenter's own reason, given under tip 3, is that you need enough shows to have "enough data to understand the platform". [026fb6c3c7e0] [20f3563bbac8]

4. **Be consistent; do not judge the platform on 1, 2 or even 5 shows.** Condition: deciding whether to keep going. The presenter compares it to YouTube, where his channel took about 9 months of weekly uploads before it could make money. He cites group members who did 2 one-hour shows, sold 5 items, and quit. He also admits Whatnot "doesn't work for everybody". The 9-month figure is his YouTube history, not a Whatnot benchmark. [20f3563bbac8]

5. **Whatnot is partly entertainment. Viewers browse, they do not come to buy a set item.** Condition: all shows. The presenter says people "go to whatnot to just peruse" and see if the seller can entertain them. His causal claim: the longer you keep someone in the show, the more likely they buy. Evidence: his opinion; no watch-time or conversion data. [5b0898e60baf]

6. **Have fun and be energetic. Never show resentment at low bids.** Condition: all shows. He contrasts fun hosts with shows that feel "like funeral homes". A seller who starts at $1 and gets upset when an item sells for $5 pushes viewers to leave "and find somebody else that's more fun to watch and more appreciative of their attention". [5b0898e60baf] [89b5796ecdf1]

7. **Start the auction at the lowest price you will accept.** Condition: auctions. Source: "if you want to get $20 fore item you need to start it at $20". A low start is a choice to accept a low result. This sits beside his praise of a top seller who starts at $5 and the golf seller who starts hats at $1 (see section 9 on how these fit together). [89b5796ecdf1]

8. **Add a reason to stay that is not the product.** Condition: when a viewer may not care about the item on screen. His test: if a show only sells camera batteries, a viewer who does not want camera batteries has no reason to be there. Examples he gives: singing Disney songs, podcast-style story talk while selling, a friend's $100 Buy It Now that earned the buyer's username tattooed on the seller, and an untested idea of 1 drop of hot sauce on a chicken wing per $1 bid. He says of the tattoo "not something I would personally do" and of the hot sauce "I'm not saying that's a good idea or not". These are illustrations of the principle, not recommendations. [5b0898e60baf]

9. **Sell things people buy on impulse.** Condition: when you want top price from end buyers. Test: would a browsing viewer think "that's cute", "that's interesting" or "that's useful"? Very specific items (his example: a box of size 17 men's orthopedic shoe inserts) need 1 exact buyer to be live at that moment, so they sell poorly. His mental model is the checkout-line area at TJ Maxx, because that store "has done studies" on what people buy unplanned. That is an analogy, not Whatnot data. Categories he says sell well in his experience: Disney items, small coffee mugs, small aprons, gourmet snacks and candy. He says candy works because it is consumable and people will pay $5 or $10 for it. [3549708097d1]

10. **Or sell to resellers, and accept a lower price.** Condition: specific or niche items, bulk lots, and any show before you have an audience. Resellers will buy almost anything if they can flip it, but you get less money. Price below the item's resale value so the buyer still profits. He says selling to resellers is much easier than selling to the final buyer when starting out. [66873165c10c]

11. **Buyers expect deep deals, around 90% off, not 15 to 20% off.** Condition: pricing and sourcing. His basis: large clothing sellers selling $200 to $300 dresses for $15 that they buy wholesale at about $3 to $5 each, so they still "double, triple" their money. His rule: source so you can sell at $5, or even $2, and still profit. Evidence: his observation of 2 large sellers, not platform data. [1f45deb42b47] [3549708097d1]

12. **The fast nickel beats the slow dime while you build an audience.** Condition: cold start, items you could also list on eBay. Selling a $60 to $65 item for $30 on Whatnot trades margin for speed and zero listing work. The eBay route costs 6, 8 or 10 photos, a title, a fault description, required item specifics, a 30 to 60 day wait, buyer questions, and a likely return from a buyer who did not read the description. He frames the low price as a short-term sacrifice: at 200 to 300 viewers per show he says he "sometimes" gets full market value with no listing work. [66873165c10c] [1f45deb42b47]

13. **Use the buyer appreciation giveaway only once you have regulars.** Condition: about 20 to 40 people coming to your shows on a regular basis. "I probably wouldn't worry about it at first." Mechanic: anyone who buys during the show, by auction or Buy It Now, can enter a draw for a better item at the end. It makes "bulk mediocre items" easier to sell, and he says the extra money from those items usually pays for the prize. [89b5796ecdf1] [1f45deb42b47]

14. **Talk as if 100 people are there, even when 0 or 1 are.** Condition: every show, most of all the first. He names the emotional difficulty of talking to an empty room as the hardest part of a first show, and says "fake it till you make it". Greet each person who arrives. [fb5bc410481b] [5b0898e60baf]

15. **Be plain and honest about condition.** Condition: any flawed item. In his test he said several times on stream that the Game Boy did not turn on, lights up when charged, and has a couple of scratches. The card adds "honest in the title and thumbnail"; the actual title was "help me sell this game boy", which does not state the fault, so the disclosure was on stream. [0a79429bbe38] [fb5bc410481b]

16. **Learn the technical side from Whatnot's own Seller Academy.** Condition: listing, shipping, labels. He says the academy videos are short and "explain everything perfectly straight from the source", and that he thinks Whatnot should make it mandatory with quizzes; it is not mandatory. [8119cbfa7173] [verify UI]

17. **Use the first-show incentive as a cheap test, not a strategy.** Condition: new sellers. He reports Whatnot matches first-show sales up to $100, later shown as $150 on the apply screen. The card adds that because it is capped it is runway for testing, not scaling; that framing is the card's, not the presenter's. [8119cbfa7173] [web check: current first-show match amount and terms]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Cold-start test show (the presenter's own run)

Goal: sell 1 item on an account with no audience, to learn the platform.

1. Apply to sell. He says the form takes about 2 minutes and most people are approved the same day, or within 24 to 48 hours. [8119cbfa7173] [web check: current approval time]
2. Watch the Whatnot Seller Academy videos for listing, shipping and labels. [8119cbfa7173] [verify UI]
3. Pick 1 item. Prefer one a reseller would buy and flip. His pick: a Game Boy Advance SP, NES edition, that would not turn on, sold as parts only. [66873165c10c]
4. Check the resale comp. His eBay comp for the same model, parts only: $60 to $65 plus shipping. [66873165c10c]
5. Set the price so a reseller can profit. His price: $30, with the buyer paying about $5 shipping. [66873165c10c] [needs brand data: item cost and fees to confirm the price is still profitable]
6. Schedule a show for the same day. Do not promote it for weeks. [026fb6c3c7e0] [verify UI]
7. Choose the category where a buyer for that item is most likely browsing. His: video games. [0a79429bbe38]
8. Take 1 photo of the item and use it as the thumbnail. [0a79429bbe38]
9. Write a short, plain title. His: "help me sell this game boy". He did not test it against other titles. [0a79429bbe38]
10. Go live. He did not show his face or his usual background, to stay anonymous; this was for the video experiment, not a general rule.
11. Talk the whole time, even to an empty room. [fb5bc410481b]
12. Greet each viewer by name as they arrive, thank them for stopping by, and ask if they want the item. [fb5bc410481b]
13. Answer condition questions plainly. He told a viewer: it lights up when charging, it will not turn on, physically fine, a couple of scratches, charger somewhere but not found. [fb5bc410481b]
14. When it sells, thank the buyer warmly and ask the room to follow the buyer. [fb5bc410481b]
15. Ship (section 3.3).

Timing he reported: started at about 1:17 or 1:18 pm, show set up and item photographed by 1:21 pm, sale made with 6 to 7 viewers present, done by 1:33 pm. He says "less than 10 minutes" for the sale and "like 15 minutes for the whole thing" including set-up. Example, not a promise. [026fb6c3c7e0] [0a79429bbe38]

### 3.2 Building from zero after the first show

The source gives no step list for this. What it does give:

1. Run several short shows instead of 1 long one (his example: 4 shows of 15 minutes on 4 days, not 1 show of 1 hour). [026fb6c3c7e0]
2. Keep going past 5 shows before judging. He gives no target number. [20f3563bbac8]
3. Keep the energy up and add an entertainment layer (principle 8). [5b0898e60baf]
4. Sell impulse items or sell to resellers (principles 9 and 10). [3549708097d1] [66873165c10c]
5. At about 20 to 40 regular viewers, start using the buyer appreciation giveaway (section 3.4). [89b5796ecdf1]
6. He says a show with 10, 15 or 20 viewers on average "becomes a lot easier". He does not say how long that takes. [fb5bc410481b]

The card adds "stay on a fixed schedule so returning viewers know when to find you" [20f3563bbac8]. The presenter says "stay consistent" but does not mention a fixed time slot. Treat the fixed schedule as a card inference.

### 3.3 Shipping the sale

1. Estimate the packed weight when listing. His estimate: 11 oz. [ab2463ddeda4]
2. Pack the item and weigh it. His actual: 9.9 oz, "pretty spot on". [ab2463ddeda4]
3. Confirm the weight in the app, then click "generate label". It makes the shipping label and the packing slip together. [ab2463ddeda4] [verify UI]
4. If the label comes out in 8.5 x 11 format, go to settings, change the label size to 4 x 6, and regenerate. He hit this issue. [ab2463ddeda4] [verify UI]

He does not say what happens if the weight is far off. The card says "ship promptly" and "same-day fulfillment"; the presenter only says "let's go ship it". Same-day shipping is a card inference. [ab2463ddeda4]

### 3.4 Buyer appreciation giveaway

1. Wait until you have about 20 to 40 regular viewers. [89b5796ecdf1]
2. Pick a prize worth more than the items in the show. His example: a Scotty Cameron putter, about $250, at a golf show. [89b5796ecdf1]
3. Auction low-excitement items cheaply. His example: basic golf hats at $1 each. People paid $5 or $10 for hats they might not have bought, to get a chance at the putter. [89b5796ecdf1] [1f45deb42b47]
4. Every buyer during the show, auction or Buy It Now, is eligible to enter. The draw happens at the end of the show. [89b5796ecdf1] [verify UI: how entry works and any platform rules]
5. Check that the extra revenue from the small items covers the prize cost. He says it "usually" does and "it all cancels out". No numbers beyond the example. [89b5796ecdf1] [needs brand data: item cost, prize cost, show sales]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **First-show over-build check.** How: before the first show, count items, photos per item and days of promotion. Good: 1 item, 1 photo, same day. Bad: dozens of items, multi-photo listings, weeks of promotion. Next: cut to 1 item and go live today. [026fb6c3c7e0]
2. **Quit-too-early check.** How: count shows run before judging results. Bad: judging after 1 to 5 shows, or after 2 long shows. Next: commit to a longer run of short shows. The source gives no number that counts as "enough". [20f3563bbac8]
3. **Impulse test per item.** How: ask whether a browsing viewer with no plan would say "cute", "interesting" or "useful". Good: yes, and low price. Bad: needs 1 exact buyer (size 17 insoles). Next: sell bad-fit items in bulk to resellers, or elsewhere. [3549708097d1]
4. **Reseller margin check.** How: comp price minus your price minus shipping. Good: the buyer can still flip it (his case: $60 to $65 comp vs $30 plus about $5 shipping). Bad: no room left for a reseller. [66873165c10c] [needs brand data: your cost and platform fees to confirm your own margin] [web check: Whatnot seller fees]
5. **Sourcing floor check.** How: compare unit cost to a $2 to $5 sale price. Good: still profitable. Bad: thrift cost of $5 to $8 per piece against a likely $5 sale. Next: change sourcing, or use the giveaway once you qualify. [3549708097d1] [1f45deb42b47]
6. **Start-price check.** How: before each auction, ask whether you would be happy at the start price. Bad: starting at $1 while wanting $20. Next: start at the price you want. [89b5796ecdf1]
7. **Host attitude check.** How: watch a replay or ask a friend. Bad: flat, uninterested ("funeral home"), or visible annoyance at low bids. Next: raise energy, thank every bidder. [5b0898e60baf] [89b5796ecdf1]
8. **Reason-to-stay check.** How: for each show, ask what a viewer who does not want the current item gets from staying. Bad: nothing. Next: add talk, stories, music or a bid-linked stunt. [5b0898e60baf]
9. **Giveaway readiness and math.** How: count regular viewers; add up expected extra sales from small items vs prize cost. Good: 20 to 40 regulars and extra sales cover the prize. Bad: launching it at 0 followers. [89b5796ecdf1] [needs brand data: regular viewer count, show sales, prize cost]
10. **Label check.** How: look at the first generated label. Good: 4 x 6. Bad: 8.5 x 11. Next: change the setting and regenerate. [ab2463ddeda4] [verify UI]
11. **Follower-to-repeat-buyer check.** The card says to track whether early shows turn new followers into returning buyers [1f45deb42b47]. The presenter does not say this or say how to measure it. Card inference. [needs brand data: follower count and repeat buyers by show]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Over-preparing the first show: scheduled 2 months out, promoted daily, 100 items with 3 or 4 photos each. [026fb6c3c7e0]
2. Reading a weak first show as proof the platform does not work. [026fb6c3c7e0]
3. Quitting after 2 one-hour shows, or after 5 shows. [20f3563bbac8]
4. Running a flat, joyless show ("funeral home"). [5b0898e60baf]
5. Starting at $1, then getting angry at buyers when it sells for $5. "That's not their problem, that's your problem." [89b5796ecdf1]
6. Selling only 1 narrow product with no reason for a non-buyer to stay (camera batteries example). [5b0898e60baf]
7. Listing very specific items for end buyers (size 17 orthopedic insoles). [3549708097d1]
8. Pricing too high: Whatnot buyers want deep deals. [1f45deb42b47]
9. Sourcing at prices that cannot be profitable at a $2 to $5 sale (thrift polos at $5 to $8). [3549708097d1]
10. Starting the buyer appreciation giveaway before you have regular viewers. [89b5796ecdf1]
11. Going silent while waiting for viewers to arrive. [fb5bc410481b]
12. Learning the technical side from third-party videos instead of the Seller Academy. [8119cbfa7173]
13. Printing labels at 8.5 x 11 without checking the label size setting. [ab2463ddeda4]
