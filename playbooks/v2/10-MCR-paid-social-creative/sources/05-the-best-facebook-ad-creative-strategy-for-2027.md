## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator plan where Meta ad creatives come from and what mix of formats to run. It gives a sourcing split (the "33% rule": in-house, UGC creator army, AI), a funnel split (top, middle, bottom), a map of formats to 5 awareness stages, a case against making many variants of 1 winner, a TikTok creator-seeding workflow, a "creative flywheel" loop, and "personification" (new avatars) as a scaling lever for large accounts. Use it to build a monthly creative plan and a format checklist. It is thin on kill rules, test reads, budgets and any numeric health threshold.

## 2. Principles

1. **Creative supply limits scale.** Source says accounts fail to scale because founders do not supply the volume, diversity and styling of creative the account needs to hit its KPIs and spend goals. No numbers given. [7ef548d9c0df]
2. **The 33% rule, sourcing side: 1/3 in-house, 1/3 UGC, 1/3 AI.** In-house means anything the team makes (Canva, own filmed video). UGC means a "creator army" from TikTok. AI means cloned or generated ads. Condition: applies to any spend level the source names ($1,000 to $1M a month). Evidence: presenter opinion from his $600M claim; the card calls it a starting heuristic, not a measured optimum. [7ef548d9c0df]
3. **The 33% rule, funnel side: 1/3 top, 1/3 middle, 1/3 bottom of funnel.** Source's worked number: "five top of funnel, five middle of funnel and five bottom of funnel ads", so 15 ads. Top of funnel is "educational", "discovery style". Then each funnel third is itself split across AI, UGC and in-house. The source does not say how the 5 awareness stages map onto the 3 funnel thirds. [7ef548d9c0df]
4. **Run all 3 pillars, never only 1.** Each does a different job. AI gives testing speed; UGC gives volume and authenticity; in-house gives trust and control. "You want to have a healthy mix of this because it's performing best this way." No data shown for "performing best". [7ef548d9c0df] [c7a8123193e9]
5. **AI pillar: fast and cheap angle testing, weak on trust.** Source says you can "copy and replicate any static ad creative" with Higgsfield, Imaginative or Foreplay, in minutes, for "a few dollars" each. Limits: AI UGC video is "not necessarily the most cost efficient"; AI cannot replace the trust of real UGC or founder content "if people recognize that it's AI". Source predicts the edge will shrink as competitors "catch on". Condition: see section 9 on copying competitor ads. [9395469a9874] [7ef548d9c0df]
6. **UGC pillar: a creator army, not a few creators.** Get many TikTok creators across ages, backgrounds, niches and demographics, so you reach more audiences and "fill the feeds" with volume. Source also says it adds net new customers to your community. Source calls it "by far one of the biggest levers a founder can make for your content volume". Limit: UGC cannot give warehouse, behind-the-scenes or founder-story content. Evidence: Kiara brand example (800+ paid collab ads) and one agency case (section 7). [f06f1f445e76] [7ef548d9c0df]
7. **In-house pillar: the trust anchor, least scalable.** Founder story, team in videos, product versus others, behind the scenes. Source says it is "the most authentic place to create content", lets buyers "empathize with the founder", and "is one of the greatest ways to scale your brand". Limits: it is capacity bound (team must make every asset) and "very easy for your competitors to replicate". [e7ff1f72cfb2]
8. **Founder stories are worth running; the source disagrees with the "build a product brand, not a founder brand" camp.** Source: "a lot of people out there say you shouldn't be making founder story ... I totally disagree." Good material: quitting a job, a shortcoming with the product, a supplier that "messed up the design of your clothing". Reason given: it is relatable and people "want to support small business". Condition: the presenter's opinion; the source itself says many advise the opposite. [e7ff1f72cfb2]
9. **Map every creative to a buyer awareness stage.** 5 stages: unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware. Each stage gets its own formats (section 3.3). The goal is to walk a buyer "from being unaware to being most aware in buying". [86dcadbf0b6b] [d2e3d5f918af]
10. **Put all stages in 1 ad set; let Meta pick who sees what.** "You're not segmenting these out into different audiences and directing Meta yourself." Source's reason: the Andromeda update "is favoring this structure" and the algorithm "knows when to show the creative at the right time based off of the buyer's awareness stage". Evidence: presenter claim, no test shown. `[web check: current Meta guidance on Andromeda and creative diversity in 1 ad set]` (deployment, neighbour folder 24-DEP) [14533d4ab993] [86dcadbf0b6b]
11. **Old way ("2026 strategy"): find 1 winning format, then make small variants. Source says this fails.** Variants change the background or small details of the same format. Result per source: "almost all of your ad spend ends up flowing into one to two creatives" (elsewhere "top three or four"), and when that format fatigues "your ad account tanks because there's no other creative styles for that budget to flow in". Source concedes it "somewhat works" but "completely misses the point". [80dbff5fec17] [fa341581e505]
12. **New way ("2027 strategy"): 10 to 15 distinct formats and angles at once.** "No one ad is alike." Each covers a unique angle and sits in the funnel. If 1 stops working "we have 14 other ad creatives". Named formats: three reasons why, before and after, try-on videos, comparisons, educational breakdowns, ingredient breakdowns. Condition: needs the 3-pillar supply in place (card caveat). [80dbff5fec17]
13. **A weak creative in a valid funnel slot stays on.** "Even if a creative out of those 15 you launch isn't necessarily performing, it still has a place in your ad account." "You're not turning off necessarily the bad ads." Some ads will do 2x, 3x, 5x, 7x ROAS. The source gives no rule for when an ad is truly dead. `[needs brand data: per-ad spend, purchases and margin over several windows before keeping a low-ROAS ad]` [fa341581e505] [80dbff5fec17]
14. **Levelled spend across creatives is the source's sign the structure is right.** "Your spend is leveled across all of your ad creatives. And this is how you know you did it right." Spend stuck in 1 to 4 creatives is the sign of the old way. No numeric threshold given. The card for this adds a check the source does not: judge purchases and contribution margin too, not spread alone. [5e88d4de1c4e] [80dbff5fec17] [14533d4ab993]
15. **Diverse formats slow fatigue and hold ROAS as spend rises.** Source's diagram: with variants, ROAS falls as spend rises, holds briefly after new variants, then drops at fatigue, in a repeating cycle. With 10 to 15 formats across the journey, "creatives aren't fatiguing very quickly and your ROAS is holding". Evidence: drawn curves only. [c3b5ba2b7356]
16. **Each pillar has a different speed, so blend them.** AI: minutes. In-house: about 30 minutes. UGC: 1 to 4 weeks, but massive volume. In-house also gives control, since AI "can sometimes drift off that actual formula" and creators "can also drift away from the script". [c7a8123193e9]
17. **The creator army is a standing sales force.** "This isn't a one-time thing." For any new product or offer, go back to creators who already have your product and get "thousands of videos back in a matter of weeks". Source calls it "one of the easiest and quickest ways you can raise ad spend". [f06f1f445e76]
18. **Use 2 brief types: wide for discovery, specific to multiply winners.** Wide brief: leave it open, let creators interpret the product and their own story. Specific brief: show top ads with their numbers and say "go make ads like these". The source gives no split between the 2. [58ba8efa52c9]
19. **The creative flywheel: winners from 1 pillar feed the other 2.** UGC surfaces an angle; tell creators it won; derive statics; make AI and in-house versions of it. Goal: perpetual fresh creative for Andromeda and stable ROAS while scaling. [9e6943f760aa] [58ba8efa52c9]
20. **Personification: when 1 avatar is maxed out, open new avatars.** Condition: source says it is for brands "spending thousands a day, 10,000 a day, and millions of dollars a month", after a plateau from "millions of impressions" over "many months or even years". Personify audiences, creatives and copy. Evidence: IM8 Health named as doing it; no numbers. [4db4b950e221]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Plan a 15-ad batch with the 33% rule
1. Set the batch at 15 ads: 5 top of funnel, 5 middle, 5 bottom. [7ef548d9c0df]
2. Within those, source about 1/3 from AI, 1/3 from UGC, 1/3 in-house. The source does not show a grid of which pillar makes which stage; section 3.4 shows AI filling missing formats. [7ef548d9c0df] [c7a8123193e9]
3. Make sure the 15 are distinct formats and angles, not variants (section 3.3 list). [80dbff5fec17]
4. Tag each asset with its pillar so you can see which source makes winners (card step, not in the source text). [7ef548d9c0df]
5. Launch all into the same ad set (section 3.2). [14533d4ab993]
`[needs brand data: current ad count by format, stage and source, to find gaps]`

### 3.2 Build the ad set (deployment, neighbour folder 24-DEP) [verify UI]
1. Build creatives, each mapped to 1 awareness stage. [14533d4ab993]
2. Load all of them into 1 ad set. Do not split stages into separate audiences. [14533d4ab993] [86dcadbf0b6b]
3. Let Meta serve each creative to the right person. [14533d4ab993]
4. Check that spend spreads across creatives from all stages. Skewed spend means the structure is wrong, per source. [14533d4ab993] [5e88d4de1c4e]
5. When you add new distinct formats, add them to the same ad set. [14533d4ab993]
The source gives no budget, bid, audience, placement or run time. Rest of the ad account setup is promised in "the next video", not here.

### 3.3 Fill the awareness-stage format map
Use this as the brief list. Examples are the source's; swap in your own true product facts. [86dcadbf0b6b] [d2e3d5f918af]
1. **Unaware** (buyer does not know they have a problem): educational, statistic-based ads that name the problem ("74% of people suffer from gut issues", with signs like feeling groggy, upset stomach, poor bowel movements); founder story on why you started the brand and the problem you faced; reveal a downside of something they already use ("did you know if you're taking GLP-1 there's side effects"), if your product solves it. Goal: move them to problem aware. `[needs brand data: a real, sourced stat for the brand's problem]`
2. **Problem aware**: before and after ("here's what life could look like on the other side"); feature call-outs ("this gummy is going to support your glow ... healthier skin ... fix your gut issues"); three reasons why ("the missing piece to what you're taking", "why gummies over other alternatives"). Goal: frame your product as the solution before they shop around.
3. **Solution aware** (they know how to solve it and will look at other products): us versus them comparisons ("here's why you should go with us"); social proof with reviews and quotes next to the product shot; testimonial UGC from creators who have used it over time and share personal stories.
4. **Product aware** (they researched you, visited the site, may have added to cart): limited-time offers, buy-one offers, bundles, plus more testimonial UGC "to push them over the edge". `[needs brand data: offers the brand can actually run]`
5. **Most aware** (have not converted yet): very direct ads. "You should go ahead and buy our product. Here's the final offer ... You've left this item in your cart." Sends them to the site to check out.
6. Brief every format as distinct. Do not restyle 1 winner across stages. [d2e3d5f918af]

### 3.4 Use the AI pillar
1. Pick a proven static, yours or another brand's, and clone or reproduce it with an AI tool (Higgsfield, Imaginative, Foreplay named). "We can launch and clone ads in minutes." See section 9 before copying anyone else's ad. [9395469a9874]
2. Produce many angle variants cheaply ("a few dollars") to find which hooks and claims land. [9395469a9874]
3. Fill format gaps the other pillars have not covered yet. Source names "three reasons why, educational breakdowns, ingredient breakdowns, whatever ad creative formats we're missing". [9395469a9874] [c7a8123193e9]
4. Feed AI winners to creators and the in-house team to remake in higher-trust formats. [9395469a9874]

### 3.5 Build a UGC creator army [verify UI]
1. Send mass invites to TikTok creators with an automation tool (Yuko named for invite automation). [f06f1f445e76]
2. Receive sample requests; approve creators. [f06f1f445e76]
3. Ship samples. Expect about 7 days shipping, then creators may hold the product about a week behind other brand work, "and then they may or may not even post". Total 1 to 4 weeks to get a creative back. [f06f1f445e76] [c7a8123193e9]
4. Ask for 2 to 3 videos per sample sent ("two to 3x the video to sample product"). The source does not say how to enforce this. [f06f1f445e76]
5. Expect content types such as reviews, unboxing and problem-solution angles. [f06f1f445e76]
6. Run the videos in TikTok GMV Max campaigns (transcript: "GMBB Max"), then move them to Meta ads so there is "constant ad creatives going into our ad account". The source does not give a rule for which videos move; the card says "the winners". [f06f1f445e76]
7. Run a creator community: Tribe (paid, named), or Discord (free). Invite creators to a private community with VIP incentives, and teach them what is working so they can make more videos "and money". [f06f1f445e76] [58ba8efa52c9]
8. Keep the army for every future launch or offer. [f06f1f445e76]
`[web check: current Tribe, Yuko and TikTok GMV Max features and prices]` `[web check: TikTok and Meta rules on moving creator content into paid ads and paid collab ads]`

### 3.6 Brief creators
1. Wide brief: give only the product; let creators tell it through their own story. Purpose: find angles you would not script. [58ba8efa52c9]
2. Specific brief: show top ads with real numbers. Source's example line: "this ad has gotten a 7x ROAS, 10,000 in spend. It's one of our best creatives. Go make ads like these." [58ba8efa52c9]
3. Post the winners to the whole community (Tribe or Discord) so volume shifts toward proven formats. [58ba8efa52c9]
4. When a UGC angle wins, hand it to in-house and AI to make more of that exact angle. [58ba8efa52c9] [9e6943f760aa]
`[needs brand data: which ads are top performers, with spend and ROAS, before writing a specific brief]`

### 3.7 Run the creative flywheel (repeat monthly, per card)
1. Source and automate UGC (section 3.5). [9e6943f760aa]
2. Brief creators on script styles (section 3.6). [9e6943f760aa]
3. Wait for videos; see what performs. [9e6943f760aa]
4. Tell the community which videos won so they make more like them. [9e6943f760aa]
5. Derive static formats from winning UGC videos. [9e6943f760aa]
6. Make AI and in-house versions of the winning angle. [9e6943f760aa]
7. Keep in-house producing brand-controlled assets in parallel. [9e6943f760aa]
8. Keep launching, find winners, scale, stabilise ROAS. The source does not say how to judge a winner or over what window. [9e6943f760aa]

### 3.8 Personification for a plateaued account
1. Name the current single avatar (source example: "people who struggle to fall asleep", sleep gummy). [4db4b950e221]
2. Confirm the plateau: spend has hit a ceiling after millions of impressions over months. The source gives no metric for this. `[needs brand data: spend, frequency and CPA trend for the main avatar]` [4db4b950e221]
3. List other problems the same product truly solves, each a different person. Source examples: people who sleep but wake up groggy; people who fall asleep fast but wake through the night (for example to use the restroom) or toss and turn. [4db4b950e221]
4. Check each avatar is a new problem, not a reworded one ("One struggles with falling asleep, the other does not"). [4db4b950e221]
5. For multi-benefit products, also list identities and use cases. IM8 Health examples: athlete, everyday mom who works out 3 days a week, busy professional, office worker, constant traveller, buying a nutritionist gift for a friend. [4db4b950e221]
6. Make new creatives and copy for each avatar. The card adds running the awareness-stage map inside each avatar; the source does not say this. [4db4b950e221]
7. Only claim benefits the product really has ("if our product also solves that problem"). `[needs brand data: substantiated benefits per avatar]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Spend concentration check.** How: pull spend by ad for the ad set or account. Good (per source): spend spread across many creatives of many formats. Bad: nearly all spend in 1 to 4 creatives of 1 format family. Next: add distinct new formats, do not make more variants; do not kill non-spenders first. Caveat from cards: some accounts concentrate spend for valid reasons (narrow targeting, product stage), and spread alone is not success; check purchases and margin. `[needs brand data: spend, purchases and margin by ad]` [5e88d4de1c4e] [80dbff5fec17] [fa341581e505]
2. **Variant check.** How: group live ads by format (for example "person walking in swimwear"). Bad: most live ads are the same format with a new person, background or small edit. Source's swimwear example shows this. Next: replace with distinct formats. [fa341581e505] [80dbff5fec17]
3. **Stage coverage check.** How: label each live ad with 1 of 5 stages. Bad: a stage has no ads, or all ads are education or all are offers. Next: fill gaps with section 3.3 formats, using AI for speed. [86dcadbf0b6b] [d2e3d5f918af]
4. **Pillar mix check.** How: label each ad by source (AI, UGC, in-house). Bad: 1 pillar only. Next: add the missing pillar; pre-seed UGC weeks ahead. [7ef548d9c0df] [c7a8123193e9]
5. **Fatigue cycle check.** How: plot spend versus ROAS over time (weekly, per card). Bad: ROAS falls as spend rises, recovers briefly with new variants, then drops again. Next: rebuild around distinct formats across the funnel. Card caveat: fatigue also depends on audience size, frequency and budget, which the source does not measure. [c3b5ba2b7356]
6. **Before killing an ad.** Ask: does it hold a distinct stage slot? If yes, source says keep it. The source gives no test for "truly dead"; the card suggests looking across several reporting windows. [fa341581e505] [5e88d4de1c4e]
7. **Avatar plateau check.** Signal per source: spend stops rising after long success on 1 avatar. Next: personification (section 3.8). No metric given. [4db4b950e221]
8. **UGC pipeline check.** Signal: creative supply cannot keep up with spend. Next: check lead time (1 to 4 weeks) and seed earlier; ask for 2 to 3 videos per sample. [c7a8123193e9] [f06f1f445e76]

## 7. Examples and case results (examples, not promises)

1. **Creator seeding case (unnamed client):** 100,000 invites in 60 days, 6,000 creators onboarded, about 5,800 videos, launched on TikTok and moved to Meta. That is under 1 video per creator, below the 2 to 3 per sample target. [f06f1f445e76]
2. **Kiara:** launched over 800 paid collab ads using creator seeding. No results given.
3. **Ingredient-label flywheel case (unnamed client):** client made all creative in-house, leading on benefits and ease of use, never on other products' labels. A creator video compared long ingredient labels on other products with the client's simple label and said they were switching. After finding that winner, account ROAS went from 1.7x to 4x "across his entire ad account". The client then made more AI and in-house creatives on that angle, and raised spend with ROAS "stable". No time frame or spend level given. [58ba8efa52c9]
4. **Sleep gummy angles (old way example):** 3 angles: fall asleep faster; your partner cannot sleep because you are up at night (partner may buy it for you); a quick fix for people who will not change their routine (phone, TV). The mistake is picking the best one and only making variants. [80dbff5fec17]

6. **IM8 Health:** addresses athletes, everyday moms who work out 3 days a week, busy professionals, office workers, constant travellers, and gift buyers for a nutritionist friend. [4db4b950e221]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Not supplying enough creative volume, diversity and styling, then wondering why the account will not scale. [7ef548d9c0df]
2. Relying on only 1 pillar (only AI, only UGC or only in-house). [7ef548d9c0df]
3. Using AI and expecting it to carry trust; audiences may recognise it. [9395469a9874]
4. Skipping founder and behind-the-scenes content because others say to build only a product brand. [e7ff1f72cfb2]
5. Launching pillars "for the sake of it" without mapping formats to stages: "you can still burn money fast". [86dcadbf0b6b]
6. Splitting awareness stages into separate audiences and "directing Meta yourself". [14533d4ab993]
7. Finding 1 winner and making only small variants of it (new background, small edits). [80dbff5fec17] [fa341581e505]
8. Ending up with only 1 format family (for example all education ads); when it fatigues the account tanks. [80dbff5fec17]
9. Turning off low-ROAS ads that still hold a funnel slot. [fa341581e505]
10. Getting only 1 video back per sample. [f06f1f445e76]
11. Treating the creator army as a one-time push instead of a standing asset. [f06f1f445e76]
12. Speaking to only 1 avatar at high spend and hitting a ceiling. [4db4b950e221]
13. Building great creative but a badly set-up ad account: "the best creative in the world still loses money if the ad account it runs in is built wrong". The fix is not in this source.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

1. **Copying other brands' ads conflicts with ecomOS rules.** The source says to "copy and replicate any static ad creative that we want" and "clone any ad". ecomOS rules say competitor ads are signals for layout and mood only; never copy text, logos, products or faces. Use AI to reproduce your own winners, or a layout idea only. [9395469a9874]
2. **"Keep the bad ads" conflicts with common kill-rule practice.** Most ecommerce buyers cut ads that spend without purchases after a set threshold. The source gives no threshold and no way to tell a stage-supporting ad from a dead one. The cards add hedges (margin, several windows) that are not in the source. `[needs brand data: kill and keep thresholds from the brand's CPA and margin]` [fa341581e505] [5e88d4de1c4e]
