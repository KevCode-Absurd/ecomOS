## 1. What this source is for

It helps a creative strategist or media buyer decide what to do when a proven winning video ad fatigues: keep the body and test new hooks, or scrap the asset and film new concepts. It gives 1 case (a testimonial ad revived with new hooks from a different person), 4 levers for making hooks truly different (angle, offer, avatar, format), a volume rule (more than 3 hooks), an effort argument (10 hooks cost about the same as 1 body), and a diagnosis for "we tried new hooks and none worked". It is thin on testing setup, budgets, kill rules and how to measure a hook.

## 2. Principles

1. **Do not stop hook testing because of Andromeda.** Condition: you have a proven ad body and are tempted to drop hook tests on the belief that Andromeda groups all minor variations into 1 ad. The source calls "stop hook testing because Andromeda wants diverse creative, so you need completely new assets every time" terrible advice. Evidence: 1 client case plus "what we're seeing performs well on accounts". The card adds that Andromeda groups visually and contextually identical ads (the header calls these Entity IDs), and that substantially different hooks avoid this; the transcript itself only says "it might count the bodies as the same ad and group them together. Well, I can tell you it doesn't, because this is still working." That is a performance claim, not a direct check of grouping. [2d5a90d21b0d] [web check: Meta documentation on Andromeda creative similarity and Entity ID grouping]
2. **Creative diversity is good, and always was.** Condition: always. The source agrees that new formats, angles and approaches are valuable, "not just because Andromeda rolled out, but forever". It rejects only the jump from "diversity matters" to "stop hook testing". It also agrees that avoiding "too many small variations of one ad" is "generally speaking good advice", but says it misleads people into under-testing hooks. [2d5a90d21b0d] [59cf922e4996]
3. **The hook is the highest-ROI part of a video ad.** Condition: video ads where most viewers drop before the body. Source's premise: 80 to 90% of viewers do not make it past the hook, so "90% of your advertising dollars are wasted after the hook" if the hook fails. He cites Ogilvy: "unless your headline sells your product you've wasted 90% of your money", and reads the video hook as the headline, so "90% of your effort should go" there. The card calls this a directional principle, not a literal budget split. [2e9838db8377] [2d5a90d21b0d] [needs brand data: hook rate or 3-second view rate and drop-off curve per ad] [web check: exact Ogilvy wording]
4. **A fatigued hero ad can often be revived by replacing only the first 3 to 5 seconds.** Condition: a proven body (the case is a testimonial) that has run a long time, stopped getting results and stopped scaling. Do this before filming new concepts. Evidence: 1 case, see section 7. [c8317f1a2291]
5. **New hooks must be new footage, ideally a different person, not cuts from the old video.** Condition: reviving a fatigued ad. Reason: most of your market has already seen the ad, saw the old hook, did not watch past it and moved on. Re-cut hooks from the same footage show them the same thing again. The goal is to re-serve the ad to people who already saw it, with a hook that makes them hold and watch through. [c8317f1a2291] [876d09a8a01a]
6. **Wording changes help some viewers; visual changes are needed to reach the rest.** Condition: when choosing how far to vary a hook. "Often just a change in wording can get a lot of people in, but it's not going to get everyone in. We do need a change in visuals." [876d09a8a01a] [ec923b82af65]
7. **Make hooks different on 4 levers: angle, offer, avatar, format.** Condition: planning hook variations for a proven body. You do not have to change all 4; the case changed 2 (angle and avatar). Changing all 4 is "definitely going to work, but only through volume" (source's claim). [ec923b82af65]
8. **Hook testing needs volume; 3 hooks is usually too few.** Condition: unless you are an exceptional scriptwriter who knows the audience deeply. The odds that 3 new hooks hit the market and sustain the old spend level are "pretty low". Chasing 1 perfect hook is also low odds; volume in the part that matters is preferred. [59cf922e4996] [2e9838db8377]
9. **The mechanics have not changed; the quality bar has gone up.** Condition: diagnosing failed hook tests. The source says hook testing has not changed "from 5 months ago to now", but "the bar of quality, the bar of requirement to make this work has gone up". Failed tests are usually weak or repurposed hooks, not proof the method is dead. [876d09a8a01a]
10. **Hooks are cheap relative to bodies, so put iteration effort there.** Condition: body far longer than the hook. Source's estimate for a roughly 75-second video: 10 versions of a 3 to 5 second hook take about the same effort as 1 body, because most shooting and editing effort sits in the body. [59cf922e4996] [2e9838db8377]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Revive a fatigued hero video ad with new hooks

The source gives this as a sequence of decisions, not a checklist. Steps below follow its order; nothing is added.

1. Confirm the ad was a true winner and is now fatigued: it ran for months, took most of the spend, and is "no longer getting results". The source gives no metric or threshold for fatigue. [c8317f1a2291] [needs brand data: ad-level spend, CPA or ROAS, and frequency over the ad's life]
2. Keep the proven body. Do not first reach for new testimonials, a full rework, or new formats of the whole ad (the "typical Andromeda creator advice"). [c8317f1a2291]
3. Do not re-cut hooks from the existing footage. [c8317f1a2291] [876d09a8a01a]
4. Pick which levers to change (section 3.2). The case changed angle and avatar. [ec923b82af65]
5. Shoot completely new hooks with a different person. In the case, the brand side (EGC, employee generated content) filmed hooks "talking about the testimonial", which then lead into the customer testimonial body (CGC, customer generated content). The source "recorded a bunch of different hooks with this particular client". [8fa61a97ae26] [c8317f1a2291]
6. Produce volume: more than 3 hooks. The case ran 4 hooks on each of 2 bodies (the bodies were also "reworked a little bit"). The source's general target is 10 versions of 3 to 5 seconds. [59cf922e4996]
7. Launch and judge against the old level: the case measure was performance "just as well as it ever has" at "the exact same amount of daily spend". The source does not say how the new ads were launched (same ad set, new ad set, budget) or how long it waited before judging. [c8317f1a2291] [needs brand data: pre-fatigue daily spend and CPA or ROAS as the bar]
8. If the first set fails, raise volume and vary more levers, up to all 4, before concluding the approach is dead. [ec923b82af65] [876d09a8a01a]

### 3.2 Build hook variations on the 4 levers

1. **Angle.** Change the messaging or context. Case: the testimonial's wording made it feel dated, so the hook was made "contextually relevant to this time of year" (seasonal framing). [ec923b82af65]
2. **Offer.** Change what is being offered or the call to action. Often the offer is not in the spoken hook at all; it may live in the Meta ad's primary text and headline. Examples: move from 1 pushed product to a bundle, or to a sale. The source had not yet tested this lever on the case ad ("we will do this as well on this ad eventually"). [ec923b82af65] [verify UI: where primary text and headline sit in the Meta ad setup]
3. **Avatar.** Change who delivers the hook. Case: an EGC presenter with a "very, very different" profile from the person in the body, which "hooks a bunch of new people that otherwise wouldn't have been hooked by the original avatar". [ec923b82af65] [8fa61a97ae26] [needs brand data: which people (staff, founder, customers) are available to film and how they differ from the body's presenter]
4. **Format.** Change the visual form of the first 3 to 4 seconds. Options the source lists: (a) a static hook for 3 seconds, then cut to the video; (b) a GIF or motion-graphic hook, for example a 3-second B-roll clip with a written testimonial or hook text over it, then transition into the video. [ec923b82af65]
5. Combine levers as needed: 1 lever, 2 levers (the case), or all 4. Pair any combination with volume. [ec923b82af65]

### 3.3 Structure an EGC hook plus CGC body ad

1. Keep the proven CGC testimonial body unchanged underneath. [8fa61a97ae26]
2. Film a new 3 to 5 second opening with an employee or brand-side person whose profile differs clearly from the customer in the body. [8fa61a97ae26]
3. Have the EGC hook talk about the testimonial, then transition into it. [8fa61a97ae26] [c8317f1a2291]
4. Where the body's wording feels dated, add an angle change in the EGC hook (for example seasonal context). [8fa61a97ae26] [ec923b82af65]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Is the winner really fatigued?** Check the ad's results over time at its usual daily spend. Bad: results falling for weeks while spend holds. The source gives no metric, window or threshold; set these from the account. [c8317f1a2291] [needs brand data: ad-level CPA or ROAS trend, frequency trend and spend over the ad's life]
2. **New hooks failed. Were they re-cuts?** Check whether the new hooks came from the existing video's footage or presenter. If yes, that is the likely cause: the audience already saw and rejected that opening. Next: shoot new footage with a different person. [876d09a8a01a]
3. **Did only the wording change?** If the hooks differ only in script, expect some lift at best. Next: add a visual change (new avatar, new format intro). [876d09a8a01a] [ec923b82af65]
4. **How many hooks were tested?** If 3 or fewer, the test was likely too small to find a winner. Next: produce more, toward 10. [59cf922e4996]
5. **How many levers moved?** Check each hook against angle, offer, avatar, format. If most hooks share the same lever values, they are near-duplicates. Next: spread hooks across different levers. [ec923b82af65]
6. **Is the body still sound?** The source keeps the body intact and "proven". If the body itself is dated in wording, use an angle change in the hook; the case also lightly reworked the body. [ec923b82af65] [59cf922e4996]
7. **Is Andromeda really grouping your variants?** The source says test on your own account rather than assume. It gives no method to check grouping. [2d5a90d21b0d] [verify UI: whether Ads Manager shows similar-ad grouping or creative similarity flags] [web check: Meta guidance on creative similarity]
8. **Before blaming the algorithm**, also rule out poor scripts or a mismatched audience (card caveat). [876d09a8a01a]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Stopping hook testing because "Andromeda will group variations". [2d5a90d21b0d] [59cf922e4996]
2. Throwing away a proven winner and filming new concepts from scratch when it fatigues. [c8317f1a2291]
3. Re-cutting new hooks from the existing video; the market has already seen and rejected them. [c8317f1a2291] [876d09a8a01a]
4. Changing only the script wording and expecting it to reach everyone. [876d09a8a01a]
5. Testing only 3 hooks out of fear of putting too much variation of 1 concept into the account. [59cf922e4996]
6. Pouring all effort into 1 "perfect" hook instead of volume. [2e9838db8377]
7. Concluding "hook testing no longer works" after a weak test, instead of raising quality and volume. [876d09a8a01a]
