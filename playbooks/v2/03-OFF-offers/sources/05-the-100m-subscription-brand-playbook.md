## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator design a subscription-first offer for a consumable product and raise first-order subscription take rate: what goes on the subscription option, what stays off the one-time option, how to lay out the buy box, how to align the pop-up, how to choose gifts, and how to match cadence to consumption. It then covers the rest of the subscription life cycle (onboarding, billing reminders, order confirmations, subscriber campaigns, cancel flow, win-back) at a practitioner level with example numbers.

## 2. Principles

1. **Retention is the bigger profit lever than CAC.** Condition: brands with a repeatable consumable. Source says top brands optimize for the 3rd to 4th order, not the first order, while others grind to cut CAC by 5 to 10%. Repeat orders come from customers you already paid to acquire. Opinion from agency experience. [9d7f6e7eec35]
2. **Step 0 is the product and its consumption model.** Condition: before setting any subscription target. A daily ritual product (supplement powder like AG1) retains better than occasion products (CBD gummies taken when wanted, an alcohol-alternative social drink, seasonings bought as needed). Seasonings may not suit a set subscription program at all. Do not compare your retention to a brand in another niche. Better product, better retention. The category ranking is his observation, not a measured benchmark. [9d7f6e7eec35] [needs brand data: own cohort retention by product and usage frequency]
3. **The offer is king.** Condition: acquisition of subscribers. Evidence: the Bobbie baby formula story. To slow demand during a supply shortage the brand turned off ads, then email and SMS, then flows, and new customers kept coming. Demand only fell when it removed its trial offer (trial bundle, large discount, subscribe and save). Single anecdote, told second hand. Source says a strong offer does not mean slashing margins. [7860f83f10e6]
4. **Optimize for the first order being a subscription.** Condition: consumable with repeat use. Evidence: 1 client's cohorts show 6-month LTV about $130 without a first-order subscription vs about $195 with one (+50%), about 2x at month 12 and 3x at month 24. He calls these "bare minimum standard numbers". Card caveat: part of the gap is selection bias, since subscribers may already be more committed. [d99fcf6edcf0] [needs brand data: 6, 12 and 24 month LTV split by first-order subscription yes or no]
5. **Put all of the offer on subscription; none on one-time.** Condition: you want to push take rate on a consumable. One-time stays at full price, small and discreet. Discount, welcome kit, guarantee and program access go on subscription only. Reason given: "the psychology that somebody buys when they place a subscription is just different". Pattern seen on IM8, H1, Everyday Dose. Card caveat: test margin impact and one-time demand before removing all one-time discount. [7860f83f10e6]
6. **Default bias decides take rate.** Condition: the buy box. People choose what is pre-selected and lean to middle options. So auto-select subscription, and make 2 of 3 options subscriptions so subscribing reads as what "everybody" does. [d8cbf6d9333d]
7. **Every channel shows the same subscription offer.** Condition: any pop-up, banner or email capture. A 10% pop-up that only works on one-time purchase makes a 20% subscription offer look weak and pulls buyers to one-time. The pop-up offer should be the subscription offer. [7860f83f10e6]
8. **People cancel because they do not use the product enough.** Condition: consumables. Low use leads either to no visible results or to too much product on hand, and both end in cancels. "Too much product" is usually the biggest cancel reason. So gifts, cadence, onboarding and reminders should all push consumption. [503d2fb72fc1] [167cf8e69e33] [1ae7b0ed8e41]
9. **Gifts must help the customer use the product.** Condition: welcome kit and first-order gifts. Low-cost tools that make daily use easier (frother, good spoon, bottle), not stickers or generic swag. Later gifts can shift to revenue expansion. Never gift more of the same product. [503d2fb72fc1]
10. **Cadence and pack size must match real use.** Condition: every subscription plan. A non-daily product on a 30-day plan over-supplies and drives angry cancels. A daily product with 30 servings on 30 days fits. [167cf8e69e33] [needs brand data: servings per pack and actual use rate per customer]
11. **Onboarding is the most underrated and biggest lever.** Condition: after first subscription order. Use post-purchase data (cancel reasons, usage surveys) to find what keeps people and teach it. [2762401ae2f2] [33976dbed0ac]
12. **Every message to a subscriber is a reminder that they can cancel.** Condition: all subscriber email and SMS. Send less around risky moments and to active subscribers, but do not ghost them; there is a middle ground. [33976dbed0ac] [1b7f42b5f9e3]
13. **Gifts only work if you tell people about them.** Condition: any gift roadmap. Communicate upcoming gifts in billing reminders and order confirmations. [503d2fb72fc1] [32e1b81135d0] [e9c9f0ad1a68]
14. **Set long timelines for results.** Condition: daily-use products. Tell customers to use the product 90 to 120 days or more before judging. Brands that promise early results see cancels at the promised point. "People need to be reminded more than they need to be taught", so repeat. [2762401ae2f2]
15. **Testing the offer is the biggest lever, in acquisition and in win-back.** Condition: throughout. "Just test, test your offers." [7860f83f10e6] [552ee801d868]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build the subscription-first offer (03-OFF core)
1. Classify the product by natural use frequency (daily ritual vs occasional). If occasional, plan for higher churn and design cadence around use rather than forcing 30 days. [9d7f6e7eec35]
2. Pull cohort LTV split by first order with or without subscription at 6, 12 and 24 months. Use the multiple to size how much discount and kit cost you can afford on the first subscription order. [d99fcf6edcf0] [needs brand data: cohort LTV, gross margin, kit cost]
3. Set the one-time price at full price, with no discount and no gifts. [7860f83f10e6]
4. Put a large first-order discount on subscription. Source examples: 30% off a 90-day supply (IM8), 55% off (Everyday Dose), a saving of €40 (H1). The source does not give a rule for how large. [7860f83f10e6]
5. Build a welcome kit: physical items that help consumption, each with its dollar value shown on the page (IM8 shows a kit worth $89). [7860f83f10e6] [503d2fb72fc1]
6. Add zero-cost digital value: a course, a wellness booklet, or program access (IM8: a 90-day transformation program). [7860f83f10e6]
7. Add a guarantee on the subscription option (IM8 shows one; terms not given). [7860f83f10e6]
8. Plan the in-subscription gift roadmap (section 3.3). [503d2fb72fc1]
9. Set the email pop-up and all site offers to the same subscription offer. No separate one-time discount. "No need to give any extra discount." [7860f83f10e6]
10. Test the offer. The source gives no test design or sample size. [7860f83f10e6]

### 3.2 Lay out the PDP buy box for take rate
1. Auto-select subscription on page load. Bloom's page is the source's example of what not to do: one-time pre-selected. IM8 and AG1 pre-select subscription. [d8cbf6d9333d] [verify UI: subscription app widget setting for default selection]
2. Show 3 options, 2 of them subscriptions. Example: 90-day subscription, 30-day subscription, one-time. With 2 options, one-time is 50% of the choices and the jump to subscription is small. [d8cbf6d9333d]
3. Make the option you most want (IM8: the 90-day subscription, about $250) easy to pick; people lean to middle options, so order matters. The source does not say which position to use. [d8cbf6d9333d]
4. Make the one-time option small and visually recessed. [7860f83f10e6] [d8cbf6d9333d]
5. On the subscription options show: the correct price, the amount saved, the free gifts (with pictures if possible) and all other benefits. [d8cbf6d9333d]
6. A/B test take rate and later retention (card caveat). [d8cbf6d9333d] [needs brand data: first-order subscription take rate before and after]

### 3.3 Choose gifts and plan the gift roadmap
1. Find what stops daily use (mixing, measuring, dosing). [503d2fb72fc1]
2. First-order gifts: only low-cost tools that remove that friction. Everyday Dose: free creamer, frother, spoon. Another brand: frother plus a bottle. [503d2fb72fc1]
3. Optional: samples of other products as gifts (AG1 shows travel packs and a vitamin product). Source likes this less for the first order but says it can work as a cross-sell play. [503d2fb72fc1]
4. Place later gifts at the months the source calls highest churn: month 2, 4 and 6. Everyday Dose gives a gift every order (months 2, 3, 4, 5); source says you do not need to go that far, do what is low cost. IM8 is shown as another example of gifts at set points. [503d2fb72fc1] [needs brand data: churn by order number or month]
5. Later gifts lean to revenue expansion: a sample pack of another product alongside the core product (for example month 3), plus branded items (a tumbler). [503d2fb72fc1]
6. Never give more of the same product as a gift. [503d2fb72fc1]
7. Announce each upcoming gift in the billing reminder and order confirmation before it ships, for example "free workout candy sample in your next order, no action required". [503d2fb72fc1] [e9c9f0ad1a68]

### 3.4 Set cadence and quantity
1. Estimate real daily or weekly use per subscriber. [167cf8e69e33]
2. Set default cadence so people run out about when the next order arrives. 30 servings on 30 days only for daily use. [167cf8e69e33]
3. Where use varies by household, let the customer choose quantity within a cadence (a beverage brand lets buyers pick can count on a 30-day plan; 48 cans a month for 1 person is the mismatch to avoid). [167cf8e69e33]
4. Watch "too much product" cancels as the signal that defaults are wrong. [167cf8e69e33] [needs brand data: cancel reasons by plan]

### 3.5 Onboarding sequence (neighbour folder 14-EML)
1. Email 1, right at order: founder thank-you and welcome. Future-pace, give education, end with a personal PS ("just hit reply, I actually read these"). [33976dbed0ac]
2. Or add a more official welcome: "your subscription is officially live, here is what your subscription means". Be open that they are subscribed. [33976dbed0ac]
3. Days 1 to 7, before delivery: daily emails, or every other day at minimum. Heavy education: consumption, ingredients, behind the scenes, timeline. [33976dbed0ac]
4. Include at least 1 pure consumption and pacing email (creatine example: scoop once a day, keep going at least 4 months, testimonials of results after 120 days of use). [2762401ae2f2]
5. Include a quality email that makes them feel good about the purchase. [33976dbed0ac]
6. Include a "what your subscription gives you" email: manage deliveries, delay if running low or travelling, swap products, update payment, pause, track, earn rewards, with a link to the portal. Mention upcoming gifts. [33976dbed0ac]
7. Include a steps-to-use email (for example 3 steps to start). [33976dbed0ac]
8. Send a delivery email with quick consumption tips when the order is about to arrive. [33976dbed0ac]
9. Finish with a best-practices email that repeats the basics (creatine: 5 g daily, any time of day, any method, what to expect in week 1, no life-changing results yet). [2762401ae2f2]
10. Then back off. Days 7 to 14 bring cancels as people try the product; do not send daily then. Optional 1-week or 2-week check-in. [33976dbed0ac]
11. SMS: same messages, every other day, not daily. [33976dbed0ac]
12. Optional extras: package inserts on consumption, a handwritten founder note arriving with delivery (PostPilot or "Paper Run" as captioned) [verify UI], a landing page with a founder welcome video, a short course around day 14, a 90-day daily-use challenge with a reward. Source says these are not needed at the start. [33976dbed0ac]

### 3.6 Find a habit stack from your own data (14-EML)
1. Survey customers on how and when they use the product. [2762401ae2f2]
2. Compare retention and LTV by usage context. [2762401ae2f2] [needs brand data: survey answers joined to retention]
3. Rewrite onboarding to repeat the best context. Creatine example: morning coffee users retained best, so the brand repeated "creatine in your coffee". Validate before rewriting everything, since the link may be correlation (card caveat). [2762401ae2f2]

### 3.7 Billing reminder and order confirmation (14-EML)
1. Keep sending the pre-charge billing reminder. Source says it is legally required in some states and skipping it is poor practice. [32e1b81135d0] [web check: current US state auto-renewal notice rules, and rules in the brand's own market]
2. Move the reminder from the subscription platform (Recharge, Skio) into the ESP (Klaviyo) for design control. [32e1b81135d0] [verify UI]
3. Rewrite it from a scary charge notice into benefit-led, motivational copy ("your next order ships soon, get ready", "you're building a strong habit with every delivery"). Show perks (up to 20% off in the example), free gifts, and "pause, skip or cancel anytime". [32e1b81135d0]
4. Near the top: a 1-click "delay my order" link, built from the subscription platform's action links. [32e1b81135d0] [verify UI]
5. Below that: upsells and cross-sells. [32e1b81135d0]
6. At the very bottom: "need to adjust your subscription? manage my subscription". No cancel or manage button higher up. [32e1b81135d0]
7. Build separate reminder versions by rebill number (1st, 2nd, 3rd, up to 6th) and by product. If a gift ships on an order, make that reminder about the gift ("your premium supplements plus a free gift are coming soon"). Keep delay on top and manage at the bottom in every version. [e9c9f0ad1a68] [verify UI: per-order dynamic content in ESP]
8. Order confirmation: check your churn by day since subscribing. If there is a spike on the first rebill day (day 30 in the client example), rebuild the confirmation. [3d13b507d1db] [needs brand data: churn count by day since subscription start]
9. Turn off the native Shopify order confirmation and rebuild it in the ESP. The transcript is contradictory on who can do this (see section 9). [3d13b507d1db] [web check: current Shopify rules for disabling order confirmation on Plus and non-Plus plans]
10. New confirmation: celebrate the order ("peak performance is on the way, your consistency is about to pay off"), dynamic order content, product benefits, testimonials. Customize by subscription vs one-time and by order number. Feature any milestone item (example: free coffee booklet at subscription start). [3d13b507d1db]
11. Apply the same treatment to other transactional emails where practical. [3d13b507d1db]

### 3.8 Campaigns to active subscribers (14-EML)
1. Exclude active subscribers from all sales and standard promos, so they do not cancel to buy on sale. [1b7f42b5f9e3]
2. Send them 1 to 3 thoughtful campaigns a month: monthly founder newsletter, community news, exclusive giveaways, team spotlights. Goal: they feel exclusive. [1b7f42b5f9e3]
3. On sale days give subscribers their own deal on a 1-time add-on added to their next order (Prime Day example: 30% off protein powder for a creatine subscriber, auto-added on click). [1b7f42b5f9e3]
4. Track "subscription cancelled" (from Recharge) against each campaign to spot sends that cost more subscribers than they start. [1b7f42b5f9e3] [verify UI]

### 3.9 Cancel flow and win-back (neighbour folders 17-CXP and 14-EML)
1. In the portal (Skio named), add a splash screen on the cancel page: a founder video thanking them and restating the mission, pushing delay instead of cancel. [1ae7b0ed8e41] [verify UI]
2. If you have a loyalty program in the platform, show the points they would lose. [1ae7b0ed8e41]
3. Ask why they are cancelling and branch: [1ae7b0ed8e41]
   - want a different product: swap product, optional discount
   - taking a break: skip order or change next order date
   - don't love the taste: swap product
   - too much product (usually the biggest reason): skip or change next order date; edit frequency
   - too expensive right now: segment further and give a special offer
4. Track save rate against the bands in section 4. [1ae7b0ed8e41] [needs brand data: current save rate]
5. Right after a cancel: 1 or 2 short text-based emails split by cancel reason. Confirm the cancel, speak to their reason, show the offer, put "reactivate my subscription" up front (people come back to this email later), add a heartfelt note and founder faces. Optional 1 "last chance" email. Do not over-invest; this flow rarely works right away. [552ee801d868]
6. Put cancelled customers back in the normal campaign schedule; some will re-buy from campaigns. [552ee801d868]
7. 30 to 60 days after the cancel, if they have not come back: win-back flow segmented by cancel reason, with a bigger first-order-back offer (30%, 40% or 50% off instead of 15%) that then steps down to the standard 15%. Link to the portal or the platform's resubscribe action. Restate all perks. [552ee801d868] [verify UI]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Take rate check.** Look at the share of first orders that are subscriptions. Good: rising after changes. Bad: low, with one-time pre-selected. Fix: section 3.2. [d8cbf6d9333d] [needs brand data: take rate]
- **Offer alignment check.** Open the site as a new visitor and read the pop-up. Bad: pop-up gives a % off that only applies to one-time purchase, or is bigger than or competes with the subscription offer. Good: pop-up states the subscription offer. [7860f83f10e6]
- **Buy box check.** Bad (Bloom example): one-time pre-selected, 2 options only. Good (IM8, AG1): subscription pre-selected, 2 of 3 options are subscriptions, savings and gifts shown. [d8cbf6d9333d]
- **Gift check.** Bad: stickers, generic swag, or more of the same product. Good: tools that make daily use easier, later samples of other products. [503d2fb72fc1]
- **Cadence check.** High share of "too much product" cancels means cadence or pack size is wrong for real use. [167cf8e69e33]
- **LTV case check.** Compare first-order subscription vs not at 6, 12 and 24 months. Note that selection bias inflates the gap. [d99fcf6edcf0]
- **Churn by day chart.** Plot cancels by day since subscription start. The source's client shape: small spike on day 0 (deal seekers), near zero until delivery, cancels as people try the product (about days 7 to 14), a rise at the billing reminder, the largest spike on day 30 at the first rebill confirmation, then repeating reminder and confirmation spikes. Fix whichever message sits under a spike. [3d13b507d1db] [33976dbed0ac] [needs brand data: days until churn]
- **Billing reminder audit.** Bad: platform default, charge amount front and centre, large "manage subscription" button near the top. Good: benefits, gifts, 1-click delay near the top, manage at the bottom. Measure cancel rate per reminder send; source baseline was 8.6%. [32e1b81135d0]
- **Campaign to subscriber audit.** For each campaign compare "subscription cancelled" vs "subscription started" among recipients within a time window. Bad: net negative (client example: 9.4K cancels shown on one email and a net negative program). Fix: exclude active subscribers from promos. [1b7f42b5f9e3]
- **Save rate.** Under 10%: rework the flow. 15%: good. 20%: very good. [1ae7b0ed8e41]
- **Onboarding promise check.** Read all onboarding copy for early-results promises. Bad: results promised in days. Good: 90 to 120 day horizon. [2762401ae2f2]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating the first order as the win and only chasing lower CAC. [9d7f6e7eec35]
- Benchmarking retention against a brand in another niche or with another use pattern. [9d7f6e7eec35]
- Slashing margins in the name of a strong offer. [7860f83f10e6]
- Pre-selecting one-time purchase. [d8cbf6d9333d]
- A 2-option buy box where one-time is half the choices. [d8cbf6d9333d]
- A pop-up discount that only applies to one-time purchase and undercuts the subscription offer. [7860f83f10e6]
- Useless gifts (stickers) and gifting more of the same product. [503d2fb72fc1]
- Putting a non-daily product on a 30-day plan, or oversized packs (48 cans a month for 1 person). [167cf8e69e33]
- Promising results too early. [2762401ae2f2]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single presenter, agency pitch.** Three sales pitches sit inside the video. Numbers are single clients with no sample sizes. Community size is stated as both 145 and 250+.
- **Arithmetic check.** 8.6% to 5.8% is a 32.6% relative drop, so "33%" is right; the captioned "28%" looks like a fragment. $130 to $195 is +50%. 19% to 13 to 14% churn is a 26 to 32% relative drop, consistent with "about 30%".
- **Shopify Plus statement contradicts itself.** The transcript says you must be on Plus to turn the confirmation off, then that Plus stores must keep it on and non-Plus stores can ask support to turn it off. The card follows the second reading. [web check: current Shopify policy] [3d13b507d1db]
- **Onboarding window.** Says "first 7 days" and elsewhere "first 10 days". Delivery timing drives it. [33976dbed0ac] [needs brand data: days from order to delivery]
- **Pacing conflict inside examples.** Source says tell customers 90 to 120 days, but an example email he praises says most notice a difference after about month 1. [2762401ae2f2]
- **Legal and regulatory risk, not addressed.** Burying the cancel link, a founder video before cancel, pre-selected subscription and no one-time option discount may clash with auto-renewal, easy-cancel and dark-pattern rules in some markets. The source only mentions that billing reminders are legally required in some states. [web check: FTC negative option rule status, US state auto-renewal laws (for example California), EU and UK rules on pre-selected options, India's 2023 dark pattern guidelines ("subscription trap") for Gehna-type brands]
- **Conflict with common ecommerce advice.** Common practice is a 10% welcome pop-up for everyone and discounts on one-time orders; this source says no one-time discount at all and a subscription-only pop-up. Common welcome flows email steadily through the first 2 weeks; this source front-loads days 1 to 7 and backs off in days 7 to 14. Common save flows offer a discount first; this source uses reason-based branching and reserves discounts for "too expensive" (card caveat: limit discounts so people do not learn to cancel for them).
- **Selection bias** in the LTV gap (card caveat). The source does not control for it. [d99fcf6edcf0]
- **Missing how.** No guidance on the size of the subscription discount vs margin, no take-rate benchmark, no churn or retention benchmark by category, no test design or sample size, no guarantee terms, no pricing for the 90-day vs 30-day gap, no rule on which position the preferred option should take, no copy for the cancel splash screen. The 1 to 3 campaigns cap, day 7 to 14 back-off, months 2, 4 and 6 and 30 to 60 day win-back are opinions, not tests.
- **Fit.** Built on supplements, powders, drinks and coffee. Not tested for non-consumables, occasional-use products or one-time purchase categories such as jewellery.
