## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator design a first-order offer on a product page that raises AOV enough to buy cold traffic on Meta. The core pattern: pick variant first, then a quantity ladder (buy 1, 2, 3) with a savings figure per tier, free gifts that grow with units, an optional pre-built starter kit, and a per-serving or per-unit price at every tier. It adds a scaling check (AOV x conversion rate > 250) and a set of page and cart nudges. It is strong on what the pages look like and weak on why they work: no numbers on results, no margin math, no test data.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 AOV and conversion rate are the 2 bottlenecks to scaling spend, and must be read together
The presenter names AOV and conversion rate as 2 of the funnel bottlenecks that stop efficient new-customer acquisition and scaling. His diagram: scaling potential rises with AOV; you hit a ceiling and break through to the next ceiling by raising AOV or conversion rate. The 2 "play in balance": a low AOV with a high conversion rate can offset each other, "and vice versa. We've seen that happen." Condition: this video does not cover creative, which he says also drives the brand's results. Evidence: his agency experience, no data shown. [34682db50bb8]

### 2.2 The scaling benchmark: AOV x conversion rate (number without the % sign) should be above 250
"Usually as a rule, your average order value multiplied by your conversion rate, just removing the percentage sign, should be higher than 250." Below 250 means there is work to do on the offer, pricing or landing pages. Above 250 you can still push higher to break the next ceiling. He lists it as a new-customer acquisition growth lever, raised through offer structure, pricing and landing pages. Worked example from the card, not the source: AOV 65 x 4% = 260. Arithmetic note: AOV x conversion rate in percent equals revenue per 100 sessions, so 250 is the same as 2.50 revenue per session. The source never names a currency; he mixes £ and $ in the video. Condition: a presenter heuristic, not an industry figure. It ignores margin, price point, repeat rate and channel. `[needs brand data: AOV, session conversion rate, currency, gross margin, CAC]` For a brand priced in INR or another currency, the 250 number does not carry over as is. [34682db50bb8]

### 2.3 Layering offers makes the offer stronger than any single mechanic
"Layering offers" means combining different offer types, for example a quantity break plus a pre-built bundle, into one structure. The presenter says this "increases the effectiveness and the strength of the offer". Space Goods layers 3: subscription-only purchase, a choice of starter kit (pre-built bundle with free accessories) or the product alone, and a buy 1/2/3 quantity break with free gifts that grow with units. Evidence: observation of the page, plus his earlier videos on the concept. No result numbers. [01902b9597c7]

### 2.4 Presentation is as hard as structure: a layered offer must not confuse
"The hard part is not only structuring the offer, but it's also about presenting it in a way that makes sense and is not confusing." Space Goods is praised for both. The source gives the order it saw (variant, then quantity tiers with savings and gifts) and the per-serving display as the clarity tools. It gives no clarity test or check. [01902b9597c7] [4dc9316eaaf5]

### 2.5 Always show the serving price (or unit price) on a quantity break
"Every time you look at a quantity break offer, we always show the serving price." The starter kit shows the serving price "immediately". The "Jabi" page shows a per-bottle price at each tier. The point: break a large total into a small amount per serving or per bottle. Transcript figures: "$29 per bundle or $24 per bag" on Space Goods; "15.95 each, $79.95 each" on "Jabi". Condition: the source applies this to quantity breaks and high-AOV bundles. The presenter later widens the unit options to "price per serving, the price per bottle, the price per unit, the price per day". Unit framing changes perception, not the charge. [4dc9316eaaf5]

### 2.6 Free gifts should scale with units bought
Space Goods gives more free mugs as you buy more bags. "Jabi" unlocks gifts by bottle count: 1 bottle = 1 gift, 2 bottles = 1 gift, 3 bottles = 3 gifts, 4 bottles = 4 gifts. Note: that ladder is not strictly 1 gift per unit; tier 2 gets no extra gift, which makes the jump to tier 3 bigger. The card and the header summarize it as "1 gift per bag, 3 gifts for 3 bags". Condition: gifts must "make sense to the product", be "high value" to the buyer and "not cost you a lot". [01902b9597c7] [e339ff439cf1]

### 2.7 A starter kit is a first-order offer for new customers
A pre-built starter kit (product plus relevant, cheap-to-you accessories) raises the perceived value of the first order. "If you get them to buy 2 bags or more, AOV starts to ramp up pretty quickly." The presenter says adding gifts to create a bundle "increases perceived value and maybe you can charge a little bit more for that". Space Goods offers the starter kit next to the product alone, so a buyer who wants less still has a path. Condition: aimed at new customers; the source does not discuss repeat buyers or what happens after order 1. [e339ff439cf1]

### 2.8 Subscription-only gating is one valid option, not the only one
Space Goods sells subscription only, not "one-time and subscribe". Love Wellness uses 1 month, 3 month and 6 month tiers (buy 1, 3, 6) with both subscribe-and-save and one-time. The presenter: "there's no one-size-fits-all to this kind of offer structure". He does not say when to gate. The card condition "where the product suits repeat use" is a card addition. `[needs brand data: repeat purchase rate, product use cycle, subscription churn]` [01902b9597c7] [58e1159fc052]

### 2.9 Keep the same offer structure across sibling products
A second Space Goods product (Astro Dust in the transcript) uses "exactly the same" page layout and offer: starter kit or product alone, buy more save more, free gift. The only difference is fewer flavors. The source states this as an observation; the card adds "so shoppers learn the layout once". [01902b9597c7]

### 2.10 Put the variant choice before the quantity choice
"First of all you choose your flavor." If you have variants (flavors, sizes, colors, designs), show them clearly so the buyer can choose, then show the quantity break. The presenter lists "the way the variants are laid out before the quantities" as an element worth copying. [01902b9597c7] [4dc9316eaaf5] [58e1159fc052]

### 2.11 Reinforce the offer with promises, delivery date and a guarantee near the buy box
Space Goods shows a promise row: free shipping, never run out of product, skip or cancel at any time (transcript: "skip accounts at any time"), 67% off. Then "in stock, ready to ship" and "delivered on Friday 23rd of May", then "no risk 60-day money back guarantee". The presenter says these "reinforce the offer itself to get customers over the line". Delivery rule: an exact delivery date is strongest ("think about how Amazon does this"); "shipped within 24 hours" is "not as strong" but "still strong enough". Condition: only promise what fulfilment meets (card caveat). `[needs brand data: real dispatch and delivery times by region]` Routed to 07-LPG by the header. [d7f2ba51f82c]

### 2.12 On-page upsell to a bigger kit
Space Goods offers "upgrade to a day and night starter kit and save" on the product page, so buyers can add both products. The presenter: this raises AOV. No numbers given. [01902b9597c7]

### 2.13 Tune quantities, prices and savings by take rate, within unit economics
"Playing around with the quantities, playing around with the pricing, the savings, just making sure that the unit economics for you work on the back end, you can actually support customer acquisition for that offer. Once you see the take rate of the different quantities of the offer." You do not always need buy 1/3/6; buy 1/2/3 also works. If you can get buyers to buy more, with 3 as the middle option (transcript: "3B in the middle option"), AOV rises. Condition: the source gives no margin floor, discount cap or take-rate target. `[needs brand data: landed cost per unit, gift cost, shipping cost, contribution margin per tier, take rate per tier]` [58e1159fc052] [01902b9597c7]

### 2.14 Start by testing 1 thing
Action step: study the Love Wellness page, the "Jabi" bundle builder and the Space Goods page, then decide what to apply. It may be a full offer overhaul to test, or single elements: price per serving, bottle, unit or day; variant layout before quantities; free gifts to build a bundle. "Ideally, you want to start by testing one thing." Test significance is routed to 09-CRO. [58e1159fc052]

### 2.15 Listicle advertorial as a secondary traffic route, with an early CTA (07-LPG)
Most Space Goods ads go to the product page; some go to a listicle. Headline (transcript): "I started drinking Space Goods [product] before bed. Here are 7 reasons why I don't plan on stopping." The listicle has a featured review carousel followed by "thousands of five-star reviews" that buyers can read before clicking through. Purpose: social proof and trust. [b7975111e758]

## 3. Procedures

The source gives page anatomy, not build steps. The sequences below are the order the source shows or recommends. Steps marked "card" come from the card, not the transcript.

### 3.1 Build a layered first-order offer on the product page
1. Run the scaling check first (3.2) so you know if AOV, conversion rate, or both need work. [34682db50bb8]
2. Decide the purchase mode: subscription only (Space Goods) or subscribe-and-save plus one-time (Love Wellness). The source gives no rule for choosing. `[needs brand data: repeat rate, subscription churn]` [01902b9597c7]
3. Build a starter kit: hero product plus accessories that fit the product, feel high value, and cost you little. Offer it next to "product alone". [e339ff439cf1]
4. Place the variant selector (flavor, size, color, design) above the quantity tiers. [01902b9597c7]
5. Build the quantity ladder: buy 1/2/3 or buy 1/3/6. Show the savings on each tier (example: save £62 / £100 / £153). [01902b9597c7]
6. Attach gifts that grow with units (example "Jabi": 1, 1, 3, 4 gifts for 1 to 4 bottles). [01902b9597c7]
7. Show the price per serving or per unit on every tier, right with the price. [4dc9316eaaf5]
8. Add the promise row, stock status, delivery date or ship-by promise, and guarantee under the offer (07-LPG). [d7f2ba51f82c]
9. Add an on-page upsell to a bigger kit if you have a second product (example: day and night starter kit). [01902b9597c7]
10. Before launch, check unit economics at the deepest tier, including gift cost (card step). `[needs brand data: margin per tier]` [01902b9597c7] [e339ff439cf1]
11. Copy the same structure to sibling products. [01902b9597c7]
12. After launch, read take rate per tier and adjust quantities, prices and savings. [58e1159fc052]

### 3.2 Run the AOV x conversion rate check
1. Pull AOV and conversion rate for the same period and traffic `[needs brand data: AOV and conversion rate, new-customer traffic if available]`. The source does not say which sessions, which period, or new vs all customers.
2. Multiply AOV by the conversion rate with the % sign removed (4% becomes 4). Do the arithmetic in code.
3. Above 250: offer can likely support acquisition; still look for gains. Below 250: work on offer, pricing and landing pages before pushing spend.
4. Look at AOV and conversion rate separately too; one can mask the other (card caveat).
5. Re-check after each offer or page change (card step). [34682db50bb8]

### 3.3 Build a listicle advertorial (07-LPG)
1. First-person headline: "I started [using product] before bed. Here are 7 reasons why I don't plan on stopping."
2. Add an author byline and a "last updated" date.
3. Present each reason in turn with images side by side.
4. Place a CTA high on the page; add more CTAs higher up if heatmaps show shallow scroll.
5. Add a featured review carousel, then the bulk reviews.
6. Link to the product or offer page. [dc35cd6b47b8] [b7975111e758]

### 3.4 Set up the side cart (07-LPG)
1. Show the main product and a free-shipping progress bar.
2. Optional: chain unlocks on the bar (free shipping, then a free gift).
3. Add an "add to your order" block of low-priced items with 1-click add.
4. Offer an in-cart upgrade from one-time to subscribe-and-save (app named: "Reby", likely Rebuy) `[verify UI]` `[web check: app and feature]`.
5. Make the checkout button sticky.
6. Show correct price and savings.
7. The source shows a reservation timer; see caveats in section 9 before using one. [3817d0f2245c] [2b91c05f98d2]

### 3.5 Extend proof into checkout (Shopify Plus only, per the source)
1. Add reviews to checkout.
2. Repeat perks from the product page (easy refunds and returns, customer count, product claims), only if true for the brand.
3. Match button colors and styling to the store. `[verify UI: which checkout customizations your Shopify plan allows]` [b7975111e758] [2b91c05f98d2]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- A layered offer that confuses the buyer. Structure and presentation both matter. [01902b9597c7]
- A quantity break with no serving or unit price shown. [4dc9316eaaf5]
- Gifts that do not fit the product or cost you a lot. [e339ff439cf1]
- Discounts and tiers your back-end unit economics cannot support for acquisition. [58e1159fc052]
- Judging AOV or conversion rate alone. [34682db50bb8]
- Pushing spend with AOV x CR under 250. [34682db50bb8]
- Copying one layout as if it fits all; "no one-size-fits-all". [58e1159fc052]
- Changing everything at once instead of testing 1 thing. [58e1159fc052]
- Only putting the CTA at the bottom of a long page most visitors do not finish. [dc35cd6b47b8]
- Making buyers scroll to find the checkout button. [3817d0f2245c]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- Evidence is thin. It is a teardown of other brands' pages by an agency owner selling services. No test data, no before and after, no margins. The $10M figure is unsourced.
- The 250 rule is unusual. Common practice judges scale with contribution margin, CAC vs first-order or 90-day profit, and revenue per session by channel. The rule has no margin term, so a low-margin brand can pass 250 and still lose money, and a high-margin brand can scale below it. It is also currency-bound. Keep it as a quick screen, not a gate. `[needs brand data: margin, CAC, currency]`
- Subscription-only gating conflicts with common advice to keep a one-time option to reduce first-order friction. The source shows both models and does not say when gating wins. `[needs brand data: subscription vs one-time conversion and retention]`
- Reservation timer "due to high demand": the source presents it as a conversion tactic with no caveat. Fake or unconditional urgency and scarcity can breach consumer protection rules and platform policies (for example the FTC in the US, CMA in the UK, and India's 2023 dark patterns guidelines, which list false urgency). Only use if the reservation is real. `[web check: current dark pattern rules in the brand's market]` The cards also flag honest use. [3817d0f2245c] [2b91c05f98d2]
- "67% off" and claims like "safe and effective" and "over 150,000 customers" are that brand's. Do not copy claims; each must be true and supportable for the brand. `[needs brand data: substantiated claims]`
- Early CTA on the listicle goes against the usual convention; the presenter frames it as something the brand may be testing.
- Transcript garbles: "skip accounts" (likely skip or cancel), "Jabi/Javi/Jaby", "Reby", "astronauts", "cream of dust", "3B in the middle", "This is hasting". The per-bottle figures "15.95 each, $79.95 each" do not look like 2 unit prices at 2 tiers; one may be a total. Do not quote these numbers as facts.
- Currency mixes £ and $ within one page teardown.
- Missing: how to set tier discounts, a margin floor, gift cost limits, which tier to default or highlight, take-rate targets, test size or duration, how to measure AOV x CR (period, traffic, new vs returning), mobile layout.
