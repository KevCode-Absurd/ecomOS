## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator build and test a pre-purchase offer: do the unit math per bundle tier first, then choose a structure (bundle and save, dollar off, percent off, buy X get Y free, free gift, subscription), frame it so the buyer does no math, and judge variants on profit per session instead of AOV or revenue. It also treats the offer as the whole value package (desire, proof, delivery speed, risk and effort) carried from ad to landing page, using Hormozi's 4-part value equation and Schwartz's awareness stages. It is strong on framing rules. It is thin on test design, sample size, and how to compute profit per session in practice.

## 2. Principles

1. **An offer is the whole package, not the discount.** Condition: always. The presenter defines the offer as everything the customer gets in total: product, price, bonus, claims, guarantees, conditions, and the value they perceive at purchase. Later he adds that the offer "runs from ad to the landing page". Evidence: his definition. [0a570cacb952]

2. **Offers matter as much as creatives.** Condition: when a store gets clicks but not sales. "Your ad gets the click, but your offer needs to close the sale." He rejects the advice to "just test more creatives". Evidence: opinion plus 2 secondhand store results (section 7). The header note flags this motivational framing as low value; keep the rule, not the hype. [0a570cacb952]

3. **Know your numbers before you build an offer.** Condition: before any new offer goes live. Round costs up and underestimate, because the goal is more profit, not more revenue. If you do not know your profit in and out, get an accountant. Evidence: 1 anecdote of a merchant whose "buy 2 get 1 free" offer grew revenue but lost money because he did not reset profit targets. [471fc0881800] [1cda5c627ada] [needs brand data: unit cost, shipping, pick and pack, processing fees, price per tier]

4. **Reset ROAS and CPA targets for every new offer.** Condition: any change to price, bundle depth or free units. Use a profit calculator to find target ROAS and break-even CPA for the new offer. A deeper bundle changes margin and cost structure, so the old targets are wrong. [471fc0881800] [needs brand data: current break-even ROAS and CPA per offer]

5. **Fixed per-order costs fall per unit as units per order rise.** Condition: products where shipping cost stays flat across 1 to 4 units (the source's example is a light skincare item). Shipping, pick and pack labour and processing fees are saved on bigger orders, so margin per order rises and break-even ROAS falls. The header note says to use that saving to fund the bundle discount; the presenter only says "you make more money". Evidence: worked example in section 7. [471fc0881800] [needs brand data: real shipping cost by units per order and weight band]

6. **Higher AOV is not automatically better. Balance AOV against conversion rate.** Condition: when adding large packs, raising price, or limiting choice to high-ticket options. His example: if the only option is a year's supply at $360, AOV rises but conversion falls, "because not everyone is going to buy a year's worth of supplements", and profit per customer drops. [90e80c5c7974] [1cda5c627ada]

7. **Profit per session is the main metric for offer tests.** Condition: comparing offer variants. Header formula: Profit Per Session = Total Net Profit / Total Sessions. Track it per offer. The presenter says it is "made out of" conversion rate and AOV. Judge on profit, not revenue. [1cda5c627ada] [needs brand data: sessions and net profit per offer variant]

8. **There is no magic formula offer.** Condition: always. He rejects claims that "buy 1 get 1, buy 2 get 2, buy 3 get 3 free" is a universal winner. The 4 brands he shows at $100k a day all run different offers. Whether "buy 2 get 2 free at a higher price" beats "buy 2 get 1 free at a lower price" is unknown until you test it for your brand. [1cda5c627ada] [ae0a3d67b0b3]

9. **Give the buyer a reason to buy more.** Condition: tiered bundles meant to raise AOV. Tie each tier to a physical use: 1 unit covers 700 sq ft, 2 cover 1,400 sq ft; a small vacuum for a small living room, a bigger one for a big room; "how many kids do you have?" for a kids supplement. Evidence: observed brand examples, no lift numbers. [fc891407623e]

10. **Rule of larger numbers: show the discount as whichever number is bigger, dollars or percent.** Condition: any discount label or tier badge. "10% off" loses to "$178 saved"; "$40 saved" loses to "61% off". Evidence: 2 observed brand examples, no test data. [9f02d954cf3b]

11. **"Free" beats an equivalent percent.** Condition: multi-unit offers where the economics match. "People like free", so "buy 2, get 1 free" reads better than a percentage label. Evidence: presenter's preference from brand examples. See section 9 for a math problem in his example. [ae0a3d67b0b3]

12. **Do not make the buyer do mental math.** Condition: all offer copy. "The easier it is for them to understand the value proposition, the better it's going to do." Show dollars saved, cost per kit, cost per day, and saving per tier, so the buyer sees the value without calculating. "67% off" on a crossed-out price is simpler than a sum; "get 1 free" is simpler still. [5f2ab9d96cc7]

13. **Offers can skip bundles entirely.** Condition: replenishment products or when bundle discounts do not fit the economics. 1 brand he shows (likely Everyday Dose; he is "not sure" of its revenue) runs no bundle offer. It optimises for subscription opt-ins and uses a free gift offer instead. [c2bff6b33753]

14. **All offers are built from the same few parts.** Condition: when designing a new offer. Buy more, get better stuff, and show how much they save. The extra value can be a free gift, more money saved, or free units of the same product. More unusual types exist (gift cards, gifting offers). [c2bff6b33753] [0a570cacb952]

15. **Creative and site change AOV and conversion too, so test offers and creatives together.** Condition: any offer test. A new winning ad lifts conversion rate. An ad aimed at a person buying for their family instead of for himself changes AOV. The site also moves both. The presenter says "you need to test both at the same time". The cards add that you should isolate the offer (keep creative constant) when you want to attribute a result to it. That is a card addition, not the presenter's advice. [1cda5c627ada] [90e80c5c7974]

16. **Raise value beyond the discount with Hormozi's 4-part value equation.** Condition: an offer that already works and needs more perceived value without a deeper discount. The 4 parts: dream outcome, perceived likelihood of achievement, time delay, effort and sacrifice. "If you can improve 4 of these things, your offer becomes better." The source lists the parts but not how they combine. [4cc6038f44bf] [web check: Hormozi's value equation in $100M Offers; dream outcome and likelihood raise value, time delay and effort lower it]
    - Dream outcome: sell the strongest desire, not the feature. His example: not "look slimmer" but "finally your husband will think you're hot again", with the whole landing page built around that desire. Second example: a minivan sold as "be a better mother" (be seen as motherly by other moms) rather than "fit more kids". [4cc6038f44bf]
    - Perceived likelihood: show it will work for this buyer. His example: a kids supplement page with many UGC videos, ingredient and nutrient-gap breakdowns, benefits and families it worked for, answering "will this work for my kid?". Reviews, guarantees and unboxing videos do the same job. He says these page elements raise both AOV and conversion rate. Analogy: you pay more for an experienced plastic surgeon. [4cc6038f44bf]
    - Time delay: shorten the time to the benefit. Next-day shipping beats competitors; 2-week shipping from China "is no" longer acceptable; deadlines like "get it before Christmas" add urgency. For subscription brands, shipping time "can be the biggest factor in churn rate". [4cc6038f44bf] [c2bff6b33753]
    - Effort and sacrifice: lower the work and the risk. His example: a weight-loss drug (likely Ozempic) costs more than diet and exercise but is effortless, so people buy it. In ecommerce: try before you buy, buy now pay later (Shop Pay, Affirm, Afterpay, Klarna), guarantees. He also says hard-to-use products get no posts and bad reviews. [4cc6038f44bf]

17. **Match claims to the buyer's awareness stage (Eugene Schwartz).** Condition: writing claims for ads and pages. The claims and offers on ads and sites differ by stage. Humidifier example: problem aware = dryness in the morning; solution aware = eliminate seasonal allergies; product aware = easiest humidifier to clean. The presenter treats the stage claim as the dream outcome for that stage. [f3e0c5293ed8]

18. **The offer starts in the ad.** Condition: all paid traffic. A stronger desire, proof from people like the viewer, fast delivery and risk-free trial stated in the ad already improve the offer. When the buyer lands and sees the same things on the site, they are "more ready to purchase". [0a570cacb952] [f3e0c5293ed8]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Build a first profitable offer (the presenter's order)
1. List your numbers per tier: product cost, shipping per order and per unit, pick and pack labour, processing fees, price, margin. Round costs up and revenue down. [471fc0881800] [needs brand data: full landed cost per tier]
2. Put the table into a spreadsheet or an AI assistant and ask: "Here is my new offer, my prices, my costs and shipping. How much profit will I make? What are my margins?" He also suggests asking the assistant to question you on how to build the offer. The header note flags this step as basic; it is a planning check, not bookkeeping. [471fc0881800]
3. From the result, set target ROAS and break-even CPA for each offer. [471fc0881800]
4. Draft offer candidates that push AOV or conversion: a price increase, more bundle tiers, "buy 2 get 2 free at a higher price", and so on. [1cda5c627ada] [90e80c5c7974]
5. Pick the structure: bundle and save, dollar off, percent off, buy X get Y free, buy X get Y% off, free gift, or subscription opt-in. [ae0a3d67b0b3] [c2bff6b33753]
6. Frame it: give each tier a physical reason, label savings with the larger number, prefer "free" to a percent, show per-kit or per-day cost. (Section 3.3.) [fc891407623e] [9f02d954cf3b] [5f2ab9d96cc7]
7. Raise perceived value with the 4 value-equation levers on both the ad and the page. (Section 3.4.) [4cc6038f44bf]
8. Test and measure profit per session per offer. Keep testing, as with creatives. [1cda5c627ada]

The presenter's own 3-step summary: do the quick math; test ways to raise conversion rate and AOV (higher price, more bundles); then make the offer stronger from ad to landing page with the 4 value levers.

### 3.2 Compare offer variants on profit per session
1. Define profit per session per variant as net profit / sessions (header note formula). The source does not say whether ad spend, returns or refunds are deducted. Decide and write it down before the test. [1cda5c627ada] [needs brand data: which costs count in net profit]
2. Before testing a higher-AOV offer, estimate how far conversion could drop. (Card step.) [1cda5c627ada]
3. Run the variants. The presenter says test offers and creatives at the same time. The cards say hold creative constant when you want to credit the offer. [1cda5c627ada]
4. Pick the variant with higher profit per session, not higher revenue or AOV. A lower-AOV offer with clearly higher conversion can win. [1cda5c627ada] [90e80c5c7974]
5. Re-test after a change in traffic source or creative, because a new audience shifts AOV and conversion. (Card step, based on the presenter's family-buyer example.) [90e80c5c7974]

The source gives no sample size, test length or significance rule. [web check: minimum sessions or orders per variant for a reliable offer test]

### 3.3 Frame a bundle ladder so the buyer does no math
1. Give each tier a real-world reason: room size (sq ft), room type (small or big living room), number of kids, individual vs family. [fc891407623e]
2. Let the buyer self-select by their own context ("How many kits do you need?"). (Card adds explicit labels such as "for small living rooms".) [fc891407623e]
3. For each tier, compute dollars saved and percent off. Show the bigger number. [9f02d954cf3b]
4. Where the mechanic is extra units, say "Buy 2, Get 1 Free" instead of a percent or "X% off the 3rd unit". [ae0a3d67b0b3] [5f2ab9d96cc7]
5. Show cost per kit or cost per day next to the tier price, and the saving per tier, so there is no subtraction to do. [5f2ab9d96cc7]
6. Keep the offer simple: few mechanics to parse. [5f2ab9d96cc7]

### 3.4 Raise perceived value without deeper discounts
1. Dream outcome: find the strongest desire behind the purchase and use it in the ad hook and across the landing page. [4cc6038f44bf] [needs brand data: customer desire research, reviews, survey answers]
2. Likelihood: add proof for the buyer's real worry: UGC, unboxing videos, reviews, ingredient breakdowns, guarantees. [4cc6038f44bf]
3. Time delay: offer faster shipping than competitors; add delivery deadlines. [4cc6038f44bf] [needs brand data: actual delivery times by region] [web check: competitor delivery promises]
4. Effort and risk: add try before you buy, BNPL, or a guarantee. Check ease of use. [4cc6038f44bf] [web check: BNPL providers available in the brand's market and their fees]
5. Put the same desire, proof, speed and risk reversal in the ad, so the page confirms what the ad promised. [0a570cacb952]

### 3.5 Write claims by awareness stage
1. Decide the stage of the traffic: problem aware, solution aware or product aware. [f3e0c5293ed8]
2. Write the claim for that stage (humidifier: morning dryness / seasonal allergies / easiest to clean). [f3e0c5293ed8]
3. Carry the same claim from ad to landing page. [f3e0c5293ed8] [0a570cacb952]
The source does not say how to tell which stage an audience is in; the header note puts that research in 02-AVT.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Running a bigger offer without resetting profit, ROAS and CPA targets. Revenue grows, profit falls. [1cda5c627ada] [471fc0881800]
2. Treating higher AOV as the win, or offering only the most expensive pack. [90e80c5c7974]
3. Believing in a magic formula offer (buy 1 get 1, buy 2 get 2, and so on) instead of testing for your brand. [1cda5c627ada]
4. Only testing creatives and ignoring the offer. [0a570cacb952]
5. Making the buyer do math: fractional percents, no per-tier saving, no per-unit cost. [5f2ab9d96cc7]
6. Showing the smaller of the 2 discount numbers. [9f02d954cf3b]
7. Asking buyers to buy more with no reason why. [fc891407623e]
8. Selling a bland feature ("look slimmer") instead of the desire. [4cc6038f44bf]
9. Slow shipping (2 weeks from China); it hurts conversion and, for subscriptions, churn. [4cc6038f44bf] [c2bff6b33753]
10. Products that are hard to use: no posts, bad reviews, fewer buyers. [4cc6038f44bf]
11. Not knowing your costs in and out, and not using an accountant. [471fc0881800]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Internal tension on AOV.** He says "you always want to be increasing your AOV", and also that higher AOV is not better and must be balanced against conversion. Follow the balance rule and profit per session. [90e80c5c7974]
- **Test design conflict.** The presenter says test offers and creatives at the same time. Card [1cda5c627ada] says hold creative constant to credit the offer. Both are kept. Parallel testing finds combinations; a fixed creative is needed to say the offer caused the result.
- **Profit per session is not fully defined.** The presenter says it is made of conversion rate and AOV and leaves out margin. A deeper discount can raise both and still cut profit. The header formula (net profit / sessions) fixes that, but the source never says which costs count (ad spend, returns, fulfilment). [1cda5c627ada]
- **Loose discount equivalence.** He compares "buy 2 get 1 free" with "save 60%". The real equivalent is 33.3% off the total (section 4). "Free" framing also costs real inventory; check margin per order. [ae0a3d67b0b3]
- **Rule of larger numbers has no test data.** It is a framing default. Test when the 2 numbers are close. A bigger-looking number can still be a discount you cannot afford. [9f02d954cf3b] [web check: Jonah Berger's "rule of 100", which gives the same split at a $100 price point]
- **Value equation described, not quantified.** He lists the 4 parts but not the formula. He also misspeaks: a low-effort product "increases effort and sacrifice"; he means it lowers effort and so raises value. Guarantees and BNPL have real costs; assess against margin. [4cc6038f44bf]
- **Header note on fixed costs goes further than the presenter.** "Use operational savings to fund bundle discount depth" is in the header note and card, not in the presenter's words. It is a sound planning rule but not his. [471fc0881800]
- **Card additions not in the transcript:** keep a low-friction entry tier and keep its promise intact [90e80c5c7974]; repeat the upgrade reason near the tier selector [fc891407623e]; keep choice to 1 selection per category [c2bff6b33753]. On the last point the transcript is garbled: he describes choosing 1 item in each of several categories and combining them, which the card reads as "keep it simple".
- **Card [c2bff6b33753] caveat:** the presenter is unsure of the no-bundle brand's revenue, so treat subscription plus free gift as an alternative, not a better option.
- **Thin on how:** no sample sizes, test length, traffic split, stopping rules, guarantee terms, free-gift selection, subscription pricing, or how to find the dream outcome (header note sends avatar and desire research to 02-AVT). Landing page layout goes to 07-LPG; formal P&L and landed cost go to 06-MEA and 20-FIN; ad copy and deployment go to 11-ADC.
- **Not in conflict with common ecommerce advice on most points.** Profit over revenue, bundle tiers, "free" framing and BNPL are mainstream. The one soft conflict: many operators judge offers by AOV or ROAS alone; this source says profit per session instead.
