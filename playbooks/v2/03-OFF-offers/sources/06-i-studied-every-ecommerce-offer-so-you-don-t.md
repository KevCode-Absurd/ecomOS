## 1. What this source is for

Pick an offer structure that improves the brand's economics, not just a bigger discount. The core job: decide which of 3 trades to make with unit margin (for conversion rate, for AOV, or for LTV), model the downstream effect on CAC, ROAS and profit, and avoid the offers that raise revenue while losing money. It also covers welcome pop-up offers (mystery discounts) and the cash-flow limits of subscription plays.

## 2. Principles

1. **An offer is a trade of unit margin.** Unit margin is what you keep per sale. Every offer spends part of it to buy 1 of 3 things: (a) a higher conversion rate, (b) a bigger cart (AOV), (c) repeat purchases (LTV). Condition: applies to every offer, including free shipping, free gifts and email-capture discounts. Evidence: the speaker's framing, no data. [e311b1e255a4]
2. **The offer is the most sensitive lever in the business.** Source says it is the one lever that moves margin, conversion rate and CAC at the same time. The same offer that takes one brand to 9 figures can break another brand selling the same thing. Evidence: opinion from his client work. [e311b1e255a4]
3. **Every offer has a downstream chain.** Judge it by the second, third and fourth effect, not the first. A multi-pack tier raises AOV, then revenue per order, then ROAS, then lets you afford a higher CAC and scale spend. A mystery pop-up raises opt-in, then lowers CAC, raises ROAS and lets you spend more. Condition: model the chain before launch. [e311b1e255a4] [0090d4f61912] [42cf8bc4ce3a]
4. **Discounting in exchange for a bigger order beats a flat discount.** Examples given: bundle 3 and take 15% off; unlock 10% off once the order crosses $120. "You're still discounting, but you're discounting in exchange for a larger order." [b331e530dfcf] [e311b1e255a4]
5. **A low AOV cannot carry paid acquisition.** A $14 single item with a ~$14 to $25 CAC loses money on every order. Condition: single hero SKU, low price, paid traffic. Fix with a multi-pack, not with free shipping. [52c6ca3043ae] [0090d4f61912]
6. **Free shipping on a low-AOV item raises ROAS and still loses more money.** Conversion and revenue go up, CAC goes down, but the brand now pays shipping on each $14 order with no extra units to cover it. Header calls this the "Death" offer. [52c6ca3043ae]
7. **A good offer gives the customer a better deal and the brand more margin.** The multi-pack lowers price per unit for the buyer and raises profit per order for the brand. The speaker calls this "a win-win" and "the beauty of a good offer". [0090d4f61912]
8. **Brands with 1 product can still bundle by adding related gear.** Example: Grind Machine added free socks and a basketball and cut $2,500 to $2,000. Condition: the add-ons must relate to the product (the card adds this condition; the speaker only shows the example). [0090d4f61912]
9. **Email capture is one of the strongest offers; skipping the pop-up is a mistake.** Source says founders skip it because they think pop-ups and emails are annoying, and calls this "one of the biggest mistakes founders make". A 10 to 15% off opt-in converting at 10 to 18% "substantially" moves back-end margin. [42cf8bc4ce3a]
10. **Hiding the same discount behind a mystery reveal lifts opt-in.** Same 10% discount, full-width pop-up, copy says "Get your mystery offer". Source claims ~7% becomes ~15% opt-in (17.46% at 1 client). Reason given: it is interactive and the shopper feels they might win something. [42cf8bc4ce3a] [63e5c2109fe1]
11. **Subscription offers are an auction play.** Higher LTV lets you pay more per customer and outbid rivals. "The best ad doesn't win... whoever can afford who pays the most wins." Condition: only if you have the cash to carry first-order losses. [5ea541b8c8d2] [0e9661473b27]
12. **A subscribe-and-save offer needs a compelling discount.** Source says a simple 10% off is not enough value to get someone to commit to monthly payments. Example: Symbiotica at 34% off vs $88 one-time. Brands accept a likely loss on order 1 to win orders 2, 3, 4. [00081cdadc00]
13. **Done wrong, a subscription offer is "one of the quickest ways to bleed your business dry".** Stores without cash reserves will "quite literally run out of cash". [00081cdadc00] [5ea541b8c8d2] [0e9661473b27]
14. **Match offer boldness to cash.** Cash-tight store: bundle offers at a profitable ROAS so each ad dollar rolls back into spend. Funded store: subscription with a first-order loss. Aggressive brand: lose even more on order 1 to acquire as many customers as possible. [0e9661473b27]
15. **The fix is structure, not depth.** "It's not just a bigger discount. It's really a different structure in how you package things." Discounts move margin to the customer; a strong structure makes the customer worth more to you. [e311b1e255a4]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Choose an offer type (from the speaker)
1. Work out unit margin: money kept per sale after product and delivery cost. `[needs brand data: price, COGS, shipping and fulfillment cost per order, payment fees]` [e311b1e255a4]
2. Name the goal: more first purchases or emails (trade for conversion), bigger carts (trade for AOV), or repeat orders (trade for LTV). [e311b1e255a4]
3. Check cash position. Limited cash: pick an AOV or bundle offer that is profitable on order 1. Cash reserves: an LTV play with a first-order loss is possible. `[needs brand data: cash on hand, monthly ad spend, payback period]` [0e9661473b27]
4. Model the downstream chain for the chosen offer: CVR, AOV, CAC, ROAS, net profit after COGS and shipping, and for LTV plays month-6 LTV. [e311b1e255a4]
5. Reject the "where brands die" options (10% off the hero product alone, free shipping on a very low order, BOGO without margin to support it). [d5d7d14f51e0]

### 3.2 Fix a low-AOV single hero product (the $14 sock model)
1. Model 3 versions at the same ad spend: (A) single item, customer pays shipping; (B) single item, free shipping; (C) discounted multi-pack. [52c6ca3043ae]
2. For each, compute orders, revenue, ROAS, CAC, COGS, shipping cost and profit after ad spend. Do the math in a sheet or code. [52c6ca3043ae]
3. Build the multi-pack so the per-unit price is lower than buying singles. Source example: 4-pack at about $50 vs 4 x $14 = $56 (about 11% off; he calls it "10% off a four-pack"). [0090d4f61912]
4. Once AOV rises, recompute the CAC you can afford and scale spend against it. [0090d4f61912]
5. If the product cannot be multi-packed (a one-time purchase), bundle related add-ons and discount the set (Grind Machine example). [0090d4f61912]

### 3.3 Add cart-level AOV levers (from the speaker)
1. Pick one: spend threshold discount (10% off over $120), bundle discount (3 items, 15% off), build-your-own bundle (Limmy), cart upsells, free shipping unlocked by adding a product (Dr. Squatch), second-order discounts in cart. [b331e530dfcf] [0090d4f61912]
2. Frame it as "the more you add, the more you save". [b331e530dfcf]
3. Check that the larger cart covers the discount and raises net margin per order. [b331e530dfcf]
The source does not say how to set the threshold level. `[needs brand data: current AOV distribution to place the threshold]`

### 3.4 Welcome pop-up offer (from the speaker)
1. If there is no pop-up or only "join our community", add an offer. Source ranks: no offer (1 to 5% opt-in) < static 10% off (~7%) < mystery 10% off (~15%). [42cf8bc4ce3a]
2. Keep the real discount the same (10 to 15% off). Hide it. Use a full-width pop-up. Copy: "Get your mystery offer". [42cf8bc4ce3a]
3. Pick a reveal format: scratch-off (Giggly), choose a category then reveal (jerky / meat sticks / both; or women's / men's / bestsellers), play a game to see what you win, spin the wheel, quiz to unlock. [63e5c2109fe1] `[web check: your pop-up tool supports this reveal format]` `[verify UI]`
4. Measure opt-in rate, email revenue per subscriber, ESP cost increase and discount cost against the prior version. [42cf8bc4ce3a] `[needs brand data: site visitors per year, current opt-in rate, email revenue per subscriber, ESP pricing tiers]`

### 3.5 Subscription offer (from the speaker, with header additions marked)
1. Estimate month-6 LTV for a one-time buyer and for a subscriber. Source example: $165 vs $289. `[needs brand data: month-6 revenue per customer by acquisition type]` [5ea541b8c8d2]
2. Set the CAC ceiling from subscriber LTV. Source: $289 LTV at "3:1" supports about $95 CAC. [5ea541b8c8d2]
3. Set a subscription discount large enough to beat one-time purchase (Symbiotica 34%, Rise 40% and 50% on the full set). Header only: 25-30% off order 1 plus 5-10% recurring. [00081cdadc00]
4. Optionally add gifts to push opt-in (Everyday Dose mixer, can, journal, scooper, sticks; Rise free products). Header only: gifts must aid use of the product. [eaf22ff0c7f6]
5. Check cash: confirm reserves cover losses until payback. If not, run bundle offers first. `[needs brand data: cash reserves, months to payback]` [0e9661473b27]
6. Consider prepaid 90-day or annual plans at a major discount to pull cash forward. No discount level is given. [90826afade8a]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Free shipping check.** Compare profit after ad spend, COGS and shipping for paid vs free shipping at the same spend. Good: profit rises. Bad: ROAS rises but profit falls. In the sock scenario B breaks even before shipping (100 orders x ($14 - $4) = $1,000 = ad spend), so any shipping cost above about $6.17 per order makes B worse than A's -$617 (our arithmetic). Next: move to a multi-pack or a threshold. `[needs brand data: shipping cost per order]` [52c6ca3043ae]
- **ROAS vs profit.** Do not read a higher ROAS as a win. The speaker's B case has 1.4x ROAS and loses more than the 0.56x case, per the speaker. Always check net profit. [52c6ca3043ae]
- **AOV vs CAC.** If first-order AOV is at or below CAC on a single-item order, the offer loses money on order 1. Fix AOV (bundle) before scaling. `[needs brand data: first-order AOV, blended CAC]` [0090d4f61912]
- **Pop-up check.** Look at the current pop-up. No offer or "stay up to date" copy: add an offer. Static % off: test a mystery reveal with the same discount. Measure opt-in rate and email revenue, net of ESP cost and discount cost. `[needs brand data: opt-in rate, email revenue share, ESP cost]` [42cf8bc4ce3a]
- **Subscription readiness.** Can the brand carry a first-order loss until payback? Compare month-6 LTV for subscribers vs one-time buyers. If cash does not cover the gap, do not run an aggressive subscription offer. [5ea541b8c8d2] [0e9661473b27]
- **Hero discount check.** If the main promo is "10% off" the hero product alone, the speaker says it only buys a small CVR bump and puts you back in the same position. Replace it with a structural offer. [d5d7d14f51e0]
- **Gift check (header only).** For each free gift, ask: does it help the customer use the core product? Tote bags fail; a frother for coffee passes. Gift COGS still count against order-1 margin (card caveat). [eaf22ff0c7f6]
- **Value Equation audit (header only; card adds the scoring idea).** Check the PDP for reviews and demo videos, shipping speed, fast checkout (Shop Pay, 1-click), and an outcome-led name. [7bd7a76b5ba7]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Treating "offer" as "a random discount". [e311b1e255a4]
2. 10% off the hero product alone: moves margin to the customer for a small CVR bump. [d5d7d14f51e0]
3. Free shipping on a very low-AOV order ($14 socks). [52c6ca3043ae]
4. BOGO when unit economics do not support it. [d5d7d14f51e0]
5. Skipping the email pop-up because it seems annoying. [42cf8bc4ce3a]
6. A pop-up with no offer ("join our community", "be the first to know"). [42cf8bc4ce3a]
7. A weak subscription discount (simple 10% off) that does not justify a monthly commitment. [00081cdadc00]
8. Running a first-order-loss subscription offer without the cash to carry it. [5ea541b8c8d2] [0e9661473b27]
9. Reading a higher ROAS as a better result without checking profit. [52c6ca3043ae]
