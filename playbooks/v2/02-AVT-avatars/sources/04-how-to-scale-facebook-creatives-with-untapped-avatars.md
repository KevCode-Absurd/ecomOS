## 1. What this source is for

It gives a 4-step manual research pipeline for finding a buyer group ("avatar") that wants an already-saturated, commodity dropshipping-style product's outcome but is not currently being advertised to, so a smaller operator can avoid competing head-on with bigger-budget incumbents. It covers: mapping the mainstream avatar via the Facebook Ad Library, mining Amazon reviews for outlier buyer stories, validating a candidate's size and pain on Reddit, and running an AI "deep research" prompt to turn a rough sketch into a full written avatar report. It also defines the fixed set of categories a finished avatar record should contain before it is handed to copywriting. It does not cover writing the ads, media buying, or landing pages.

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Map the mainstream avatar in the Facebook Ad Library
1. Open the Facebook/Meta Ad Library and search the product keyword (source example: "ab stimulator"). [verify UI: Ad Library search and filters change] [b399ed19a017]
2. Scan for brands that look like they are scaling: many creative variations, repeated ads, professional production. [b399ed19a017]
3. Play video ads and note the visible person's approximate age, gender and body type. 4. Note the desires and USPs the creative leans on. Source example: "get more defined abs," hands-free use, usable "while working" or "while cooking," and "better than other ab stimulators" — the last one signals a product-aware audience that already knows the category. [b399ed19a017]

6. Repeat across as many scaling competitors as you can find, and write the demographic/psychographic picture down. Source note: an outside data point put this competitor's audience age at 47, which he says roughly matched his visual estimate. [b399ed19a017]

### 3.2 Mine Amazon reviews for candidate avatars
1. Go to the highest-review-count listing in your general product category — it does not need to be the exact product you sell. [083c7052a4f0] [verify UI: Amazon's review sort/filter options change over time]
2. Sort by "Top reviews." If available, also filter to reviews with images or video, and scan positive reviews. [verify UI] [083c7052a4f0]
3. Skip short, generic reviews ("works great"); the source says they rarely carry anything usable. [083c7052a4f0]
4. Look for long reviews that read like a life story — weight history, surgery, stress, age, physical limitations. The source calls these the "gold mines." [083c7052a4f0]
5. Log each distinct profile as a numbered candidate in a document. Source example: "Avatar 1: Anna Z," drawn from a review by "Anna Zagorski" describing losing 35 pounds but retaining stomach fat attributed to a C-section and stress. Do not store the reviewer's full name or other identifying detail beyond what the avatar profile needs — the source raises this himself as an ethical line. [083c7052a4f0]
6. Continue until you have 3 to 4 distinct candidate avatar ideas. Source's own second, undeveloped example: men around 65 with pre-existing conditions who cannot easily get to a gym but still want visible ab results, reasoned by analogy to older men buying Viagra ("it's probably stands to say they want abs too"). [083c7052a4f0]

### 3.3 Validate a candidate on Reddit
1. Turn the review's specific detail into a plain Google search. Source example: "post C-section weight gain Reddit." [70b5dd026992]
2. Open several resulting threads and judge volume and recency: many active threads signal a common, recurring problem rather than a one-off. [70b5dd026992]
3. Read for verbatim vocabulary (example: "C-section pooch"), existing beliefs (example: "the only way to have a flat stomach after a C-section is with surgery"), failed solutions, and emotional state (examples: "so depressed over it," references to body confidence and self-image). [70b5dd026992]
4. Confirm the product's actual outcome matches the pain found. Source's own read: an ab stimulator promising a tighter midsection fits a postpartum woman who "desperately wants" that outcome. [70b5dd026992]

### 3.4 Assemble the preliminary avatar profile
1. Write a rough demographic band from the ad-library and review/Reddit research. Source examples: mainstream competitor avatar = men roughly early 40s to mid-60s; candidate avatar = women late 20s to early 40s, postpartum, gave birth via C-section. [208f632d3ddc]
2. State the core problem in the avatar's own words (example: "C-section pooch," "flabbiness around the midsection"). [208f632d3ddc]
3. List desires and USPs already known to matter in the category (example: hands-free, usable while working or cooking). [208f632d3ddc]
4. Record known beliefs and failed solutions (example: believes only surgery fixes it; may have tried cheap knockoff devices). [208f632d3ddc]
5. Mark the document as preliminary. The source repeats, per candidate, that this is "not yet a real avatar." [208f632d3ddc]

### 3.5 Prioritize among candidates
1. Rank candidates primarily on differentiation — which is least served by existing Facebook advertisers. [09f09782497e]
2. Before committing to one, check all 3: the avatar genuinely struggles with the problem, enough such people exist, and they desperately want the product's outcome. [09f09782497e]
3. Keep adjacent unserved pockets found during review mining as a backlog in case the top choice fails validation (source's own aside: older men with mobility limits). [09f09782497e]
4. Take the strongest-evidenced, most-isolated candidate into deep research first. [09f09782497e]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Mainstream-avatar overlap check.** How: compare your candidate's demographic/psychographic notes against the Ad Library mapping from 3.1. Good: clearly different age/gender/desire profile. Bad: same profile as a brand already scaling in the category. Next: if bad, expect to compete on CPA against a bigger budget and a better offer; return to review mining for a different candidate. [b399ed19a017] [0934edc554c3]
- **Review-depth check.** How: read the length and narrative content of an Amazon review. Good: a multi-sentence account of the reviewer's situation (medical history, life stage, prior attempts). Bad: short, generic praise or complaint. Next: skip bad reviews; only "gold mine" reviews get logged as candidates. [083c7052a4f0]
- **Market-size and pain check.** How: count and read the recency of Reddit threads matching the candidate's problem, from a plain-language search. Good: many active, recent threads describing the same pain in similar words. Bad: 1 old thread, low engagement. Next: do not move an unproven candidate into deep research; this is the source's "heavy burden of proof." [70b5dd026992]
- **Preliminary-vs-final record check.** How: check whether the avatar document has sourced verbatim quotes, failed-solution history and limiting beliefs, or only a demographic guess. Good: full categories filled in from deep research. Bad: the bare-bones sketch from 3.4 is still what is being used to brief copy. Next: run, or finish, the deep-research step before handoff. [208f632d3ddc] [2b67b2edcb1a]
- **Competitive-isolation check.** How: search the Ad Library for the candidate avatar's likely angle/keywords. Good: little or no advertising aimed at this exact group. Bad: another advertiser already owns this avatar too. Next: re-run the 3-part proof test (struggle, size, desire) rather than assuming it is still a "gold mine." [09f09782497e]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Trying to sell a commodity/dropshipped product to the same avatar a bigger-budget competitor already owns, without a genuine new mechanism — expect "scraps" at best. [0934edc554c3] [60a58f44e42d]
2. Claiming a "new mechanism" without real visual, mechanical or functional product difference — the audience will not believe it. [60a58f44e42d]
3. Reading short, generic Amazon reviews for avatar insight; they rarely carry anything usable. [083c7052a4f0]
4. Storing a reviewer's full name or other identifying personal detail when logging a candidate — the source raises this himself as an ethical line and only logs a short identifier (example: "Anna Z"). [083c7052a4f0]
5. Skipping the Reddit market-size/pain validation and moving straight to deep research on an unproven candidate. [70b5dd026992]
6. Treating the bare-bones ads/reviews/Reddit sketch as a finished avatar; it is explicitly only a starting input for deep research, not something to write copy from directly. [208f632d3ddc]
7. Choosing an avatar for novelty or isolation alone, without confirming real struggle, real market size and real desire for the outcome. [09f09782497e]
8. Pasting AI deep-research output straight into ads without reading it and checking the cited sources yourself. [be3576be73a2]
9. Stopping at the shallow ad-library/review picture instead of running deep research; the presenter calls that early picture "very shallow." [b399ed19a017] [be3576be73a2]
