## 1. What this source is for

A pep-talk plus lightweight method for building customer avatars that are specific enough to differentiate a creative or offer, aimed at ecom brand owners whose ads have stopped scaling. It argues the fix for stalled creative testing is deeper customer research, not more creative variations, and gives a short list of places to mine verbatim buyer language: competitor 1-star reviews, condition-specific Reddit forums, Shopify post-purchase surveys, and direct phone/DM outreach. It stops short of a repeatable research protocol (sample sizes, interview scripts, coding method) and defers "the exact ways we do it" to the author's paid program.

## 2. Principles

1. A demographic label is not an avatar. "30+ year old woman with knee pain" fails because a huge number of competing products already target that same label, so nothing about the creative stands out. [f6f18000b06c]
2. Test whether you actually understand your avatar by asking three questions: what do they vent to a friend about, what have they already tried, what promises are they sick of hearing. If you can't answer these, the source's claim is that your ad concepts are guesswork, not problem-solving. [f6f18000b06c]
3. The source states there are only two ways to win against competitors: a genuinely superior product, or a much more specific avatar than they use. Ideally you have both; if you lack a superior product, niching the avatar is presented as the main remaining lever. This is the source's framing, not a proven law. [f6f18000b06c]
4. The gap between the avatar you assume and the real one is claimed to bleed into creative strategy, CRO, offers, and retention, i.e. every downstream decision, not just ad copy. [f6f18000b06c] [a968d0d00a11]
5. Universal baseline for any avatar record: quoted evidence (verbatim buyer language), a trigger moment (the situation that puts them in-market), and a job statement (the day-to-day progress they want, including restored identity markers like a sport or hobby). The source calls this baseline universal but explicitly says the *number* of avatars a business needs is not universal. [c5efc108c9c9] [f6f18000b06c]
6. Research should happen in as many places as possible, not just one. A single forum or single review set is called an unrepresentative slice. [912d3aa3c168]
7. Copy should reuse buyers' own phrasing rather than being brainstormed by the marketer; the source's phrase is "you don't reinvent the wheel." [2fd2d4d2c90b] [9b44ebb5a6e9]
8. Two buyers with the identical diagnosis are different avatars if their age cohort, motive, or platform differs (example: a 20-year-old wanting competitive running vs. a 50-year-old wanting to run for fun, both post-knee-surgery). Map the desire, not just the diagnosis. [9b44ebb5a6e9]
9. Creative, pre-lander, and landing page must stay congruent on the same named condition and the same buyer journey; switching conditions between funnel stages (e.g. targeting a patellar tendon graft in the ad but talking about arthritis on the landing page) is called out as a conversion killer. [873f2122bf91]
10. The offer is not just price or bundle structure. The source's framing: the way you phrase the promise and set expectations *is* part of the offer, and avatar-specific framing can make a generic product feel "one of one and bespoke" without changing the underlying product. [a968d0d00a11]

## 3. Procedures

**A. Mine competitor 1-star reviews**
1. Go to Amazon (or wherever your category is reviewed) and pull up competitor listings. [web check: identify the specific competitor ASINs/listings relevant to your product]
2. Read the 1-star reviews specifically (not all reviews). [2fd2d4d2c90b]
3. Catalog, verbatim: explicit failure points, mechanical/physical friction (the source's example: "I was in serious pain and couldn't get the brace off my knee fast enough"), broken trust markers (things buyers say they no longer believe), and repeated product claims that feel exhausted from overuse. [2fd2d4d2c90b]
4. Flag any angle competitors are not targeting that shows up in the complaints (the source's example: reviews mentioning post-knee-surgery use suggested an underused "post-surgery recovery" angle for a knee brace). [2fd2d4d2c90b] [912d3aa3c168]
5. Carry the exact phrases forward into pre-lander, landing page, and creative copy rather than paraphrasing them.

**B. Mine condition-specific forums (Reddit and niche groups)**
1. Find forums built around the specific condition or problem, not general social feeds. [web check: locate the relevant subreddits or niche forums for your category] [912d3aa3c168]
2. Read what people actually discuss in their own words (example: "post-op knees hurt") and log it as candidate copy.
3. Segment by consequence, not just condition: e.g. "can't do their job" vs. "can't play sports" are treated as different avatars needing different messaging.
4. Harvest specialized procedural/clinical vocabulary (example: "patellar tendon graft"). The source's claim is that using the exact term makes the affected person feel immediately seen, while it means nothing to a general audience. Verify the term is actually in common use in that community before anchoring a hook on it. [912d3aa3c168]
5. Cross-reference forum findings against reviews, Google search, and social platforms rather than treating one forum as sufficient. [912d3aa3c168]

**C. Run direct outreach (phone calls and DMs)**
1. Call existing buyers on an ongoing basis, not as a one-off study. The source cites (unverified, self-reported) a "nine-figure" acquaintance whose whole team still calls customers just to ask how things are going. [99043b84fe3d]
2. DM buyers directly and ask what their concerns are; specifically ask what surprised them about the product or their situation. The source calls DM outreach underrated. [needs brand data: which channel your buyers actually use for DMs] [99043b84fe3d]
3. In both calls and DMs, probe day-to-day lifestyle limitations (e.g. inability to do a defining hobby), not just symptoms.
4. Log recurring frustrations, specific repeated phrases, and broken trust markers across calls/DMs, the same categories as the review-mining step.

**D. Run a Shopify post-purchase survey**
1. Set up an automated survey that fires after purchase. [verify UI: current Shopify post-purchase survey / app setup] [99043b84fe3d]
2. Use email as a secondary follow-up channel for the same questions.
3. Ask about day-to-day friction and restricted activities, matching the categories used in reviews and calls, so results are comparable across sources.

**E. Build and use the avatar record**
1. For each avatar, require: quoted evidence, a trigger moment, and a job statement (see Principle 5). Reject any draft that is only demographic fields with no job attached. [c5efc108c9c9]
2. Record age-cohort-specific phrasing and platform habits separately per cohort (see Principle 8), since two buyers with the same diagnosis can need different vocabulary and different channels. [9b44ebb5a6e9]
3. Store restricted lifestyle/identity activities and specific procedural vocabulary in the record so any copywriter can work from it directly without re-interviewing customers. [c5efc108c9c9]

**F. Turn the avatar into a creative/landing-page belief sequence**
1. Open the hook by naming the avatar's exact situation (source's example: "do you have a patellar tendon graft / post-op knee?"). [873f2122bf91]
2. Move into the scenario they've lived through, naming the specific failed solution (e.g. physical therapy).
3. State briefly why that prior solution failed (e.g. "it doesn't address the core problem"), which is where your product's mechanism becomes credible.
4. Show the failed attempt and the new mechanism rather than only asserting them; the source insists on depicting, not just claiming.
5. Keep the same named condition and journey across ad, pre-lander, and landing page. Do not switch conditions between funnel stages. [873f2122bf91]
6. Reuse the same "name-the-prospect" hook pattern the source attributes to Alex Hormozi-style ads (example: "are you a gym owner?") before delivering value. [873f2122bf91]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- Check: can you answer what your customer vents to a friend about, what they've already tried, and what promises they're sick of hearing? Good: specific, sourced answers. Bad: generic or "I don't know." Fix: run procedures A-D before writing more creative. [f6f18000b06c]
- Check: does your avatar record contain quoted evidence, a trigger moment, and a job statement? Good: all three present. Bad: only demographic fields. Fix: reject the record and re-run research until all three exist. [c5efc108c9c9]
- Check: are creative, pre-lander, and landing page naming the same condition and journey? Good: congruent throughout. Bad: creative names one condition, landing page names another. Fix: rewrite the weaker asset to match, don't launch until congruent. [873f2122bf91]
- Check: is the phrasing you're using actually attested in the age cohort/platform you're targeting? Good: pulled from that cohort's own posts/reviews/calls. Bad: borrowed from a different generation's platform (source's example: using TikTok phrasing for a 50+ audience). Fix: re-source phrasing from where that specific cohort actually posts. [9b44ebb5a6e9] [needs brand data: confirm your buyers' actual platform habits by age cohort]
- Check: are ad concepts stalling out (test after test, "in copium," nothing scaling)? The source's diagnosis is this is a research gap, not a creative-execution gap. Fix: treat it as a signal to restart at review/forum/call mining before writing new creative variants.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating a demographic label ("30+ year old woman with knee pain") as an avatar. [f6f18000b06c]
- Building persona documents with demographic fields and no job statement attached.
- Brainstorming ad copy from imagination instead of pulling verbatim buyer language ("reinventing the wheel").
- Doing research once instead of continuously (contrasted against the cited nine-figure team's ongoing call habit).
- Using the wrong cohort's platform phrasing (example given: TikTok-style phrasing aimed at a 50+ audience).
- Letting the creative and landing page name different conditions or journeys (patellar tendon graft in the ad, arthritis on the page).
- Asserting a failed-solution scenario in copy without showing it, i.e. claiming congruence without depicting it.
- Believing a superior avatar alone can rescue a product that fails the buyer's actual job; the source only claims niching helps when you lack a superior product, not that it replaces one.
- Relying on a single research source (one forum, one review set) instead of triangulating across reviews, forums, surveys and calls.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- All revenue, scale, and community-size figures in this source are self-reported and promotional (author's own claims plus one secondhand "nine-figure" friend). Treat as unverified; do not cite as evidence of the method's effectiveness.
- The source explicitly defers its actual depth ("the exact ways we do it") to a paid program ("evolve program"). This transcript alone gives no interview script, no coding taxonomy for organizing quotes, no target sample sizes, and no template for the avatar record beyond "quotes + trigger + job." Say plainly: thin on operational how at the sample-size and template level.
- The source states two competing claims that need to be held separately, per its own wording: (a) avatar specificity is presented as close to a universal lever ("the brands that clear the hardest are the ones that understand the customers the best"), while (b) it also says a genuinely superior product is a second, independent way to win, and ideally you have both. Do not compress these into "avatar research beats product quality" — the source does not claim that.
- Possible tension with common ecommerce/Meta advertising advice: current mainstream Meta guidance (Advantage+ / broad targeting) generally pushes advertisers toward broad audiences and letting the algorithm find buyers, rather than hand-built niche personas used for manual audience targeting. This source's audience-narrowing recommendation is about avatar-driven *messaging and creative angles*, not manual ad-set audience targeting, so it does not directly contradict broad-targeting delivery advice, but a reader could conflate the two. - The source's age-cohort-to-platform mapping (50+ on Facebook groups/Amazon, younger on TikTok) is presented as the source's own illustrative guess, not research. It should not be treated as a validated segmentation rule for any given brand. [needs brand data: verify actual platform usage by age cohort]
- No mention of statistical rigor: there is no guidance on how many quotes or calls are needed before treating a phrase as a real pattern rather than noise. Common qualitative-research practice would call for a minimum sample or saturation check; this source does not address it.
