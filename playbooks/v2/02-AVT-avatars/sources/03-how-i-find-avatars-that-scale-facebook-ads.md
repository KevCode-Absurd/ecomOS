## 1. What this source is for

It gives a manual, non-AI research protocol for building a real customer avatar from public forums (mainly Reddit) and Amazon reviews, instead of defaulting to a demographic or an AI-generated persona. The goal is to extract an avatar's exact emotional mind state, trigger moments, failed past solutions and verbatim vocabulary, then use that material to judge whether existing ad copy and hooks actually speak to the customer. It does not cover writing the hooks or scripts themselves, or designing pre-landers and product pages for a specific avatar; the source's own scope note routes those to 10-MCR and 07-LPG.

## 2. Principles

1. **A demographic is not an avatar.** Condition: any time you are about to write ad copy, a VSL or a landing page. The source's test: age and gender alone ("women 45 and up with cats") say nothing about the person's fears, dreams, current situation or how they'd describe the problem to a friend at 2 a.m. An avatar needs a specific person with situational and emotional context. Evidence: the source's own contrast between "women 45+" and Margaret, 55, an empty nester on her third cat, who has already lost two cats to chronic kidney disease (CKD) and tried fountains, different water bowls and adding water to food. [0d27b0e8ba75]
2. **The 30-second riff test gates whether you're ready to write.** Condition: before writing copy. If you cannot riff for 30 seconds, unprompted, on exactly who your avatar is, their hopes and dreams, their current situation, the problem they face, and what they'd wish for if they could snap their fingers, the source says you need more research first. This is a self-check, not a scored instrument. [0d27b0e8ba75]
3. **Struggling ads usually trace back to a weak avatar, not a weak tactic.** Condition: when ads aren't converting or scaling. The source frames this as the reason to do the research at all; it opens by dismissing questions about ad-set counts or exact cost-cap dollar amounts as a distraction from the real problem. [0d27b0e8ba75] [5d6b9c23511a]
4. **Do the research manually before using AI, especially if you're new to it.** Condition: deciding whether to run an AI deep-research prompt instead of, or before, manual forum and review mining. The source's argument: AI tools (Gemini, ChatGPT, Claude) have "unmatched efficiency and level of detail" once you know what you're looking for, but become a crutch when you don't, because polished AI output is easy to mistake for real understanding. He compares blind AI deep-research to "answering a bunch of emails and thinking you're productive" — busy, not effective. His own claim: he did more meaningful avatar work by manually reading forums than most people do "by blindly copying ChatGPT and Claude." [4e2d70bdca59]
5. **Use AI afterward, not instead, once you can evaluate its output.** Condition: after a manual research sprint. Use AI to accelerate work you can already judge, since an LLM output is only as good as the operator's inputs. The source recommends auditing your current AI-written copy against your manual research afterward, expecting to find lines that are "totally off base." [4e2d70bdca59]
6. **Two public sources carry the whole method: public discussion forums (Reddit) and Amazon reviews.** Condition: any manual avatar research sprint. Reddit is named as the largest public discussion library for understanding a customer avatar; Amazon reviews are the secondary source. The source frames the split as roughly 80% forums / 20% Amazon reviews, but see the timing conflict noted in section 9. [d75e33882fdb]
7. **Emotional mind state determines the copy register, and it differs by avatar.** Condition: once you have raw research, before choosing how to write to it. Log whether the avatar's dominant feeling is guilt, embarrassment, shame, frustration, fear, anxiety or depression. The source's contrast: a drop shipper who wastes money on a failed test feels frustration; an empty nester afraid of losing a pet feels fear, anxiety, possibly depression. Frustration copy can call out wasted effort and broken promises; fear copy should speak to loss, worst-case outcomes and protection. [dae729a4f6bb]
8. **Name the exact failed solution, not the category.** Condition: writing objection-handling copy or hooks that reference what the avatar already tried. The source's example: write "have you tried minoxidil," not "have you tried hair supplements or drugs"; write "fountains and water bowls," not "hydration products." Capture cost and extensiveness where mentioned (a forum user's $6,500 + $5,000 Neograft spend). [326d0318ebcc]
9. **A hook needs understanding before it needs curiosity.** Condition: writing a hook line. A curiosity-driving hook alone ("the easiest way to drop ship is not what you think") can still fail because it doesn't make the avatar feel understood, and the source says the target audience will "roll their eyes" at it. The fix is to lead with the avatar's specific situation and deepest fear, then layer curiosity and identity-specific phrasing on top. [301d338a9f16]
10. **An avatar record isn't usable for creative work until it has quotes, a trigger and a job.** Condition: cataloguing research into a reusable avatar record, or auditing an existing one. Required: quoted evidence from real sources, a specific trigger moment (the situation that starts the search or purchase), and a job statement of the progress the avatar wants — not just demographic fields. Generic persona templates ("30+ year old woman with knee pain") do not count. [ef0ecef3dfbb]
11. **Research depth should scale with how big a result you're chasing.** Condition: deciding how much research effort to invest before scaling ad spend. The source's claim: a $1k day still needs an avatar, but a shot at $10k days requires knowing the avatar in depth. This is presented as his own belief tied to his long-form Facebook image-ad and VSL format, with no spend, budget or statistical benchmark attached. [5d6b9c23511a]
12. **Segment by pre-existing insecurity, because it changes the copy register.** Condition: the problem you're selling against can either be a standalone issue or stack on top of an existing confidence problem. The source's example: a 31-year-old man, single, "a bit chubby," with pre-existing self-esteem issues, for whom hair loss compounds an existing struggle, versus a man whose life is otherwise "put together" who starts balding. Repeated forum preference ("better to be bald than balding," avoiding "no man's land") is evidence of how the avatar frames the comparison themselves. [de6f52d00231]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 The 60-minute manual avatar research sprint (the source's core protocol)

1. Pick a starting demographic (age and gender) if you don't already have one. The source's suggested shortcut: find the top customer demographic profile of your largest competitor using SimilarWeb, another spy tool, or by asking a chatbot directly, e.g. "what is the top customer demographic profile of [competitor]." `[needs brand data: the actual competitor(s) and their demographic profile from a spy tool or analytics access]` [d75e33882fdb]
2. Open a plain Google Doc (or equivalent) as your research library. No template or structure is needed; just paste entries with spacing between them. [3db1dccaa8da]
3. Set a total research block of 60 minutes, split into two phases (see the timing note in section 9 on the 80/20-vs-30/30 conflict). [d75e33882fdb]
4. Phase 1, public forums (Reddit named as the largest public discussion library; also applies to niche forums for other categories): mentally adopt the avatar's mind state, literally imagining you are that person sitting down with their problem. Search using the avatar's own words, not marketer language. The source's example queries:
   - "my cat won't drink water, please help, Reddit"
   - "how do I get my cat to drink more water Reddit"
   - "my cat refuses to drink water"
   - "hair loss making me less attractive Reddit" / "straight up hair loss making me less attractive, Reddit"
   Add identity or demographic qualifiers to sharpen results, e.g. adding "men" or "ask men over 30" when researching men 30-50 with hair loss. [f1ca3f6fbc70]
5. Click into threads and read them word for word inside the timed block; let the vocabulary in one thread guide the next query, since threads reveal adjacent phrasings and subreddits worth searching. [f1ca3f6fbc70]
6. Read the comments, not just the original post. The source finds some of the richest material in replies, and singles out confession-oriented communities such as r/offmychest, where people describe a problem's emotional toll in extreme detail. [f1ca3f6fbc70]
7. Copy-paste whole posts and comments verbatim into the doc; do not paraphrase or summarize. Add spacing between entries so they stay distinct. [3db1dccaa8da]
8. Bold or underline standout phrases and keywords: emotional language, named failed solutions, situational detail and self-descriptions. Screenshots are acceptable, and often faster, for a post dense with material; use bold/underline text when you want it to be findable later by search. [3db1dccaa8da]
9. Preserve exact wording, including harsh or self-deprecating phrasing, rather than sanitizing it. The source's example: capture "do you hate and feel like your balding is making you look uglier," not a softened "are you tired of looking worse." [3db1dccaa8da]
10. Phase 2, Amazon reviews: repeat the same process for 30 minutes on reviews of competing products, harvesting the same four categories: fears, desired outcomes, emotional mind states, and previously tried/failed solutions. [d75e33882fdb]
11. If you can't do the full hour in one sitting, spread it across multiple days, but complete it before writing copy. [d75e33882fdb]
12. After the sprint, log the avatar's dominant emotional mind state (frustration, fear, anxiety, depression, guilt, embarrassment or shame) explicitly, since it sets the copy register for step 13 and beyond. [dae729a4f6bb]
13. Compare your current or AI-written ad copy against the research. Look for lines that are off-base: not what the customer wants, not how they describe the problem, or not the emotion they actually feel. Use the gaps as your improvement list for the next round of ad tests. [4e2d70bdca59] [5d6b9c23511a]

### 3.2 Worked example: hair-restoration product, men 30-50 (from the source's live demo)

1. Presenter imagines himself as a 45-year-old man struggling with hair loss (his target demographic for the featured product, an "eye restore"-style red-light/laser hair therapy device claiming natural regrowth in about 3 months without drugs or surgery, per his own description).
2. Searches "hair loss making me less attractive, Reddit."
3. Finds and copies a 29-year-old's post asking whether receding hairlines make men unappealing to women 25 and older.
4. Reads the comments: one woman says she isn't attracted to bald men; another commenter states a preference by race (unverified, one person's opinion, not a demographic fact); another suggests minoxidil, which the presenter flags as a specific failed-solution to reuse verbatim in copy instead of a generic "hair supplements or drugs." [326d0318ebcc]
5. Finds a comment from a 50-year-old man with a full head of hair, reporting that women have told him he's lucky, alongside advice to "pump up what else you have going for you" (personality, physique) — captured as a counter-perspective, not the dominant one.
6. Finds a repeated preference statement across commenters: it's better to be fully bald than in the "balding" in-between stage ("no man's land"). [de6f52d00231]
7. Searches again with "men" added, then finds an r/offmychest post: a man describes depression over his hair loss, a friend pointing it out condescendingly in front of a room of people, and everyone staring. The presenter says he would build ad copy from this single account alone, since it captures a concrete worst-case emotional moment rather than an abstract complaint. [dae729a4f6bb]
8. Finds a post from a 31-year-old, single man who describes himself as "a bit chubby" with existing self-confidence and self-esteem issues, for whom hair has been one of his few sources of confidence since high school; used to define the "pre-existing insecurity" segment. [de6f52d00231]
9. Finds a comment reporting $6,500 spent on Neograft hair-transplant work on the front of the scalp a year earlier, and $5,000 more on Neograft for the crown 6 months later; the commenter frames the combined roughly $11,500 as about $300 a month "to have hair until you're 70," while also calling the upfront number "shitty." Captured as a specific, named, costed failed/considered solution. [326d0318ebcc]
10. Synthesis the presenter draws from roughly one forum's worth of threads: the avatar is a man in his 30s, often already carrying some self-confidence deficit, aware of and pricing out minoxidil and Neograft-style surgical rebuilds, whose dominant emotion is embarrassment and self-consciousness, with the sharpest trigger being public exposure or humiliation in front of others.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check:** Can you riff for 30 seconds, unscripted, on the avatar's identity, hopes, current situation, problem and snap-fingers wish? Good: yes, fluently, with specifics. Bad: no, or you default to a demographic ("women 45+"). Fix: run the 60-minute research sprint before writing. [0d27b0e8ba75]
- **Check:** Does the avatar record include a documented failed-solution history? Good: named products/procedures (e.g. minoxidil, Neograft, specific water-bowl types). Bad: blank, or a generic category ("tried some things"). Fix: the source treats an empty failed-solution field as a sign the research isn't done, since it says struggling ads usually trace back to insufficient avatar understanding. [0d27b0e8ba75] [326d0318ebcc]
- **Check:** Audit current or AI-drafted ad copy against the manual research findings. Good: copy matches the customer's own words, problem framing and dominant emotion. Bad: copy is generic, addresses the wrong emotion, or states the problem the way a marketer would rather than the way the customer does ("my product test failed" vs. "I launched and got $200 CPMs"). Fix: rewrite from the verbatim quotes; cut lines that speak to a different emotion than the one the research shows. [4e2d70bdca59] [dae729a4f6bb]
- **Check:** Does a hook lead with situational understanding, or with curiosity alone? Bad example given: "the easiest way to drop ship is not what you think" (curiosity, no recognition — invites eye-rolls). Good pattern: situational call-out + curiosity mechanic + deepest fear + identity-specific language, as in the cat-hook rebuild. Fix: rebuild any curiosity-only hook by inserting the avatar's specific situation and fear first. [301d338a9f16]
- **Check:** Does the copy match the avatar's confidence baseline? Good: copy written for an already-insecure avatar acknowledges the compounding effect; copy for an already-confident avatar treats the problem as a new, standalone threat. Bad: one-size-fits-all copy across both segments. Fix: segment by self-description found in the research (e.g. "self-confidence and self-esteem issues" language) before finalizing hooks. [de6f52d00231]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Treating a demographic (age plus gender) as if it were an avatar, e.g. "women 45+" or "women 45 and up with cats." [0d27b0e8ba75]
2. Using generic persona templates with demographic fields and no job statement, e.g. "30+ year old woman with knee pain." [ef0ecef3dfbb]
3. Jumping straight to AI-generated "deep research" personas before you know what you're looking for, mistaking polished AI output for real avatar understanding. [4e2d70bdca59]
4. Writing vague, generic pain-point call-outs ("are you tired of this pain point") instead of the avatar's own specific language. [301d338a9f16]
5. Building a hook around curiosity alone without first establishing that you understand the avatar's situation ("the easiest way to drop ship is not what you think"). [301d338a9f16]
6. Naming a generic solution category in copy instead of the exact product or procedure the avatar mentions (say "minoxidil," not "hair supplements or drugs"; say "fountains and water bowls," not "hydration products"). [326d0318ebcc]
7. Sanitizing the avatar's own harsh or self-deprecating language into softer, marketing-safe phrasing, which the source says loses authenticity. [3db1dccaa8da]
8. Reading only the original post and skipping the comments, where the source says some of the richest material lives. [f1ca3f6fbc70]
9. Generalizing from a single quote or forum user as if it represents the whole audience, rather than looking for repetition across many threads. [3db1dccaa8da] [ef0ecef3dfbb]
10. Obsessing over minor tactical variables (ad-set counts, exact cost-cap dollar amounts) instead of fixing the underlying avatar gap, which the presenter frames as the actual bottleneck to scaling. [0d27b0e8ba75]
11. Writing the same copy register to avatars with different dominant emotions, e.g. treating a fear-driven buyer the same as a frustration-driven buyer. [dae729a4f6bb]

Treat the 60-minute total and Reddit-first sequencing as reliable; treat "80/20" as the presenter's framing of Reddit's relative importance as an information source, not the timed allocation he actually demonstrates. - **All numeric outcomes are unverified.** $10k/day, $1k/day, "$200 CPMs and $5 CPCs," the competitor traffic/AOV aside, and the Neograft cost anecdote are self-reported by the presenter or quoted from unverified third-party forum comments. - **Commercial interest.** The presenter sells a drop-shipping course and "School" community built around this ad-copy style; the $10k/day framing doubles as marketing for that offer. This is a stated conflict of interest, not neutral instruction.
- **No avatar validation step.** The source never describes validating a forum- or review-derived avatar against actual buyer data, a customer interview, or quantitative sizing. It treats forum commentary as equivalent to buyer voice; someone posting about hair loss on Reddit is not confirmed to be a buyer of any product, let alone the one being marketed.
- **No rule for multiple avatars per product.** Both worked examples in this source (frustration-driven drop shippers vs. fear-driven pet owners; confident vs. insecure hair-loss segments) surface more than one plausible emotional segment, but the source gives no method for choosing among them beyond going with whichever emotion appears most often in the research.
- **No mention of ad-platform policy on sensitive claims.** The method explicitly leans into naming sensitive personal conditions and attributes (baldness, pet terminal illness, self-esteem) in hook language. - **No refresh cadence.** The source gives no guidance on when to redo the research sprint as a product ages, a market shifts, or a subreddit's active population changes.
