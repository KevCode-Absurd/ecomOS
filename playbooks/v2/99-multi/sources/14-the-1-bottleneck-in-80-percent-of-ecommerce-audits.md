## 1. What this source is for

A paid-media measurement audit script: how to explain to a client or an internal team why platform-reported ROAS is not trustworthy, how to replace it with a small stack of finance-grade and causal metrics, and how to size the response (reporting fix vs lift test vs geo-lift) to the business's revenue level. It is built for an agency running Meta-led paid acquisition for $1M to $20M+ ecommerce brands, and it is the strongest and most detailed of the finance/attribution content in this batch: it gives named formulas, a graded reliability pyramid, worked numeric examples, a 9-item mistake checklist, and revenue-banded action plans. It is thin on offers, creative, landing pages, email/retention and general ops; those domains are mentioned only in passing (see section 3).

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Diagnosis and prioritization
1. **Frame the business as 3 stacked pillars, with data integrity as the base.** Data integrity/assurance and measurement sits at the bottom, creative velocity and diversity in the middle, technical account structure on top. The presenter's claim: the top 2 layers are meaningless if the base is wrong, because you optimize creative and budget against false signals. Condition: this is the presenter's own mental model, not a cited framework. [4f1e89e8a798]
2. **Growth is solving 1 bottleneck at a time.** "A business will always not grow due to one particular bottleneck... as you grow, another bottleneck appears." The operating task is to find the current constraint fast and resource it, not to work every lever at once. [4f1e89e8a798]
3. **You can only find the true bottleneck if the data has integrity.** If the data gives false flags, you cannot diagnose anything reliably; data integrity therefore gates every other diagnosis. [4f1e89e8a798]
4. **The failure-mode test: "on the treadmill."** If dashboards show improving ROAS quarter over quarter but profit and revenue are flat, and the team still feels like it is "constantly iterating but doing nothing," that feeling is presented as a symptom of a metrics problem, not a strategy problem. [4f1e89e8a798]

### 2.2 Why platform ROAS misleads (the core argument)
5. **All ROAS is not equal, in 3 distinct ways, and none of them wash out.** (a) Across campaigns: 2 campaigns with the identical reported ROAS can carry very different margin (a discounted, lower-margin campaign vs a full-price retention campaign), so profit contribution must be compared, not ROAS. (b) Across platforms: attribution models differ so much (view-through counted or not, tile-based crediting) that a 7x Pinterest vs a 2.5x Meta is not evidence to shift budget to Pinterest. (c) Across the funnel: a cold top-of-funnel campaign at 2x can be more valuable than a bottom-funnel retargeting campaign at 7x, because growth depends on continuous top-of-funnel injection; pulling spend up the funnel to fund a high-ROAS bottom-funnel line "is when the business starts to collapse." [7c355072e26e]
6. **Cross-channel halo can fake a channel's success.** Worked illustration: launch TikTok at roughly 25% of Meta's budget, TikTok itself reads 1 to 2x (looks like a failure), but a Meta mid-funnel campaign jumps from 4x to 12x because the TikTok-driven traffic gets retargeted and converted on Meta. Reading this as "Meta cold targeting is working, increase Meta cold budget" wastes money; the lift actually came from TikTok. [7c355072e26e]
7. **The presenter's position on being ROAS-led:** "If you are north-starring against [platform ROAS], you're probably 3 years behind competitors" who use commercially-aligned metrics. Framed as opinion/rhetoric, not a measured claim. [7c355072e26e]
8. **3 sources of ROAS over-inflation, and they compound with business size.** (1) View-through conversions — Meta's 1-day view credits a conversion from a mere impression, no click or interaction required, and is the presenter's single most repeated culprit ("30 to 50% of ROAS is just coming from those view-throughs"). (2) Existing-customer bleed-over — customers served inside a nominally "cold" campaign get credited for purchases they'd likely have made anyway (worked example: a supplement repurchase near pack-empty). (3) Third-party halo effects — an off-platform activation (a pop-up, a retail event) can triple a region's digital numbers with zero causal link to the ad account; pumping spend into that region on the strength of the ROAS spike is presented as a likely bad decision. All 3 get worse as a business matures: more existing customers to bleed from, more channels (including retail stores, treated here as a form of advertising), and more organic presence inflating paid reads. [d63cd6fefbdc]
9. **The fix to over-attribution is subtraction, not addition.** The presenter says roughly half the market already understands these attribution flaws, and the common but wrong response is to add more attribution tools, dashboards, and conversion events. His claim: this compounds confusion (illustrated by a scenario where Triple Whale, an in-platform incrementality test, and a causal-inference test all disagree about Pinterest in the same meeting). The prescribed fix is a small, prioritized hierarchy of metrics, not more tools. [2d775e75662d] [5de99742a3a0]

### 2.3 Attribution windows and platform-native incrementality reads
10. **Know each platform's attribution window before comparing anything.** Stated windows: Google typically 30-day click plus 7-day view, 7-day engaged-view and 1-day view; Meta by default 7-day click / 1-day view (recently also 1-day engaged); TikTok "around about the same" but adjustable; Pinterest similar; Microsoft "a little bit different." No exact figures given for TikTok, Pinterest or Microsoft beyond "similar" — treat as directional only. `[web check: current default attribution windows for Google, TikTok, Pinterest, Microsoft Ads]`. [256e8f1a95e7]
11. **Meta's "7-day click" definition itself changed recently (dated by the presenter to March or April 2026).** Before the change, a "click" could be any engagement click (like, comment, share, click to page), not necessarily an outbound click to the site. After the change, "7-day click" requires an outbound click; the previously-included engagement clicks were moved into a separate bucket, "1-day engaged." This is why 7-day-click ROAS numbers reportedly "got a little bit worse overnight" after the change — a reporting correction, not a real performance drop. `[web check: verify current Meta click-attribution definition]`. [d233cdfd3b00]
12. **Meta's "incremental attribution" column is not a live per-account experiment.** Conceptually it looks like a holdout test (e.g. 2% of exposed users buy vs 1% of non-exposed, so incremental rate = 1%, turning a reported 5x into a 2.5x incremental ROAS). But the presenter states Meta does not run real-time holdouts in your account for this figure — it applies a factor derived from conversion-lift experiments run across your industry/niche by other advertisers. Treat the column as directional guidance on which campaigns look more additive, not as an accurate lift result for your account. `[web check: current Meta documentation on how "incremental attribution" is computed]`. [a14d468f90cf]
13. **First-click attribution is presented as effectively unknowable; last-click is not.** Last-click (in-platform) is accurate: you know when someone clicked and bought. First-click, per hundreds of hours the presenter says he spent in third-party attribution tools, produces results he calls absurd (e.g., "first click" reported as brand search for a customer whose actual first exposure was a friend wearing the product). The practical risk: trusting a first-click tool can push budget into brand search when the real driver was upper-funnel awareness or word of mouth. His recommendation: max out top-of-funnel spend for initial awareness, and treat third-party multi-touch attribution tools as adding confusion rather than truth. [5de99742a3a0]

### 2.4 The metric hierarchy and the finance-grade layer
14. **A 3-tier reliability pyramid.** Bottom: attributed platform numbers (ROAS, CPA, multi-touch attribution) — fast, but least reliable. Middle: finance-grade metrics (profit contribution, MER, AMER) — slower, more reliable, tied to actual commercial outcomes. Top: causal lift studies (control vs treatment groups) — slowest and most resource-intensive, most reliable. Rule of use: match decision size to tier — quick day-to-day directional calls can use platform metrics if you understand their attribution flaws; large budget, channel or strategy decisions should use finance-grade metrics or lift studies. [2d775e75662d]
15. **MER vs AMER, and why the CMO should be KPI'd on AMER, not MER.** MER (Marketing Efficiency Ratio) = net revenue ÷ ad spend (or the inverse as a percentage). Always use net revenue (post-returns), especially in categories with delayed returns processing (the presenter names fashion). AMER (Acquisition MER) = new-customer net revenue ÷ ad spend. Because most ad spend is meant to drive new customers rather than repeat purchase, MER can rise purely from a returning-customer bump while new-customer efficiency deteriorates — worked example: MER goes 4x (Jan) → 5x (Feb), prompting a March spend increase, while AMER over the same months quietly goes 2.0x → 1.8x → 1.6x. Scaling on the MER signal in that case erodes new-customer acquisition further. MER is presented as a valid CFO control metric (percentage of P&L to marketing, alongside gross margin and opex, to hit a profit target) but a bad metric to hold a CMO accountable to. [3c73946c2bb4]
16. **AMER targets are not comparable across businesses.** A 60%-gross-margin business can sustain a materially worse AMER than a 40%-margin business and still be equally profitable; strong retention (e.g. "50% lift by the 6-month mark") also supports a more aggressive, lower-first-purchase-margin acquisition posture. No numeric AMER target is given — judge it against your own margin and retention profile. `[needs brand data: gross margin % and repeat/retention curve]`. [3c73946c2bb4]
17. **Track new-customer revenue and new-customer orders as separate, independent lines.** Most businesses don't track new-customer revenue on its own, per the presenter. Tracking orders alongside revenue catches cases where revenue and volume diverge due to AOV swings. [6f250e030e8c]
18. **CAC formula and its relationship to AMER.** CAC = total ad spend ÷ new customers acquired in the period. AMER is presented as the better profitability read because it encapsulates AOV, which CAC does not; CAC is still useful as a leading indicator — when it moves, investigate what changed in the product/offer mix. [6f250e030e8c]
19. **Profit contribution and new-customer profit contribution, with exact formulas.** Profit contribution = revenue − cost of delivery − marketing expense (can also be expressed as a %). New-customer profit contribution = new-customer revenue − its cost of delivery − its marketing cost. This is presented as telling you directly whether acquisition itself is profitable. [6f250e030e8c] [2b34d63c3b0e]
20. **Recommended executive dashboard composition.** Headline: profit contribution (or new-customer profit contribution). Beside it: AMER and new-customer revenue/orders. Below: CAC and profit contribution detail. Stated rationale: this gives "a super high degree of accuracy because they are based on the actual financial metrics in the business," and is presented as sufficient to judge whether last week's decisions were good and whether to course correct. [6f250e030e8c] [2b34d63c3b0e]
21. **Finance-grade metrics degrade at high complexity.** Once a business spends heavily on channels that don't tie to a platform's own attributed revenue (worked example: $1M/month on influencers), a channel's reported ROAS can't be credited with the full new-customer revenue lift, because other spend is also driving it. This is presented as the point at which the business needs to move up the pyramid to lift studies. At smaller scale/fewer channels, the assumption "all spend drives all revenue" is treated as acceptable and simplifying. [6f250e030e8c]
22. **LTGP:CAC as the one metric comparable across time and across businesses.** Long-term gross profit over a chosen window, divided by CAC. Presented as superior to a single number because a ratio's two sides contextualize each other (CAC alone, or LTGP alone, has no context). Window choice: 30/90/180-day windows are fine for comparing yourself over time; extend the window for cross-business or cross-industry comparison because retention profiles differ (a CPG brand may have low first-purchase GP:CAC but high repeat rate versus fashion). [777732c1773f]

### 2.5 Data plumbing that finance-grade metrics depend on
23. **COGS must be tagged per product in Shopify, or every downstream financial metric is wrong during discount periods.** Worked numeric example: a $100 product with $50 cost of delivery has 50% gross margin. At 20% off, price falls to $80, cost of delivery is unchanged at $50, so gross profit compresses to $30 and gross margin to 30/80 = 37.5% — a 12.5-percentage-point drop, roughly a 30% relative decline. If the business instead assumes a flat 50% margin (a common practice, per the presenter, of telling an agency "our average margin is 50%"), profit contribution, LTGP:CAC and AMER all become wrong during any discount event, not just Black Friday. [b1834ecfb2a1]
24. **CAPI (Conversions API) is treated as necessary post-iOS-14 infrastructure, not optional.** Mechanism: the purchase event fires from the merchant's own server (not the browser pixel) and is relayed to Meta, carrying hashed first-party identifiers (email, phone, customer ID). Stated benefits: removes reliance on unreliable browser-side tracking (iOS restrictions, ad blockers, privacy rules), improves match rates, and enables attributing offline (in-store) purchases back to an ad if an email/phone is captured at point of sale. [2c720c61a375] [256e8f1a95e7]
25. **Shopify's own marketing/attribution reports are called unreliable and not recommended for decisions.** The presenter says he has "rarely seen helpful data" in Shopify's built-in attribution/marketing reporting, framing it as suffering the same fundamental attribution problems as everywhere else. Recommendation: build decisions on financial-KPI dashboards and platform data instead, and treat Shopify (and GA4) as secondary/redundancy layers. [7a6b5c93b855]
26. **GA4 is positioned as a fallback layer, not a daily decision tool.** Recommendation: keep GA4 configured correctly (firing, campaigns segmented) purely as a redundancy in case platform tracking breaks, but "there's nothing in GA4 you can't get out of Shopify or the platforms," so it should not be an opened, decision-driving dashboard day to day. [38c80ae68029]

### 3.1 Finance and unit economics
1. Pull net revenue (post-returns), not gross/total revenue, for every ratio calculation; this matters most in high-return categories such as fashion. [3c73946c2bb4]
2. Compute MER = net revenue ÷ ad spend for CFO-level P&L allocation control. [3c73946c2bb4]
3. Compute AMER = new-customer net revenue ÷ ad spend, and use this (not MER) as the CMO/paid-media accountability metric. [3c73946c2bb4]
4. Track new-customer revenue and new-customer orders as 2 separate lines to separate volume change from efficiency change. [6f250e030e8c]
5. Compute CAC = total ad spend ÷ new customers acquired in the period, as a leading indicator alongside AMER. [6f250e030e8c]
6. Compute profit contribution = revenue − cost of delivery − marketing expense; then new-customer profit contribution = new-customer revenue − its cost of delivery − its marketing cost. [6f250e030e8c] [2b34d63c3b0e]
7. Build the executive dashboard in this order: profit contribution (or new-customer profit contribution) as headline; AMER + new-customer revenue/orders next; CAC and profit contribution detail below. [6f250e030e8c]
8. Tag cost of goods per SKU in Shopify so gross margin is visible in real time and flows into any external reporting or spreadsheet. [b1834ecfb2a1]
9. Before/during any storewide discount, re-run margin-based reports using the discount-adjusted per-unit price, not the pre-discount assumed margin, to check the promotion still clears its contribution target. [b1834ecfb2a1]
10. Compute LTGP:CAC over a chosen window (30/90/180-day for internal comparison; longer for cross-business or cross-industry comparison) and read it against the bands in section 4. [777732c1773f]
11. Reconcile platform-reported numbers against the P&L before scaling: if platform and P&L disagree, investigate for view-through inflation, existing-customer bleed-over or a halo event before adding another attribution tool. [2b34d63c3b0e]

### 3.4 Paid media (attribution, campaign structure, experiment design)
1. Check whether campaigns still report on Meta's default 7-day click / 1-day view (/ 1-day engaged); if so, expect view-through inflation. [2dbf871d15aa] [d233cdfd3b00]
2. To remove view-through noise without touching delivery: in Meta Ads Manager, go to Columns → Compare Attribution Settings → select 7-day click reporting. `[verify UI]` [d233cdfd3b00]
3. To change delivery-level attribution: move campaigns to 7-day click / 1-day engaged (drop 1-day view). Expect reported ROAS to drop as a correction, not a real performance decline. `[verify UI]` [d233cdfd3b00]
4. To read Meta's incremental attribution: Columns → Compare Attribution Settings → Incremental Attribution, broken out by campaign; treat the output as directional (industry-benchmark-derived), not as a live per-account holdout result. `[verify UI]` [a14d468f90cf]
5. Audit for existing-customer bleed-over: confirm customer lists are actually applied and excluded from every "cold"/acquisition campaign; exclude existing customers from top-of-funnel campaigns specifically (retention campaigns intentionally target them, so this exclusion is scoped to acquisition objectives only). [d63cd6fefbdc] [b2092d04659e]
6. Audit for brand-term waste: separate brand and non-brand spend/reporting; do not let brand-term conversions inflate a blended ROAS used to justify further spend; minimize brand search spend where it is not genuinely incremental. [606c03936b6f]
7. Audit account structure for over-segmentation: check whether the number of active campaigns is disproportionate to the business's spend level or complexity; consolidate where fragmentation is making the data unreadable. [e7a390a10174]
8. Treat any very high ROAS from a low-spend tertiary channel (Snapchat, Pinterest, Reddit, etc.) with skepticism: at low spend, delivery algorithms default to already-warm, pixel-matched audiences rather than net-new reach, inflating the read. Validate with a lift/incrementality read before crediting or scaling. [94dc95d044bc]
9. Before running (or continuing) a Meta traffic/reach objective campaign, define the measurement method (a lift test or a causal-impact retrospective) up front; do not run spend you cannot validate, and do not infer impact from a before/after correlation around a seasonal peak. [703558b83184] [a7b82c68d543]
10. When reviewing DPA (dynamic product ad) performance, apply the same skepticism as retargeting/brand: a high DPA ROAS reflects bottom-funnel demand harvesting, not necessarily incremental growth; validate before increasing budget. [79b6665ba35d]
11. When evaluating a high-ROAS Google/PMax campaign (even with brand excluded), stress-test it: consider testing an incremental spend increment and checking whether topline revenue actually rises by a comparable multiple before crediting the reported ROAS. [71083548afed]
12. To run a geo-lift experiment: (a) pull historical daily ad spend, new-customer revenue and new-customer orders by state/region; (b) cluster regions that have moved together historically (the source's illustration: Arizona + Texas as one cluster, New York + California as another) — a single-state match is not required, combined clusters work and reduce single-region risk; (c) validate the model predicts one cluster from the other with high accuracy (source cites roughly 99% as an illustrative bar) — this predicted line is your counterfactual; (d) apply the intervention (e.g., 2x or 3x budget on a Meta cold campaign, or a new-channel launch) to treatment regions only, leaving control regions untouched; (e) after the test period, compare actual treatment revenue to the counterfactual prediction — the gap is incremental lift; (f) divide incremental lift by incremental spend to get incremental ROAS (worked example: $100,000 lift ÷ $40,000 extra spend = 2.5x incremental ROAS); (g) you can layer other revenue streams (e.g., retail revenue in the same regions) into the same design; (h) re-run with different region pairs for subsequent tests. [926a75d6e756]
13. In Australia specifically, the source says state-level geo-lift performs poorly (needs very large spend, and re-running soon after muddies the clustering data); use commuting-zone-level regions instead. `[web check: current geo-lift feasibility and recommended region granularity for Australia]` [926a75d6e756]
14. To run an in-platform lift test instead of geo-lift: rely on the platform (Meta, Google, TikTok) to split users at the individual level into exposed/holdout buckets and report the purchase-rate difference. Note stated minimum-spend gating: TikTok (and similarly Google) reportedly require roughly $200k of spend before the test can run; Meta is described as more freely available and re-runnable. `[web check: current minimum-spend thresholds for platform lift tests on TikTok, Google and Meta]` [d912bfa27ecc] [96e6e856fd7b]
15. To retrospectively evaluate a channel you already launched without a geo-split (e.g., you added TikTok or YouTube last quarter): identify plausible confounding variables for daily revenue (ad spend, keyword seasonality, influencer spend, inflation data, etc.), then feed the intervention point plus those variables into Google's open-source CausalImpact package to generate a predicted counterfactual revenue line; compare it to actual revenue over the period to estimate directional incremental impact (worked example: ~$10k additional revenue against $10k spend ≈ 1x ROI, i.e., much weaker than assumed). [c049a4fab120]
16. Before crediting a new channel launched into a seasonal peak (the source's example: October launch, strong November) with the ensuing lift, demand a causal design (lift test, geo-lift, or CausalImpact) rather than accepting "revenue went up after we launched" as evidence. [33775b73749d]
17. Set up CAPI on Meta: route purchase events server-side (from your own server, hashed first-party identifiers) instead of relying solely on the browser pixel, to improve match rate and to make offline (in-store) purchases attributable when email/phone is captured at sale. [2c720c61a375]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Symptom check — "on the treadmill":** ROAS improving but profit/revenue flat and the team feels it is "spinning wheels." What good looks like: profit contribution and new-customer profit contribution moving with reported efficiency. What bad looks like: the divergence itself. Next step: reconcile platform numbers to the P&L (3.1, item 11) before touching creative or account structure. [4f1e89e8a798] [2b34d63c3b0e]
- **Check 1-day view is off or reported separately.** How: Ads Manager → Columns → Compare Attribution Settings; look at 7-day click alone vs blended. Bad: a material gap between blended and 7-day-click-only ROAS. Fix: report and eventually deliver on 7-day click / 1-day engaged. `[verify UI]` [2dbf871d15aa] [d233cdfd3b00]
- **Check existing-customer exclusion on every acquisition/cold campaign.** How: audit the audience/exclusion settings of each "cold" campaign against the current customer list. Bad: customer-list exclusion missing or stale. Fix: apply and refresh exclusions; this check is scoped to acquisition objectives only, not retention campaigns. [d63cd6fefbdc] [b2092d04659e]
- **Check for halo-effect timing coincidences.** How: cross-reference regional ROAS spikes against any off-platform activation calendar (pop-ups, retail openings, PR/events) in the same window/region. Bad: a spike that lines up with an untracked offline event. Fix: flag it in reporting rather than acting on it as a channel win. [d63cd6fefbdc]
- **Check brand-term spend and its share of blended ROAS.** How: isolate brand vs non-brand campaigns/spend. Bad: brand campaigns kept in purely to prop up blended ROAS. Fix: separate reporting, minimize brand spend where not incremental. [606c03936b6f]
- **Check account segmentation against spend/complexity.** How: count active campaigns vs total spend and SKU/audience complexity. Bad: campaign count disproportionate to spend, fragmenting data. Fix: consolidate. [e7a390a10174]
- **Check tertiary-channel (Snapchat, Pinterest, Reddit, etc.) ROAS against spend level.** How: compare reported ROAS to spend share; very high ROAS at very low spend is a red flag for warm-audience-only delivery. Fix: run an incrementality read before crediting or scaling. [94dc95d044bc]
- **Check whether any traffic/reach or DPA campaign has a stated measurement method.** How: ask "how would we know if this worked?" for each such campaign. Bad: no test design, only before/after correlation, especially around a seasonal peak. Fix: define a lift test or CausalImpact analysis before continuing to fund it. [703558b83184] [79b6665ba35d] [33775b73749d] [a7b82c68d543]
- **Check whether COGS is tagged per SKU in Shopify.** How: audit product-level cost fields. Bad: missing, or a single flat "average margin" percentage used everywhere. Fix: tag per-SKU COGS; recompute margin-dependent metrics for any discount period. [b1834ecfb2a1]

## 7. Examples and case results

All numeric examples in this source are explicitly hypothetical/illustrative ("let's say", "let's use... as an example"), not disclosed real client results. None should be read as a proven outcome.
- Feedback-loop example: an ad reads 4x ROAS, prompts more spend and more similar creative; ROAS drifts to 3x at higher spend, yet the team reinforces the same decision — illustrating how an initially-wrong number compounds bad decisions. [4f1e89e8a798]
- Cross-platform halo example: Meta mid-funnel campaign jumps from 4x to 12x purely because of retargeted TikTok traffic running at ~25% of Meta budget. [7c355072e26e]
- Margin-parity example: 2 campaigns both show $10,000 attributed revenue at the same ROAS, but one is a discounted campaign eroding margin and one is a full-price retention campaign — profit contribution reveals the "true" priority differently (3x vs 4x in the illustration). [7c355072e26e]
- MER-vs-AMER example: MER 4x → 5x (Jan → Feb) while AMER 2.0x → 1.8x → 1.6x over the same period — used to argue why scaling on MER alone is dangerous. [3c73946c2bb4]
- COGS/discount example: $100 product, $50 cost of delivery, 50% GM pre-discount; at 20% off, GM compresses to 37.5% (a ~30% relative decline). [b1834ecfb2a1]
- Geo-lift example: Arizona + Texas clustered against New York + California; a hypothetical 2x Meta cold-campaign budget produces a $100,000 lift against $40,000 in incremental spend (2.5x incremental ROAS). [926a75d6e756]
- CausalImpact example: a past YouTube launch retrospectively shows ~$10,000 incremental revenue against $10,000 spend, i.e. roughly 1x ROI, far weaker than the launch had been assumed to be. [c049a4fab120]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Leaving 1-day view attribution enabled across campaigns and using it in headline reporting. [2dbf871d15aa]
2. Trusting Google/PMax ROAS at face value without accounting for the halo effect it absorbs from other channels. [71083548afed]
3. Leaving brand search spend uncontrolled because it props up blended ROAS. [606c03936b6f]
4. Over-segmenting campaign structure relative to spend/complexity, fragmenting the data needed to decide anything. [e7a390a10174]
5. Crediting small, low-spend tertiary channels (Snapchat, Pinterest, Reddit) for growth based on inflated ROAS driven by warm-audience-only delivery. [94dc95d044bc]
6. Letting existing-customer targeting leak into acquisition/cold campaigns and inflate every downstream number. [b2092d04659e]
7. Running traffic/reach objective campaigns on Meta with no measurement plan and inferring value from correlation. [703558b83184]
8. Scaling DPA (dynamic product ad) spend purely because ROAS looks attractive, mistaking bottom-funnel demand harvesting for incrementality. [79b6665ba35d]
9. Having no testing/experimental-design structure at all, so nothing run can be validated as working. [a7b82c68d543]
10. Adding more attribution tools/dashboards/conversion events to "fix" confusion instead of subtracting down to a small metric hierarchy. [2d775e75662d]
11. Crediting a new channel (TikTok, YouTube, etc.) for a strong period when it was launched into a seasonal peak with no causal test in place. [33775b73749d]
12. Using a flat assumed gross-margin percentage instead of tagged per-SKU COGS, especially heading into a discount period. [b1834ecfb2a1]
13. Relying on Shopify's native marketing/attribution reports for decisions. [7a6b5c93b855]
14. Treating GA4 as a primary, daily decision dashboard rather than a fallback layer. [38c80ae68029]
15. Trusting Meta's "incremental attribution" column as if it were a real, live per-account lift result. [a14d468f90cf]
16. Trusting third-party multi-touch attribution tools' first-click reads to redirect budget (e.g., into brand search). [5de99742a3a0]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Title vs body figure conflict:** the source's own title says data integrity is the bottleneck in "80%" of audits; the spoken transcript says "about 30%." Neither is backed by a shown data set. This mini playbook and the folder merge should carry "30%" as what the presenter states on camera, and flag "80%" as an unverified headline number `[web check]`.
- **Commercial interest throughout:** the source is agency marketing content. It ends with a hiring pitch and a free-audit offer gated at $5M+/year revenue, and repeatedly frames the agency's own practice ("our own internal software," "every single Blue Sense audit," "that's what we do for clients") as the standard other brands should meet. Treat framing of the agency's own competence as promotional, even where the underlying mechanics (attribution windows, formulas) are independently checkable.
- **No named clients, no published data, no sample sizes.** Every quantitative claim beyond platform-mechanic descriptions (attribution windows, CAPI mechanism) is either a stated personal impression ("1,000+ audits," "30 to 50% of ROAS") or an explicitly hypothetical worked example. None should be treated as a benchmark to hit.
- **Platform-mechanics claims are time-pinned by the presenter himself and should be re-verified:** the 7-day-click redefinition (dated ~Mar/Apr 2026), Meta's incremental-attribution rollout (~6 months before a stated May 2026 recording date), and the ~$200k TikTok/Google lift-test minimum spend are all candidates for `[web check]` before being acted on, since the presenter explicitly ties them to a moment in time that may have already moved.
- **Geo-lift and CausalImpact methodology is described at a conceptual level, not an implementation level.** No statistical significance thresholds, no minimum test duration, no software/tool name for running the clustering or the counterfactual model beyond "our own internal software" (undisclosed) and the named open-source CausalImpact package. An operator would need additional sources or in-house data science to actually execute a geo-lift test from this transcript alone.
- **Numeric thresholds (LTGP:CAC bands, revenue-band action tiers) are explicitly the presenter's own heuristics ("this is bad," "this is good, scale here"), not derived from a shown formula or study.** Some are self-contradictory in spirit if applied rigidly: a 3x+ LTGP:CAC is called "bad" (leaving money on the table) in the same breath the presenter says a cash-flow-constrained business is right not to scale it — so the "3x is bad" rule has a stated exception that swallows a common real-world case.
- **This source does not cover offers, creative production, landing pages, or email/retention tactics.** It touches retention and margin only as inputs to judging AMER and LTGP:CAC targets. Do not backfill those domains from this file; route to 03-OFF, 10-MCR, 07-LPG, 14-EML and 17-CXP respectively.
- **Whether a difference between 2 variants is "real" (CRO significance) is explicitly out of scope**, per the source's own header note pointing that topic to 09-CRO; this file does not attempt it.
- **`[needs brand data]` items:** gross margin % and repeat/retention curve to judge an AMER target (principle 16); accurate per-SKU cost-of-delivery data to make COGS tagging valid (principle 23); a business's own historical ad-spend/new-customer-revenue/new-customer-orders series by region, to run geo-lift clustering (procedure 3.4.12).
