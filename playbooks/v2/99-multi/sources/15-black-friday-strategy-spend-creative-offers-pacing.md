## 1. What this source is for

Gives an agency's operating playbook for planning and running paid media through a Black Friday/Cyber Monday period: how to read attribution correctly when pre-sale spend is a "lagged signal," how much of the Q4 budget to front-load into September and October, how to size and phase creative production, how to structure the offer (profit play versus inventory play, plus a fallback offer ladder), and how to pace spend hour by hour and day by day using marginal returns instead of blended averages. It is Meta-and-Google-centric and paid-acquisition-centric; it does not cover email/SMS, on-site landing-page build, or inventory/ops execution in any depth.

## 2. Principles

1. Pre-sale ad spend is a lagging indicator, not a weak signal. A sale collapses the entire top-of-funnel audience (everyone who knows the brand but has not bought) into one revenue spike, which both explains the post-sale slump and means the funnel must be built up before the sale opens. Condition: applies to any brand running a discrete, dated sale event, not just Black Friday. Evidence: presenter's own framing plus year-round and November attribution-gap data. [d243f038601b], [a0478e430dad]
2. Underspending before the sale shrinks the funnel exactly when you need it biggest, because pre-sale conversion rates dip on their own as shoppers wait to see if a sale is coming. Do not read that dip as a signal to cut spend. Condition: assumes the brand runs a known, anticipated sale (repeat customers or market awareness of the calendar). [d243f038601b]
3. Build a post-treatment measurement window into any pre-season incrementality or holdout test; the incremental lift from pre-season spend keeps showing up after the test period ends, and around Black Friday this lift is far larger than in an ordinary period. Condition: requires the ability to run or read a geo-holdout or similar experiment; category matters (fashion shows far more delayed lift than CPG in the source's cited data). Evidence: House incrementality platform data, not the presenter's own experiment. [1d313c3eb80b]
4. Compare attribution windows (for example, 1-day click versus 30-day, or 7-day click versus 30-day) to correct for reporting lag, but do not treat the resulting gap as proof of causal lift; it only tells you how much revenue is being credited late, not whether the spend caused it. Condition: needs at least one full prior Black Friday cycle of your own account data to compute a reliable gap. [a0478e430dad], [c310f482ec53]
5. Scale to marginal contribution margin (CM3), not blended ROAS or average CAC. An account's average CAC can sit comfortably under its tolerance while the newest increment of spend is deeply unprofitable. Condition: requires the ability to isolate incremental spend and incremental customers/revenue for a given period; noisy holiday days can confound naive marginal reads. [a74069ce7855], [294df46a0b6e]
6. Use revenue/spend elasticity (log-log slope) only to justify cutting spend, never to justify scaling it, because the calculation is observational, not causal; you may simply be spending more on days you already expected to be strong. [675de05124cc]
7. November is a harvesting month for reach, not a buying month. CPMs rise and total addressable reach falls, so you cannot meaningfully acquire new top-of-funnel attention in-month; you can only convert the audience already built earlier. Condition: general finding across the presenter's portfolio; smaller brands with thinner top-of-funnel history will feel this harder. [9bbe6d4b46a1]
8. Audience saturation during Black Friday is usually offer or creative fatigue, not running out of people, because frequency barely moves. Diagnose fatigue before broadening audiences. Condition: portfolio data is from large-spend brands; small brands with narrow audiences can still hit genuine reach limits. [ce65e92b40c4]
9. Enter the period with two modeled budgets (base and stretch) and pre-agreed thresholds that authorize the team to move between them without a live approval call. The stated goal is never to spend the full budget; the budget is a ceiling to deploy against incremental returns, not a target. [71ba87612a01]
10. Treat Google Ads as a preparation platform, not a scaling platform, for Black Friday. The lift comes from feed, asset and promotion prep done well in advance, not from raising budgets mid-weekend; Google's own efficiency (CPA, ROAS, impression-to-purchase) tends to worsen during the peak even while blended reporting looks better, because branded search and Meta-driven traffic spill into Google's numbers. [16f43795044b]
11. Cost-cap campaigns, avoided by this agency year-round for the volatility and creative-volume burden they create, become useful during Black Friday specifically as a diagnostic tool to map the account's marginal frontier, not as the main scaling lever. [4afe3a4577de]
12. Build per-hour delayed-attribution multipliers before doing any intraday pacing, because spend commonly peaks 5 to 7am while revenue peaks 7 to 11am; an early-morning ROAS reading will look artificially weak unless corrected. Condition: only worth building at high enough spend and offer-rotation frequency to justify the operational load. [6f476966cae3]
13. Decide the sale's core objective (maximize contribution profit versus clear slow/Grade C inventory) before setting CAC tolerance or KPIs, because the two objectives pull in opposite directions and cannot both be optimized at once, though a tiered gift-with-purchase can partially serve both. [9b4e9226481]
14. Pre-build a full offer ladder (fallback promotions) with creative and landing pages ready before the sale if the brand is trade-reactive, because pivoting an offer across hundreds of live creatives in real time is very difficult and stressful. [e2262e74c26f], [d6b1b339b607]
15. Forecast required creative volume from peak daily ad spend divided by a realistic per-ad spend ceiling, not from a target number of assets; "12 to 18 ads" and "12 to 18 ads that can each hold $10,000/day" are very different requirements. [075f80613bc3], [b5d2b85f4f87]
16. Pareto's principle (top 20% of ads holding 80% of spend) does not hold during Black Friday in this portfolio; spend is spread much more broadly, so creative volume across the tail matters, not just a handful of winners. Bigger accounts are less concentrated at the top than smaller ones. [291583358578]
17. Use a diversified creative-format mix by planned share of spend (offer statics, offer-overlaid evergreen, creator/founder content, top-of-funnel product/VSL, early access) rather than chasing whichever format shows the highest raw ROAS, since high-ROAS formats like catalog retargeting are naturally bottom-of-funnel and will overstate their own value if over-indexed. [0fd306b87cfb]
18. Never turn off evergreen campaigns for Black Friday; duplicate the best performers (including fatigued ones) and badge them with the offer instead, because evergreen campaigns carry campaign-level conversion history that resets if paused. The source calls disabling evergreen "the single biggest mistake people make during Black Friday." [7c0dc6951b2c]
19. During Black Friday, the offer and product discount corrupt the normal creative feedback loop exactly when the most data is being generated, so ROAS alone cannot tell you whether a creative is good; use creative-level diagnostic metrics (CTR, CPC) instead, and judge at the ad-set level rather than the ad level because multi-touch optimization, last-click attribution and Meta's breakdown reporting effect make ad-level ROAS unreliable in this window specifically. Condition: the presenter is explicit this is a Black Friday-only exception; the rest of the year, CPC/CTR are described as having no meaningful correlation with ROAS in this portfolio. [88407c078150], [f9f39dcd376e]
20. Treat creative as a CPM lever, not only a conversion lever: in the presenter's data, higher creative diversity and volume qualitatively tracked with lower CPMs during a period when the overall auction was 72% more expensive, likely because more creative variety wins more auctions and accrues more impression share. [9f9579f3d2c0]
21. Phase creative production and rotation across four stages: pre-launch teaser/early-access, launch, mid-sale refresh, and final 24 to 48 hour scarcity/deadline messaging; lock offers before producing creative because launch and scarcity assets depend on knowing the offer. [d6a9aa0c6869]
22. Model November-cohort lifetime value before setting a November CAC tolerance; in a 10-brand sample, November cohorts repeated at 38.5% (6-month repeat lift) versus 63% for February cohorts, roughly half, but 2 of the 10 brands showed no difference at all, so this must be checked per brand, not assumed. [7473b6195d7f], [d661eb7e3ac0]
23. Required break-even ROAS rises as discount depth rises because gross profit compresses; this must be modeled against the brand's actual discount and margin numbers, not assumed from a generic multiplier. [61f3aa873861]
24. Write down scaling rules before the window opens: use a 3-day rolling marginal-contribution read as the default (with day-to-day used situationally based on total spend and pacing velocity); increase spend when the 3-day rolling CM3 is improving, hold when flat, decrease after 2 consecutive poor reads. Do not judge daily performance against a fixed 8am target during the peak window. [3b63d06d5486], [294df46a0b6e]
25. Build the pacing sheet from the prior year's actual hourly revenue and spend curves, with an attribution-multiplier correction baked in, and use it (not flat daily caps) as the primary budget yardstick from mid-November through the first days of December. [5afc0c5d082f]
26. Front-load roughly 31 to 43% of the September-through-December budget into September and October in this portfolio; treat this as an observed range to calibrate from, not a universal target. [62e69271e95c], [a464d35942c4]
27. The cheapest media of the entire cycle runs from around December 16 to 31 ("Q5" in the presenter's jargon), because early December still carries gifting-driven demand and expense; this window suits a deliberately prepared offer, not a leftover budget dump. [5eab80b6afef], [3561b527a1f8]
28. Do not carry the prior year's CPM levels into this year's forecast; the presenter expects Black Friday 2026 to run at higher CPMs into a weaker consumer than 2025, based on macro indicators (interest rates, inflation) and a slowdown in Meta's own ad-inventory growth. [6a2ae399039b]
29. Roll multiple offers through the window (an offer ladder) rather than one static promotion, to prevent offer fatigue and preserve the ability to react if trade underperforms; lock the ladder before creative production starts. [d6b1b339b607]
30. Treat attribution correction, cohort-LTV correction and margin correction as three separate, mandatory fixes to apply to raw Q4 numbers before building any forecast or scaling plan; skipping any one leaves the plan built on a distorted base. [ebb016b64886], [c310f482ec53]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Offers and discount design

**A. Choose the sale's objective before setting any KPI**
1. Decide whether the primary goal is maximizing contribution profit (profit play) or clearing slow-moving/Grade C inventory (cash/inventory play). Cash and margin set the ceiling on what can be discounted either way.
2. If running a pure profit play, authorize the team to spend to the marginal frontier where CM3 flattens, and hold the line if incremental returns go negative.
3. If running a pure inventory play, explicitly authorize the team to accept negative incremental contribution margin late in the weekend if it clears stock; otherwise the team will pull back on its own once returns turn negative.
4. If both goals matter, consider a tiered gift-with-purchase bundle: it moves slow inventory as the "free" item while the paid product stays undiscounted, though the source is explicit that this will underperform either pure strategy on its own dimension (a straight 80% off on the same slow inventory is a purer clearance play than bundling it as a gift).
5. Communicate the chosen objective to the media-buying team before the weekend starts; it changes both CAC tolerance and how performance gets judged in real time (for example, pushing spend late Sunday night despite negative marginal profit, if clearance is the goal). [9b4e9226481]

**B. Build the offer ladder**
1. Identify backup/fallback offers in advance: a gift-with-purchase, a referral incentive (the source uses the shorthand "TAF," likely "tell a friend"; `[web check: confirm this abbreviation's exact meaning and mechanic in current use]`), free shipping, a bundle, and the deepest available site-wide discount.
2. Build the landing page and ad creative for every backup offer before the sale, and preload the creative into the ad account even if it may never go live; treat this as insurance, not wasted production.
3. Set explicit trade-number triggers that decide when to switch to each fallback offer.
4. If a switch happens, redirect traffic to the new pre-built landing pages and turn on the pre-loaded creative rather than trying to edit live creative at scale under time pressure. [e2262e74c26f]
5. Map every offer in the ladder to the creative phase it belongs to (teaser/early access, launch, mid-sale refresh, final scarcity) so creative production is not caught without an offer to build against. [d6b1b339b607], [d6a9aa0c6869]

**C. Model break-even ROAS against the chosen discount**
1. Get the brand's actual gross margin and planned discount depth; without both, the agency states it cannot model this and will not guess. `[needs brand data: discount depth by SKU/tier, current gross margin]`
2. Recompute net selling price and net contribution after the discount for the actual product mix, not a single flat percentage assumption.
3. Recalculate break-even revenue ROAS from the new, compressed contribution rate; treat the source's own worked numbers (a normal break-even of 2x moving to 4.5x at a 40% discount) as an illustrative example, not a formula that transfers directly, since the mechanics described do not cleanly reproduce that jump from the stated inputs. [61f3aa873861]
4. Feed the resulting break-even ROAS into the CAC-tolerance and pacing-sheet targets before the window opens.

### 3.2 Creative

**A. Forecast required creative volume**
1. Pull last year's data on the single highest-spending ad's peak daily spend, and the realistic spend ceiling for a typical "winner" ad; the source's own AUD-account numbers were a $12,500/day high, $4,000 to $8,500/day for most evergreen ads, and roughly $8,000 to $13,000/day as a realistic working ceiling (simplified to about $5,000/day average, $10,000 to $12,000/day winner ceiling).
2. Divide your planned peak-day spend target by that per-ad ceiling to estimate how many ads must be able to carry real volume (the source's example: $150,000 peak day divided by $10,000 to $12,000 per ad implies needing 12 to 18 ads that can each hold that spend, not merely 12 to 18 ads that exist).
3. Add a volume buffer for the tail: in this portfolio, the bottom 80% of ads still held about 54% of total spend, so a handful of winners will not carry a large account alone.
4. If the resulting creative-volume requirement exceeds current production capacity, either lower the spend/revenue target or raise production and, if needed, ad-account/page capacity, before locking the plan. [075f80613bc3], [291583358578], [b5d2b85f4f87]

**B. Set the creative-format mix**
1. Plan spend share roughly as: offer statics about 19%, offer-overlaid evergreen catalog about 18%, creator/founder content at the largest available share (redistribute this share elsewhere if the brand has no founder/creator content), top-of-funnel product/VSL ads at a small share, and early access/VIP ads only if that mechanic is in use.
2. Do not over-index into whichever format shows the highest raw ROAS in isolation; catalog/evergreen retargeting will show very high ROAS because it sits at the bottom of the funnel with high frequency, which is a structural artifact, not proof it deserves more spend share. [0fd306b87cfb]

**C. Produce offer statics**
1. Keep the message to discount and end date; make it legible at thumbnail size as a hard test (shrink the asset and check if the offer is still readable).
2. Build 3 variants per offer by changing one of: the person/model in the creative, the product shown, or the offer itself; this alone can generate roughly 50 variants quickly.
3. Check that 9:16 feed placements do not crop off the offer text.
4. Put the offer and/or deadline in the first line of ad copy, since Stories and some feed placements truncate copy. [0fd306b87cfb], [088ef4385388]

**D. Produce offer-overlaid evergreen**
1. Take the best-performing evergreen ads, including previously fatigued winners, and duplicate them.
2. Badge the duplicates with the Black Friday offer; never disable the original evergreen versions.
3. Decide, per account, whether to also edit copy on the live evergreen ads to be Black Friday-specific; weigh the benefit (more bottom-of-funnel messaging) against the cost (resetting the ad's learning phase). The source treats this as a judgment call, not a rule. [7c0dc6951b2c]
4. In DPA/catalog ads, write actual sale messaging into the catalog copy; the source's own scrape of cold brands found 74% shipped catalog ads with no written messaging at all and only 3% included sale messaging, so this is an easy, low-competition win. [7c0dc6951b2c]

**E. Phase and schedule creative**
1. Build assets in 4 phases: pre-launch teaser/early-access, launch, mid-sale refresh, final 24 to 48 hour scarcity/deadline.
2. Lock the offer before producing launch or scarcity assets, since copy for those phases depends on knowing the exact offer.
3. During the peak window (roughly Nov 17 to Dec 2 in this source's calendar), refresh creative on the pre-planned schedule rather than only reacting after performance already drops. [d6a9aa0c6869]

**F. Judge creative performance correctly during the window**
1. Do not rank creative by raw ROAS during Black Friday; a deeply discounted best-seller will show a strong ROAS regardless of creative quality, and a genuinely strong ad on a full-price, slow-moving product will show a weak one.
2. Use creative-level diagnostic metrics (CTR, CPC, scroll-stop-type signals) to judge creative quality instead, and treat this as a Black Friday-specific exception, not a year-round practice.
3. Where possible, evaluate at the ad-set level rather than the individual ad level, since multi-touch optimization plus last-click attribution plus Meta's ad-breakdown reporting compound ad-level noise during this period. [88407c078150], [f9f39dcd376e]

**G. Pre-empt the Meta page limit**
1. If planning a large ramp in creative volume, set up multiple pages (cloned or otherwise) ahead of time so ad launches are not blocked by hitting a page limit. `[verify UI: current Meta page/ad-account limits and how to add pages]` [9f9579f3d2c0]

### 3.3 Paid media spend and pacing

**A. Correct the attribution read before judging pre-season spend**
1. In October, run a historical comparison of 1-day click versus 7-day (or 30-day) click attribution on the account's own prior Black Friday data.
2. Use the size of that gap to estimate how much last year's pre-season spend was actually underspent versus how it initially appeared.
3. Derive an attribution multiplier from that gap and apply it to real-time reads during the current pre-season and peak window, rather than trusting the raw platform number.
4. Build a post-treatment measurement window into this analysis so delayed conversions are captured before judging a period's true return. [c310f482ec53], [a0478e430dad]
5. During the sale period itself, move to a shorter attribution setting, 7-day click or 7-day click plus 1-day engaged, and compare multiple attribution settings rather than trusting any single one, since the source states there is no single source of truth. [a0478e430dad]

**B. Scale on marginal returns, using a written rule set**
1. Write the scaling rule down before the window opens.
2. Default to a 3-day rolling read of marginal contribution margin (CM3); use day-to-day reads situationally, based on total spend level and how fast the pacing sheet shows spend moving.
3. If the 3-day rolling marginal CM3 is improving, increase spend.
4. If it is flat, hold spend where it is.
5. If there are 2 consecutive poor reads, decrease spend.
6. Do not evaluate performance against a fixed daily target measured at 8am; the source is explicit that this specific habit misreads the day. [3b63d06d5486], [294df46a0b6e]

**C. Build and use the pacing sheet**
1. In (or before) early September, pull the prior year's actual hourly revenue and spend curves for the sale window.
2. Build a sheet mapping expected spend against expected revenue by time block, incorporating the attribution multiplier from procedure A, so the sheet shows whether current spend is ahead or behind the expected pattern, not just behind a flat daily number.
3. Depending on sales structure and spend volume, extend the sheet to cover late October, all of November, and sometimes December.
4. For very high-spend accounts running multiple rotating offers and large creative volume, build the sheet down to the intra-hour level; the source is explicit this is unnecessary overkill for many accounts and should not be assumed as a default.
5. For large retailers with high organic/non-paid revenue dependence, expect hourly pacing signals to reflect correlation with organic performance rather than a causal paid effect; treat those reads accordingly. [5afc0c5d082f]

**D. Run intraday pacing with delayed-attribution correction**
1. Expect spend to commonly peak between 5 and 7am while revenue peaks between 7 and 11am.
2. Do not compare an early-morning ROAS reading directly against the daily target; it will look artificially weak.
3. Build per-hour delayed-attribution multipliers (the source's example: a 5am reading of 1x ROAS should be read as roughly 2x once later-in-day revenue backfills it) before making push/pull decisions on early reads.
4. Pull conversion rate by hour as a supplementary, less-lagged real-time signal; the source calls this a multi-year "huge unlock" for reading true intraday performance. [6f476966cae3]

**E. Use cost caps as a diagnostic, not a default**
1. Recognize that this agency avoids cost-cap campaigns year-round due to account volatility and the creative-volume load they demand.
2. During Black Friday specifically, run a limited set of cost-cap campaigns alongside standard maximize-volume campaigns.
3. Read what the cost-cap campaigns spend up to; use that ceiling as a read on the account's marginal frontier and elasticity.
4. Use that read to decide how hard to push the maximize-volume campaigns; do not use cost caps as the account's primary scaling mechanism. [4afe3a4577de]

**F. Prepare, but do not scale, Google Ads**
1. Update titles, descriptions and assets across Search and PMax to be contextualized to the sale.
2. Roll over Merchant Center feeds and add promotional pricing lines so discounted prices flow correctly.
3. Set up promotion feeds and add promotional assets/titles to Search and PMax campaigns.
4. Do all of this well in advance of the peak window; the source frames this preparation, not budget increases, as the main lever for Google performance.
5. During the peak weekend, resist the urge to raise Google budget just because ROAS looks strong; check whether that strength is genuine Google efficiency or simply captured demand (branded search, Meta-driven traffic) by comparing against incrementality signals where available. `[verify UI: current Merchant Center and PMax promotion-feed setup steps]` [16f43795044b]

**G. Model base and stretch scenarios**
1. Build two spend/efficiency models: a base case and a stretch case, each with explicit numeric gates (for example, a CM3 floor, a marginal CAC ceiling, or a ROAS band).
2. Agree in advance on the exact conditions that authorize the team to move from base to stretch, and separately the conditions that trigger a pullback, so neither decision needs a live approval call over the weekend.
3. Communicate the stretch target to the working team as a goal to aim for, not only a contingency.
4. Update the pacing sheet daily, or intraday at very high spend levels, against these thresholds. [71ba87612a01]

### 3.6 Inventory and ops

**A. Decide what can be discounted**
1. Set the discount ceiling from actual product margin; you cannot discount past what margin allows.
2. Identify Grade C or otherwise slow-moving inventory as the natural candidate for the deepest discounts or as the free item in a gift-with-purchase, since Black Friday is a stated opportunity to clear stock that has not moved. [9b4e9226481]

**B. Prevent a mid-sale ad-account block**
1. If planning a large creative-volume ramp, set up additional or cloned Meta pages ahead of time so the account does not hit a page limit mid-campaign. `[verify UI]` [9f9579f3d2c0]

### 3.7 Measurement (attribution, CAC, LTV)

**A. Correct the three reporting distortions before forecasting**
1. Correct the attribution basis: derive and apply an attribution multiplier from the prior year's short-window versus long-window click comparison (see 3.3.A), so early spend is not misread as weak and the peak weekend is not misread as artificially strong.
2. Correct the cohort-value basis: pull 6-month repeat-rate data for a prior November cohort against a normal-month cohort (the source uses February) for the last 2 available years; in a 10-brand sample the November figure averaged 38.5% versus 63% for February, but 2 of the 10 brands showed no gap at all, so check the brand's own data rather than assuming the gap applies. `[needs brand data: 6-month repeat-rate cohort data by acquisition month, at least 2 prior years]`
3. Correct the margin basis: model discount depth against actual gross margin to compute a true, discount-adjusted break-even ROAS (see 3.1.C). `[needs brand data: gross margin by SKU or category, planned discount depth]`
4. Only after all three corrections, model required creative volume from the corrected spend target and check it against the pacing sheet and production capacity. [ebb016b64886], [d661eb7e3ac0]

**B. Compute marginal versus average CAC**
1. Take a period's baseline spend and CAC, and the incremental spend and incremental new customers added by a specific spend increase.
2. Compute marginal CAC as incremental spend divided by incremental new customers; compare this against the CAC tolerance, not the blended average CAC for the whole account, since the blended number can look acceptable while the marginal number is far above tolerance. [a74069ce7855]

**C. Compute revenue/spend elasticity, for cutting only**
1. Compute log(revenue on day 2 / revenue on day 1) divided by log(spend on day 2 / spend on day 1).
2. Only compute this when spend actually changed between the two periods being compared; treat near-zero denominators as unstable.
3. Use a low or negative reading as a hypothesis to test a spend cut, never as justification to scale up, since the measure is observational (you may simply be spending more on days already expected to perform well) and can lead to severe overspending if used to push. [675de05124cc]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Is pre-sale ROAS actually weak, or is it lagged?** Compare a short attribution window (1-day or 7-day click) to a long one (30-day) on the same period once it has matured. A large gap (source's own account: ~40% normally, ~80% in November) signals the short-window read is understating true performance; do not cut spend on the short-window number alone. `[needs brand data: at least 1 prior year of both attribution windows]` [a0478e430dad]
- **Is the account actually saturated, or just offer/creative-fatigued?** Check frequency against your own historical baseline. If frequency has barely moved (source's own range: 1.75 to 1.96), the response drop is more likely offer or creative fatigue than a reach ceiling; rotate creative and offers before broadening audiences. Exception: if pre-sale spend was cut and the funnel was never built up, this can be a genuine reach shortfall. [ce65e92b40c4]
- **Is spend actually still profitable at the margin, or just on average?** Compute marginal CAC (incremental spend / incremental new customers) for the latest spend increase and compare it to CAC tolerance, not the account's blended average CAC. A comfortable average with a bad marginal number means the next dollar is unprofitable even though the dashboard looks fine. [a74069ce7855]
- **Should this spend cut be trusted?** Compute the log-log elasticity of revenue over spend for the period in question. Use a low or negative reading only to support a cut decision; never use a high reading to justify scaling, since the number cannot separate causation from days that were simply going to perform well anyway. [675de05124cc]
- **Is Google's apparent efficiency real?** Check whether Google ROAS improvement during the peak weekend lines up with an actual increase in Google-native discovery traffic, or whether it is mostly branded search and Meta-driven spillover. Where available, use incrementality/holdout data to separate the two; do not scale Google budget on ROAS alone during the peak. [16f43795044b]
- **Is the account creative-constrained?** Divide the target peak-day spend by the realistic per-ad spend ceiling (own historical data, or the source's ranges as a starting point) to get the number of ads that must be able to hold real volume. If current live/ready creative count for that ceiling is short, the constraint is creative, not budget. [075f80613bc3], [b5d2b85f4f87]
- **Is a Black Friday "winning ad" actually good creative?** Check whether the win correlates with a deep discount on a best-seller, or with the creative's own diagnostic metrics (CTR, CPC) independent of the offer. If the win tracks the offer, do not carry the "learning" about the creative itself into next year without re-testing it off-discount. [88407c078150]
- **Is the November cohort actually worse?** Pull 6-month repeat-purchase rate for a November-acquired cohort versus a normal-month cohort (source uses February) across at least 2 prior years. If there is no material gap, keep the existing CAC tolerance; if there is, tighten it and reflect the lower expected LTV in the CAC math. `[needs brand data: cohort repeat-rate history]` [7473b6195d7f]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Cutting spend in the run-up to Black Friday because early ROAS looks weak, when the weakness is a lagged attribution artifact, not real underperformance. [a0478e430dad], [d243f038601b]
- Scaling spend based on blended average CAC/ROAS instead of marginal returns, which hides the fact that the newest spend increment can already be unprofitable. [a74069ce7855], [294df46a0b6e]
- Using observational spend/revenue elasticity to justify scaling up spend; the source says to use it only to justify cutting. [675de05124cc]
- Blindly raising Google Ads budget during the weekend because Google's ROAS looks strong, when that strength is often captured demand from Meta and branded search rather than genuine Google-driven incrementality. [16f43795044b]
- Turning off evergreen campaigns to make room for Black Friday creative; the source calls this the single biggest mistake people make during the period, since it resets the campaign's conversion-history-based learning. [7c0dc6951b2c]
- Judging Black Friday creative purely on ROAS, when the discount and product mix, not the creative, are driving most of the variance in that number during this period. [88407c078150]
- Reading intraday performance against a fixed morning (8am) target without correcting for the fact that spend and revenue peak at different hours. [3b63d06d5486], [6f476966cae3]
- Treating "12 to 18 ads" as sufficient creative volume without checking whether those specific ads can each hold the required per-ad spend. [075f80613bc3]
- Assuming a small set of top-performing ads (a Pareto-style 20/80 split) will carry a Black Friday account, when this portfolio's data shows spend spread much more broadly than that. [291583358578]
- Ramping ad-account creative volume without checking Meta page-limit capacity in advance. [9f9579f3d2c0]
- Treating the prior year's CPM and consumer-sentiment levels as this year's baseline without checking macro and platform-inventory signals first. [6a2ae399039b]
- Ignoring discount compression and cohort-quality differences when setting the November CAC allowance, described as making the default CAC allowance "wrong twice." [d661eb7e3ac0], [7473b6195d7f]
- Committing to a single fixed offer for the whole window instead of a pre-built offer ladder, which leaves no fast way to react if trade underperforms. [d6b1b339b607], [e2262e74c26f]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Conflict of interest**: the presenter's agency (Blue Sense) sells the exact media-buying, pacing-sheet and creative-strategy services being described as "what we will do for you." Every claim of "this works" doubles as a reason to retain the agency; treat efficacy claims with that lens, though the mechanics described (marginal-vs-average scaling, attribution-window comparison, cohort-LTV checks) are standard, verifiable analytical techniques independent of who applies them.
- **All performance figures are self-reported and unverified**: CPM percentages, frequency numbers, creative-concentration percentages, cohort-repeat figures and revenue multiples all come from the presenter's own client portfolio or from third-party reports (Triple Whale, House, Motion) cited secondhand, with no link to the original reports provided in the source text. None of this playbook's numeric thresholds should be treated as validated for any specific brand.
- **Reports disagree with each other on magnitude**: the source itself flags that Triple Whale's 7.8% Meta CPM inflation figure and Meta's own 6% price-per-ad / 18% impression-growth figures for 2025 are measured differently and are not directly comparable; the 50% Meta CPM increase cited from Triple Whale's weekend-specific report is a different measurement window again. Do not average these into a single number.
- **The break-even ROAS discount example does not check out as stated**: the source's own worked example (break-even moving from 2x to 4.5x at a 40% discount) does not follow cleanly from the general formula it also gives (break-even ROAS = 1 / gross margin); this playbook flags it as illustrative framing rather than a formula to reuse without independently modeling the brand's own numbers.
- **Geography and channel scope**: the macro framing (RBA cash rate, Australian inflation) is Australia-specific; the presenter's own portfolio is described as spanning "the US and other core markets," so the macro argument for a "weaker consumer this year" should be re-checked for any non-Australian market. `[web check: current interest-rate and inflation conditions for the target market]`
- **No independent Q4 2026 outcome data exists in this source**: everything about "this year" (2026) is a forecast made from macro indicators and early-2026 auction trends (Q1/Q2 2026 impression compression), not an observed Black Friday 2026 result, because the source predates the event itself.
- **Domain gaps for the folder merge**: this source gives almost no content on email/SMS, on-site landing-page build/design, or fulfillment/staffing operations, despite Black Friday planning normally spanning all three; those sections above are marked as gaps and should be filled from other sources in this folder or from the dedicated 14-EML, 07-LPG, and operations folders.
- **No conflicting position from a second source is present here**: this is the only source in this mini playbook; anywhere this file states "the source says," check the folder-level PLAYBOOK.md for whether other Black Friday or paid-media sources agree, qualify, or contradict it, especially on scaling cadence (3-day rolling vs. other windows), cost-cap usage, and evergreen-vs-refreshed-copy tradeoffs, which the source itself treats as account-specific judgment calls rather than settled rules.
