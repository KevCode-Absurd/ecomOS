## 1. What this source is for

This is a single agency operator's own end-to-end system for diagnosing and fixing the three things he claims gate ecommerce growth (measurement, finance, and paid-media structure), taught as a stacked pyramid: get the numbers right first, then get the money math right, then get the offer right, then get the ad-account structure right, then (in part 2) get the creative and Google Ads right. Part 1 is most useful for an operator or agency team that suspects its reported ROAS doesn't match its bank account, that is scaling revenue without scaling profit, that runs a rotating discount calendar without knowing its true margin impact, or that has an ad account with either no clear structure or excessive fragmented segmentation. It gives specific formulas (break-even ROAS, LTGP:CAC, contribution-margin waterfalls) usable at almost any revenue size, but the bulk of its structural advice (geo-lift testing, page-cap workarounds, spend-tier account structures) is explicitly aimed at brands doing $1M/year and up, with the deepest material aimed at 8- and 9-figure brands. It does not cover, in the part of the source read here, landing-page design, email/SMS flow design, or organic/content strategy in any depth - those may surface in part 2, but are absent from part 1.

## 2. Principles

1. [MEA] Treat an ecommerce business as three stacked pillars: data integrity/measurement at the base, creative velocity/diversity in the middle, technical account structure on top. Condition: account structure is meaningless without adequate creative volume, and both are meaningless if the metrics driving decisions are wrong. Evidence: presenter's own audit framework, no external study cited. [4f911dc82a03]
2. [MEA] Do not compare ROAS across campaigns with different margin profiles as if equal ROAS means equal value; compare profit contribution instead. Condition: applies whenever two campaigns' underlying products or offers have different gross margin (e.g., one discounted, one full-price). Evidence: worked example, two campaigns both at 4x/3x ROAS on $10,000 revenue with different margin profiles reranking their true value. [4ba4c1567d4d]
3. [MEA] Do not compare ROAS across ad platforms. Condition: platforms use different attribution models (view-through inclusion, click-window length) and sit at different funnel stages, so identical ROAS numbers are not commensurable. Evidence: Pinterest-vs-Meta example (Pinterest counts tile-based view-through with no click required). [4ba4c1567d4d]
4. [MEA] Do not shift budget toward the funnel stage with the higher reported ROAS. Condition: bottom-of-funnel/retargeting campaigns structurally over-report versus top-of-funnel cold campaigns; cutting cold spend to fund a "better" retargeting number can collapse the business since retargeting depends on cold spend upstream. Evidence: illustrative example (2x cold vs. 7x retargeting) plus a cross-channel example where added TikTok spend inflated Meta's mid-funnel ROAS from 4x to 12x via retargeted TikTok traffic. [4ba4c1567d4d] [ae6e998429c5]
5. [MEA] Treat Meta's 1-day-view attribution setting as the primary source of ROAS over-inflation; report and optimize on 7-day-click (plus 1-day-engaged) instead. Condition: applies specifically to Meta's default attribution windows; other platforms have different defaults and should be checked individually (Google: ~30-day click + 7-day view + 7-day engaged + 1-day view, per the source). Evidence: presenter's stated estimate that 30-50% of reported ROAS can come from 1-day-view alone; unverified. [21ae950f3d4b] [d18061f1791b]
6. [MEA] Existing-customer exposure inside a "cold" campaign inflates ROAS without being causally incremental, even after removing user-level attribution, because a returning-customer purchase is not proven to be caused by the ad. Evidence: supplement-replenishment illustration (existing customer would likely have bought anyway). [21ae950f3d4b]
7. [MEA] A "halo effect" from an offline or out-of-account activation (e.g., a pop-up event) can lift in-platform ROAS in that geography with no causal link to ad spend; do not scale spend in response to such a lift without verifying the real cause. [21ae950f3d4b]
8. [MEA] The correct response to unreliable metrics is subtraction (fewer, better metrics, one clear hierarchy), not addition (more attribution tools, more dashboards, more conversion events). Condition: this is presented as a general operating philosophy once a business already has "enough" data sources; it does not apply to a business with no tracking at all. Evidence: presenter's observation that conflicting outputs from multiple attribution/incrementality tools cause executive paralysis, not clarity. [7500786dbfd9]

Condition: incrementality testing is only relevant, per the presenter's own estimate, to roughly the top 20% of the audience ($10M+/month, multi-market or omnichannel); the other 80% should focus on finance-grade metrics. [OFF] Match cross-sell/upsell placement and offer generosity to funnel-stage friction: in-cart/checkout offers face medium-to-high friction (typical take rate 5-15%) and should favor low-friction add-ons (e.g., shipping protection) over full products at checkout specifically; post-purchase (after payment is captured) is near-zero friction, one-click, and can sustain much higher take rates. [3d3e2b815f9b]
59. [OFF] Sequence post-purchase upsells by anchoring high first, then downselling if declined, rather than leading with the cheapest offer (a common inversion), because a cheap first offer price-anchors low and makes every subsequent higher-value offer look comparatively expensive. [835b52ea1691]
60. [OFF] Apply five rules to in-cart upsells: one offer per cart-drawer slot (avoid decision fatigue), prioritize relevance over price, favor high-margin SKUs for the slot, keep the message simple (unlike the front-end offer, which can be layered/complex), and measure success by gross-margin lift, not AOV lift. [1902fe9818ae]
61. [OFF] Track revenue-per-visit (RPV = post-purchase revenue ÷ post-purchase funnel visits) for post-purchase upsells, targeting roughly 10-15% RPV as a benchmark for what a well-built post-purchase upsell program should add to AOV. [26de2145522c]
62. [OFF] Segment exit-intent/abandonment offers by funnel stage: be generous with browsers (lowest commitment), keep cart-abandon offers small (they likely buy within 1-2 days anyway), and avoid discounting checkout-abandoners at all (use risk reversal like free shipping instead, since they are close to buying regardless). [03b1898ead39]
63. [OFF] Select and iterate a front-end offer using four diagnostic questions: what is the true gross-margin impact; is the AOV distribution single-peak or binomial; is there one hero SKU or many; and what is the buyer's repeat-purchase behavior - then test candidate offers live on dedicated landing pages/funnels, since only market testing (not a priori reasoning) reveals which of the many "good-looking" offers actually wins. [54348c1adb1f]
64. [ADC] Meta's account structure now favors consolidation over segmentation because conversion data is siloed at the campaign level on every major ad platform, and splitting limited conversion volume across more campaigns starves each one of the learning signal it needs; the presenter's rule-of-thumb inflection point is roughly 100-200 conversions/month before an account's optimization meaningfully improves. Condition: this reflects a structural shift since the presenter's own 2018 experience, when interest-based segmentation was genuinely favorable due to random small-sample bias in early audience discovery - attributed to iOS 14's signal loss and Meta's AI-driven broad-matching improvements since. [d39e9023291d] [f72ca32e9529]
65. [ADC] Ad-set-level segmentation matters far less than campaign-level segmentation (more data sharing occurs within a campaign, and conversion data cascades down from it); the presenter's stated range for the efficiency swing from ad-set-level consolidation vs. segmentation is roughly 5-10%, more noticeable at scale. [f72ca32e9529]
66. [ADC] Avoid Meta's multi-creative-per-ad feature (called "flexible ads" at time of filming - name likely to have changed, `[verify UI]`) despite it being consolidation-friendly, because it destroys per-creative attribution: you cannot tell which of the bundled creatives is actually driving results, making it impossible to iterate. [d39e9023291d]
67. [ADC] Full consolidation (one campaign, one ad set, everything else in it) works for roughly 10% of brands, per the presenter's own estimate, but fails for the other 90% under three conditions: materially different unit economics across the product portfolio (would over-allocate spend to the easiest-converting but lowest-true-margin items); genuinely non-overlapping personas (audience confusion, cross-serving men's ads to women and vice versa); or high-AOV/low-conversion-volume accounts (too little purchase signal forces the platform to optimize on weakly-correlated proxy signals like CTR/CPC instead of revenue). 72. ## 3. Procedures

Grouped by domain, per the folder's multi-topic routing convention. Part 1 has no material for "Product" beyond inventory grading (placed under Ops below) and no material at all for "Creative," "Landing pages" (beyond a passing mention) or "Email and retention" beyond what's noted; those domains should draw primarily from part 2 and other sources in this folder.

### Finance and unit economics

1. Build the P&L waterfall for the business: net revenue → cost of delivery (COGS + freight-in + shipping-out + transaction fees + refund/discount allowance) → gross margin → marketing (MER) → contribution margin → operating expenses → EBITDA → interest → net profit. Track each line as a % of revenue monthly. [29f6666ae310] [f82391a766c6]
2. When any line misses its target %, look upward in the waterfall for the cause before assuming the line itself is broken (e.g., a missed contribution-margin target is a marketing-efficiency or cost-of-delivery problem, not a "contribution margin" problem per se). [29f6666ae310] [2ba72747cc86]
3. To find true gross margin per product: start from retail price, subtract unit product cost, subtract shipping-to-customer, subtract pick-and-pack fee, subtract the transaction-fee %, subtract any flow-through sales tax collected, subtract per-unit landed freight, subtract a refund-rate allowance (fashion can run 10-80%, per the source) and a discount-usage allowance. The result is true gross margin - do not use "price minus unit cost only" (product margin) for any marketing-budget decision. [7d67a42b0d81] [2ddbe78b9877]
4. To price a new product with waterfall pricing: (a) set a target net-profit %; (b) add known fixed opex % to get a required contribution-margin %; (c) estimate CAC for the product (from the ad account or comparable products); (d) set a return/shrink allowance %; (e) get a shipping/fulfillment cost quote; (f) know COGS; (g) solve upward for the price that clears the contribution-margin target after subtracting CAC, allowance, shipping and COGS; (h) benchmark the resulting price against competitors - if it is far above them, either reposition into a premium/blue-ocean niche that can sustain the price, or drop the product. [2ddbe78b9877] [312d5fc90827]
5. To decide if a discount is viable: compute baseline break-even ROAS (1 ÷ gross margin %) and break-even CAC (the gross-margin dollar amount); recompute both at the proposed discounted price using ALL cost lines (not just the price cut); compare the new required ROAS/CAC to what is realistically achievable on your platforms; if the jump is implausible (e.g., needing to move from a 2x to a 12x ROAS), redesign the offer instead of running the discount as planned. [c92a82747e36] [b539f0c8f5a4]
6. To choose where product-level paid spend should go: compute contribution margin per product (price − CAC − true cost of delivery), not CPA or blended ROAS; segment ad sets/campaigns by product category so budget can be deliberately steered to the higher-contribution-margin product rather than left to the platform's default "maximize conversions" (lowest-CPA) bidding, which will over-favor the lower-margin product if its CPA happens to be lower. [812745ee3d05]
7. To forecast returning-customer revenue: for every historical acquisition cohort, multiply (cohort size) × (that cohort's historical repeat-purchase rate at the relevant age-in-months) × (average returning-customer AOV), sum across all cohorts, then layer in seasonality (from ~3 years of history) and any known upcoming promotional events not present in the historical base. [2f48255ebfdf]
8. To forecast new-customer revenue: plot ~1 year of daily acquisition MER against daily spend, remove outlier days (major sale periods) and days below a spend floor, fit a regression, then use the fitted curve to check whether a target spend level can realistically produce the revenue needed to hit a stated goal; if not, identify what NEW lever (marketing event, product launch, new channel, more creative volume) could shift the curve rather than just raising the spend input. [a3b5f20b67e2]
9. To size existing-customer ad spend: decide the target frequency per period, multiply by the number of existing customers, multiply by the segment's actual CPM (pulled from the platform's audience-segment breakdown, not assumed), and treat the result as the spend ceiling; monitor trailing frequency (target under 7-8, per the source's two slightly different restatements) as the ongoing guardrail. [df7a0134cc56] [587d3d72e000]

### Product

10. Grade every SKU by sell-through velocity (A = fast, B = medium, C = slow, D = not moving; Shopify auto-ranks this under its Products tab, `[verify UI]`) and actively rationalize SKU count as new products launch, since most launches (outside categories like supplements that can reposition one SKU across many benefit claims) underperform and land in grade C/D. [8669aea558c2]
11. When a product lands in grade C/D, deliberately route paid spend to it at breakeven or a small loss, isolated in its own funnel (can bypass the main site via dedicated landing pages), specifically to convert the dead stock back into cash; coordinate this explicitly with whoever is KPI'd on ROAS/profit, since that team will otherwise starve the inventory of the spend needed to clear it. A named (unverified, self-reported) tactic: push grade-D inventory into a different country market and discount heavily there to avoid damaging brand equity in the primary market. [8669aea558c2]

### Offers

12. Answer the four offer-selection questions before designing anything: (a) what is the true gross-margin impact of the candidate offer; (b) is your AOV distribution single-peak or binomial (check the actual distribution, not the mean); (c) do you have one hero SKU or many (single-SKU brands lean on repositioning then GWP then disciplined discounting; multi-SKU brands can add native bundles, cross-category upsells and threshold unlocks); (d) what is the buyer's repeat-purchase behavior (high-repeat categories lean into subscription/lock-in mechanics; low/no-repeat categories should maximize first-order margin instead). [54348c1adb1f]
13. To build an "evergreen" (non-promotional, year-round) high-AOV offer: reposition around a longer commitment (e.g., 90-day supply vs. 30-day) and use bundle-driven shipping/fulfillment economies of scale (per-shipment cost rises much slower than linearly with unit count) to keep true gross margin healthy even at a headline discount; gate the deepest discount behind a subscription opt-in to both lift retention and let you model the offer against a longer, lower required-ROAS horizon; add a low-cost or partner-sourced mystery gift (target $0-3 COGS) only if it demonstrably moves take rate, and survey customers after ~90-120 days to prune any component that isn't earning its complexity. [f2929b62a6cb] [8aec1db231ba] [afe614ed1856]
14. To place cross-sell/upsell offers: put threshold unlocks or complimentary-product-at-threshold offers in-cart; put low-friction, non-product add-ons (e.g., shipping protection) at checkout, keeping the primary offer message simple; put the highest-value, one-click offers post-purchase (after payment is captured), sequenced high-to-low (anchor with the biggest offer first, downsell if declined) rather than cheap-to-expensive. [3d3e2b815f9b] [835b52ea1691]
15. To measure and prune upsells: track incremental contribution profit (take rate % × AOV lift × gross margin %) per offer, not take rate or AOV lift alone; track RPV (post-purchase revenue ÷ post-purchase visits) against a 10-15% benchmark; apply the five in-cart rules (one offer per slot, relevance over price, high-margin SKUs, simple messaging, measure by gross-margin lift not AOV lift). [af664909cd4e] [26de2145522c] [1902fe9818ae]
16. To handle exit intent: for browsing exits, be generous with a return incentive and urgency; for cart-abandon exits, offer something small (they likely buy within 1-2 days anyway); for checkout-abandon exits, do not discount - use risk reversal (e.g., free shipping) instead. [03b1898ead39]

### Paid media (Meta account structure - part 1 covers consolidation theory through the $50k-$250k tier)

17. Before structuring any Meta account, answer the five diagnostic questions (product-category count, margin spread across categories, non-overlapping persona count, region/country count, creative throughput) and confirm the structure will satisfy the two non-negotiables (data integrity, commercial alignment) - do not start from a generic template if these answers suggest real segmentation is needed. [f7b8e05cda3d] [34509036c871]
18. Sub-$50k/month generic structure (only when the five diagnostics don't clearly call for something else): one dedicated testing campaign (ABO or CBO), ad sets organized by concept (persona × angle × offer), 3-5 creatives per ad set, existing customers excluded, attribution set to 7-day click; KPI at the ad-set level over a rolling window no shorter than about 5 days at this scale (shorter only at much higher conversion volume); raise budget in 20%/day increments when an ad set hits KPI, diagnose/iterate/wind down when it misses. Add an optional scaling campaign only once a winning ad set has plateaued at a meaningful daily spend (rule-of-thumb ROI worth it from roughly $400/day; not worth it around $60/day). Add an optional existing-customer retargeting campaign, sized to the brand's repeat-purchase profile, frequency-capped. [5de48d3e386a] [81e15385dc8b] [1e8b0ae41022]
19. $50k-$250k/month structure: keep the existing-customer retargeting campaign (website-visitor retargeting still rarely needed as its own campaign); expand the cold side to 2-3 campaigns ONLY when justified by genuinely distinct funnels/personas/regions (e.g., two age-based angles for the same SKU that need different creative styles, landing pages and possibly different Pages; or a distinct high-LTV category earning its own, more aggressive CPA target); continue concept-level testing (persona × angle × offer × ad-type matrix) as the core skill unlock at this tier, aiming to find the roughly 20% of concepts able to sustainably absorb hundreds of thousands of dollars/month. [1fe205b60578] [3b07bb97fef4]
20. To size any new-concept test: set the budget at target CPA × 3 (or × 5 for a stricter, single binary decision) before deciding keep/kill; set the minimum test DURATION separately (about 1 week by default; 3-4 days for large/stable, fast-purchase businesses; 10-14 days for small businesses with slow purchase cycles), because daily seasonality and multi-day time-to-purchase both bias a too-short read regardless of budget spent. [1e8b0ae41022] [2dbc68e5e82e]
21. To work around Meta's per-Page active-ad cap (~300-500 ads, `[verify UI]`): use multiple duplicate Pages (cosmetic variation only), whitelisting (a self-created, third-party-styled Page running the same or lightly reframed ads, distinct from paid partnership/creator-page ads), and regional Pages when campaigns are already segmented by country (since each country campaign accumulates toward the same shared Page cap even with identical ads). [c9a888b1f02e] [0dd3aee5c557]

### Ops

22. Before committing to an aggressive growth-rate target, model the cash conversion cycle (DIO + DSO − DPO) and the cash required to fund the inventory that growth implies; negotiate better supplier payment terms (e.g., partial deposit instead of full upfront) or shorten manufacturer lead time before resorting to inventory financing, since financing is expensive, often requires personal collateral, and can compress margins toward zero as spend scales to match the financed growth rate. [7e066cd286cd] [eecd54f14ae1]
23. When cash is tight from unsold prior-period inventory, do not default to cutting marketing spend; first determine whether marketing is the actual revenue driver (in which case cutting it can spiral the business downward) and whether the real fix is instead a deliberate, short-term increase in MER specifically targeted at clearing the grade-C/D inventory backlog. [2ba72747cc86]

### Team

24. Classify every staff cost correctly: fulfillment-facing staff (pick/pack/ship) go into cost of delivery, allocated per order; everyone else (marketing leadership, ops leadership, etc.) goes into operating expenses. [f82391a766c6]
25. Treat opex growth as a red flag until the business can demonstrably hire top-tier talent (source's own framing: 7-figure brands often lack both the scale to attract strong candidates and the interviewing experience to identify them); until then, prioritize marketing spend over headcount/software/office expansion as the primary growth lever. [9840c923e170]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- Check: is your reported ROAS trustworthy? How: switch Meta reporting/attribution to 7-day click (drop 1-day view), via Columns → Compare Attribution Settings `[verify UI]`. Good: 7-day-click ROAS tracks tightly with acquisition MER. Bad: a large gap remains, signaling other over-attribution sources (existing-customer bleed, halo effects, DPA/bottom-funnel concentration) still need addressing. [21ae950f3d4b] [d18061f1791b]
- Check: is a high-ROAS placement/segment (via Meta's Breakdown feature) actually worth chasing with a dedicated campaign? How: before building a standalone campaign for it, consider whether Meta has already tried pushing more spend there and found no incremental return - trust the platform's blended distribution unless you have expert-level ability to verify incremental return yourself. Good: no action needed, spend distribution is already near-optimal. Bad (rare, expert-only): you can independently confirm real incremental headroom exists in the higher-blended-ROAS segment. [b3d6e61e0a7a]
- Check: is your reported gross margin real? How: rebuild it as retail price minus ALL variable delivery costs (product, freight-in, shipping-out, transaction fee %, flow-through sales tax, refund/discount allowance), not just price minus unit product cost. Good: the recomputed number still clears your marketing-spend assumptions. Bad: it is materially lower (source's own worked example: 70% assumed vs. 24% actual), meaning your marketing budget ceiling and break-even ROAS targets are wrong. [7d67a42b0d81] [53c86fa371bb]
- Check: is a discount you're about to run actually viable? How: recompute break-even ROAS/CAC at the discounted price using the full waterfall (true AOV after any units-per-transaction shift, true COGS against the pre-discount retail value). Good: the resulting required ROAS is realistically achievable on your platforms. Bad: it requires an implausible jump (e.g., 2x to 12x) - redesign the offer instead of running it as planned. [c92a82747e36] [9ea687d35127]
- Check: is your AOV distribution single-peak or binomial? How: plot actual order-value distribution, not just the mean AOV in your reporting tool. Good: a single, clear cluster - one threshold offer can work. Bad: two distinct peaks - a single mean-based threshold will fail on both sides; set two separate threshold offers instead. [1a706c3de836]
- Check: is a product-level bidding allocation actually maximizing profit? How: compute contribution margin per product (not CPA or ROAS) and compare to where the platform's default bidding is actually sending spend. Good: spend concentrates on the higher-contribution-margin product. Bad: spend concentrates on a lower-margin product because it has a lower CPA - segment by product/category to correct it. [812745ee3d05]
- Check: does the business actually have a cash problem or a marketing-efficiency problem? How: read the balance sheet (unsold inventory, cash position) alongside the P&L profit figure, not the P&L alone. Good: P&L profit and cash position move together. Bad: P&L shows profit while cash is flat or negative - the real constraint is likely the cash conversion cycle / inventory buildup, not marketing efficiency, and cutting ad spend will not fix it. [731ac683ccf3] [7e066cd286cd]
- Check: is a Meta account over-segmented for its actual conversion volume? How: total monthly conversions ÷ number of active campaigns. Good: comfortably above the presenter's ~100-200/month inflection point per campaign. Bad: well below it - consolidate campaigns before troubleshooting anything else. [d39e9023291d]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Comparing ROAS across campaigns, platforms, or funnel stages as if a higher number always means a better decision. [4ba4c1567d4d]
- Leaving 1-day-view attribution active and using it in reporting, inflating ROAS by a claimed 30-50%. [21ae950f3d4b]
- Adding more attribution tools/dashboards/conversion events to fix confusing metrics, instead of subtracting down to a small, agreed hierarchy. [7500786dbfd9]
- Relying on blended MER (rather than acquisition MER) to judge paid-media performance, since it can rise even as new-customer efficiency is deteriorating. [1dfa80844f46]
- Using blended/gross AOV and GP% instead of new-customer-only figures when calculating CAC targets. [82a77847f675]
- Reporting product margin (price minus unit cost only) as if it were true gross margin. [7d67a42b0d81]
- Using keystone pricing (2x cost) or simple markup pricing (3-5x cost), which ignores true delivery cost, price elasticity and competitor pricing. [2ddbe78b9877]
- Booking interest expense to operating expenses instead of treating it as a financing cost below EBITDA. [f82391a766c6]
- Booking transaction fees to opex instead of cost of delivery. [f82391a766c6]
- Booking warehouse fulfillment staff cost to opex instead of allocating it per order into cost of delivery. [f82391a766c6]
- Over-inflating operating expenses (people, software, office) under a "reinvest in the business" narrative when the primary DTC growth lever is marketing spend. [9840c923e170]
- Letting the platform's default "maximize conversions" (or "maximize conversion value") bidding allocate spend by CPA/ROAS alone, ignoring true per-product contribution margin. [812745ee3d05]
- Discounting without modeling the full waterfall first (true AOV shift, true COGS against retail value, resulting break-even ROAS) - can hide a large, sometimes unreachable, jump in required efficiency. [9ea687d35127]
- Setting AOV-based threshold offers off the mean instead of the actual (possibly binomial) distribution - the source estimates roughly 80%+ of brands make this mistake. [1a706c3de836]
- Choosing a gift-with-purchase item the customer doesn't actually value, or that isn't cheap/high-margin enough to matter, just because "gift with purchase" sounds good. [afe614ed1856]
- Running the same offer to new and returning customers, when the two need different objectives. [a1fd76b8d908]
- Running a 52-week rotating flat-discount promo calendar and calling it "offer rotation." [a1fd76b8d908]
- Optimizing cross-sell/upsell selection purely on product margin, ignoring take rate and AOV lift, which can pick a worse-performing add-on overall. [af664909cd4e]
- Leading post-purchase upsell sequencing with the cheapest offer first, which price-anchors low and makes later, higher-value offers look expensive. [835b52ea1691]
- Stacking multiple upsell offers into one cart-drawer slot, causing decision fatigue and lowering take rate. [1902fe9818ae]
- Over-segmenting a Meta account relative to its actual conversion volume, starving each campaign of the learning signal it needs. [d39e9023291d]
- Setting cost caps or complex bidding controls at the ad level instead of the ad-set level. [d9ebea42b405]
- Relying on Meta's breakdown feature to justify new dedicated campaigns without understanding that blended numbers don't reflect incremental return at the margin. [b3d6e61e0a7a]
- Continuing to rely on interest targeting and lookalike audiences under current broad/creative-driven Meta matching. [4bddfe132ec2]
- Spending significant time on daily bid tweaks or daily budget-pacing changes instead of creative production or landing-page testing. [6d6ffaa950ec]
- Adding new campaigns simply because ad spend has grown, without a specific commercial or data-integrity justification. [1fe205b60578]
- Rolling a barely-spending ad (e.g., ~$60/day) into a scaling campaign too early, adding structural complexity for negligible gain. [81e15385dc8b]
- Setting ad-set test budgets off a generic "50 conversions in 7 days" rule of thumb instead of back-calculating from target CPA and AOV. [1e8b0ae41022]
- Reading a new-concept test result too quickly (before daily seasonality and time-to-purchase have had a chance to play out). [2dbc68e5e82e]
- Pursuing an aggressive growth-rate target without modeling the cash conversion cycle and the inventory financing it would require. [eecd54f14ae1]
- Reflexively cutting marketing spend to fix a cash crunch caused by unsold prior-period inventory, when marketing may be the actual revenue driver and the real fix is a targeted, temporary MER increase to clear the dead stock. [2ba72747cc86]
- Letting a paid-media team KPI'd purely on ROAS/profit quietly starve grade-C/D inventory of the spend needed to clear it. [8669aea558c2]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- The presenter's agency sells both a free brand audit (for $5M+/year revenue brands) and hiring services throughout this video; every claim of the form "we see this in every audit" or "we've worked with 60+ 8/9-figure brands" is a self-reported, unverifiable credibility claim serving that pitch, not independently audited data. Treat all such figures (audit counts, client counts, tracked ad spend, interview counts) as marketing claims.
- The LTGP:CAC benchmark bands are stated twice in this same source, once framed around a 180-day window (bands: <1, 1-2, 2-3, 3+) and again framed around 30/90-day windows with slightly different supporting language (e.g., "0.01% of businesses" can safely run sub-1). These are the same underlying opinion restated, not two independent confirmations - a folder merge should treat this as one benchmark, not corroborating evidence.
- The existing-customer frequency cap is stated as "under 8" on a trailing-7-day basis in one place and "under 7" on a trailing-30-day basis in another. These are materially different measurement windows; the source does not reconcile them. Use as directional guidance only, and verify the actual window definition before hard-coding either number into a check.
- The 25%-off discount worked example's arithmetic (AOV $122, retail value "$162," COGS "$65," required ROAS "8.97") does not fully reconcile against the stated inputs when re-derived independently; it is reproduced here as the presenter's own spoken figures, not independently re-verified math. Treat the DIRECTION of the conclusion (required ROAS rises sharply, roughly 50%, under this discount) as the reliable takeaway, and re-derive the exact numbers from your own brand's data before using them.
- The layered supplement-offer worked example (AOV ~$190, per-shipment costs of $12/$14/$17 for 1/2/3 units) is presented with some numbers that appear approximate or rounded in the presenter's live narration (e.g., "90-day is $21" appears to be a mis-transcription or verbal shorthand); use the qualitative mechanism (shipping economies of scale lower true cost of delivery in a bundle) rather than the exact dollar figures.
- Several numeric claims have no independently locatable card id in the provided card set (TikTok's $200k lift-test minimum spend, the ~20-30% YoY Meta CPM inflation figure, the ~10-20% media-buying skill lift at $100k-$300k/month, the 35-40% post-purchase upsell take rates, and the $4.99/~30%-take-rate shipping-protection tactic); these are marked `[web check]` in section 4 and should be independently verified before being relied on operationally, since they may reflect the presenter's dated or approximate recollection rather than a documented figure.
