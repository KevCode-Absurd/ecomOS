## 3. Procedures

The source gives few explicit numbered step sequences; most of its content is stated principles and heuristics (see section 2). Where a repeatable sequence is actually described, it is captured below, grouped by domain for folder routing.

**Positioning**
1. List what top competitors and top content creators in the category are already doing (formats, hooks, tone, page layout).
2. Ask "how can I do the exact opposite?" for each element; keep the reversal that still serves the offer. `[3e457e257de6]`
3. Confirm the parent market has proven demand (search trend data, existing competing offers, existing category spend) before committing to it.
4. Identify a narrow slice of that market (life stage, use case, single angle) with few or no dedicated competitors.
5. Build product and PDP positioning around that slice so the buyer feels the offer was made specifically for them. `[0a484f196d18]`

**Offers**
1. Establish current AOV and repeat purchase rate for the store.
2. For consumable products, add a subscription/auto-replenish option matched to a realistic reorder cadence (30/60/90 days) at checkout or post-purchase.
3. For non-consumable or service-style offers, design an explicit "next step" (upsell, ascension tier) rather than relying on repeat identical purchases.
4. Track repeat rate and LTV as standing KPIs, separate from ad reporting. `[3d5541e52ed0]`
5. Compute CAC and per-customer value (AOV, then LTV); if your per-customer value is higher than a competitor's at the same CAC, you have headroom to bid higher for the same auction — use that headroom to scale rather than only protect margin. `[81635c2e6b25]`

**Creative**
1. Do the research first (customer calls/transcripts if you have customers; public research and, failing that, an AI-simulated "customer persona" if you don't) before writing anything. `[62247a5dc49e]` `[5cc2f2f1185c]`
2. Extract exact customer phrases (voice of customer) from that research.
3. Write the first one to two sentences of the hook first, aiming for a "punch in the gut" — visceral, plain, roughly 5th-8th grade reading level; strip anything written to sound clever. `[7d460350a380]`
4. Build the scroll-stopper visual: pick a clip that passes the "what the f*** did I just watch?" test.
5. Add a simple voiceover/audio layer last; treat it as the smallest lever.
6. Before producing a new ad from a reference/competitor ad, write down the underlying framework (belief installed, mechanism claimed, objection pre-empted, sequence) rather than rewording the original copy; keep these frameworks in a swipe file organized by principle, not by ad. `[12f592a7e391]` `[5c6cc037d679]`
7. Review ("chief") the draft against the customer's likely reaction and against copywriting craft; check for clarity over cleverness one more time before shipping.

**Paid**
1. Before testing a new creative on a new product, cross off every controllable unknown you can verify in advance — most importantly, whether people already buy this category of product. Prefer proven-demand products for new creative tests. `[1d7f15eafafc]`
2. Launch and track CPM, CPC, CTR at the ad layer; treat "cheap traffic" alone as a false win if it doesn't convert (the source's example: sub-$1 CPMs and sub-1-cent CPCs in low-cost geos with no buyers). `[cd2ec58854db]`
3. When an ad shows an early positive signal with meaningful data, iterate on that specific ad partway through the same day rather than waiting for a full standard test cycle. `[899c0cfa0cb1]`
4. Reconcile in-platform conversion counts against a third-party attribution tool before scaling or killing a campaign on the platform's numbers alone. `[64c447f737c2]`

**Organic**
The source does not give an organic/content-marketing procedure. Its only organic-adjacent point is the copy-vs-content distinction (principle 15): content is for top-of-funnel attention and audience building, not for driving sales directly. `[eb0b904a4894]` No further organic steps are given; treat this domain as thin in this source.

**Email**
No email-specific procedure is given. The awareness-stage framework (principle 9) and the hook/subject-line "punch in the gut" rule (principle 13) are stated as applying to subject lines and email flows by extension, but the source gives no email-specific sequence, cadence or test method. `[f24960c6f7b6]` `[7d460350a380]`

**Measurement**
1. Instrument every funnel step separately: ad metrics (CPC/CPM/CTR), landing-page-view-to-add-to-cart, add-to-cart-to-checkout, checkout-to-purchase.
2. When one step underperforms while upstream steps look healthy, treat that as a signal to investigate, not the root cause itself; keep drilling until you find the actual constraint (his real example: a checkout with no payment processor configured). `[4fc18ca8c1c4]`
3. Before spending on traffic, deliberately try to break the funnel yourself: sold-out variants, expired coupons, discount stacking, mobile form fields, interrupted/refreshed checkout, post-purchase email. `[5d4f23b0fe19]`
4. Compute LTV ÷ CAC; compare against the 3:1 (standard) or 7-10:1 (high-ticket) baseline for your model. `[a90f86deab35]`

**Team**
1. In the early stage, have the founder personally write the first ads/copy and build the first creatives rather than delegating, to build first-hand intuition; only bring in specialists once you understand which hooks and angles convert. `[11e43df4c9d2]`
2. As volume grows, identify where the next bottleneck will appear (creative production, order processing, support, inventory) and add one of three forms of "energy": more of your own hours, a hire, or a system/AI tool — matched to the actual bottleneck, not added generically. `[25d1aef326ee]`
3. Keep an outside reviewer (partner, mentor, peer) in the loop, since the source argues no operator can see every problem in their own business alone. `[25d1aef326ee]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Funnel bottleneck check.** Compare ad-layer metrics (CPC, CPM, CTR) against funnel-layer conversion rate. If ad metrics are healthy but conversion rate is low, that is a signal the constraint lives in the funnel, not the ad — drill into landing-page-view → add-to-cart → checkout → purchase step by step to find where the drop actually happens. Good: each step shows an expected pass-through rate for the category `[needs brand data: category-typical step-through rates — the source gives none]`. Bad: a specific step (e.g., checkout) shows an outsized drop versus the rest. Next step: investigate that step directly (the source's real example was a missing payment processor). `[4fc18ca8c1c4]`
- **Pre-launch break test.** Before sending traffic, walk the full path as a first-time buyer and deliberately try to break it: sold-out variants, expired coupons, stacked discounts, mobile form entry, interrupted or refreshed checkout. Bad: any of these produces an error, a stuck cart, or a failed charge. Fix before spending on ads. `[5d4f23b0fe19]`
- **Attribution reconciliation.** Compare in-platform reported conversions against a third-party attribution tool. Bad: platform numbers diverge from the reconciled number by double digits (the source's estimate: 5-10% typical, up to 30-50% in worse cases). If they diverge, decide which number you'll act on and apply it consistently rather than switching per campaign. `[64c447f737c2]`
- **LTV:CAC health check.** Divide LTV by CAC. Good: at or above 3:1 (standard) or 7-10:1 (high ticket). Bad: below that band — treat as a signal to fix AOV, retention or acquisition cost before scaling spend. `[a90f86deab35]`
- **Hook quality check ("what the f*** did I just watch?" test).** Show the opening 3-5 seconds of a video ad/scroll-stopper to someone cold. Good: a genuine, visceral reaction of surprise. Bad: no reaction, or a reaction of recognizing it as "an ad." `[7c32ef7f2d80]`
- **Clarity check for creative.** Read or watch the ad without assuming context: does a stranger understand what's sold, who it's for, why it's different, and what to do next within the first seconds? Bad: the message requires the viewer to already know the brand or product to make sense of it — cut or demote purely aesthetic elements that delay this. `[42a366d5ecc7]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Copying a competitor's ad copy word-for-word ("swiping blindly") instead of extracting the underlying principle — the source says this builds no durable skill and stops working as soon as the swipe fatigues. `[12f592a7e391]` `[5c6cc037d679]`
- Testing a brand-new, unproven product with brand-new, unproven creative and unproven media buying all at once — stacking unknowns on unknowns. `[1d7f15eafafc]`
- Optimizing for aesthetics/production value instead of message clarity in paid creative. `[42a366d5ecc7]`
- Trusting a platform's own conversion count as exact, especially post-tracking-policy changes, and scaling spend toward ads that are actually unprofitable as a result. `[64c447f737c2]`
- Treating a weak funnel metric as the root cause instead of an indicator, and fixing the wrong thing. `[4fc18ca8c1c4]`
- Launching a funnel without personally trying to break it first — the source's real example is a checkout with no payment processor configured, discovered only after launch. `[5d4f23b0fe19]`
- Comparing your early results to other operators' public wins instead of your own data; the source frames this as demoralizing and unproductive, not as useful benchmarking. `[391c879b2d86]`
- Acting on a strong gut feeling about a new product or ad without having enough personal test history to justify treating that feeling as anything other than a hypothesis. `[455d2f7749e9]`
- Delegating all copy and creative production away from the founder before the founder has learned, first-hand, what converts. `[11e43df4c9d2]`
- Writing one piece of copy that mixes multiple awareness stages, effectively speaking to no one. `[f24960c6f7b6]`
- Assuming a business will keep running the same way as it scales without adding hours, people or systems — the source calls this ignoring entropy. `[25d1aef326ee]`
