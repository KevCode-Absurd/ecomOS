## 2. Principles

**Part 1 — Bloom Nutrition (Greg Lavecchia)**

1. Build creator sourcing in-house rather than through an agency, so outreach stays cheap and fast; use scouts whose job is discovery and contact, not marketing polish. Condition: works when someone can do outreach daily; not a side task. [2cd965d92f02]
2. Give creators a product and a few talking points, not a shot-list script, because platform-native content the source says outperforms brand-directed content. Condition: regulated claims (supplements) still need compliance review of what the creator actually says. [e4aa7b7065d9]
3. Price flat-fee creator payouts off your own current Meta CPM, not the creator's quote or an industry rate. Condition: only sound if you accept creator content as upper-funnel and not directly attributed. [a5e7b7863c43]
4. It is possible to run an influencer program at scale on zero link/code attribution, judged instead on reach, content volume and blended signals (branded search, new customers, retail sell-through). Condition: depends on scale; a small store may need at least coarse tracking to justify the spend. [6a3c3dd9f14f]
5. Use a VPN-based alternate account to break out of your own algorithm bubble when scouting creators on TikTok, since a normal account keeps surfacing the same names. Condition: check current platform terms before relying on this; feed behaviour changes. [063f442d74a8]
6. Pay macro-influencers to seed a format or claim, then book cheaper micro-influencers once they start imitating it, rather than paying micro creators to originate ideas. Condition: needs a niche where micro creators actually imitate macro trends. [38e857b80873]
7. A cheap digital lead product (the source's $9.99 PDF guide) plus an owned community channel can fund and validate a brand's first physical product. Condition: value is list-building and validation, not margin. [491bb6180c60]
8. A visible, on-brand freebie attached to a purchase threshold can buy repeated product placement in creator content while also lifting AOV. Condition: only works if creators actually use it on camera; test AOV/repeat impact before rolling out permanently. [9d911f4de73e]
9. "Restock" and sellout messaging functions as social proof ("validity marketing") stronger than a discount, but only when the scarcity is real. Condition: loses effect with constant use; needs real gaps between drops. [b7d4878acc8a]
10. Real-world events can be run purely as content and flavour-testing engines, with free food/drink as the acquisition cost for footage and reactions, not for immediate sales. Condition: judge by content and feedback produced against cost, not day-of revenue. [096660c8b6a2]
11. ## 3. Procedures

Organised by domain so the folder merge can route each block.

**Influencers / creators (Bloom, part 1)**
1. Set up in-house scouting: hire 1-2 scouts, give them platform access, have them message creators in volume expecting most non-replies, and log every contact (followers, niche, audience geography, contact route, status) so the pipeline survives staff turnover. [2cd965d92f02]
2. VPN scouting: create a separate research account (not the brand's main account), route it through a VPN into another English-speaking market, let the algorithm serve local creators, shortlist from the resulting feed rather than searching a known list, then verify each candidate's actual audience location before outreach. Repeat across a few markets to build a bigger candidate pool than the domestic one. [063f442d74a8]
3. Two-tier seeding: identify the macro creators whose formats get imitated in your niche, pay them to seed a claim/format/sound, wait for micro creators to echo it organically, then book the micro tier cheaply to blanket the format. [38e857b80873]
4. Brief creators loosely: send product plus a short list of talking points (benefit, taste/feel, one CTA); do not send a shot list or require pre-approval of every cut; keep only legal/claim/safety guardrails non-negotiable; secure raw-footage usage rights in the contract for later paid-ad recuts. [e4aa7b7065d9]
5. Price a flat-fee deal: pull the creator's recent videos, take a median (not best-hit) view count, halve your current Meta CPM to get a target ceiling CPM (source's own figure: roughly $2), multiply expected views by that target CPM for the offer ceiling (example given: 200k median views x $2 CPM ≈ $400 flat fee), then compare actual post-performance against the estimate to adjust future rates. [a5e7b7863c43]
6. Run the program on zero attribution: no affiliate codes, no UTMs; set success metrics as views/impressions, content volume and cost-per-thousand-views; track blended signals instead (branded search volume, new-customer count, Amazon/retail sell-through, total revenue versus periods of creator push); reserve codes for specific paid offers only, not as the program's accounting system; recut top-performing creator videos into paid social ad creative, which is where measurable conversion is captured. [6a3c3dd9f14f]

**Offers / post-purchase (Bloom, part 1)**
7. Launch a low-cost digital lead product (source example: $9.99 PDF guide via PayPal) bundled with an owned community channel (Facebook group) and free updates; treat the buyer list/group as a research panel; let the community's recurring requests decide the first (or next) physical product category; validate with a small batch/limited run before full-line commitment. [491bb6180c60] [b3ef8af35c4c]
8. Gift-with-purchase: attach a visible, on-camera-usable freebie (source example: a physical mixer) to a purchase threshold or bundle; send it as part of creator seeding so it appears in videos; test AOV and repeat-purchase impact with/without the freebie before making it permanent. [9d911f4de73e]

**Creative / brand signalling (Bloom, part 1)**
9. Restock/validity messaging: only announce "back in stock" or "sold out" when genuinely true; pair the announcement with social proof (creator clips, event footage, waitlist size); keep a waitlist and notify it first on restock; use the restock as the campaign trigger instead of inventing new discount occasions. [b7d4878acc8a]
10. IRL events as content engines: define the event's output (content volume, flavour feedback) before booking a venue; make entry free (source example: free food/drink at a retro diner takeover in Austin) to maximise turnout and on-camera crowd density; seed unreleased products/flavours and film reactions; staff it as a shoot; edit footage into short-form creator-style content and reuse as paid social creative; judge it against cost, not day-of sales. [096660c8b6a2]

**Paid media / channel sequencing (Bloom, part 1)**
11. Omnichannel sequencing: shift focus and paid/creator traffic to Amazon first to build rank, accept a temporary DTC slowdown, then use the Amazon rank/sales data as evidence in retail buyer pitches (source's own path: Amazon → Target → ~50k doors); only treat the resulting broad retail footprint as a later-stage demand source, not the entry channel. [aefe14739fde] [5630f92d6cff]

**Product / market selection (both parts)**
12. White-space entry into a saturated category: pick the category, then look specifically for gaps in accessibility, price, or an unaddressed demographic (source example: female-positioned energy drinks); keep the physical format conventional (standard can) rather than reinventing it; validate with internal team consumption and community requests before committing. [66cddfb2c547]
13. Product-aware-not-brand-aware selection: use trend/ad-library tools (source names Trend Rocket — his own tool — Exploding Topics, Mania, and the free Facebook Ads Library) to find a trending product with thin brand ownership; confirm the product serves one clear function so a brand can be built around that function later. [8b75ede22872]
14. Multi-avatar creative test: map every use case for a generic product (source example: a TENS device — period pain, muscle cramps, ultramarathon-runner recovery); launch a relatively generic store; run separate ad creative per avatar simultaneously; scale dedicated landing pages and brand only around the avatar that takes off. [3e91d8f9ae2c]
15. Inverse international sequencing: launch first in Australia, then UK, then Europe, then Canada, then the US, porting the same proven creative/offer into each new under-served market; validate each with a small test budget before committing inventory/fulfilment; accept the US may end up the smallest market. [9036dbdf257e]
16. Product flywheel: once the hero trend product is declining, invest its cash into evergreen secondary products built around the same core function (source example: a comfort-hoodie hero funding sleep tees, nighties, dressing gowns, bath hoodies); do not drop the hero prematurely — ride it for cash while the secondaries ramp. [03c8d7a43d08] [36738f949d61]

**Team / operations (Fogerty, part 2)**
17. Operational sequence after a product wins: prove it with ads while competition is low, then hire out the team so the business does not depend solely on the founder, then invest in brand and evergreen products. [a0f3b2b3fa6b]
18. Hiring your CEO replacement: list the exact archetype the business needs at this stage; avoid hiring someone whose strengths are the mirror opposite of your own (source's near-miss: a marketing-led founder hiring a purely operational CEO); source internally, via a recruiter, or through a trusted contact; trial for about 6 months where possible. [116713e4f02a]
19. Peak-season protection: confirm every required 3PL/4PL document is signed for every region before peak trading begins (do this on a fixed pre-peak checklist, not during the event); sequence any acquisition due-diligence process around peak trading rather than through it, since a live deal can collide with a logistics failure. [ac76fdafcc3e]

**Finance / exit (Fogerty, part 2)**
20. Before targeting an exit, build cash flow and skill first; treat merit-based value drivers (recurring/contracted revenue, hard-to-win customers, demonstrated scale and growth trajectory) as the things to build deliberately, in that order, before shopping the business. [9c1c2bba079a] [0355e66068]
21. When assessing your own or a target's exit value, apply the source's rough multiple bands (see section 4) but explicitly discount for outlier assumptions and current market timing. [dc145902632]

**Founder mindset (Fogerty, part 2 — flagged as personal practice, not measurable business method)**
22. Use bounded exposure (a recorded, editable opportunity) to push through anxiety about a growth opportunity rather than declining it outright; rehearse the actual skill beforehand (source's team ran ~12 practice pitches over about a week before their Shark Tank taping). [0bccc8b217ae]
23. Read your own or a case-study founder's track record by the specific skill each failure taught, not by the headline product outcome. [b7325aad598c]
24. Choose associates who raise your capability in your weak areas rather than critical ones; keep a set of close relationships where business is never discussed as a stabiliser. [f42f58122d64] [e68d1fc98494]
25. When presenting or pitching, reduce anxiety by not comparing your own scale/story to the audience's; build tolerance incrementally through lower-friction formats (source used camera-off Zoom before addressing his team live). [dd33fa2d9cdb]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- Creator program health (Bloom): check whether performance is being judged by views/content volume/blended signals, not a tracked link; if someone is asking "what's the ROAS on this creator," that is a signal the zero-attribution approach has not been agreed internally. [6a3c3dd9f14f]
- Creator fee sanity check: recompute the target CPM from your current Meta CPM before every new round of flat-fee offers; if you have not checked your Meta CPM recently, the $2 anchor is stale. [a5e7b7863c43]
- GWP/freebie check: pull AOV and repeat-purchase rate for orders with vs without the freebie; if there's no lift, the freebie is a pure margin cost with no data to justify it. [9d911f4de73e]
- Restock-claim check: before sending "back in stock" messaging, confirm the SKU actually sold out; false scarcity is flagged as a trust and (in some markets) consumer-law risk. [b7d4878acc8a]
- Peak-season check: before a Black Friday/Cyber Monday period, confirm every 3PL/4PL region has signed paperwork on file; treat a single missing signature as a full stock-seizure risk, based on this source's repeated failure. [ac76fdafcc3e]
- Exit-readiness check: before assuming a high multiple, check whether the business actually has recurring/contracted revenue, hard-to-win customers, and demonstrated growth — the source says outlier multiples require this foundation, not just a return of category. [dc145902632] [0355e66068]
- CEO-hire fit check: list the archetype the business needs, then check the candidate against it directly rather than against your own profile — the source flags "hiring your opposite" as an active mistake he nearly made. [116713e4f02a]
- Founder skill-gap check: after a fast product win, list what actually drove it (timing, product, one channel) versus what skills are still missing (systems, delegation, brand); the source's own repeated failure mode was skipping this check. [3ae237242a99]
