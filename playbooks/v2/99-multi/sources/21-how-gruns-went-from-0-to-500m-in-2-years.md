## 1. What this source is for

Gives a creative-strategy pattern ("product repositioning") for scaling ad spend on a single hero SKU by marketing it as the solution to many unrelated problems, instead of launching new products. Useful for acquisition creative planning, LLM-assisted angle generation, landing-page-to-ad congruence, and the product-portfolio/cash-flow trade-off between repositioning and new launches. Not a source for ROAS, bidding or attribution mechanics (folder note explicitly sends that to 11-ADC).

## 2. Principles

1. **Product repositioning over product launches.** Grow spend on one SKU by marketing it to entirely different customer avatars with distinct problem-solution angles, rather than shipping new physical products. Best suited to multi-benefit products (supplements, functional goods); the narrator's own opinion is that it works less well for narrow, single-function products (e.g., stand-alone creatine). [b59db770ce4e]
2. **Every angle has a finite TAM and a spend ceiling.** A single angle can only absorb ad spend up to the size of the audience that actually has that problem; past that, frequency spikes. Stacking several angle-specific TAMs is what lets one product absorb far more total spend than any single angle could. No numeric ceiling is given anywhere in the source. [b59db770ce4e][b2da5bc185dd]
3. **Use unexpected, specific personas, not reworded demographics.** The test for a real repositioning angle is that it targets a genuinely different audience (e.g. "high-performance entrepreneurs" vs. "puberty-age teens" vs. "gamers"), not the same group with a different headline. [2ed7b60540d7]
4. **Match each angle to a congruent, dedicated landing page.** Hyper-specific ad traffic must land on a page built entirely around that one persona's problem, not a generic homepage or PDP, or the narrative breaks and conversion drops. [00e148e0211]
5. **Map the product's real benefit pillars onto audience segments.** List every functional benefit the product can credibly claim, and build a creative pillar per benefit so almost any visitor finds one pillar they care about. Only advertise a pillar the formulation genuinely supports. [e92050bf8727]
6. **Repositioning protects cash flow and inventory versus new launches.** New launches risk turning cash into unsold inventory (requiring heavy discounting to recover it) and add 3PL/operational complexity, especially selling internationally. A single, heavily-repositioned SKU keeps inventory concentrated. [8edaaa4485cc]
7. **Large-assortment categories don't need this as much.** Fashion, furniture and drop-ship catalogs with dozens or hundreds of SKUs can open new spend by rotating to a fresh SKU instead of re-engineering one item's positioning; repositioning is still "a good exercise" for them but less necessary. [7a75df0c27c6]

## 3. Procedures, by domain

### Product
1. List every functional benefit the product can credibly support (source example, a multi-ingredient supplement: gut health, energy, immunity, recovery, beauty, cognition — 6 pillars). [e92050bf8727]
2. For each candidate pillar, confirm the formulation actually supports the claim before building ads around it; do not advertise a benefit the product can't back up. [e92050bf8727]
3. Before adding a new SKU, weigh it against repositioning the current SKU: a new launch costs cash upfront and adds inventory/3PL load; repositioning does not. Choose repositioning when the product is broad enough to carry multiple angles; choose new launches/SKU rotation when the category naturally rewards variety (fashion, furniture). [8edaaa4485cc][7a75df0c27c6]

### Creative (LLM-assisted angle generation)
This is the source's most concrete procedure — an LLM prompt the narrator built and ran live. [cd1f18839b86][2ed7b60540d7]
1. Set the LLM's role: "act as a DTC growth expert and creative director" whose goal is to split one product's identity into multiple distinct angles for entirely different customer avatars, similar to how a brand like Gruns markets the same product to unrelated audiences.
2. Give context: paste the brand name and website URL so the model analyzes the real product and its stated benefits.
3. Ask for 10 to 15 distinct angles. Require each angle to output: the angle, the target audience, a hook/headline, and the logic mechanism (why it works).
4. Instruct the model explicitly to avoid generic angles and to write in direct-response style. If the product allows, require at least one angle per relevant health/benefit concern.
5. Run the prompt on a "thinking" model, not a fast default, so the model reasons through the problem rather than pattern-matching a quick answer. [verify UI: which current models are "thinking" models]
6. Run the same prompt in both Gemini and ChatGPT — the narrator says the two often return meaningfully different angles, effectively doubling the idea pool.
7. If one concern recurs in nearly every output and is off-brand or irrelevant (the narrator's example: GLP-1 appearing even for unrelated products), remove or exclude it from the prompt and re-run.
8. For each surviving angle, plan a distinct shoot/aesthetic — a "cinema" angle, a gaming angle and a Gen-Z TikTok angle should look visibly different from each other even though the underlying product is identical.

### Paid media
1. When a previously-scaling angle stops absorbing more daily spend or frequency starts climbing, first diagnose it as an angle-TAM ceiling, not a broken ad or a bidding problem. [b2da5bc185dd]
2. Reason from the demand pool: ask how many people in-market genuinely have the specific problem the angle targets (the source's examples: GLP-1 side-effect sufferers, "fiber believers," gut-issue sufferers, daily-vitamin seekers) — that pool size, not the ad itself, is usually the limiter.
3. Instead of forcing the saturated angle further, add a new, distinct repositioning pillar aimed at a different persona to open a new spend reservoir.
4. Treat total spendable capacity on the product as roughly the sum of each pillar's individual ceiling; keep layering new pillars as each one saturates. ### Offers / landing pages
1. For every repositioning angle running as an ad, build a dedicated landing page or advertorial that speaks only to that persona's single problem — never route that ad to a generic homepage or generic PDP. [00e148e0211]
2. Keep the page's narrative continuous with the ad's hook: if the ad says "this fixes your zinc-related fertility issue," the page should open on that same problem, not immediately introduce the product as a 20-benefit multivitamin.
3. Structure example seen in the source: an educational page built around one persona's problem (e.g. "7 Benefits of GLP-1 Support"), the offer placed further down, product presented last.
4. After running several angle/page pairs, identify the one or two core personas or angles that actually convert, and invest further landing-page/creative work into molding pages around those winners rather than spreading effort evenly.

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check:** an angle's daily spend has plateaued or frequency is climbing.
  **How:** review spend and frequency trend for that ad set/angle. `[needs brand data: your own frequency and spend-by-angle history]`
  **Good:** frequency stable, spend still growing without a jump in frequency.
  **Bad:** frequency climbing while spend growth stalls — read this as an angle-TAM ceiling.
  **Fix:** launch a new persona/pillar angle rather than tweaking creative or bids on the saturated one. [b2da5bc185dd]

- **Check:** a hyper-specific angle gets good ad engagement (hook lands) but weak on-site conversion.
  **How:** check what landing page that ad's traffic actually hits.
  **Good:** dedicated page/advertorial that opens on the same specific problem the ad named.
  **Bad:** traffic lands on a generic homepage or a broad, multi-benefit PDP.
  **Fix:** build a custom landing page or advertorial dedicated to that one angle's persona and problem. [00e148e0211]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Diagnosing a plateaued or frequency-spiking angle as a creative or bidding failure instead of an audience-size (TAM) ceiling. [b2da5bc185dd]
- Sending hyper-specific ad traffic to a generic homepage or broad PDP: the source's own walk-through example is a zinc/fertility ad leading to a $64, 20-benefit multivitamin PDP — the buyer thinks "I'll just buy zinc" and does not convert. [00e148e0211]
- Mistaking demographic reslicing (e.g., "toddler parents" reworded) for real repositioning; a genuine angle must reach a different TAM pool, not the same one restated. [2ed7b60540d7]
- Letting one LLM-suggested concern (the source's example: GLP-1) dominate every generated output when it is off-brand, irrelevant or compliance-sensitive, without filtering it out of the prompt. [cd1f18839b86]
- Assuming every business needs aggressive repositioning; large-assortment categories can usually open new spend by rotating to a new SKU instead. [7a75df0c27c6]
