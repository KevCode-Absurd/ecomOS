## 1. What this source is for

A rebuttal script against 5 ecommerce metric and growth myths: treating paid media as a retention channel, treating "50% of revenue from Klaviyo" as a benchmark, treating ROAS as the north-star KPI, treating scaling as just spending more, and treating retention as an email-flows problem. It gives an operator a mental checklist for which KPIs to trust (ones that hold up in every context) and where self-reported ROAS and Klaviyo-attribution numbers are likely lying to them. It does not give step-by-step platform instructions; it is diagnostic and opinion-based, meant to change what an operator measures and believes, not to walk them through a specific tool.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Myth 1: paid media is a retention channel
1. **Paid media is rarely incremental when targeting existing customers.** The host says he is "a strong believer that paid media is not incremental for targeting existing customers" except at very low budget allocation, and that "people significantly overspend" on it. Condition: this is the host's stated belief plus his audit pattern, not a controlled study. [722e2752eec7] [53cff4b8747]
2. **The core mistake is not segmenting new vs. existing customer spend.** Many brands "don't delineate at all between new customer and existing customer ad spend," so they cannot see that reported ROAS is being propped up by people who would have bought anyway. Fix: index KPIs against new-customer revenue, not total revenue, because returning-customer revenue is mostly a function of product/model/industry, not incremental ad spend. [722e2752eec7] [9497f75a4f1]
3. **Existing-customer ad frequency is a fast, informal diagnostic.** The host pulls "frequency on existing customers... every 30 days" in every audit and commonly sees 20 to 100 exposures/month; he offers "10" as a gut-check reasonable level, explicitly not a benchmark. [722e2752eec7] [ca90d7c1ca85]
4. **A frequency cut is a hypothesis, not a guaranteed win.** Moving frequency down (e.g., cutting budget ~90% after finding 100x/month) "might" cost some incremental revenue in the 10-to-20 range, "most of the time it isn't" incremental, but confirm with a test rather than assuming. [722e2752eec7]
5. **Confirm with a platform conversion-lift (geo-holdout) test, not with reported ROAS.** Example given: on "a multi-nine-figure CPG brand" with an existing-customer base bigger than any Australian brand the host works with, a conversion-lift test found the existing-customer spend "wasn't incremental at an unbelievably low ad spend" — offered as an extreme case showing even big, mature audiences can test as non-incremental. [722e2752eec7]
6. **The right amount of existing-customer spend depends on your category's repurchase cycle.** High-ticket furniture ("once every 5 years") has poor cohort lift, so the host advises against spending on people who already bought. Fast-moving consumables (FMCG) have frequent repeat purchases, so a small amount of spend can be justified on narrow segments the brand's owned channels can't reach anymore — the example given is people who purchased and then unsubscribed. [c1b04bc58a5a]
7. **Even "smart" re-acquisition segments should be tested, not assumed.** The host notes that even the purchased-and-unsubscribed segment "we put it in a lift and did nothing" in one case, but calls it still "smart" relative to blindly targeting all existing customers. [c1b04bc58a5a]
8. **A second, related trap: hammering your own high-intent-but-unconverted organic audience.** Angus describes brands with organic followers who haven't purchased; paid spend "hammers those people for the next 3 to 4 months" until they convert, producing a short-term high ROAS that "dries up" once that pool is exhausted — he treats this the same as retargeting existing customers, just "tons harder to navigate" because these people haven't bought yet and so are plausibly more incremental than a cold audience. [706b85f45d0] [9497f75a4f1]

### 2.2 Myth 2: 50% of revenue from Klaviyo is the benchmark
9. **The "50% of revenue from Klaviyo" claim is called a marketing gimmick, not a rule.** The host reacts to an Instagram ad pushing this figure as "leaving profit on the table" if you're below it, and calls the underlying logic "the dumbest metric to make a guarantee by," because the right share depends heavily on business model (his example contrast: furniture vs. supplements). [0c79d63f1e16]
10. **Klaviyo's default attribution is essentially a view-through metric.** Described as "5-day open, 5-day click"; the open component "is like a view-through metric" that credits an email for a purchase a high-intent, likely-returning customer was going to make anyway. `[verify UI: confirm current Klaviyo default attribution window]` [0c79d63f1e16]
11. **Klaviyo-attributed revenue is still a workable directional north star, with a caveat.** If it's rising, "you're reaching people with your emails who are converting" — a real correlation with performance — but it is not the definitive measure of retention success, and it should be split by new vs. returning customer, since email's impact on a new customer is "far less incremental" than on a returning one. [0c79d63f1e16]
12. **Optimizing for revenue-per-campaign is a specific, named mistake.** The host has spoken to brands whose "main KPI for Klaviyo is like revenue per campaign they send," and argues this just pushes brands to "flog emails" at people who were already likely to buy — functionally the same problem as an Advantage+ campaign aimed at repeat customers. [0c79d63f1e16]
13. **Chasing attribution/aggressive send cadence can actively damage the brand.** Any agency optimizing purely for Klaviyo attribution is "optimizing essentially for aggressiveness and cadence," touching customers as many times as possible before a likely purchase, which can do "a lot of damage to your brand... a lot of damage to your customer attention." [0c79d63f1e16]
14. **Retention's real drivers, in the order the host lists them: product quality, portfolio, repeatability, customer experience, competitive landscape, pricing, the economy — then communication (Klaviyo/email) on top.** Email is framed as an amplifier of those fundamentals ("a way to amplify all of the rest"), not a lever on its own. Good brands reportedly use it for community, loyalty/VIP programs and strong abandon-checkout sequences rather than pure revenue-per-send. [0005f05fcb3]

### 2.3 Myth 3: ROAS is the KPI that matters
15. **ROAS has 3 structural flaws, stated by the host.** (1) It can be skewed by existing customers; (2) it credits the last click, not what actually created the awareness or intent; (3) it is distorted by whichever attribution settings are in use. As a result, branded search, retargeting and TikTok-retargeting campaigns show artificially high ROAS, while genuinely awareness-driving spend (his example: TV) can show "no ROAS" even though "there is a return... it's unmeasurable" because credit flows downstream into, e.g., branded Google campaigns. [53cff4b8747] [706b85f45d0]
16. **This produces systematic budget misallocation toward the bottom of the funnel and toward existing customers**, because people fund what reports well rather than what is actually driving results, and "a lot of the times it's just fugazi because people use the wrong attribution settings." [53cff4b8747] [706b85f45d0]
17. **The host's test for whether a metric is trustworthy: does it hold up in every context?** He argues incrementality, net profit, customer lifetime value (CLTV) and new-customer acquisition cost pass this test in "every single context window," while ROAS and Klaviyo attributed revenue are "nuance," providing context in some situations but breaking, or failing to reflect net profit, in others. Demote the latter to supporting/context metrics rather than north-star KPIs. [53cff4b8747] [c2f447b2849]
18. **Where ROAS is still used, aim for the lowest ROAS you can acquire profitably at, not the highest.** The host cites unnamed "real killers" among US performance marketers who "are optimizing for the lowest ROAS possible" (i.e., the cheapest profitable customer acquisition cost), calling this the inverse of the instinct most people entering ecommerce have (chase a "good ROAS"). He also cites, as a mocking example, someone else's Instagram claim of a "300x ROAS" static ad for a client, to illustrate how meaningless an isolated high-ROAS claim is. [53cff4b8747]
19. **Repositioning executives away from ROAS is described as slow and political, especially at large/older retailers.** Worked illustrative example: an owner sees "Google's giving us an 8x, Meta's giving us a 6x, Klaviyo's giving us a 60x" and concludes spend should shift toward Klaviyo and Google — missing that the Google number is "all brand" (bottom-funnel, high-intent) and the Klaviyo number is inflated attribution. The host frames this as typical of traditional P&L-driven leadership structures where marketing reports numbers up to finance, and says small businesses "just can't" afford to make this mistake "or you go broke." [53cff4b8747]
20. **Diagnostic pattern: healthy-looking ROAS with flat topline revenue means check segmentation before anything else.** If the reported number is "fine" (his example: 4-5x) but revenue "hasn't moved... plateaued year on year," check first whether new vs. existing customer spend is even separated; the likely cause is spend concentrated on existing/high-intent buyers producing short-term ROAS that dries up as that pool is exhausted. Reframe around new-customer revenue, not total revenue, to see the real acquisition trend. [9497f75a4f1]

### 2.4 Myth 4: scaling is just increasing spend
21. **Increasing spend is not the same as growing the business.** The host (a marketer who "runs my own ads") says simply spending more would make him "the fastest scaling person in the world" by that logic, but it "doesn't mean you're making any more money." [7ea79f0c239]
22. **The industry narrative has shifted from growth-at-all-costs (2020-2022-era) to profit/cash-flow focus**, coinciding with the end of the era when businesses (in ecommerce and beyond) were exiting for large revenue multiples because PE firms were paying for growth. [7ea79f0c239]
23. **"Just bump your budget" advice was arguably reasonable in that earlier window for small-to-mid brands, but is not reliable now.** The host concedes coaching groups that pushed "just spend more" gave some businesses the confidence to raise budgets they were too timid to raise on their own (illustrative example: a brand reluctant to go past $200/day being told to try $500/day by a coach citing 2 competitors already doing it), and it sometimes worked because people simply lacked the guts to test a higher budget. But this only works if revenue returns are watched tightly, and blind budget bumps now are described as risky. [7ea79f0c239]
24. **Coaching-group-era advice is blamed for real financial damage at some brands.** The host says these groups "did amazing things" but also "did a lot of damage," citing brands with poor P&Ls after "your not scaling hard enough" advice was taken too far. He names a commentator, transcribed as "Paul Wy," as an example of the newer profit-first correction, attributing to him a rule that if you "can't take a healthy dividend and pay yourself a good salary and... not doing 20% net profit," the business is "a joke" — this is presented as a third party's stated standard, not the host's own tested figure `[web check: confirm the name transcribed as "Paul Wy" and his actual stated position/figures]`. [7ea79f0c239]
25. **Scaling decisions should be judged against net profit, CLTV and cash flow, not revenue or ROAS alone**, and cross-channel reported returns (e.g., a much higher attributed number from email than from paid) should be treated skeptically since they can mislead resourcing decisions at the executive/owner level. [7ea79f0c239]

### 2.5 Myth 5: retention is a flows problem
26. **Retention is downstream of the business, not of email.** Email flows, campaigns and other owned-media communication sit downstream of product, product quality, the economy and customer experience; flows can influence whether the customer experience is good or bad, but they are not the root cause of retention problems. [0005f05fcb3]
27. **The most common misdiagnosis: brands with deep retention problems go to an "email agency" asking to redo flows to bring customers back.** The host's counter: "if you have no customers coming back now, extra marketing is not going to make customers come back." A healthy business should already have some portion of customers returning on their own because the product is good; if that baseline doesn't exist, more email activity won't create it. [0005f05fcb3]
28. **A concrete flows mistake: skipping product-experience content in favor of upsell/cross-sell.** For complex or instruction-dependent products (needs explanation of how/when to use), many brands' flows go straight for an upsell or cross-sell instead of explaining the product, which the host frames as actively hurting the customer experience the flow could have improved. [0005f05fcb3]
29. **Closing caveat, stated by the host directly:** despite the critical tone toward Klaviyo throughout, he explicitly does not want it read as "Klaviyo is a stupid channel" — it's "an important channel for all of your own media communication," and the objection is specifically to which metrics get optimized and trusted, not to using email/retention platforms at all. [0005f05fcb3]

## 3. Procedures

The source gives few clean numbered how-to sequences; it is a conversational rebuttal, not a manual. Steps below are extracted and grouped by domain for folder routing. Where the source names a platform screen but not exact clicks, it is flagged `[verify UI]` rather than invented.

### 3.1 Paid media / attribution (route toward ad deployment or measurement/attribution folders)
1. Split ad account reporting into new-customer and existing-customer spend/revenue as separate lines; do not let them blend into one campaign total. [722e2752eec7]
2. Set new-customer revenue, not total revenue, as the primary KPI you index against. [722e2752eec7] [9497f75a4f1]
3. Pull the existing-customer audience's ad frequency over the trailing 30 days in the ad platform. [722e2752eec7] [ca90d7c1ca85]
4. Compare that frequency to a rough sanity check (source's reference point: around 10/month is "probably reasonable"; 20-100/month is what audits commonly find and is a signal of likely overspend). [722e2752eec7] [ca90d7c1ca85]
5. If frequency is high, treat a budget cut as a hypothesis: reduce existing-customer spend, then confirm impact with a platform conversion-lift / geo-holdout experiment (source refers to "the experiments tab" in Meta) rather than trusting ROAS before or after the change. `[verify UI: current location/name of Meta's conversion-lift/geo-holdout experiment tool]` [722e2752eec7] [706b85f45d0]
6. Before allocating any existing-customer budget, estimate the category's natural repurchase cycle (long cycle -> cut existing-customer spend; short/frequent cycle -> a small, segment-specific allocation may be defensible). [c1b04bc58a5a]
7. If you do spend on existing customers, scope it to narrow segments owned channels can no longer reach (source's example: purchased-and-unsubscribed), not the whole existing-customer pool. [c1b04bc58a5a]
8. When reported ROAS looks healthy but topline revenue is flat or plateaued year over year, first check whether new/existing spend is segmented at all; if not, that is the likely explanation, not a real efficiency gain. [9497f75a4f1]

### 3.2 Email / retention (Klaviyo) (route toward retention or lifecycle-marketing folders)
1. Pull Klaviyo's attributed revenue and split it by new vs. returning customer before using it in any KPI conversation. [0c79d63f1e16]
2. Note which attribution window Klaviyo is using (source states the default as 5-day open / 5-day click) before comparing your numbers to anyone else's claims. `[verify UI: current Klaviyo attribution settings]` [0c79d63f1e16]
3. Stop optimizing individual campaigns for "revenue per campaign sent"; if that is currently a tracked KPI, replace it as the primary send-level success measure. [0c79d63f1e16]
4. Before redesigning flows to "fix retention," check whether any customers return on their own without marketing intervention; if that baseline is absent, do not expect a flows rebuild to create it. [0005f05fcb3]
5. Where flows do get rebuilt, prioritize content that improves product experience (usage instructions, timing, complexity) ahead of upsell/cross-sell placement for complex or instruction-dependent products. [0005f05fcb3]

### 3.3 Measurement / KPI selection (route toward measurement and analytics)
1. List current top-level KPIs and test each against every business model, time window and channel you can think of. [c2f447b2849]
2. Keep metrics that survive that test as top-level (source's examples: incrementality, net profit, CLTV, new-customer acquisition cost). [c2f447b2849]
3. Demote metrics that break in some contexts to supporting/context indicators (source's examples: ROAS, Klaviyo attributed revenue). [c2f447b2849]
4. When a context-dependent metric moves sharply, investigate the cause before reacting or reallocating budget on it. [c2f447b2849]
5. Revisit this KPI list periodically, since platform attribution behavior changes over time. [c2f447b2849]

### 3.4 Scaling / budget decisions (route toward growth/financial planning)
1. Before raising ad budget, define the profit metric you are protecting (net profit, CLTV, cash flow), not just a revenue or ROAS target. [7ea79f0c239]
2. If you raise budget, watch revenue and profit returns closely in the following weeks and be ready to reverse the change. [7ea79f0c239]
3. Treat cross-channel "reported return" comparisons (e.g., a channel showing a much higher attributed number) as a prompt to investigate attribution quality, not as an automatic signal to shift resourcing. [7ea79f0c239]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

- **Check:** Is new-customer spend/revenue tracked separately from existing-customer spend/revenue? **How:** Pull ad account reporting split by audience type. **Good:** clear separation exists. **Bad:** everything blended into one campaign/account total ("most brands don't delineate at all"). **Next step:** rebuild reporting to separate the two before drawing any KPI conclusions. [722e2752eec7] [9497f75a4f1]
- **Check:** What is existing-customer ad frequency over the last 30 days? **How:** pull the frequency metric for that audience in the ad platform. **Good:** roughly single digits to ~10. **Bad:** 20-100+ (source's common finding). **Next step:** treat as a candidate for budget reduction, then confirm with a lift test; don't just cut and assume it's a win, since 10-20 "might" still be incremental. [ca90d7c1ca85]
- **Check:** Does reported ROAS match the topline revenue trend? **How:** compare account-level ROAS to overall revenue over the same months. **Good:** they move together. **Bad:** ROAS "healthy" (e.g. 4-5x) while revenue is flat or plateaued year over year. **Next step:** check segmentation first (new vs. existing, high-intent audiences); this pattern usually means spend is harvesting people who would buy anyway. [9497f75a4f1]
- **Check:** Is Klaviyo-attributed revenue split by new vs. returning customer? **How:** segment the attribution report. **Good:** split exists and is monitored separately. **Bad:** one blended "attributed revenue" number reported up, especially if it's being chased as revenue-per-campaign. **Next step:** stop optimizing sends purely for that number; validate real incrementality if budget decisions ride on it. [0c79d63f1e16]
- **Check:** For any KPI you currently report, does it hold up across different business models, time windows and channels? **How:** mentally (or on paper) stress-test each KPI against those 3 axes. **Good:** it stays meaningful (incrementality, net profit, CLTV, new-customer acquisition cost, per the source). **Bad:** it breaks or stops correlating with profit in some context (ROAS, Klaviyo attributed revenue, per the source). **Next step:** demote the ones that break to supporting/context metrics. [c2f447b2849]
- **Check:** Before rebuilding email flows to fix retention, do any customers already come back on their own without marketing prompts? **How:** look at repeat-purchase rate without attributing it to any specific flow. **Good:** a meaningful baseline of unprompted repeat purchasers exists; flows can then amplify it. **Bad:** near-zero organic repeat purchase. **Next step:** if bad, treat retention as a product/business-model problem before investing further in flows. [0005f05fcb3]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Not segmenting ad account reporting or budget by new vs. existing customer, so overspend on existing customers is invisible. [722e2752eec7] [9497f75a4f1]
- Treating a healthy blended ROAS as proof of performance when topline revenue is flat or declining. [53cff4b8747] [9497f75a4f1]
- Blindly targeting all existing customers with paid media regardless of category repurchase cycle. [c1b04bc58a5a]
- Trusting Klaviyo's default attribution (5-day open/click) as a real measure of retention or incremental impact, especially for new customers. [0c79d63f1e16]
- Treating "50% of revenue from Klaviyo" (or any single attribution-share figure) as a universal benchmark to chase. [0c79d63f1e16]
- Optimizing individual email sends for revenue-per-campaign, which drives overly aggressive cadence and can damage brand and customer attention. [0c79d63f1e16]
- Redoing email flows as the default fix for a retention problem that is actually rooted in product, pricing, or customer experience. [0005f05fcb3]
- Skipping product-usage/experience content in flows in favor of pushing upsell or cross-sell. [0005f05fcb3]
- Assuming higher ad spend automatically means a bigger or better business, without checking net profit or CLTV. [7ea79f0c239]
- Letting executives make budget-allocation decisions off raw cross-channel ROAS comparisons (e.g., "Klaviyo gives us 60x") without understanding what each platform's number actually measures. [53cff4b8747]
- Adding more attribution complexity or tools as a response to distrustful metrics, instead of fixing what is measured and how it's used (implied throughout, though the source does not name specific extra tools to avoid). [c2f447b2849]
