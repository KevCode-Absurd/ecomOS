## 2. Principles

1. **Paid ads buy attention; email converts it cheaply.** Cold traffic converts at about 1% or less (store-wide 2 to 3% including repeat buyers). Capture 10% of visitors by email, then send content at "a fraction of a cent" instead of paying to retarget. The source says it is "not anti paid ads": email needs ads to grow the list. Condition: applies when most traffic is paid and cold. [12ae02fd4ccd] [36e7bc2aac2f] [e4411105b23e]
2. **Buyers need many exposures before they trust you (the "trust threshold").** Content moves a prospect through 4 levels: do not know or trust, intrigued, interested, ready to buy. Brand B seen 8 times across channels beats brand A seen 3 times in ads. This is a model, not a measured count. [76c9f6876c1e] [6a7d3a3bd48a] [af45850fd4fc]
3. **New subscribers are worth the most ("honeymoon phase").** From opt-in to the first or second purchase interest is highest. Prioritize list growth and fast welcome content. Qualitative claim. [b8c17a6d4d2d]
4. **Nail the basics before advanced work.** Basics carry "80% of the results". Build the core flows and a strong popup before cross-sell splits or hyper-segmentation. Source test: is the cart flow about 8 emails and is the popup above 3%? If not, fix those first. [05d68e6eea8b] [b88c44dec23c] [31dde167599f]
5. **Flows and campaigns should each bring about 50% of email revenue.** 60/40 or 40/60 is fine. Flows at 30% and campaigns at 70% means the flows need work. Heuristic. [0de6758fd7b5] [8dcc18667354]
6. **The offer is the most important part of a popup.** Copy matters much less than the offer. Test: "Would I trade my email for this?" [9eebdb581427] [60f8b169fd9a]
7. **Ask for one input per step.** Email first, SMS on a separate next step. Never both on screen 1. [7ea9228eeda8] [2a3063dcd723]
8. **Bigger, simpler popups win.** About 75% of the screen, under 10 words of copy, the offer as the largest element. The source says "bigger forms always win" in their tests. Trade-off: more intrusive. [7ea9228eeda8] [ff4c8404916f] [7bf46bd3662b]
9. **Break the reflex close.** Shoppers hit the top-right X by reflex. Hide or move the X and add a visible "No thanks" button so they must decide. Keep a clear exit. [397f14a8dca9]
10. **Separate cart and checkout abandonment.** Added to Cart and Checkout Started are different events. Build 2 flows with the same content and different triggers. [8d43aac07129] [d962429e9e36] [b6389565ed83]
11. **Recovery flows must match the decision cycle.** 6 or more emails over 1 to 2 weeks (about 10 days), not 1 or 2 emails. [ed0f5c1eb0f6]
12. **In recovery emails the item does the selling.** Showing the exact abandoned item "carries 80% of the sales" (presenter estimate). Copy only removes objections: shipping, use, reviews, FAQs. Assume a wary 2024 buyer who compares brands and has been burned by late shipping. [abb2fb64e79c] [8640fed0045c] [099c7444116a]
13. **Hold discounts back in flows.** Browse and cart flows start without a discount. Discounts go only to people who never bought (or not in 60 to 90 days), late in the flow. Past buyers get a founder support email instead. Place urgency and risk reversal where the remaining objection sits, not in email 1. [f0dfe6920491] [2fd76c2c2442] [b9c909970c5b] [84ab96a56baa]
14. **Personal founder emails often perform best.** Plain text, first name, mission, support offer, one link. Used in welcome, cart, post-purchase and winback. Needs a real founder and a real reply inbox. [287733c58cdd] [38c0bd0fee2b]
15. **Send 3 to 4 campaigns a week, mostly non-discount.** Source tested "2 per day to 2 per month": 3 to 4 gave the highest revenue and lowest unsubscribes. 1 to 2 a week is forgotten; 5 to 7 raises unsubscribes and complaints and revenue plateaus. Condition: only if content is good ("if your emails suck, 3 to 4 is too much") and only to engaged segments. [dfbf925e6f5d] [a91ac0807b44] [d435b73cfe27] [98e2eee7c689]
16. **Needy discount content creates sales resistance.** Constant "buy or die" content annoys the list, wears out discounts and makes the brand look like it begs. Value content positions it as sought after. Opinion. [dfbf925e6f5d] [4a80d22c069f]
17. **Education plus positioning equals sales without discounts.** Teach a problem, then a process, then position the product as the solution. [e7738e173006]
18. **One email, one micro-topic, one takeaway.** An email gets 3 to 5 seconds. Ask: "What is the one thing I want the customer to know after this email?" [998246dfc602] [99679e755f67]
19. **Frequency builds an opening habit.** Regular good sends create habitual opens and the exposure effect; sending less can lower open rate. [6927559464b4]
20. **Use campaigns as the test lab for flows.** Campaigns give 24-hour data; flow tests are slow and hard to track. Port winning campaigns into flows. [18ba82281157] [debbcf824284] [1b02d04b4fff]
21. **Send more content to more people before segmenting.** "3 to 4 broad campaigns to 50,000 beat 1 to 2 targeted campaigns to 10,000, 99 times out of 100." Presenter experience. [24e40adf1b4f]
22. **Never send campaigns to the whole list.** Send to an engaged segment. Exceptions: maybe Black Friday or a massive announcement, and 1 campaign a month to the wider list after recovery. [e385d9458d21]
23. **Deliverability is a sender credit score driven by engagement.** Good = primary tab, middle = promotions, bad = spam. Fix content first, then reputation by sending only to people who open. [61807f09ea38] [b7b58b63ae33]
24. **Subject lines set the frame for sales, not opens.** Opens should come from sender name, content and cadence. Judge subject line tests on placed orders and revenue. Opinion; he still writes them carefully. [5143f6e2959a] [0a74cf1bac46] [c4cf02ddd233]
25. **Write for the skim.** Avoid text blocks, bold main points, 1 to 2 mobile scrolls max, at least 1 infographic per email. [e5a610bd2f19] [ddcf74804625]
26. **Vary emails and match the brand's real world.** Identical templates every send lose attention. Match email imagery, fonts and voice to the site and Instagram. [a744284f4af9] [f4b51595abf4] [9b7f9bd54fc3]
27. **Every email needs a clear, early, big button.** First button above the fold, large, thumb-reachable; one decision per email even with many buttons. The source also says "you don't need a button at the top every single time; mix it up". [ca47b8d3f740] [ccc4b7310f94] [8d705e1b1a26]

### 3.2 Popup: the 5-step build
1. **Establish the offer.** [9eebdb581427] [60f8b169fd9a]
   - AOV under $100: percent off (at $50 AOV, "10% off" sounds better than "$5 off").
   - AOV over $100: dollars off. Example: $500 AOV, "5% off" sounds weak, "$25 off" is the same money; "we've tested this".
   - Other offers: mystery discount, free gift with order, free shipping (even if shipping is already free), monthly giveaway entry, free guide (e.g. "how to install this car part"), early access (must be concrete, not vague).
   - Weigh margin against opt-in: e.g. $10 off gets 4%, $15 off gets 12%; the extra $5 may be worth it (hypothetical). Judge offers also on how subscribers convert later.
   - `[needs brand data: AOV, gross margin, current opt-in rate]`
2. **Choose the form type.** 5 types. Start with classic vs micro-commit as the first test. His favorites: quiz, micro-commit, classic. [92626e4998da] [0419dcd84c79]
   - Classic: straight to the email field.
   - Micro-commit: step 1 asks "Do you want 10% off your first order?" with Yes / No thanks; Yes shows the email step. Reason: commitment bias.
   - Quiz: answer a question to "earn" the discount; tag the answer (e.g. interested in scars) for later emails. Popular on Amped.
   - Spin to win: "tacky", "makes you look cheap", "converts like crazy"; the wheel always lands on the top prize (Attentive example, 20%).
   - Uncover your discount: pick a box to reveal the offer.
3. **Create the form** in Klaviyo or Amped (3.3 to 3.5).
4. **Run the checklist** (3.6).
5. **A/B test, monitor, iterate** (3.7). Expect a zig-zag path: 2.5%, 3.3%, 4.1%, 3.7% and so on; the 8.75% case form was the 5th or 6th version. [1c9b788208ca]

### 3.6 Popup pre-launch checklist [ff4c8404916f]
- 1 input per step; email and SMS never together.
- Delay 4 to 12 seconds.
- Loads instantly, no lag (compress images; no image on mobile).
- Covers at least 75% of the screen.
- Under 10 words of copy.
- Offer is the largest element.
- No visible X: a close-form button, an unclear X, or X on the left.
- Close works on every step, including success.
"You can get away with one being a little different."

### 3.7 Popup A/B tests, in priority order [b5dc319521e1]
Cadence: "at least weekly"; elsewhere "one every couple of days". +1 point of opt-in on 10,000 visitors = 100 more subscribers.
1. Offer (highest lever). Include a mystery offer. Judge opt-in and later purchase rate.
2. Form type: popup vs full page vs fly-out, and features within a type.
3. Close functionality: button vs X; placement left, centered, off-center.
4. Time delay: pick the delay that gives the most total submits, not the highest rate. A 5% form at 4 seconds can beat a 10% form at 30 seconds. Start 4 vs 12 seconds, then close the gap.
5. Photo content and placement (including as background on mobile).
6. Copy: short vs longer; "Unlock" vs "You've got" vs "Claim". Small lever.
7. Colors: simpler is better.

### 3.9 Welcome flow [2d5485f41fc3] [3b8a84dc1d94] [5b03b18152ec]
Setup:
1. Trigger: subscribes to the list the popup writes to. Recheck whenever you add a form or list; a mismatched list means the flow never fires.
2. Filter: Placed Order zero times since starting this flow. Optional stricter filter: zero times over all time, so past buyers never enter. Buyers exit to post-purchase and the campaign list. Card caveat: for high-frequency consumables, excluding all past buyers may be wrong.
3. Delays: 1 to 2 days between emails; 2 to 3 if cautious; he "errs on the side of more emails".
4. Length: 7 to 14 days for typical AOV. High AOV (e.g. $5,000) can run over 2 months. `[needs brand data: AOV and typical days to first purchase]`
5. Deliver the welcome discount. He argues it pays back through list value and names Ridge, Dr Squatch and AG1 as brands that do it.
6. Remind the discount in every email ("my biggest pet peeve" when missing).
7. Keep each email under about 100 words, readable in 3 seconds; readers spend 5 to 7 seconds at most. [b0ac0c5d9d9b]

Emails:
1. **Strong intro.** Welcome plus discount code and Shop Now button at the very top, no scroll. Header photo with brand look. Brief brand headline (example Alpen Rock: "Discover conscious luxury"; he says about 10% read it). 1 social proof line, 1 to 2 USPs, discount and button, bestsellers, buttons at the bottom too. "Welcome to the fam" example. [1fef69d7fc18]
2. **Brand story.** Founding story in about 2 sentences, mission, a real face (founder, couple, team), social proof sprinkled in ("100,000 five-star reviews"), discount above the button. ### 3.10 Site abandon flow [75b807a7abce]
1. Trigger: Active on Site (visits, views no product).
2. Filters (exit): Viewed Product 0 times, Added to Cart 0, Started Checkout 0, Placed Order 0 since starting this flow; has not been in this flow in the last 30 days.
3. Delay: 2 to 4 hours ("found to be the best"; test). A later test found 4 hours beat 30 minutes on placed-order rate (see 3.25).
4. 1 email: "Need a helping hand?" / "Lost in choices? Let us point you in the right direction." Bestsellers, light social proof, shop by category. Keep it simple.

### 3.11 Browse abandon flow [f0dfe6920491] [6b256b3baf2e]
1. Trigger: Viewed Product. Filters: Added to Cart 0, Checkout Started 0, Placed Order 0 since starting this flow; not in this flow in last 30 days.
2. Email 1 at 45 minutes (test it): simple reminder with a dynamic block of the exact viewed item and price ("Something catch your eye? We saved your gear", button at top, "You may also like" recommendations).
3. Then 1 to 2 days between emails. "Don't wait a week."
4. Email 2: brand info and risk reversal (example: "Full refund heal-fast guarantee, free 2-day shipping, trusted by doctors, easy returns"), other products.
5. Email 3: testimonials and social proof on the viewed item, dynamic block.
6. Email 4: discount, "flash 48-hour" framing. No discount earlier, to protect margin.
7. Email 5: closer, "expires in 24 hours".

### 3.19 The 29 campaign frameworks
Detailed 14:
1. **One benefit.** List 10+ benefits per product; 1 per email; about 2 a month covers 5 months; repeat. Examples: "Banish festive fogginess" (brain fog); Seed: "61% of people experience digestive discomfort... strains clinically shown to reduce bloating after 2 weeks". [a18c13f52100]
2. **One feature.** 1 ingredient, material or spec per email. Every protein says "gain muscle"; a feature like pre-digested whey sets you apart. "What's common sense to you may be unknown to your customer" (he credits Ogilvy). Examples: tamanu oil, "what we mean by instantized creatine". [277143b6f6c2]
3. **Single FAQ** ("one of my favorites", "most effective"). Source questions from support, reviews, "an alien from Mars" test, ChatGPT. Examples: "When can I expect creatine's effects? 7 to 14 days"; "Can whitening hurt your teeth?" then the damage-free product. [623d951e5d86]
4. **What's inside.** Bundles and consumables; mention free extras buyers don't know about (install guide, video guide, spare parts). Examples: AG1 welcome kit unboxing; "7 super ingredients" in a short list. [dac289376b1c]
5. **How it's made.** Steps from materials to factory to warehouse to shipment, or 1 highlighted step ("we run it through 3 filters"). Say it even if competitors do the same. Examples: beer "malt, mash, boil, ferment, bottle, age"; cultivated meat process. [532cdab329e6]
6. **How to use.** Steps from using it yourself; for simple items, "how to get the most out of" or "how to style". Also fits post-purchase. Examples: "How to pull a shot of espresso"; perfume tips. [532cdab329e6]
7. **Back in stock** (a favorite). Popularity plus urgency; usable for any new inventory, not only real sell-outs. Examples: "The Spidey Suds is back", "The full glass is back" in a new style. Do not fake sell-outs. [a1e654a1f091]
8. **Trending.** Frame future, not past: "hottest trend for summer 2024" beats "popular last summer". Link to a curated collection page. Lean on seasons ("Green with everything" in March). "You can't send too many of these." Example: checkered phone cases. [4e7b9fc24619]
9. **Reviews and testimonials.** Max 2 long reviews, or several short social comments with dates. Example greens brand: "Can't go a day without my greens". [78a2320e9eca]
10. **Us vs them.** A table; state facts, don't beg. [50ed5320f524]
11. **Myth vs fact.** 1 myth per email, rapid-fire, or a series ("worked well"). Condense copy. Example: sunscreen myth-busting. [255dda5b5409]
12. **Blog promo.** Sneak peek only; "sell the click"; button high; products lower down. Examples: olive oil benefits; he says both examples gave away too much. [0fc7fc3f1f45]
13. **Research study.** Google Scholar, read abstracts, pick 1 that busts a myth or backs an ingredient, connect it to the brand; "a couple of minutes". Example: "New study reveals the link between soft gums and inflammation". `[web check: verify any study before citing; never cite an AI-found study unread]` [fe4206a3d106]
14. **Obscure holiday.** checkiday.com, calendar pages or ChatGPT. Examples: Jameson on National Irish Coffee Day; AllTrails on National Hiking Day. [eb45bf2a9d5c]
Rapid-fire 15 [5960dbd3e3b0]: progress update; customer transformation; before and after; puzzles and riddles; note from the founder; gift guide (e.g. wedding season); treat yourself; media publication (news, Shark Tank); behind the scenes; tips and tricks; UGC; sneak peeks; Flashback Friday or Throwback Thursday; staff picks ("Tracy's favorite"); brand values.

### 3.21 Core segments (exact definitions) [c049ecf47935] [e385d9458d21] [29b075b3e1cf] [aa2e8130acf6] [766ca8aa5269] [fbb4bfea66bf] [68cf12bbb813] `[verify UI]`
Lists are static; segments are dynamic. Both sit under Lists and Segments in Klaviyo. [24e40adf1b4f]
1. **X-day engaged** (the main send segment; "90% of our emails go here"): can receive email marketing AND (opened email at least once in last X days OR clicked email at least once in last X days) AND bounced email 0 times over all time. The OR covers missed open tracking. Default X = 60. New to segmentation: start at 30 and widen to 60, 90, 120 as opens rise. Stay at 40%+ opens; widen when you reach 50% (opens drop to about 30% after widening, then climb again). Stop at 90 or 120.
2. **Unengaged:** can receive email marketing AND opened at least once over all time AND not opened or clicked in the last 90 days (120 also fine). Send about 1 campaign a month: usually a normal campaign sent to engaged plus unengaged, or a "take 10% to come back" email. Can suppress instead.
3. **High potential purchasers:** can receive email marketing AND placed order 0 times in last 30 days AND (active on site OR viewed product OR added to cart OR started checkout, at least once in last 30 days). Overlap is deliberate in case a trigger is broken. Send 1 to 2 extra emails a month, e.g. a sale you don't want to give everyone.
4. **VIP:** can receive email marketing AND placed order at least 5 times over all time. Optional: AND spent $500+. He prefers order count: after the 4th order you can say "1 order away from VIP and a free gift"; a spend goal can frustrate a buyer at $950 of $1,000 who buys a $20 item. Max 1 extra email a month; a VIP flow on entry (thanks, exclusive content, generous offer); a VIP offer at least once a quarter. `[needs brand data: order-count distribution]`
5. **Winback potential:** can receive email marketing AND placed order at least once in last 150 days AND placed order 0 times in last 90 days (a 60-day window). Trigger a flow on entry; discount later in that flow. Tune to the buy cycle: hot tubs = years; 30-serving supplements = start around day 45. About 1 extra email a month.
6. **Suppress:** opened email 0 times in last 180 days OR bounced at least once OR marked as spam at least once over all time. Review monthly and mark suppressed from the segment settings; saves Klaviyo cost (billed by profiles).
7. **Interested in X:** can receive email marketing AND (viewed item where category = X OR added to cart where category = X OR started checkout where collection = X OR placed order where collection = X), all over all time. Klaviyo-made metrics use category; Shopify-native metrics use collection. 1 to 2 extra emails a month for related launches.
8. **X cross-sell:** can receive email marketing AND placed order at least once over all time where collection = protein AND placed order 0 times over all time where collection = pre-workout. Flow on entry: "You bought protein; get more from your workout with pre-workout". White shoes to white socks example.
Other segment ideas from the same video:
- Email engagement: clicked in 60 days (most engaged), subscribed in last 30 days (fresh), bounced at least once (high risk), marked spam (suppress), no open in 120 days (churned; maybe Black Friday only). [e385d9458d21]
- Store engagement: viewed product 30 days no purchase (interested), added to cart or started checkout 30 days no purchase (high likelihood), placed order (recent buyers), 5+ orders (VIP), viewed 3+ products no purchase (window shoppers). Use as a funnel-leak check. [29b075b3e1cf]
- Location: city for a pop-up store (San Diego example), country stores, compliance. [cd65fcd0d48d]
- Klaviyo predictive analytics ("not perfect"): predicted AOV (store AOV $50, segment $100+ for big bundles or "spend $500, get 50% off" clear-outs), predicted CLV for VIPs, average time between orders (reminder flow on day 40 for a 45-day cohort), predicted gender. [7b644e511db9]

### 3.23 Copy workflow [95dff9b04773] [6471cdaa0f28] [d4d9215eb873] [b33ded12e90b] [9569a09dc9cc] [f248facd3631] [8051e7b15977]
1. Find angles in the brand's own Instagram language and blog (Gymshark treats "gym" as a verb: "do gym"). [3c8f273100f4] Pick an example email as the layout template (reallygoodemails.com, milled.com, your own swipe inbox). [d3a4af2f518c]
2. Map it in a Google Doc with spacing and labels (headline, subheadline, "hero section with GIF", "table graphic", "fan favorites", Shop Now). This gives the copywriter control over the design.
3. Prime ChatGPT once per chat: "I'm going to give you a bunch of background information on my ecom brand. I want you to process the information so I can later ask you to write email marketing copy." Then: brand and products, mission and goal, brand USPs, product USPs, customer age and demographic, what they enjoy, brand voice, customer goals and aspirations, why they are unique. Use full paragraphs. Paste the website link. Ask "Are you ready to write copy based on the topics I give you?"
4. Request: "We are creating a graphic-based email campaign with minimal copy to send to our customers. The topic is [topic and USPs]. Using the information I gave you on the brand and the customer, write me a quick and punchy headline. Then write a slightly longer subheadline, then 2 to 3 sentences of body copy leading to a Shop Now call to action." Without "graphic-based" you get a letter; without "punchy" you get a 20-word headline.
5. Iterate: "make it more unique", "make the headline more conversational", "give me more options". Take fragments. Gymshark demo moved from "Meet your MVPs" to "Your new go-to for PR day" and "PR Day dominance awaits".
6. Write product blurbs yourself from real product pages ("Minimal and smooth, perfect for arm day").
7. Edit out AI tells: "Well, you're in luck", "crafting", "enthusiasts", "diving into the exciting world", "Unleash your potential". AI takes you "90% of the way"; "always better to write it yourself".

### 3.24 Subject line and preview text [966ff3299f67] [d97f313a3c32] [7a64062e31d6] [106de14b286c]
Subject line: 2 to 5 words; Title Case ("found it has a positive effect on open rates"); open a curiosity loop; sometimes 1 to 2 emojis (not every email; test start vs end; if 2, keep them together). Examples: "A Taste Of Sunshine", "Myth. Busted.", "They're Finally Here", "It's Literally This Easy". Periods between words add suspense ("Wet. Hot. Summer."). Avoid "secret" (overused).
Preview text: about 1 sentence, longer than the subject (e.g. 2-word subject, 5+ word preview), normal capitalization, adds to the subject, often ends with "...". Examples: "This wasn't supposed to be made, but now it's a bestseller...", "It's available, but the last drop sold out in 24 hours...", "If you want the cold hard facts, we got them...", "No one is talking about this, but it will make you more money...".
Live rewrites: sale plus restock became "They're Back" plus "And they're on sale for now" (normally avoid 2 topics). National Coconut Day became "We're Going Coco-Nuts" plus "They made a national holiday after this?". Travel became "Vacation Time" plus "You won't be able to travel the same after this". Inbox fixes: "All New Summer Hiking Essentials" is too long, try "New New"; "The Sale?" plus "Red, white and all you..."; "Built For Summer"; "Don't know what to cook tonight? We got you covered" is a reusable question-answer pattern; "Tallow Won't Fix This" plus "The grass-fed elephant in the room" rated excellent. All caps "sometimes works" for urgency. [32434c0d9bd2]

### 3.28 Six-week deliverability recovery [0f792188c5ee] [e5dfe8e98a49] [20fd44bca0dd] [b7b58b63ae33]
Precondition: open rate under 40% and "technical stuff in place" (he says: if Klaviyo shows no domain warning at the top of the home page, you are probably fine). `[web check: current Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender rules (SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe, spam rate) before trusting that shortcut]`
1. First, improve the emails: varied topics and layouts, clean design, real value. He calls this the number 1 lever.
2. Build engaged segments for 14, 30, 60 and 90 days: can receive email marketing AND (opened at least once in last N days OR clicked at least once in last N days) AND bounced 0 times.
3. Weeks 1 to 3: turn off all flows except welcome, browse abandon, cart abandon, checkout abandon and post-purchase. Send 3 highly engaging campaigns a week to the 14-day segment only.
4. When the 14-day segment holds 50%+ opens for 3 campaigns in a row, move to the 30-day segment (weeks 3 to 4), still 3 a week.
5. Weeks 5 to 6: keep opens at or above 40 to 50%. Widen to 60-day when it reaches 50%. Tighten again if opens fall.
6. Week 6 onward: the 90-day engaged segment is the base list. Send 1 campaign a month (a normal email) to the full list; anyone who opens rejoins the 90-day segment.
Why 3 a week: 1 a week takes 3 weeks to do what 3 do in 1 week.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Treating a Checkout Started flow as a cart flow. [d962429e9e36]
- Copying checkout dynamic content into the cart flow. [c42f60fa5fca]
- 1 or 2 recovery emails that let buyers "off the hook" before their decision cycle ends. [ed0f5c1eb0f6]
- Welcome flows that are 1 email, or 2 to 3 dense emails; welcome emails that never mention the discount. [b0ac0c5d9d9b]
- Discounting at the start of browse or cart flows; discounting past buyers. [f0dfe6920491] [2fd76c2c2442]
- No buyer exit filter, so buyers keep getting welcome discount emails. [3b8a84dc1d94]
- Chasing advanced segmentation or cross-sell splits before the basics work. [05d68e6eea8b]
- Set-and-forget flows; designs "not updated since we had to wear masks". [b568ff652c08]
- Popups with a weak offer ("sign up and be my friend"), 5% off on a $500 AOV, many fields, instant or 30-second fire, slow images, small size, "exclusive offers" copy, busy design, a buried offer, an easy top-right X. [7ea9228eeda8]
- Using exit intent (misfires); using the teaser; stopping at Klaviyo's default template. [e5cbaa0f3a12] [1c9b788208ca]
- Judging popup delay by rate, not total submits. [b5dc319521e1]
- Sending only sales emails ("needy", "buy or die"); monthly discounts that burn margin. [dfbf925e6f5d]
- Sending 1 to 2 campaigns a week (forgotten) or 5 to 7 (unsubscribes, dilution). [a91ac0807b44]
- Sending campaigns to the whole list. [e385d9458d21]
- Emails with several takeaways, text blocks, fluff, boring corporate lines ("the finest cotton tees on earth"), no value ("Elevate your journey"). [99679e755f67] [28bbeaa03336]
- Judging subject lines on opens. [c4cf02ddd233]
- Same template every send; studio photos unlike the brand's Instagram. [a744284f4af9]
- First button far down; small top-left buttons; nav rows at the top; too many CTAs; product tiles that don't look clickable; emails with no button. [ca47b8d3f740] [26cdbc32e54d]
- Oversized image slices; uncompressed images. [1fa479fcc307]
- Shipping ChatGPT copy as is. [8051e7b15977]
- Making deliverability sound complicated to sell a service. [61807f09ea38]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

Internal conflicts:
- **Revenue claims** differ by video: $15M, $20M, $40M, $50M. Email ROI stated as $36 per $1 and $44 per $1.
- **Flow share**: "50% of email revenue" vs "about 20% of total Shopify revenue". Different bases; not a true conflict, but do not merge them.
- **Winback timing**: 60 days (flow table) vs 2x the repurchase interval (flow video) vs segment window 90 to 150 days vs a predictive reminder at day 40 for a 45-day cohort.
- **Post-purchase**: 3 emails (social at +2 days, how-to at +5 days) vs 6 emails (social at about day 7).
- **Cart flow**: 6 to 8 emails vs "3 to 8" in the same video; email 2 is a founder nudge in version A but a social proof reminder in version B.
- **Added to Cart**: "Klaviyo added it with the Shopify integration around September 2024" vs "not tracked by Klaviyo, you must install it yourself". `[web check]`
- **Engaged window start**: 60 days (segmentation default), 30 days (if new), 14 days (recovery).
- **Categories vs products**: categories won for a higher-ticket store; products won for a high-SKU store. He says it flips by brand.
- **Subject lines**: "should not matter for opens" vs "Title Case has a positive effect on open rates".
- **Button at top**: "always" vs "you don't need a button at the top every single time".
- **Site abandon flow** is not in the list of flows kept on during deliverability recovery, so it is switched off then.
- **Long vs short test** is described once as an abandonment email and once as welcome email 2 with the same numbers.

Card errors found against the transcript:
- [d5e95a86111e] says the short email had 0.2% placed order and 2.4% clicks. The transcript gives those numbers to the long email. [481bda83c865] is correct.
- [70e9cc171c68] says the 30-minute send had fewer opens and clicks. The flow video says the 30-minute send had better opens and clicks; the weekly-test video is ambiguous.
- [7ea9228eeda8] names tinypng.com; the transcript names iLoveIMG.
- [40faa5864b0a] calls the footer text "visible but tiny"; the transcript sets it to the background color so it is invisible.
- [a91ac0807b44] is titled "post-purchase email frequency" but covers campaign frequency.

Conflicts with common ecommerce and deliverability practice:
- **Open rate as the deliverability KPI.** The source never mentions Apple Mail Privacy Protection, which inflates opens. His 40%, 50% and 60% thresholds may read differently on Apple-heavy lists. Clicks, placed orders, spam complaints and inbox placement tests are safer checks. `[web check: current MPP impact and Klaviyo's handling of machine opens]`
- **Authentication shortcut.** "No Klaviyo warning means you're fine" skips SPF, DKIM, DMARC, one-click unsubscribe and spam-rate limits. `[web check: Gmail and Yahoo bulk sender requirements]`
- **Invisible footer text.** Hidden text (same color as background, tiny font) is a known spam-filter signal. The card itself flags this. He also pastes another brand's About Us text in a demo; use only your own text.
- **Pre-checked marketing consent** at checkout is not valid consent in some regions. `[web check]`
- **Hidden close button and 75%-screen popups** may hurt accessibility and can conflict with Google's mobile intrusive-interstitial guidance. `[web check]`
- **Image-only emails** (sliced designs) hurt accessibility and live-text rendering; many practitioners keep key copy as live text. His alt-text view ("under 1% block images") is his claim.
- **Discounting cart abandoners** can train customers to abandon; the card suggests testing a no-discount branch.
- **Third-party statistics** (70 to 85%, 26%, 59%, 30%, 27%, 80 to 85%, 68%, $36 or $44 per $1) come without named sources on the transcript. `[web check]`

What is missing or thin:
- No SMS strategy beyond the popup step.
- No review-request email or loyalty program steps, though both are named as goals.
- No statistics method: no significance, few sample sizes, no holdouts.
- Welcome "metrics to shoot for", subject line swipe lists, the popup swipe file and the micro-topic prompt results sit in external docs, not in the transcript.
- No list of which revenue attribution window Klaviyo uses; flow and campaign "revenue" are taken at face value.
- No guidance for very small lists, where weekly A/B tests cannot reach a result.
- No compliance guidance (CAN-SPAM, GDPR, TCPA or regional SMS rules).
- Brand-specific inputs needed before use: `[needs brand data: AOV, margin, opt-in rate by device, flow vs campaign revenue split, open and click rates by segment, days between orders, order-count distribution, product collections, review counts and ratings, guarantees and shipping times, founder name and photo, support reply capacity, discount policy, send volume per week]`
