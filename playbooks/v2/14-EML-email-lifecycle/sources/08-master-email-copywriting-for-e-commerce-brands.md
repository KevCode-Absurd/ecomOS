## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator write short, design-led ecommerce emails without long-form direct-response training. It gives 2 things: (a) a research routine to find the audience's pains, deeper insecurities and pre-purchase objections before writing, using Ad Library, Amazon reviews, podcasts, review sites and social profiles; (b) a modular way to write an email as a stack of blocks (banner, CTA, reviews, product display, benefit highlights, content or story, countdown, featured on, guarantee), with copy guidance for each block. It says nothing about subject lines, preview text, flows, segmentation, send timing or metrics.

## 2. Principles

1. **Ecommerce email copy is short-form and design-based, so skip classic direct-response tactics.** Condition: brand campaign and flow emails. Does not apply to long-form advertorial funnels. Source says most ecommerce emails are "100, 200, maybe 300 words", versus "thousands of words" for advertorials. Evidence: presenter opinion. [effdf39b2845] [14d83f5d3f3e]
2. **Copy that does not sound generic comes from knowing the customer's psychographic.** Condition: before any drafting. Source says once research is done, copy "flows naturally" because you speak to the reader like a friend you are trying to help, and you feel more empathy for their problem. Evidence: presenter's process with his team. [4dac36087f55] [14d83f5d3f3e]
3. **Invest research time up front; writing then gets fast.** Condition: new brand, new product or new angle. Source: spend "at least like 2, 3, 4 hours" on research; then copy takes "10 to 20 minutes per email". Presenter's estimate, not a benchmark. [4dac36087f55] [effdf39b2845]
4. **Split benefits into perks and insecurity anchors.** Condition: problem-solution products. Source: boosted energy and detox are "perk benefits" you "can't really dive into insecurities" with; fat loss, muscle gain, hair loss and low testosterone are "a massive point of insecurity for a lot of guys". Write deep emotional copy only for anchors. Evidence: presenter's reading of one supplement category. [14d83f5d3f3e] [fac6b8c9debd]
5. **Extrapolate each benefit down to the insecurity under it.** Condition: longer benefit copy and story copy. Chain: benefit, then its immediate result, then the social or identity fear (for example muscle gain, then confidence, then controlling the room, then closing more deals and making more money; acne, then assuming others see what you see in the mirror). Evidence: presenter's own technique, no research cited. [14d83f5d3f3e]
6. **Shift the blame away from the customer.** Condition: problem-solution products where the buyer may feel at fault. Source: "you want to always divert the blame from them because people don't like taking accountability". Blame an external cause (chemical hair dye companies, a biological deficiency) and present the product as the fix for damage "which was not your fault in the first place". Source applies it to "all problem solution type of products". The document header calls it "an ethical copywriting tactic"; the transcript itself does not discuss ethics. The card adds the condition that the external cause must be true. [b5dbf59a5f38]
7. **Regulated claims: source says imply, do not state.** Condition: supplements and health products. Source: "we can't mention those things cuz those are regulated claims however you can kind of imply it", using the competitor's "bigger pickle" ad angle as the example. This is the source's position, not a compliance rule. Implied claims are usually regulated too [web check: implied health claim rules (FTC, ASA, FSSAI or local body) in the selling market]. [4dac36087f55] [14d83f5d3f3e]
8. **Trust third-party reviews over product-page reviews.** Condition: objection research. Source: product-page reviews "can be faked", so mine Amazon; filter to Most Recent because "top reviews are not always like that great". [d4e3dc152180]
9. **Most competitor ad variations suggest a working angle.** Condition: choosing whom to study in Ad Library. Source compares a brand with 85 versions of an ad to one running 5. This is a signal, not proof of performance. [4dac36087f55]
10. **An email is an assembly of blocks; learn to write each block and the email follows.** Condition: ecommerce campaigns and flows built in an ESP. Only the content or story block needs a lot of copy; logo and footer blocks need none. [effdf39b2845]
11. **Design for the phone screen before writing.** Condition: banner and product display blocks. Layout decides how many words fit. [0dec9bea1128] [974a7ea84ff1]
12. **Social proof must answer an objection, not just praise.** Condition: every review block. "I love this product" by "Lauren from Texas" does not help the reader decide. [6e5742c076f2]
13. **Facts sell without frameworks.** Condition: founders or beginner writers short on time. A plain study fact or a plain founder story "still sells" with "no copywriting tactics involved". Presenter opinion. [e36a249f114a]
14. **Urgency must be backed.** Condition: launches and sales with a real expiry. The countdown timer "backs that claim". The card adds that a fake or resetting deadline damages trust. [8ae593fcc226]
15. **Guarantees reverse the fear of loss, and longer windows return less.** Condition: most of all for lower-ticket consumables and first-time buyers. Source: guarantees "don't really increase return rates or refund rates that much"; a 30-day window gets more returns than a 180-day window because people forget. Presenter's claim, no data shown. [e447e1b8be41] [needs brand data: refund rate by guarantee window]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Baseline audience data (if you have it)
1. Open Google Analytics or Facebook audience analytics and note age ranges, gender split and interests. [verify UI] [94e80b540e5c] [needs brand data: GA4 and Meta audience demographics]
2. Treat this as the baseline only. The rest of the routine adds the psychographic layer. Source says run the full routine if you lack this data or want a better understanding. [94e80b540e5c]

### 3.2 Competitor ad research in Facebook Ad Library
1. Search the product category (demo: "sea moss"). [4dac36087f55]
2. Set country to the main market of your target demo (demo: US, "the biggest market for DTC right now"). [verify UI] [needs brand data: main selling country]
3. Ad category: All ads. Media type: Video. Apply. Source reason: only "slightly bigger brands" can run UGC video ads, and image ads are "not really that telling". [verify UI] [4dac36087f55]
4. Scan results and open "See summary details" to see how many versions an ad has. Prefer competitors running many (demo: 85 versions) over those running few (demo: 5). [verify UI] [4dac36087f55]
5. Go to the competitor's Page > About > Page transparency > Ads to see their full ad history (demo showed 610 results with no active ads; the presenter still studied it because he knew the angle worked). [verify UI] [4dac36087f55]
6. Note the lead angle (demo: a "bigger pickle" angle selling a sea moss and shilajit bundle to men). [4dac36087f55]

### 3.3 Competitor product page review
1. Open the competitor's product page. If it blocks copy-paste or is built for mobile, the presenter switched to a mobile view in browser developer tools (source's navigation steps are marked Skip in the document header). [verify UI]
2. Record ingredients, USPs, claims, press ("as seen on Shark Tank"), bundle offers and reviews. [4dac36087f55]
3. Do not rely on the reviews shown on this page (can be faked). Use section 3.6. [d4e3dc152180]

### 3.4 Build the research doc
1. Open a plain Notion page. Source: "I don't really have a template to follow", it is an "idea dump". [4dac36087f55]
2. Write keywords and angles (demo: "hormonal function for men", "bigger pickle angle", "increased levels of testosterone"). [4dac36087f55]
3. List each benefit and its mechanism (demo: ashwagandha and black seed oil for hormones; "fat loss" as increased metabolism; muscle gain as a result of testosterone). [4dac36087f55] [14d83f5d3f3e]
4. Mark each benefit as perk (energy, detox, cruelty-free, clean ingredients are "more standard") or insecurity anchor (fat loss, muscle gain, hair loss, low testosterone, acne-prone skin). [14d83f5d3f3e]
5. List all pains. Start with the primary pain (demo: a consequence of low testosterone), then write the insecurities that pain creates (demo: unable to approach girls, insecure in social settings, feeling inferior). [14d83f5d3f3e]
6. For each anchor benefit, push 2 to 3 levels deeper (demo chain: muscle gain, feeling and looking more confident, controlling the room, showing up better in interviews and sales calls, closing more deals, making more money). [14d83f5d3f3e]
7. Add the blame-shift line for each pain: what external cause you can honestly name (demo: nerves before approaching someone are "a biological thing", "not cuz you're just a bit shy"). [b5dbf59a5f38]
8. Repeat across products and keep adding findings from 3.5 to 3.9. This doc feeds banner angle, review choice, benefit copy and guarantee copy. [4dac36087f55]

### 3.5 Thought-leader and podcast research
1. On YouTube, search the ingredient broadly and with doubt phrases (demo: "shilajit", "does shilajit work", "shilajit legit podcast"). [4dac36087f55]
2. Note which niche the talk sits in (demo: "very much fitness related"). [4dac36087f55]
3. Watch a thought leader or KOL in the space (demo: Andrew Huberman) for the science. [4dac36087f55]
4. Turn a statistic into a direct-response angle and ask which insecurity it attaches to. Source's invented example: a quoted study saying shilajit boosts testosterone "by 200% within 2 weeks", which attaches to the low-testosterone insecurity. The statistic is hypothetical. [web check: any study you plan to quote]
5. Read podcast comment sections for objections too. [d4e3dc152180]

### 3.6 Amazon review mining for objections
1. Go to the Amazon of your target country (demo: amazon.com). [d4e3dc152180] [needs brand data: target country marketplace]
2. Search your category and adjacent ones (demo: shilajit and sea moss). [d4e3dc152180]
3. Pick products with many reviews. Open the review section. Filter to Most Recent. [verify UI] [d4e3dc152180]
4. Write down every practical complaint. Demo findings, shilajit: "difficult to get out of" (thick tar that air-dries when the cap is left open; bottom 20% hard to scoop), lead content and no lab testing (sourced from different regions), benefits lost during purification ("C60" process), no energy increase, "doesn't work", "tastes like crap", very bitter, hard to swallow, "tastes like medicine". Demo findings, sea moss: mostly 4.5 stars, praise for "easy to swallow", "no bad taste after", "lots of energy", "great for detoxing", some "didn't notice any change". [d4e3dc152180]
5. Log category-level objections, not only one brand's (lead content across shilajit products). [d4e3dc152180]
6. Read positive reviews too: repeated taste praise for sea moss shows taste is the category's buying barrier. [d4e3dc152180]
7. Note positive signals that reveal demand (demo: a product "already selling really well in Amazon" means "a lot of demand"). [d4e3dc152180]
8. Decide how your own product answers each objection (demo: "our shilajit goes through a very different purification process that doesn't lead to the loss of minerals, let's say"). The demo line is hypothetical. [d4e3dc152180] [needs brand data: product process, lab tests and proof for each objection answer]
9. Time: "at least like 30 minutes to an hour" of review reading per category, inside the "2, 3, 4 hours" total. [d4e3dc152180]

### 3.7 Other review and comment sources
1. Trustpilot and the Better Business Bureau for more reviews. [verify UI] [d4e3dc152180]
2. Comment sections of competitor ads and of the brand's latest Instagram posts. [d4e3dc152180] [94e80b540e5c]

### 3.8 Follower profiling
1. For a brand with a niche audience, open its Instagram followers list and view the profiles of the top followers. [verify UI] [94e80b540e5c]
2. Check "between 50 to like 75" profiles. [94e80b540e5c]
3. Record gender, age range and recurring interests (demo: motorbikes, fishing, holistic wellness). Infer the buyer type. [94e80b540e5c]
4. Add to the research doc. Card caveat: this is inference, not a valid sample, and platform terms apply. [94e80b540e5c] [web check: Instagram terms on viewing and recording profile data]

### 3.9 Problem-keyword search in Ad Library
1. Search the problem, not the product (demo: "hair loss"). [verify UI] [100c73502444]
2. See which solutions already fill the market (demo: minoxidil). [100c73502444]
3. Note what people dislike about them (demo: presenter says minoxidil is effective but has "hormone influences and side effects"; his assertion, not a checked medical fact). [web check: side-effect claims before any use in copy]
4. Position your product for buyers looking for the alternative (demo: "holistic natural alternatives" such as sea moss or collagen). [100c73502444]
5. Card adds: cross-check the complaint in Amazon reviews of the incumbent before committing, and feed the angle into banner and benefit copy. [100c73502444]

### 3.10 Write an email from blocks
1. Pick the blocks the email needs from the list: banner, CTA, review, product display, benefit highlight, content or story, countdown timer, featured on, guarantee. Logo and footer blocks need no copy. [effdf39b2845]
2. Source's gallery example: banner at top, a content block telling a story about whiskey glasses, then product display rows highlighting USPs, then footer. Source also names offer blocks, more story blocks and USP blocks. [effdf39b2845]
3. The card suggests combinations (launch email: banner + countdown + product display + guarantee; story email: banner + long content block + review). These are the card writer's examples, not the presenter's. [effdf39b2845]
4. Write each block separately from the research doc (3.4), using the specs in 3.11 to 3.19. [effdf39b2845]
5. Hand the block-level copy to the designer, or design it yourself. Source uses Canva for brands under $50k a month and Figma for more professional output. [verify UI] [effdf39b2845]
6. Keep a reusable library of block designs so future emails are assembled. [effdf39b2845]
7. The source promises a worked example email for this product in the next module. It is not in this document.

### 3.11 Banner block
1. The banner is what a phone shows before any scroll. [0dec9bea1128]
2. State the purpose or offer of the email, like opening a letter with "I am writing to [purpose]". [0dec9bea1128]
3. Make it eye-catching. [0dec9bea1128]
4. Size to the phone aspect ratio so all needed information shows without scrolling and no text is cropped. [0dec9bea1128]
5. Short banner or longer banner with a CTA inside: both are valid. [0dec9bea1128]
6. Why: a clear full banner "massively" increases how many readers reach the next block (source claim, no number). [0dec9bea1128] [needs brand data: mobile open share and inbox clients on the list]

### 3.12 CTA block
1. Button text only. Standard: "Shop Now", "Order Today", "Learn More". [075c5b390cd2]
2. Tell the reader where to click and what you want them to do. "Not that hard to write." [075c5b390cd2]
3. Card adds: match the label to the block's intent, keep it short enough not to wrap on mobile, keep persuasion in other blocks. [075c5b390cd2]

### 3.13 Review block
1. List the top objections and insecurities from the research doc. [6e5742c076f2]
2. Pick one review per objection. Demo picks for sea moss and shilajit: one review about better hair and nails; one from "a busy entrepreneur" with low energy who got high energy "without changing my horrible lifestyle habits". [6e5742c076f2]
3. Skip reviews that only say "I love this product". [6e5742c076f2]
4. Optional UGC: show the review with a profile photo of the reviewer. Mix and match. [6e5742c076f2]
5. Do not put a face next to a sensitive objection (source example: erectile dysfunction). Source: "use it with caution". [6e5742c076f2]
6. Card adds: keep review text short enough for a mobile block. [6e5742c076f2] [needs brand data: real reviews tagged by the objection they answer, and reviewer photo consent]

### 3.14 Product display block
1. Decide the layout before writing copy. [974a7ea84ff1]
2. One product with image, description and CTA: one per row (row by row). [974a7ea84ff1]
3. Several products with little description: 2x2 grid (image plus title, text stays readable). [974a7ea84ff1]
4. Avoid 3x2: on mobile products look "too small for people to distinguish the text". [974a7ea84ff1]
5. Write the copy to the space the layout gives. [974a7ea84ff1]

### 3.15 Benefit highlight block
Route A, icons (several benefits at once):
1. Short labels. Demo: "boosted energy", "fat loss and muscle gain", "thicker fuller hair", "intense detoxification". [fac6b8c9debd]
2. Replace the generic tick with an icon that fits each label (demo: lightning for energy, flexed bicep for muscle, hair massage emoji for hair, meditation emoji for detox), or design custom icons. [fac6b8c9debd]

Route B, longer copy (one benefit per block):
1. Pick one insecurity-anchor benefit. [fac6b8c9debd]
2. Open with the insecurity. Source's rough first draft: "are you someone that every time you look in the mirror, rather than seeing the attractive handsome 20-something year old that you are, all you see is a young man with an acne-ridden face that everyone is going to notice as soon as you go outside". [fac6b8c9debd]
3. Resolve with the mechanism and product benefit, blame shifted (demo: "it just means you're deficient in a ton of minerals, and by adding these minerals your acne scars can heal over time"). The mechanism is illustrative, not a checked claim. [fac6b8c9debd] [needs brand data: substantiated mechanism for the brand's product]
4. Refine the draft. Source calls his own draft "terrible copy" written in "5 seconds" that he "would refine into a proper sentence structure". [fac6b8c9debd]

### 3.16 Content and story block
The only block that needs a lot of copy. [effdf39b2845]

Content block (facts):
1. State the fact plainly, for example a study result or research paper that backs the product claim. [e36a249f114a]
2. Source's invented example: "studies show that taking 200 mg of shilajit over a 2 week period increased the testosterone of the control group by 42% on average". Not a real citation. [web check: the actual study, dose and effect before quoting any number]
3. Add a graph if you can. That is the whole block. [e36a249f114a]

Story block (founder facts in first person):
1. Tell what happened, in order, sticking to facts. [e36a249f114a]
2. Source's invented example: a founder started the brand 3 years ago out of frustration at genetically receding hairlines in the family; learned the best cure for hair loss is prevention; found micronutrient deficiencies in his diet; found sea moss in 2018 when it was hard to get; had relatives in Jamaica source it and ship it to the US, with customs headaches; used it himself with results; friends loved it; started selling. [e36a249f114a] [needs brand data: the real founder story]
3. Other story types named: founding story, content documenting a research paper. [e36a249f114a]

### 3.17 Countdown timer block
1. Use only for launches and sales events with a real deadline (demo: a discount code expiring in 24 hours). [8ae593fcc226]
2. The timer backs the urgency claim; it is not copy. [8ae593fcc226]
3. Tool: countdownmail.com, "very cheap", "like 10 bucks a month", "infinite uses". [web check: countdownmail.com current pricing and ESP support]
4. Setup steps are promised for a later "build email" module; not in this source. [verify UI]

### 3.18 Featured-on block
1. Two wordings only: "As seen on" or "Featured on". [f0580c1b1e80]
2. Add the logos of outlets where the brand genuinely appeared (source examples: Shark Tank, CNBC, tech magazines and channels for a tech brand). [f0580c1b1e80] [needs brand data: real press and media appearances]
3. Purpose: authority and trust. [f0580c1b1e80]

### 3.19 Guarantee block
1. Use especially for consumables and first-time buyers. [e447e1b8be41]
2. It answers the fear of loss: "I'm going to get scammed", "it might not work for me", "it's expensive". [e447e1b8be41]
3. Offer an unconditional guarantee. Source: "don't be too afraid"; "you're not going to lose money, you're just going to get a ton of conversions". [e447e1b8be41]
4. Consider a long window. Source claim: a 30-day guarantee has a higher return rate than a 180-day one on lower-ticket consumables. [e447e1b8be41] [needs brand data: return and refund rates, product cost, margin]
5. Card adds: repeat the guarantee in flows most likely to hold the first purchase, and pair it with objection-handling reviews. [e447e1b8be41] [web check: consumer law on guarantee and refund wording in each selling market]

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

| check | how | good | bad | next step |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Research doc exists | Open the doc for this product | Pains, insecurity chains, objections with sources, blame-shift lines | Only a feature list | Run 3.2 to 3.9 before drafting [4dac36087f55] |
| Copy sounds generic | Read the draft aloud against the doc | Names a specific pain or objection from research | Could fit any brand | Rewrite from an insecurity anchor [14d83f5d3f3e] |
| Deep copy on the right benefit | Check which benefit the long block uses | Insecurity anchor (hair, skin, weight, strength) | Perk (energy, detox, clean ingredients) | Move perks to icon rows [fac6b8c9debd] |
| Blame direction | Look at who the copy says caused the problem | External, true cause | "Your hair sucks because you dyed it" | Rewrite toward the outside cause; drop it if no true cause exists [b5dbf59a5f38] |
| Claim risk | Flag any health, medical or performance claim, stated or implied | Substantiated and allowed in market | Regulated claim, invented study, unverified mechanism | Remove or substantiate [web check: local ad claim rules] |

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Overcomplicating ecommerce copy with traditional direct-response tactics. [effdf39b2845]
2. Writing before you understand the audience, which gives generic, boring copy. [4dac36087f55]
3. Trusting reviews on product pages (can be faked). [d4e3dc152180]
4. Reading Top reviews instead of Most Recent. [d4e3dc152180]
5. Studying competitors who run only a few ads. [4dac36087f55]
6. Studying image ads for angles (source: "not really that telling"). [4dac36087f55]
7. Trying to write insecurity copy for perk benefits like energy or detox. [14d83f5d3f3e] [fac6b8c9debd]
8. Blaming the customer for their problem. [b5dbf59a5f38]
9. Stating regulated claims outright (source's fix is to imply; see section 9). [14d83f5d3f3e]
10. Banner text cropped or key information below the fold on mobile. [0dec9bea1128]
11. Generic "I love this product" reviews that do not help a decision. [6e5742c076f2]
12. Putting a customer's face next to a sensitive objection. [6e5742c076f2]
13. 3x2 product grids that shrink products and text on mobile. [974a7ea84ff1]
14. Writing product copy before deciding the layout. [974a7ea84ff1]
15. Shipping rough first-draft sentences without refining. [fac6b8c9debd]
16. Reaching for copy frameworks when short on time, instead of stating facts. [e36a249f114a]
17. Being afraid to offer an unconditional guarantee for fear of losing money. [e447e1b8be41]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **No subject lines, preview text or sender name.** The source covers none of them. Nothing here should be cited for inbox-level copy.
- **No flows, segmentation, timing, frequency or deliverability.** The source is block copy only. Welcome, abandoned cart and post-purchase specifics are absent.
- **No results.** No open, click, conversion or revenue numbers. Every copy number is invented for illustration.
- **Implying regulated claims conflicts with common compliance practice.** Ad regulators generally treat implied claims as claims. The source's "imply it" advice should not be followed for health, medical or sexual-performance topics without a compliance check. [web check: implied claim rules in each market]
- **Blame shift can become an unsubstantiated accusation.** The demo blames "evil giant corporations" and chemical dye makers. The card adds the honesty condition; the transcript does not. Only name an external cause that is true and provable, and do not name competitor companies. [b5dbf59a5f38]
- **Insecurity-led copy is aggressive.** The source's examples lean on shame (acne, sexual performance, "a bit of a [ __ ]"). Brand voice may rule this out. [needs brand data: brand voice limits on fear or insecurity angles]
- **180-day guarantee claim conflicts with some common advice and rests on customers forgetting.** Common advice agrees guarantees lift conversion, but the "longer window means fewer returns" claim is one presenter's experience. It also relies on buyers not using a right they were given, which some brands will see as poor practice. Test on own refund data. [e447e1b8be41] [needs brand data: refund rate by window]
- **CTA copy is dismissed as "not that hard".** Common CRO practice tests CTA wording; the source treats it as low effort. Treat as low priority, not as untestable. [075c5b390cd2]
- **Short-copy rule is one view.** Some brands run long plain-text emails well. The 100 to 300 word range is presenter opinion. [needs brand data: past email length versus click and revenue]
- **Video-only Ad Library filter skips image ads that may perform.** Card caveat. Competitor ads are signals, not proven winners. [4dac36087f55]
- **Many ad versions is a proxy, not proof.** A brand may run many versions without success. [4dac36087f55]
- **Follower profiling is not a valid sample** and may be limited by platform terms. [94e80b540e5c]
- **Ingredient and medical statements in the demo are unverified** (minoxidil side effects, lead in shilajit, purification losses, mineral deficiency causing acne). Do not reuse any of them in copy without a source. [web check: each claim]
- **Examples come from one category.** All demos are men's supplements. Transfer to other categories (fashion, jewellery, home) is the operator's judgement; the source does not show it.
- **Missing how:** no full worked email (deferred to the next module), no countdown setup steps (deferred), no design steps (deferred to a Canva module), no review-block layout specs, no guidance on how many blocks per email.
- **Sales pitch content:** $50k a month cut-off, deck, 250 templates and Klaviyo audit are lead magnets, not advice.
