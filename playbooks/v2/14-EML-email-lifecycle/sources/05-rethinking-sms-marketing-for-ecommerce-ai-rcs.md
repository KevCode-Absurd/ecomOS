## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator decide 4 things for an SMS program: how many texts to send (and how to prove more sends pay), who owns the pop-up and welcome flow (acquisition, not only retention), how to start conversational SMS (add a question to existing flows, mine the replies), and whether to pilot RCS. It also gives a starting order for a $5M to $10M brand. It is a strategy talk. It is thin on setup steps, compliance, copy, segmentation and exact test design.

## 2. Principles

1. **More sends usually mean more conversions, as long as unsubscribes do not rise.** The guest frames each send as a new impression to an audience that already showed intent ("manufacturing infinite impressions"). There are diminishing returns, but each send brings some new conversions, including first-time buyers. Condition: SMS is a "toll road": every text costs money, unlike email, so the extra revenue must beat the per-message cost. Evidence: vendor view plus one holdout test (principle 3). 2. **Use data, not fear, to set frequency.** "If you annoyed people, it would show up as unsub rate." Postscript watches unsubscribes in the first 30 days of a subscriber's life as the key signal. Condition: this is the only list-health signal the source names; it does not mention complaints, carrier filtering or opt-out keyword rates. [52f701ac2a63] [bb28c48148d6]
3. **Prove incrementality with a holdout before scaling send volume.** Source ran a 3-cell test with a guest brand: 50% normal cadence, 25% no messages, 25% double cadence (1 text a week to 2). Result: the 2x group produced significantly more revenue than the holdout, with "98% certainty", and "more sending produced a higher ROI". Condition: one brand, campaign cadence only, no sample size, length or dollar figures given. Treat as an example. [651884bb18e3]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Holdout test for SMS send frequency
Source gives the cells and the result, not the length or sample size.
1. Split SMS subscribers into 3 random cells: 50% keep the current cadence, 25% get no messages, 25% get double cadence (in the source: 1 campaign text a week moved to 2). [651884bb18e3]
2. Run long enough to read revenue. The source gives no duration or minimum size. `[needs brand data: list size, weekly revenue per subscriber, to size the test]`
3. Compare total revenue per subscriber in each cell against the no-message cell, not platform-attributed revenue. [651884bb18e3]
4. Subtract message cost from the extra revenue of the 2x cell. Every SMS has a cost. `[needs brand data: cost per SMS or RCS message]` [651884bb18e3]
5. If the 2x cell beats holdout with confidence, raise cadence. If not, keep the baseline. Source result: 2x won at 98% certainty with higher ROI (one brand). [651884bb18e3]

### 3.2 Find your cadence ceiling by stepping up (the guest's operating heuristic)
1. Record the baseline: messages per subscriber per month, revenue per message, total channel revenue, and unsubscribe rate, with focus on unsubscribes in each subscriber's first 30 days. [52f701ac2a63] [bb28c48148d6]
2. Add 1 more message a week. If that is too much for your current rhythm, add 1 every 2 weeks. [bb28c48148d6]
3. After each step, check unsubscribe rate and total channel revenue together. [bb28c48148d6]
4. Keep adding until the unsubscribe rate "starts to tick up". That point is "your new baseline". [bb28c48148d6]
5. Hold the new baseline. Change one lever at a time (cadence or format) so you know what moved unsubscribes. [3ece5fcef112]
6. Reference range while you step up: Postscript sees 8 to 10 texts per subscriber per month with "essentially zero impact" on baseline unsub rate. It is a range to test, not a target. [52f701ac2a63]
Not in source: how long to wait between steps, and what size of unsub rise counts as a "tick up". `[needs brand data: normal week-to-week unsub rate noise]`

### 3.3 Start conversational SMS by adding a question
1. Pick any existing automated message: welcome series, cart abandonment ("hey, we saved your cart, here's the link"), delivery confirmation, or winback. [c2cd726a16ab]
2. After the link, add one plain question. Source wording: "Is there anything else I can help you with today?" or "Do you have any questions I can answer?" [c2cd726a16ab]
3. Staff or tool the replies before you send. The source expects volume: brands doing $500M a year got "like 5,000 replies in the first week". Scale this to your list size. `[needs brand data: who answers replies, and in what time]` [c2cd726a16ab]
4. Optional split test, as the host ran at Ridge: control is the standard 6-message welcome flow; the variant is the same flow with a final message that asks a question. Logic: the standard flow still converts the easy 25%; the question catches marginal buyers who need a conversation. Host said he is "very happy" with it; no numbers given. [c2cd726a16ab]
5. Read the replies and tag the objection themes. [73d16bfc4a27] [5bd6379a40ae]
6. Feed the themes into flow copy, PDP copy, FAQ and ads. [73d16bfc4a27] [5bd6379a40ae]
7. Extend to more touch points: post-delivery "how is it going" questions, long-consideration prospects, and winback of people who have engaged with texts for a year but never bought. [d0012f077a0d]

### 3.4 Turn conversation replies into customer attributes
Source describes intent and Postscript's work in progress, not a step-by-step method.
1. Make replies queryable (Postscript's "Ask Shopper" lets you query conversations and see trends). [73d16bfc4a27] [verify UI]
2. Tag each fact as static (SKU bought, has a daughter) or ephemeral (child's age, seasonal location, current size). [67b11f173f1a]
3. Push attributes to the CRM or CDP so email, SMS and other channels can use them. Postscript said API and MCP access for these insights was being built. `[web check: current Postscript API/MCP availability for conversation data]` [67b11f173f1a]
4. Build triggers on ephemeral facts (next birthday, season change, size change). Source gives the idea, not a working example. [67b11f173f1a]

### 3.5 Pilot RCS
1. Confirm your platform supports RCS with SMS fallback and that your audience can receive it. Source: available on all US devices; Postscript has "dozens and dozens" of brands live. `[web check: RCS reach outside the US]` [verify UI] [30309dfff699]
2. Build an SMS-safe fallback for every RCS send. [04f7be245280] [80442f6087a2]
3. Pick the test design first. Option A (Jones Road): switch to RCS with a holdout running. Option B (Cody's guess at what Jones Road will do): keep messages close to one-to-one with the SMS version first, then test richer elements, for a cleaner read. Do not change protocol and creative at the same time if you want to know which caused the lift. Condition: Option B was a guess, not a result. [80442f6087a2]
4. After a clean read, add rich formats: carousels for recommendations, web view for browsing in-thread, action buttons for CTAs, add-to-calendar for drops. [04f7be245280] [d1d6ad0e082f]
5. Judge on incremental revenue after the higher RCS cost, not on message cost alone. `[needs brand data: RCS vs SMS cost per message]` [04f7be245280]
6. Expect the novelty lift to fade as more brands adopt. Re-test later. [04f7be245280] [80442f6087a2]

### 3.9 Starting order for a $5M to $10M brand (guest's list)
1. List growth first. Maximize subscribers through on-site opt-in (the pop-up). "Fix your pop-up acquisition rate first. If you do nothing else, that's the most important thing." ## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Is more frequency paying?** Check: holdout or step-up test (3.1, 3.2). Good: revenue per subscriber rises vs holdout and clears message cost; first-30-day unsubs flat. Bad: unsubs tick up, or extra revenue is below message cost. Next: set baseline at the last step before the rise. [651884bb18e3] [bb28c48148d6]
2. **Are you paying for dead weight?** Check: subscribers on the list 1 to 2 years who never bought. Source (Grüns logic): they do not want to pay fees for people who "didn't unsubscribe but have been there for one or two years and are never actually going to end up subscribing". Next: the source does not give a suppression rule. Options it implies: target them with questions (winback conversation) or limit spend on them. `[needs brand data: count and send cost of long-tenure non-buyers]` [52f701ac2a63] [d0012f077a0d]
3. **Is the 75% being worked?** Check: share of new subscribers with no order at 30 days, and whether any flow asks them what is stopping them. Bad: the only plan is "re-get them to click on a Meta ad". Next: add a question (3.3). [a971c5673088]
4. **Does the paid team know the pop-up?** Guest often finds performance marketers "don't even know" what the pop-up looks like. Check: can the paid lead describe the pop-up and welcome flow? Bad: no. Next: shared reporting under one leader. [a971c5673088]
5. **Did a paid or site change hurt list growth?** Check: SMS list growth vs forecast after ad or site changes (the view-optimization example). [1282cefafb08]
6. **Did a pop-up change hurt checkout?** Check: full-site conversion rate in pop-up tests, not only opt-in rate. [1282cefafb08]
7. **Is the SMS bill proven?** Check: is there a holdout-based read on SMS revenue? At $10K+ a month the source says you need one. Also ask if the next $25K of spend is efficient (the $75K vs $100K question). [9a17b76595cd]
8. **Is RCS lift real?** Check: RCS vs SMS-fallback holdout, with creative held constant. Re-check later for novelty decay. [80442f6087a2]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Setting SMS frequency on gut fear of annoying people instead of watching unsub rate. [52f701ac2a63]
2. Treating the SMS list as retention only, when most subscribers have not bought yet. [a971c5673088]
3. Sending unconverted subscribers back to Meta ads instead of handling their objection in the thread. [a971c5673088]
4. Paid teams that do not know the pop-up or welcome flow; siloed teams that miss cross-effects. [a971c5673088] [1282cefafb08]
5. Judging pop-up tests on opt-in rate alone and hurting site conversion. [1282cefafb08]
6. Letting a large SMS bill run without a holdout read. [9a17b76595cd]
7. Relying on PDPs to handle every objection. [73d16bfc4a27]
8. Never asking customers anything. "Zero people text their customers right now" (guest calls this hyperbole). [c2cd726a16ab]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Conflict of interest.** Postscript earns on sends. The 8 to 10 a month range, "send more" advice, RCS enthusiasm and "switch to Postscript" all come from the seller. The one holdout was run by the vendor with a customer.
- **Conflict with common advice.** Much ecommerce SMS advice keeps campaign texts to a few a month to limit fatigue and cost. This source says 8 to 10 a month shows no unsub rise, and some brands send 20+ in 30 days. It also treats SMS as acquisition, while most advice files it under retention.
- **Single stop signal.** The source uses unsub rate only. It does not mention spam complaints, carrier filtering, delivery rate, reply STOP rates, or long-run LTV effects of fatigue.
- **Compliance is missing.** Only "double opt-in compliant" and "compliant phone numbers" appear. No consent wording, opt-out keywords, quiet hours, frequency disclosure, age or content rules, or country differences. `[web check: TCPA and carrier rules for US; local SMS consent, DLT or sender registration, and quiet hours for other countries]`
- **RCS is US-framed.** "Available for everyone on everyone's device in the United States." No data for other countries, and pricing is only "a little more expensive". `[web check: RCS business messaging availability, pricing and verification for the brand's country]`
- **Card errors to note.** Card [52f701ac2a63] says a "subscription-first model" sends about 2 texts. The source says the opposite: large brands send about 2; Grüns, the subscription brand, sends 23 in 30 days. The same card says low-frequency brands filter long-term non-buyers; in the source it is Grüns (high frequency up front) that does not want to pay fees for long-term non-buyers. Card [651884bb18e3] says "run for a full cycle"; the source gives no duration. Card [5bd6379a40ae] says no response-rate figures were given; the source does give the "5,000 replies in the first week" anecdote.
- **Speaker names are unreliable.** The transcript mixes names ("Connor", "Bridge" for Ridge). Attributions here follow context.
- **No how-to on pop-ups.** Despite the file header promising pop-up optimization, the source gives no pop-up design, offer, timing or benchmark. See other sources in this folder.
- **AI offers need guardrails.** Price-gap offers by AI need merchant-set limits; the source does not say how Shopper sets them. [73d16bfc4a27]
- **Ephemeral data idea is early.** Postscript says it is experimenting; API and MCP access were in development. [67b11f173f1a]
- **1.5x LTV is correlation.** People who reply may already be more engaged. The source does not test cause.
- **Card [a1eee9f85670] and [3ece5fcef112] are thin.** The first comes from a Haus sponsor read; the second is a card-writer synthesis, not a stated process.
- **Privacy.** The belt example shows people share health and personal data. The source jokes "does your husband know you're telling us all this?" but gives no rules on storing or using sensitive data. `[web check: data protection rules for storing health or family details from SMS replies]`
