## 1. What this source is for

It helps an email marketer plan and write a monthly broadcast campaign program that sells at full price instead of through constant discounts. It gives: a cadence (3 to 4 sends a week to the engaged list), 4 non-discount content pillars plus an optional sales pillar with about 100 idea types, a graphic-to-text email ratio, 3 copy rules, 9 infographic types, a standard email layout, a subject line view, and an AI workflow for calendars and copy with a worked Magic Mind example. It does not cover automated flows (welcome, abandoned cart, post-purchase, winback), segmentation logic, email design, deliverability setup, or how to measure results. It is strong on content ideas and layout. It is thin on proof and on how to know whether the program is working.

## 2. Principles

1. **Stop relying on list-wide discounts.** Sending "20% off sales to your whole entire email list" as your only strategy trains customers never to buy at full price, cuts margin every time, and hurts the brand. Replace it with content that handles objections one by one and "slowly educat[es] the customer into buying our product, all at full price". Condition: his stated approach, with no before and after data. [46a5333c2392]
2. **Email earns the trust that a crowded market now demands.** His market picture: about 1,000 new brands launch every week, the average customer sees "over 170 different ecom brand ads every single day", shoppers enjoy browsing but have been scammed and ask "who is this brand?", "why should I go with them over the other 50 brands?" and "what are people saying about this brand?". The brand that answers these questions best wins the sale. Ads can do this but are "very expensive"; emails "cost pennies on the dollar". Condition: both statistics are unsourced `[web check: source for "170 ecom brand ads per day" and "1,000 new brands per week"]`. [46a5333c2392]
3. **3 ways brands get email wrong.** (a) Discount after discount. (b) Under-sending: 1 or 2 random campaigns a week, "leaving hundreds of thousands of dollars on the table". (c) Right frequency, about 4 a week, but the wrong content, so it does not convert. The fix is "the right mix of content at the right frequency". Condition: the "hundreds of thousands" figure depends on list size and is not a benchmark. [46a5333c2392] [1dbc06e4564f]
4. **Send 3 to 4 times a week to the engaged list.** "Every brand is different", but in general he sends to the engaged list "roughly 3 to 4 times per week", which he says he tested across 200 plus brands. He also says "landing in that 2 to 4 per week range, ideally that 3 to 4". Condition: a starting point to test, not a law; he gives no test data. `[needs brand data: current sends per week, unsubscribe rate per send, revenue per send, by week]` [1dbc06e4564f]
5. **Too few sends: the customer forgets you.** At 1 to 2 a week, people forget the brand, cannot form a habit of opening, do not become familiar, and money is left behind. If a new subscriber gets only 1 email in their first week, they have "already made the decision on what brand they're going to go with". Low volume also hurts deliverability: you cannot "build up a reputation in Gmail and land in their main inbox". Condition: the deliverability claim is his, with no data `[web check: Gmail guidance on sender reputation and send volume]`. [1dbc06e4564f]
6. **Too many sends: fatigue.** At 5 to 7 a week, unsubscribes get "super high", customers get annoyed, the message is diluted, and revenue "does start to plateau". [1dbc06e4564f]
7. **At 3 to 4 a week, results depend on content.** He says this range gives higher revenue and lower unsubscribes "as long as you're sending the right content", keeps the brand top of mind, gives each email weight, and raises the chance of landing in the inbox on a day the reader is "in a buying mood". [1dbc06e4564f]
8. **Use 4 non-discount pillars plus an optional sales pillar.** Educational, social proof, community and brand, product or collection highlights, then sales. Sales is optional: "You don't need to send discounts." [9077e5d437cb]
9. **About 1 sale or offer a month keeps people subscribed.** He "usually" recommends 1 sale, discount or offer a month so subscribers feel rewarded and do not unsubscribe for fear of missing a future deal. "We don't want to overdo it." Conflict: his sample 10-email month has 2 sales emails, and the AI calendar he praises has 3 (section 9). [46a5333c2392] [9077e5d437cb]
10. **Keep the pillars in roughly equal amounts.** "A good cadence is going to have a similar amount of all this." His numbers do not add up; see section 4 and section 9. [9077e5d437cb]
11. **Specific beats general.** "That's what makes winning non-discount emails is how specific your points are." Not "our product is amazing" or "get better overall health", but "ingredient deep dive: why we use adaptogens" or "how to use [product] for 30 days and here's what to expect". The ingest header calls this "microtopics": one problem, process or solution per email. [c6824f14bcd2]
12. **Tie social proof to a specific experience.** Example: an "I finally ditched coffee" email built from quotes by people who switched from caffeine-heavy routines. Go to the website and find testimonials that match that exact experience. `[needs brand data: reviews and testimonials tagged by the problem or switch they describe]` [c6824f14bcd2]
13. **Text-based emails are his best performers.** Plain-text emails "show up completely native in the customer's inbox" and look "like a friend is actually sending you a message". "All of my best performing emails of all time are these text based emails. They're the secret sauce." Condition: no numbers given. [65281424ea63]
14. **Graphic emails still carry most of the load.** They are "great for educating people" with infographics people can skim. Mix about 3 to 4 graphic emails for every 1 text email. [65281424ea63]
15. **Different email types need different templates.** "A plain text founder email is going to have a completely different objective than some sort of educational FAQ email." Forcing all types into one copy or design template gives campaigns that "don't convert and don't make any sense". [7c77592817cb] [65281424ea63]
16. **You have about 3 seconds.** "The average customer is kind of scared of reading." Long form "doesn't convert nearly as well", though "at times long form content can work". So make emails "quick, snappy, digestible". Condition: 3 seconds is his framing, not a measured number. [28952f8a6673]
17. **Write copy and design together.** "As we write copy, we need to be thinking about how this is going to look as an email." People do not just read and do not just look at design; the two must be "intertwined". [28952f8a6673] [5b56681c9114]
18. **Every email must be skimmable, clear and concise, and engaging.** Skimmable: no blocks of copy, break things up, bold points and headlines. Clear and concise: short; nobody wants "to read an essay when they're in their inbox". Engaging: not boring; humour, fun, or genuinely educational content. Stats and fun facts are strong hooks: "Anytime I see a number, I love it." [28952f8a6673]
19. **Overload on infographics.** "This is the cheat code for email engagement." "Nobody wants to read blocks of copy. Your copy is just not going to be read." Point to AG1's ad library as a brand that shows everything visually rather than writing straight copy. Condition: his opinion, no test data. [5b56681c9114]
20. **Subject line and preview text are the headline and sub-headline.** He says subject lines "don't have a massive effect on open rates" and that "people should be opening emails based off your sender name rather than the subject line". He calls this "kind of controversial". Write for intrigue without telling the full story, so the reader opens to find out why. [32c744be62a5]
21. **Do not over-segment broadcasts.** "You can just send it to your engaged email list. You don't need to go too crazy on segmentation, especially in 2026." He never defines "engaged". `[needs brand data: the brand's engaged segment rule, for example opened or clicked in the last N days, and its size]` [c284594478331]
22. **AI makes the strategy easier, but review everything.** A properly trained custom GPT gives "a really good starting point"; "you might have to go back and forth with it". "It's not going to be perfect all the time. So make sure that you review this." [8e823019286f]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Plan the month (manual or AI)

1. Set the monthly send count. His default: about 4 a week, about 16 a month, to the engaged list. [1dbc06e4564f]
2. Assign every slot a pillar before writing anything: educational, social proof, community and brand, product or collection, sales. Keep the 4 non-discount pillars roughly even. Keep sales small, about 1 a month by his main rule. [9077e5d437cb]
3. Assign every slot a format: graphic or text. Target about 3 to 4 graphic for each text email; at 16 a month, about 12 graphic and 4 text. Put text emails where a personal voice fits (founder note, mission, community); the card adds that the community and brand pillar is the natural home. [65281424ea63]
4. Fill each slot with one specific microtopic from the idea lists in section 7.1. Reject anything generic. [c6824f14bcd2]
5. For social proof slots, name the specific experience first, then pull matching testimonials from the website. [c6824f14bcd2]
6. Check the finished calendar: count per pillar, count per format, sends per week, number of sale emails. The source does not give a check step; the card adds "verify the output's pillar counts". [8e823019286f]

### 3.2 AI calendar and copy workflow (his custom GPT)

The ingest header and card describe it as 2 steps. [8e823019286f]

1. Prime: give the model the brand, product details, brand voice, target audience and unique selling points. In his demo the GPT asks questions about the brand and he fills them in from the brand's site. `[needs brand data: brand voice, product facts, audience, USPs]`
2. Ask for a calendar balanced across the 5 content types. In his GPT: open the main menu, choose "create me an email campaign calendar" (he types "D" for "write me an email marketing calendar"), or "give me email campaign ideas" and build the calendar yourself `[verify UI]`.
3. Review the calendar. His sample returned 3, 4, 3 and 3 campaigns over 4 weeks, with 4 educational, 3 social proof, 2 community and brand, 2 product and collection, and 3 sales. These totals do not match (13 sends vs 14 typed emails); check counts yourself.
4. Ask it to write one campaign. In his GPT, the calendar ends with "Do you want me to write copy?", or you choose "B" for "write me an email marketing campaign" `[verify UI]`. It splits the email into sections and gives several variations per section, plus subject lines and preview text.
5. Instruction shape (from the ingest header, not spoken in the talk): a punchy headline, a sub-headline, and exactly 2 to 3 sentences of body copy leading to a CTA.
6. Pick the best variation per section. Ask for extra pieces as needed, for example "give me the copy for this infographic".
7. Fact-check every product and ingredient claim against the brand's own site. In the demo the infographic copy was "pulling this straight from the website". [8e823019286f] `[needs brand data: approved product and ingredient claims]`
8. Fix what the AI missed. His founder email draft had no CTA; he prompted "You forgot a call to action." [8e823019286f] [65281424ea63]

### 3.3 Build a graphic email (his "winning layout")

1. Headline that names a specific problem the reader has. Example: "Brain fog has met its match." [7c77592817cb]
2. Sub-headline that adds the mechanism or intrigue. Example: "What ancient remedies and modern science agree on." [7c77592817cb]
3. Short body copy, a few sentences, that explains the mechanism. Do not add much. [7c77592817cb]
4. First CTA button, for example "Shop now". [7c77592817cb]
5. Hero graphic or image. [7c77592817cb]
6. Infographic section that educates on the mechanism, for example an ingredient breakdown with one short label per ingredient. [5b56681c9114]
7. Final product and CTA section. Choose the offer to push (his options: starter kit, 15-pack, or subscription; he chose the 15-pack) and link the CTA to the product page. [7c77592817cb]
8. Footer.
9. Before design, lay it out in Google Docs as a table with one row per section and paste the chosen copy into each row, so you can see the email. Then hand to design. [7c77592817cb]
10. Keep total copy very low. "Notice here again how little copy there is because this is the strategy." [7c77592817cb]

He says design is covered in a separate video. This source gives no design specs (widths, fonts, image sizes, mobile rules).

### 3.4 Write a text-based founder email

1. Ask for a founder note as a text-based email, for example "Write me the note from the founder: why I built [brand]." [65281424ea63]
2. Keep a first-person, casual voice from a real person. Examples: "Hey, James here..." and Breeze's "Hey yo, the most common question I get is, 'What does this taste like?'" signed "much love". [65281424ea63]
3. Keep it short. Tell a before state, the problem, why the product exists, and who it is for.
4. Add a PS if useful (his example: "PS: here's exactly how I use it every morning before deep work").
5. Add a CTA if the draft lacks one, for example "Here's how I use it every morning. Try Magic Mind", linked to the product page. [65281424ea63]

### 3.5 A/B test short vs long

1. Take the short text email. [b840c4db6372]
2. Ask the AI for a variation "versus a longer form one that is 250 words". [b840c4db6372]
3. Run it as an A/B test. The card adds: keep the offer and CTA destination the same so only length changes, and apply the winner to that pillar before testing other things. The source shows no test result and no sample size rule. [b840c4db6372] `[needs brand data: list size and conversion rate to size the test]`

### 3.6 Write subject line and preview text

1. Treat the subject line as the email's headline and the preview text as its sub-headline. [32c744be62a5]
2. Create intrigue without giving the full story. Example: "Why adaptogens are in every bottle of Magic Mind" or "The hidden power behind your calm focus" (does not even say "adaptogens"). [32c744be62a5]
3. Make the preview text build on the subject, for example "Discover how this keeps you more sharp". [32c744be62a5]
4. Rely on the sender name and the steady cadence to drive opens. The card adds: test subject lines only when you have spare testing capacity. [32c744be62a5]

### 7.1 Idea lists by pillar [9077e5d437cb] [c6824f14bcd2]

- **Educational**: FAQs, ingredient highlights or ingredient education, how it's made, label decoder, how you've been using it wrong, what's not in our product, what happens if you stop using it, explain a specific benefit, overcoming objections.
- **Social proof**: testimonial emails, breaking down the numbers, real people and real results, influencers, media features, customer testimonials.

- **Product or collection highlights**: trending now, product spotlights, seasonal must-haves, gift guides, product breakdowns.
- **Sales**: teasers, launch emails, gift with purchase, mystery offers.
