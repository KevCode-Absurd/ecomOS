## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator run text as a 1-to-1 conversation instead of a broadcast. It gives: a 2-part first text, a 5-minute delay after opt-in, short-message rules, a human handoff after the first reply, tone tactics, and 3 metrics (reply rate, show-up rate, close rate). It is a vendor pitch with thin evidence. It is best used as a list of test ideas for conversational text, not as proven rules. It says nothing about campaigns, segmentation, cadence or cost.

## 2. Principles

1. **Conversations, not broadcasts.** Source says trust leads to conversion, so every text should open a dialogue and end with a question that asks the lead for one more fact about themselves or their intent. Condition: fits high-consideration buys and sales-led funnels. The source does not address promos or launches to a large list. Evidence: presenter opinion. [324169289900] [dc0288dd127b]
2. **People are tired of blasted, AI-sounding texts.** Source claims 2025 saw text deliverability "tanking", more STOP replies and more junk marking, because businesses send the same texts written "with the exact same AI prompts". Condition: no data given. `[needs brand data: SMS delivery, opt-out and complaint rate trend over the last 12 months]`
3. **"Go blue": send by iMessage, not green SMS.** Source says iPhone users only welcome blue-bubble texts from people, and that SMS "is becoming a saturated and regulated industry". Condition: only where most recipients use iPhones; card adds, do not assume blue bubbles help every audience. Evidence: vendor claim, vendor product. [324169289900] `[needs brand data: iPhone share of list]` `[web check: Apple rules on business use of iMessage and account limits]`
4. **Meet them where they live.** Source says Messages is the most used native iPhone app, and tells the viewer to "forget about email", calling and DMs. Condition: opinion; it conflicts with the rest of this folder (section 9). `[web check: current usage data for the Messages app]`
5. **Be a person, not a business.** Source says people do not care about faceless brand texts, automated reminders or promos; they reply to a person. Card adds a limit: identify the store and do not pretend an automated message is a personal friend. [324169289900] [3e359a2bee9b]
6. **The first goal is one relevant reply.** Source calls the reply "the one simple urgent metric". Once the lead replies, "it's game on". [dc0288dd127b] [3f1fb0650452]
7. **Cut all form friction to get the phone number.** Source: ask only for name and phone. Drop email, company and job title. Card condition: keep clear marketing consent and sender purpose; a phone number alone is not a marketing opt-in; do not drop anything checkout needs. [59e016b15ca2]
8. **The first reply is the starting gate, not the finish line.** After a reply, the job is to keep trust and move the lead to the next pipeline stage. [3e359a2bee9b]
9. **Automation sends the first touch; a human takes the conversation.** The "human handoff rule": the moment the first reply lands, a live person takes over. This is how the source says you scale personal outreach. Card condition: needs staffing, a response-time target and the order and product context passed to the agent. [3e359a2bee9b]
10. **If it feels automated or AI-written, trust is lost.** Source: you lose "their trust", "their replies" and "their deal". Condition: opinion. [dc0288dd127b]
11. **Use rich media to build familiarity.** Source calls video and voice notes "the number one way" for leads to feel familiar with you. Card condition: use only when channel and shopper support it, show the actual SKU, and give a text alternative. [bafced8b02e6]
12. **Drop delivery and open rates; track reply, show-up and close.** Source says old metrics can be "thrown away". Card disagrees: still track delivery and opt-outs, and add support resolution, purchases and net contribution after message cost. [3f1fb0650452]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Opt-in to first reply (source's sequence)
The source gives the steps and the example lines, not platform settings.
1. Form: ask for name and phone number only. Keep the consent text and the sender purpose on the form. [59e016b15ca2] `[web check: required consent wording for marketing texts in the brand's country]`
2. Wait 5 minutes after the opt-in before the first text. Source reason: it feels like a real person saw the submission. Card: treat 5 minutes as a test start, inside any send-time rules. [dc0288dd127b] [verify UI: delay step in the flow builder]
3. Text 1: "Hey [first name], this is [your name] from [company]." Source example: "Hey, John, this is Tristan from SendBlue."
4. Text 2, 5 seconds later: 1 simple open question with low friction. Source examples (lead-gen): "Are you new to industry X?", "Have you done X type of offer before?", "How many X projects have you done this year?" Do not tell them anything yet. The only goal is a reply.
5. Card version for a store: deliver any promised signup benefit, name the store, and ask 1 useful question about product preference or intended use, only if the answer will change the help you give. Card warns against splitting 1 message into 2 just to look human. [dc0288dd127b] `[needs brand data: per-message cost, so a 2-part intro can be priced]`

### 3.2 After the first reply
1. Hand the thread to a live person at once. [3e359a2bee9b]
2. Route to a support or product-specialist queue. Pass product and order context so the shopper does not repeat it. [3e359a2bee9b]
3. Answer the question. Offer the right product, size guide or cart link. [3e359a2bee9b] [324169289900]
4. Keep each text under 80 characters, 1 or 2 lines. No "brick text". [dc0288dd127b]
5. For anything longer than 80 characters, send a voice note or a short video instead. Card: add a text alternative. [bafced8b02e6]
6. Set a response target and tell shoppers when a person is available. [3e359a2bee9b] `[needs brand data: support hours and staffing]`

### 3.3 Tone tactics (source's "expert level")
1. Use common abbreviations and small mistakes. Source example: "Just got your call booked for Tuesday. What kind of business do you have?", with "just" shortened and "Tuesday" abbreviated (the transcript loses the exact spelling). See the conflict in section 9 before using this.
2. Use iMessage text effects. Source picks: lasers for a follow-up that must stand out, invisible ink for a lead who has been ghosting, confetti to celebrate a sign-up or client win. iMessage only. [verify UI]
3. Use video: an intro before a call, a demo of the product, or as a sales video. Use voice notes: appointment reminders, or answers too long for 80 characters. [bafced8b02e6]
4. Mix formats: texts, videos, voice notes, memes and effects.

### 3.4 Split tests the source lists
1. Greeting: "hey" vs "hi" vs "hello" vs "what's up".
2. First-text delay: 5 vs 7 minutes. [dc0288dd127b]
3. Voice note vs video.
4. iMessage vs SMS reply rate. Source calls this "the most important split test". [3f1fb0650452]
5. Show-up: a voice note 15 minutes before the call vs a plain text reminder; video vs voice note.
6. Card addition for any test: consistent attribution windows and a comparable control; judge on purchases and opt-outs, not replies alone. [3f1fb0650452] `[needs brand data: baseline reply, opt-out and conversion rates for the flow under test]`

## 5. Diagnostics and checks

1. **Is the audience on iPhone?** Check device or OS share of the list. Good: a large iPhone majority. Bad: mostly Android. Next: if bad, the iMessage advice does not apply; keep only the conversation tactics. `[needs brand data: device share]`
2. **Are texts getting replies?** Check reply rate per flow and per first-text variant. Next: test greeting, delay and question (3.4). [3f1fb0650452] `[needs brand data: reply rate by flow]`
3. **Are replies turning into money?** Check purchases, support resolution and net contribution after message cost, against a control. Bad: reply rate up, purchases flat. Next: fix the handoff and the answer quality, not the opener. [3f1fb0650452]
4. **Is the handoff working?** Check time from first reply to human response, and whether the agent sees order and product context. Bad: shopper repeats the question or waits hours. [3e359a2bee9b]
5. **Is the form too long?** Compare signup completion with downstream replies, purchases and opt-outs after cutting fields. Bad: more signups, but higher opt-outs or fewer buyers. [59e016b15ca2]
6. **Does it look like a blast?** Read the first text aloud. Bad: a wall of text, a pitch in text 1, no question. Next: apply 3.1.
7. **For appointments only:** check show-up rate and close rate, and whether the sales cycle got shorter. Keep these apart from product-inquiry metrics. [3f1fb0650452]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Blasting the same text to everyone. [324169289900]
2. Texts that sound automated or AI-written.
3. Long forms that ask for email, company and job title.
4. Instant replies to an opt-in that feel robotic.
5. Telling the lead things in text 1 instead of asking a question.
6. Treating the first reply as the finish line.
7. "Brick text": a wall of words.
8. Keeping automation running after the lead replies. [3e359a2bee9b]
9. Judging text on delivery and open rates only.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Vendor bias.** Every core claim (go blue, 2x lift, SMS is dying) supports the product the presenter sells. The 2x figure is replies, not purchases. [3f1fb0650452]
- **Compliance claims are unsafe as stated.** The source says iMessage is "set free" from A2P rules and "reply STOP" language. Consent and opt-out duties under laws like TCPA may not depend on bubble colour, and Apple may limit business use. Do not remove opt-out handling on this source's word. `[web check: TCPA, CTIA, Apple policy, and local law on business iMessage consent and opt-out]`
- **Deliberate typos and "be a person" vs honest identity.** Card [324169289900] says identify the store and do not pretend an automated message is a friend. The source's mistakes-on-purpose tactic also clashes with ecomOS copy rules on clarity. Treat it as a test idea at most, never on client pages.
- **2-part message vs card.** Source sends 2 texts 5 seconds apart. Card [dc0288dd127b] says do not split 1 message just to look human. Each part may be billed. Test before adopting.
- **"Forget about email"** conflicts with the rest of this folder, which treats email as a core channel.
- **Conflict with source 05 (Postscript, RCS).** Source 05 bets on RCS with SMS fallback, more sends and pop-ups; this source bets on iMessage and 1-to-1 threads. Both are vendors selling their channel. Both agree on asking questions and mining replies. Neither has independent proof.
- **Not ecommerce-native.** Show-up and close rate fit appointments and sales calls. For a store, cards swap them for purchases, support resolution and net contribution. [3f1fb0650452]
- **Missing:** cost per message, staffing needs, send-time rules, campaign cadence, segmentation, Android handling, fallback when iMessage fails, how the 5-minute and 80-character numbers were found, and any flow steps for abandoned cart.
- **Geography.** "North Americans have iPhones" is the premise. In Android-heavy markets such as India, iMessage reach is small. `[needs brand data: device share]` `[web check: iPhone market share in the brand's country]`
