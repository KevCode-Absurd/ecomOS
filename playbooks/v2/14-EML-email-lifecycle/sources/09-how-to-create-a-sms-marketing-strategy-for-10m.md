## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator set up or audit an SMS program that runs alongside email for a Shopify brand. It covers: choosing and pricing the SMS platform, growing the phone list through the popup, controlling SMS cost (length, emojis, images), the core automated flows (welcome, 4 abandonment flows, post-purchase, replenishment, win-back), campaign frequency and calendar, and segmentation with a simple exclusion list. The source is US-focused. It gives almost no compliance detail beyond the abandonment rule and consent.

## 2. Principles

1. **The SMS platform barely matters; the basics drive revenue.** Condition: choosing or renegotiating a platform. Source says Klaviyo, Attentive and Postscript "are all great", the extra AI features are ones "you're probably never going to use", and "the basics will drive 99% of your revenue". Prefer SMS in the same platform as email for ease, but make vendors compete on price. Opinion from agency experience. [ef374a64ba7e]
2. **The SMS list only earns as much as its size, so collect phones from day 1.** Condition: any brand, even one with no SMS program yet. If you start collecting now and launch SMS in a year, you have a list; if you wait, you start at zero. [51b9472f8d06]
3. **The popup is the main list growth lever.** Condition: store with paid traffic. Source says it "cannot stress enough" how important the popup is: most forms he reviews submit at 5 to 6%, his format reaches about 15%, which he calls 3 times the leads and 3 times the list growth. Example figures, not benchmarks. [51b9472f8d06] [75893d2892aa]
4. **Engage before you ask for contact details.** Condition: popup build. Source says a quiz or micro-commitment first "is what works in 2026", then ask for email, then phone. Presenter's experience. [75893d2892aa]
5. **SMS is high-urgency and low-frequency, not a nurture channel.** Condition: all SMS sends. Source's evidence: SMS unsubscribe rates of 3 to 5% versus about 0.3% on email, because a text is a phone "ding". Nurture content (testimonials, evergreen best sellers, brand stories) "just doesn't work on SMS" and raises unsubscribes over time. Email can carry daily nurture; SMS cannot. [e6323f097649]
6. **SMS costs real money per send, so length, emojis and images are cost decisions.** Condition: all sends, and more so as the list grows (source cites 100,000 subscribers). Email costs a fraction of a cent; SMS costs 1 to 3 cents per message, typically about 1.5 cents, depending on platform and plan. [0228dbaf571e] [13f23c2e8c94] [needs brand data: current per-message SMS and MMS rates on the brand's plan and destination country]
7. **Judge images (MMS) on net profit, not revenue or clicks.** Condition: any send where you consider an image. MMS costs about 3 times an SMS. In the source's tests the image version made more revenue but got fewer clicks, because people who click after seeing the product are higher intent. Sometimes it pays, often it does not. [13f23c2e8c94]
8. **Save images for important sends.** Condition: campaign and flow design. Source points to a 9-figure brand (Grüns) that rarely uses images and keeps texts short, but adds images for a new flavor drop, a new product release, its abandonment text and its welcome flow (infographics comparing it to competitors), then drops to plain text. [13f23c2e8c94]
9. **Keep flows dead simple; the core flows make most of the money.** Condition: every brand, including 8 and 9 figure brands. Source says all other automations "come down to these core basics". [3cf40cda9103]
10. **Hit new subscribers while they are warm.** Condition: welcome flow. Send inside a tight window (about 5 days), not spread over 2 weeks. [6c2a73b99726]
11. **Do not over-send after purchase.** Condition: post-purchase flows. Buyers also receive your campaigns, so 2 well-timed post-purchase texts are enough at first. [586799a976b3]
12. **Make every campaign worth receiving; do not force sends.** Condition: campaign calendar. Source would rather you save sends for big announcements than lose subscribers, but still send at least 1 campaign a week. [ec74b2c055f2] [7eaef73fe8eb]
13. **Broadcast to the whole list; do not over-segment.** Condition: SMS campaigns. Source says people who never click a text still buy (they see the text and go to the site on their own), so narrow "clicked in 90 days" segments lose money. Only exclude chronically unengaged numbers. Presenter says this usually makes him the most money. [74853693497f]
14. **This is a starting point, not a rule set.** Condition: always. Source runs daily SMS for some brands and "super intricate flows" for others. Test delays, offers and images in your own brand context. [e62e0ea0b99b]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Choose the SMS platform
1. Default: run SMS in the same platform as email (Klaviyo with Klaviyo, Omnisend with Omnisend, Attentive with Attentive). [ef374a64ba7e]
2. Ask the current email provider (usually Klaviyo) for an SMS quote. [ef374a64ba7e]
3. Take that quote to the main competitors, Attentive and Postscript ("the big dogs"). OneText and others exist; the source prefers the big ones. Ask them to beat the quote. [ef374a64ba7e]
4. Decide on price and ease of management. Do not decide on AI features. [ef374a64ba7e] [web check: current SMS pricing and contract terms for each vendor in the brand's country]
5. Source is silent on migration, number types (short code, toll-free, 10DLC) and sender registration. [web check: sender number types and registration rules in the brand's country]

### 3.2 Build a popup that captures phone numbers (Alia walk-through)
1. Use Alia (the source says "all pop-ups.com"; card spells allpopups.com) for the popup, not the native Klaviyo form. Native Klaviyo forms are fine to use but, in the source's experience, convert lower. [51b9472f8d06] [verify UI] [web check: Alia product name and URL]
2. In Alia, go to Campaigns and create a new popup with the builder. [verify UI] [75893d2892aa]
3. Pick a template. Start with a quiz format. The shown template offers "You've got 10% off", asks the visitor a question, then moves to the email step. [75893d2892aa] [verify UI]
4. Add the logo and brand details. [verify UI]
5. On the email step, change the button text from "Continue" to "Claim now". "Continue" signals another required step (the phone number) and causes drop-off. [75893d2892aa]
6. On the phone step, replace copy like "Sign up for texts to be the first to know about news" or "Get exclusive deals" with "Finish signing up with text to activate your discount." The visitor already gave an email and wants the discount activated. [75893d2892aa]
7. Do not offer an extra discount (for example an extra 5%) for the phone number. Source has seen no meaningful impact. [75893d2892aa]
8. Final step: the setup depends on the SMS platform. On Klaviyo, source recommends a simple "Shop now" button; Postscript and Attentive need "a different sort of setup", which the source does not describe. [verify UI] [75893d2892aa]
9. Track phone submit rate as a headline metric. [51b9472f8d06] [needs brand data: current email and phone submit rates by device and traffic source]
10. Consent language and legal disclosures on the phone step are not covered by the source. [web check: required SMS consent wording for the brand's country]

### 3.3 Write and cost each SMS
1. Keep the message under 160 characters. Source says at 161 characters it becomes 2 SMS and you pay double. [0228dbaf571e]
2. Limit emojis. Source says 1 emoji uses about 60 to 70 characters. The card corrects this: an emoji or any Unicode character can switch the whole message to Unicode encoding, which drops 1 segment to 70 characters. See section 9. [0228dbaf571e]
3. Preview the fully rendered message, including personalization, links and opt-out text, and read the platform's segment counter (card step, not in the source text). [0228dbaf571e] [verify UI]
4. Check the credits each message uses before sending. [0228dbaf571e] [verify UI]
5. Only add an image when the send is important enough to justify about 3 times the cost. [13f23c2e8c94]

### 3.4 Test SMS versus MMS
1. Build 2 versions of the same message, one with an image and one without. [13f23c2e8c94]
2. Track revenue and send cost for each. [13f23c2e8c94]
3. Compare net profit: extra revenue minus extra send cost. Do not decide on revenue alone or clicks alone (image versions often get fewer clicks). [13f23c2e8c94]
4. Copy can differ by version: the no-image text "teases" the click ("click here to view the product"); the image text can say "click here to buy". [13f23c2e8c94]
5. Retest as the list grows, because cost scales with list size. [13f23c2e8c94] [needs brand data: list size, per-send SMS and MMS cost]

### 3.5 Welcome flow (trigger: joins through the popup, usually with a discount)
Goal: get the first order on the discount and introduce the brand. [6c2a73b99726]
1. Day 1, immediately: intro text with the discount and no preamble. Template: "Welcome to [brand]. [1 short remark, for example we focus on clean ingredients.] Use code WELCOME20 for 20% off your first order. Shop now." [6c2a73b99726]
2. Day 3 (about 2 days later): reminder with a bit of brand information or how the brand differs. Optional in the 2-text version. [6c2a73b99726]
3. Day 5 (about 2 days later): absolute last chance. Template: "Hey there, noticed you signed up but haven't shopped 20% off yet. Don't miss out, this is about to expire. Shop now." [6c2a73b99726]
4. In the 2-text version, the source says wait "5 days or something" between text 1 and the last chance. [6c2a73b99726]
5. Aggressive alternative (Grüns): 1 text every day; day 1 "Welcome to the family, here's your discount for 52% off", then educational texts and discount reminders. Source says it is valid but prefers the 3-text structure. [6c2a73b99726]
6. A/B test adding images (like Grüns' comparison infographics) to see if the lift beats the cost. [6c2a73b99726] [e62e0ea0b99b]

### 3.6 Abandonment flows (4 flows, 1 text each)
Definitions: [3cf40cda9103]
- Site abandoned (also called session abandoned): visited the site, viewed no product page.
- Browse abandoned: viewed a product page.
- Cart abandoned: added to cart, then left.
- Checkout abandoned: started checkout, then left.

Steps: [ee1a31c09fcc]
1. Confirm the recipient has SMS consent.
2. Send exactly 1 SMS per recipient per abandonment flow, within 48 hours of the event. Source says more than 1 is illegal in the US and shows Klaviyo enforcing 1 SMS within 48 hours in the cart abandoned flow. [verify UI] [web check: current US rule and whether it comes from law, carrier (CTIA) rules or platform policy; rules for other countries]
3. Browse abandoned: wait 60 minutes after the browse event. Text: "Have your eye on something? You have good taste. Keep shopping." Optional: MMS with the product left behind.
4. Cart and checkout abandoned: "Looks like you forgot something in your cart. Finish your order." More aggressive options: "Your order is ready to ship" or "We can only save your cart for so long."
5. Split by whether the person has bought before. Non-buyers get their welcome discount in the text (Wild example: "Use code WILDWELCOME20 at checkout to get 20% off"). Source calls this "a high lever" for 8 and 9 figure brands. [needs brand data: whether abandoners already received the welcome code and the discount policy]
6. Test plain text versus a product image.
7. Source gives no delay for site, cart or checkout abandoned texts. Only browse has a delay (60 minutes).

### 3.7 Post-purchase, replenishment and win-back flow
Source says this flow "varies so much brand to brand" that it cannot give a verified template. It gives key send points. You can put all of them in 1 flow triggered by order placement. [586799a976b3] [c2903035a7f5]
1. SMS 1, day 10 to 14 (a couple of days after delivery, the "honeymoon phase"): "Are you liking this product?" plus a cross-sell, upsell or buy-again prompt. No offer. [586799a976b3] [needs brand data: typical delivery time]
2. SMS 2, replenishment brands only: send when the customer may be running low. Example: 30-day supply plus 7 days to deliver, send about day 35: "Are you running low? Place another order." No offer at first; source says not until "later down the line". [586799a976b3] [needs brand data: days of supply per product, delivery time]
3. Win-back trigger: 60 to 90 days with no new order, or (rule of thumb) 2 times the brand's average time between purchases. Example: 45 days average, trigger at day 90. [c2903035a7f5] [needs brand data: average days between orders]
4. Win-back SMS 1 at the trigger: flash offer. Example: "Get 30% off just because we miss you. Here's an exclusive discount code. It lasts for 72 hours." [c2903035a7f5] [needs brand data: margin that can carry the offer]
5. Wait 2 days. Win-back SMS 2: "Last chance." [c2903035a7f5]
6. Do not add more post-purchase texts at first; buyers also get campaigns. [586799a976b3]

### 3.8 Campaign calendar
1. Set the base frequency: about 2 campaigns a week; at least 1 a week. [ec74b2c055f2]
2. Adjust by audience: [ec74b2c055f2]
   - Aggressive acquisition brands (Grüns, big ad spend): daily texts to people who never bought are fine, if the list is big and there is news.
   - Past buyers of one-time products or subscriptions: send less.
   - Active subscribers of a subscription brand: send no texts.
3. Fill slots only with urgent, timely news: launches, back in stock and restocks, sales and flash sales, SMS-exclusive offers, private events, insider details, "selling fast this week" trend alerts, "big news, this is happening now". [7eaef73fe8eb] [e6323f097649]
4. For a launch, send the announcement, then a reminder 2 days later (example: the 4th, then the 6th). Same for a list-exclusive flash sale (example: the 8th, then the 10th). [7eaef73fe8eb]
5. For big sale pushes, raise to 1 a day or 2 a day, then return to base frequency. [ec74b2c055f2]
6. Example monthly calendar from the source: social media giveaway, October collection launch, "running low" message, restock, flash sale with a doubled-up reminder. Source calls it a baseline and would "maybe up the frequency a little". [7eaef73fe8eb]
7. Source uses "restock" and "running low" framing even when the product did not restock or is not running low. See sections 8 and 9 before copying this. [7eaef73fe8eb]

### 3.9 Segmentation and list cleaning
1. Default audience for campaigns: all SMS subscribers. [74853693497f]
2. Add 1 exclusion segment: received at least 15 texts and never clicked any. [74853693497f] [verify UI: segment conditions in the SMS platform]
3. Anyone who ever clicked a text, even 2 years ago, stays in. [74853693497f]
4. Clean the list about once a month. [74853693497f]
5. About 1 send a month goes to the whole list with no exclusion, for the biggest news (example: New Year's Eve sale). [74853693497f]
6. Do not build "clicked in last 90 days" engaged segments as the main audience. [74853693497f]

### 3.10 Testing after launch
1. Test time delays in flows. [e62e0ea0b99b]
2. Test offers (whether a discount is needed, and how deep). [e62e0ea0b99b]
3. Test images versus no images on net profit. [e62e0ea0b99b]
4. Treat small tests as directional. [e62e0ea0b99b]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Asking only for email in the popup because "we don't have an SMS list". [51b9472f8d06]
- Using "Continue" as the email-step button; it warns people another step is coming. [75893d2892aa]
- Using generic SMS opt-in copy ("sign up for texts", "get exclusive deals"). [75893d2892aa]
- Adding a bigger discount for the phone number. [75893d2892aa]
- Agonizing over the SMS platform or paying for AI features you will not use. [ef374a64ba7e]
- Sending nurture content (testimonials, evergreen best sellers) by SMS. [e6323f097649]
- Long texts that split into 2 segments; emojis that inflate the cost. [0228dbaf571e]
- Adding images by habit; they cost 3x and often do not pay. [13f23c2e8c94]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Emoji cost conflict.** Source says 1 emoji takes about 60 to 70 characters. The card corrects it: an emoji can switch the whole message to Unicode, which cuts 1 segment from 160 to 70 characters; multipart limits differ. Follow the card and the platform's segment counter. The card cites a Klaviyo help article, which is card content, not source content. [0228dbaf571e]
- **Abandonment rule basis.** Source calls more than 1 abandonment text "illegal in the US" and shows Klaviyo's 1-text, 48-hour limit. Whether this is law, carrier rules (CTIA) or platform policy is not explained. [ee1a31c09fcc] [web check: US abandonment SMS rules, TCPA, CTIA guidance, state laws such as Florida and Oklahoma, quiet hours]
- **Country differences.** Source is US-only. SMS consent, sender registration, quiet hours, promotional templates and opt-out wording differ by country. For India (Gehna), check TRAI DLT registration, header and template approval, and promotional time windows. For UK and EU, check PECR and GDPR consent rules. [web check: SMS marketing rules for each target country]
- **Compliance gaps.** No detail on opt-in wording, double opt-in, required opt-out text ("Reply STOP"), quiet hours, frequency disclosure or data retention. Only "that person needs to have consent" is stated. [web check: consent and opt-out requirements]
- **"Five key flows" count.** Source says 5 flows, then lists 6 (welcome, site, browse, cart, checkout abandoned, post-purchase or win-back). [3cf40cda9103]
- **Welcome timing is loose.** 3-text version is day 1, 3, 5; 2-text version waits "5 days or something"; Wild's example waits 3 days then 2 days. All are fine per the source. [6c2a73b99726]
- **Replenishment math.** Example is day 35 for 30 days of supply plus 7 days delivery; the product would run out near day 37, so day 35 lands just before. Source gives no general formula. [586799a976b3]
- **Win-back trigger.** "60 to 90 days" and "2x average time between purchases" are both given; the 2x rule is the source's rule of thumb. [c2903035a7f5]
- **Frequency is opinion.** Ranges from at least 1 a week to 2 a day in sale pushes; depends on list size, brand news and consent. [ec74b2c055f2]
- **Segmentation vs common advice.** Common email and SMS advice favors engaged segments. The source argues the opposite for SMS: broadcast with a 15-sends-0-clicks exclusion. Test on the brand's own revenue per send and unsubscribe rate. [74853693497f]
- **Card 74853693497f has a garbled line** ("exclude anyone you've bought from in ways that make them a poor target"). The source's actual point is to send less to past buyers and nothing to active subscribers (from the campaign section).
- **Case numbers are loose.** Flow revenue items do not sum to the quoted $517,000; MMS test costs are stated inconsistently; "60% of ad traffic" is likely a caption error for 16%.
- **"98% open rate"** is stated without a source; do not reuse as a fact. [web check: SMS open rate claim]
- **Popup setup for Postscript and Attentive** is not described. [verify UI]
- **Missing:** SMS send times of day, A/B test sample sizes, how to measure SMS attribution versus email, how to handle overlap between email and SMS in the same flow, list import, and subscriber cost or LTV. [needs brand data: SMS attributed revenue, attribution window, overlap with email]
