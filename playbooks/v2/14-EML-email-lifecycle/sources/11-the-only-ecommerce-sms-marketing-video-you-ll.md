## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator launch a basic SMS channel for an ecommerce store, mostly in Klaviyo. It covers: why SMS is worth it, platform choice and first setup, list growth through a 2-step email then SMS pop-up, 5 sending principles (direct content, low frequency, explicit opt-in, legal send hours, pairing with email), 3 core omni-channel flows (welcome, abandoned cart, browse abandonment) with a strict message limit, a sample monthly campaign calendar, and KPIs with fixes and a review cadence. It is a baseline, not an advanced guide. It is thin on list growth beyond the pop-up, on abandoned cart and browse copy, and on legal detail.

## 2. Principles

1. **SMS is an owned retention channel, like email.** The list is yours. It does not depend on an algorithm, ad auctions or rising ad costs ("Uncle Zuckerberg or Google can't just shut you down"). Once built, it earns "month after month". Condition: only for subscribers who actually opted in to SMS. [76ea7acfff13]
2. **SMS reaches people fastest.** Source says 98% open rates and most texts opened within 3 minutes. No source is given for either figure. `[web check: current SMS open-rate and time-to-open stats and their origin]` [76ea7acfff13]
3. **Done right, SMS should be at least 5 to 15% of total brand sales.** Presenter's agency heuristic, not an industry figure. Use it as a rough bar to judge if the channel is under-built. `[needs brand data: SMS-attributed revenue as a share of total sales, and the attribution window used]` [76ea7acfff13]
4. **SMS is simple and cheap to run.** No design work, quick to set up. Source says several clients spend under $1,000 a month on their SMS bill and make "in excess of tens of thousands of dollars in revenue per month". Case claim, not a promise. `[needs brand data: monthly SMS bill vs SMS revenue]` [76ea7acfff13]
5. **SMS is becoming mobile-first table stakes.** Source says competitors are moving mobile-first and brands that treat SMS as a nice-to-have fall behind. Opinion, no data. [76ea7acfff13]
6. **The platform matters little; pick for email and SMS in one place.** Klaviyo, Attentive and Postscript "all do the same thing. They just send out simple text messages." Presenter prefers Klaviyo because email and SMS sit in 1 flow view instead of email in Klaviyo and SMS in Attentive or Postscript. Opinion. [af694ec3e7e4] [136997a1cb23]
7. **A bigger list makes more sales for the same work, with diminishing returns.** Example: 5,000 subscribers make about 100 orders a month. Double to 10,000 with the same campaigns and you get about 160 to 180 orders, not 200. Condition: the added subscribers must be qualified buyers ("so long that they're actually qualified buyers"). Illustrative numbers, not data. Not growing the list caps the channel. [76ea7acfff13] [ac0dbb555ee6]
8. **The pop-up is by far the top SMS list-growth method.** Source says it has 7 growth methods but only shows the pop-up (plus a JOIN keyword in setup). Pop-up detail is "a whole other video". [ac0dbb555ee6] [9b9305f19394]
9. **Ask for 1 thing at a time.** Email first, then phone on the next step. Smaller hurdles give "way better conversion rates" than one form asking for name, occupation, location, email and phone. Evidence: agency experience, no numbers. [9b9305f19394]
10. **Give the best offer up front on the email step; do not add a bigger SMS bonus.** Many brands give a smaller email offer plus an SMS extra (for example 15% email, extra 5% SMS). Presenter prefers the full offer on the email step to maximize email sign-ups, then re-words the SMS step so a "very good amount" still opt in to SMS. Contested: this is his preference against a common alternative. [9b9305f19394]
11. **Send only what matters.** Vague, broad, general SMS campaigns are "your worst enemy". A short text cannot hold attention with bland content. Use clear, direct reasons to click: sales, early access, restocks, new product launch, back-in-stock alerts. Condition: the card notes this does not ban a well-made brand message; vague content is the failure mode. [db06cdf5cc52]
12. **Respect the relationship: SMS is intimate.** A text makes people pick up the phone. Keep frequency low. Sweet spot about 1 campaign a week. Over 8 figures a year in revenue, test 2 a week. More than 2 is "likely going to be overkill": higher unsubscribes, diminishing returns, annoyed customers. [6f21ea67f481]
13. **Stay compliant: SMS law is stricter than email law.** Never send SMS marketing to anyone who has not explicitly opted in to SMS. Build the SMS segment with care so no one without explicit SMS consent gets in. Source names no law. `[web check: current TCPA, CTIA and state rules for the brand's send countries]` [ccceb9ed6344]
14. **Do not send before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m.** Source says you "could open yourself up to potential fines". Source does not say whose time zone. `[web check: legal quiet hours per country and US state, and whether they use recipient local time]` [ccceb9ed6344] [a9df8fb1a06e]
15. **Best send window is late morning to early afternoon, about 11:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m.** People are on lunch or a break. Anecdotal "sweet spot we found". [a9df8fb1a06e]
16. **Pair SMS with the same day's email, spaced about 5 to 6 hours apart.** Example: email at 9:00 a.m., SMS at 1:00 p.m. The customer sees the same message twice on 2 devices. Use for launches and offers ("new arrival"). Treat the gap as a starting test. [a9df8fb1a06e]
17. **SMS flows catch intent at the right moment.** Triggered by actions such as sign-up, cart abandonment or browsing. An abandoned cart text a couple of hours after abandonment can reach someone who is on their phone but not reading email that day. [136997a1cb23]
18. **Flows are about 90% build once, then keep improving.** Not truly set and forget. After setup you split test, optimize and refresh "every so often". [136997a1cb23]
19. **Most SMS flows may hold only 1 SMS message. The welcome flow may hold 2.** Source calls more than 1 SMS in, for example, the abandoned cart flow "non-compliant". This is the presenter's stated understanding. It conflicts with common practice (see section 9). `[web check: whether any law, carrier rule or Klaviyo policy limits SMS messages per automated flow]` [136997a1cb23] [ccceb9ed6344]
20. **Build all SMS flows as omni-channel.** SMS and email blended in the same flow so they work together. [136997a1cb23]
21. **An extra incentive in the SMS flow raises conversion.** Source says if you give an extra incentive in the flow message, people are "more likely to convert". Card caveat: this can train customers to abandon on purpose; the source does not address it. `[needs brand data: margin room for a flow incentive]` [136997a1cb23]
22. **Judge SMS on clicks, unsubscribes and delivery, not opens.** Open rate is a vanity metric for SMS. [02f841a00c26]
23. **Frequency tolerance depends on purchase cycle.** Apparel might be bought about once a quarter, furniture about once every 18 months. "You're not going to buy a sofa on a whim from some sort of SMS campaign." Split test cadence within your category. `[needs brand data: repeat purchase interval]` [6f21ea67f481] [77dd2ea812e0]
24. **Header-only claims (not in transcript).** Keep each SMS within 160 characters so it is not split and billed as more than 1 message. MMS (image) messages cost much more than plain text; use them only when the image is essential. Condition: carrier and platform billing differ. `[web check: current SMS segment length and MMS pricing in the brand's SMS platform]` [db06cdf5cc52] [ccceb9ed6344]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Set up SMS in Klaviyo (source demo, US) `[verify UI]`
1. Click your company name (bottom left in the demo), then Settings, then SMS, then "Set up SMS for free". [af694ec3e7e4]
2. Choose sending country. Demo picks United States. Add Canada if you sell there, but Canadian numbers cost 2 SMS credits instead of 1. `[web check: current Klaviyo credit cost per country]` [af694ec3e7e4]
3. Fill in the form. Klaviyo then verifies your toll-free number. `[web check: toll-free verification time and requirements]` [af694ec3e7e4]
4. Select your company name as the sender name. Presenter keeps the default. [af694ec3e7e4]
5. Upload existing SMS subscribers from a CSV or other file. Condition: only people with explicit SMS consent (see principle 13). `[needs brand data: existing SMS list and its consent records]` [af694ec3e7e4] [ccceb9ed6344]
6. Create an SMS signup form. Also create the email pop-up signup form. The setup screen says you need a form to collect SMS subscribers. [af694ec3e7e4]
7. On the next page, under SMS message add-ons, set opt-out language. Default can be changed, for example to "unsub". `[web check: required opt-out keywords, since carriers expect STOP]` [af694ec3e7e4]
8. Sender preferences: set Smart Sending. When on, a person gets at most 1 SMS campaign in a 24-hour span. Toggle up or down as needed. [af694ec3e7e4]
9. SMS quiet hours: presenter says leave the default. When enabled, a message due during quiet hours is not sent or delivered at that time. `[web check: Klaviyo default quiet hours and whether it skips or delays]` [af694ec3e7e4] [a9df8fb1a06e]
10. Keyword responses: set a subscribe keyword, for example JOIN. Someone who texts JOIN to your number joins the SMS list and can claim a discount such as 10% off. Presenter promises more detail later but does not give it. [af694ec3e7e4] [ac0dbb555ee6]
11. Quick responses, short links and terms of service: presenter says "nothing interesting here. You can skip it." `[web check: whether terms and privacy links are required for compliance in the brand's countries]` [af694ec3e7e4]

### 3.2 Build the 2-step email then SMS pop-up
1. Strip the form to 1 field per step. Do not ask for first name, last name, occupation, location and email at once. [9b9305f19394]
2. Step 1: email. Lead with the full offer. Example headline: "You've got 20% off." [9b9305f19394]
3. Step 2: phone number. Do not add a new incentive. Frame the SMS step as what unlocks the offer already shown. Tested copy: "Finish signing up with text to activate 20% off." Source says it tested this "across many accounts" and found it "very, very successful". No numbers given. [9b9305f19394]
4. Delay the pop-up 6 to 10 seconds after page load (header only; not said in transcript). `[verify UI]` [9b9305f19394]
5. Track opt-in rate for each step on its own so you see where people drop (card only). `[needs brand data: step-level opt-in rates]` [9b9305f19394]
6. If unsure between "best offer up front" and "smaller email offer plus SMS bonus", test both. [9b9305f19394]

### 3.3 Build the 3 core omni-channel flows
The source says welcome, abandoned cart and browse abandonment drive "majority of the revenue". Only welcome copy is given.
1. **Welcome flow** (trigger: newsletter sign-up). SMS 1: thank them, give the first-purchase code, send them to shop. Example: "Hey, thanks for signing up. Here's your 10% off your first purchase. Use code [code], go ahead and shop." [136997a1cb23]
2. **Welcome flow SMS 2** (the only flow allowed a second SMS, per source): 2 to 3 days later, remind them of the offer. Example: "Hey, your 10% off is waiting. Come and check out our bestsellers." [136997a1cb23]
3. **Abandoned cart flow**: exactly 1 SMS. Timing example: "in a couple hours or so" after abandonment. Blend with the abandoned cart emails in the same flow. Optional extra incentive in the SMS if margin allows. Source gives no copy. `[needs brand data: discount policy and margin]` [136997a1cb23]
4. **Browse abandonment flow**: 1 SMS, blended with email. Source gives no trigger settings, timing or copy. [136997a1cb23]
5. Put email and SMS in the same flow in Klaviyo `[verify UI]`. Card adds: let email carry the long content and SMS the short reminder (card only). [136997a1cb23]
6. After launch: split test, optimize, refresh periodically. No cadence given. [136997a1cb23]

### 3.4 Plan a monthly SMS campaign calendar (source example: May, women's fashion)
1. Fix the biggest event first. Q2's biggest is Memorial Day (the 26th in the example). Give it several sends:
   - Thursday before sale start: VIP or SMS-subscriber early access.
   - Friday: Memorial Day sale SMS 1 (sale start).
   - Memorial Day itself: 1 SMS "for sure".
   [77dd2ea812e0]
2. Add gifting holidays that fit the audience: Mother's Day (the 11th in the example) for a list that may include mothers and gift buyers. [77dd2ea812e0]
3. Add seasonal sends: back-in-stock, "introducing our spring collection", mid-spring clearance that spotlights the sale section (example: up to 50% off). Source says sale-section spotlights "typically do very well". [77dd2ea812e0]
4. Add a product-focus send: the hero product from the new collection. [77dd2ea812e0]
5. Add a flash sale, for example "Flash Sale Friday", a 1-day or weekend sale on 1 collection or a few products. (Transcript says "this isn't normally a very popular one", which likely means "is normally"; unclear.) [77dd2ea812e0]
6. Add 1 brand or community-focused send with a call to action. [77dd2ea812e0]
7. Check the total. The example has about 2 sends a week. Source says your brand does not need that; aim for 1 to 2 a week at most and split test. [77dd2ea812e0] [6f21ea67f481]
8. Pair key sends with that day's email, SMS about 5 to 6 hours later, inside the 11:00 a.m. to 3:00 p.m. window and never before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. [a9df8fb1a06e]
9. Before each send, confirm the segment holds only explicit SMS opt-ins. [ccceb9ed6344]
`[needs brand data: own retail calendar, launches, restocks, sale dates and send-country holidays]`

### 3.5 Review cadence
1. Daily: open Klaviyo once and see how campaigns are doing. [02f841a00c26]
2. Weekly: check trends. Line up the week's campaigns. Check flows for anything off. Ask: are results down or spiking? Why? What did we do differently? What do we change next week? [02f841a00c26]
3. Monthly: compare to prior months. Look at the whole strategy. Pick a big lever not yet tried. Examples given: send more messages, send different types, or send 2 campaigns for the next big sale instead of 1 (example: Labor Day had 1). [02f841a00c26]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Not using SMS at all, so you miss where customers' attention is. [76ea7acfff13]
2. Leaving revenue on the table: SMS should be 5 to 15% or more of sales. [76ea7acfff13]
3. Treating SMS as a nice-to-have and falling behind competitors. [76ea7acfff13]
4. Spending too much without knowing what you are doing, or burning out the list. [76ea7acfff13]
5. Not growing the list, which caps the channel "before you even get started". [ac0dbb555ee6]
6. A pop-up asking for many fields at once. [9b9305f19394]
7. Vague, broad, general SMS campaigns. [db06cdf5cc52]
8. Sending too often: more than 2 campaigns a week drives unsubscribes and annoyance. [6f21ea67f481]
9. Sending SMS to anyone without explicit SMS opt-in. [ccceb9ed6344]
10. Sending before 8:00 a.m. or after 9:00 p.m. (fines risk; waking customers). [ccceb9ed6344]
11. More than 1 SMS in a non-welcome flow (source calls it non-compliant). [136997a1cb23]
12. Treating flows as set and forget. [136997a1cb23]
13. Copying a high cadence into a long purchase-cycle category like furniture. [6f21ea67f481]
14. Judging SMS by open rate. [02f841a00c26]
15. Header only: going over 160 characters and paying for 2 messages; using MMS when plain text would do. [db06cdf5cc52]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **1 SMS per flow conflicts with common practice.** Many brands and platform templates run 2 or more SMS messages in abandoned cart flows. The source gives no law or rule for its limit. Some US state laws limit how many messages go to one person in 24 hours on the same topic, which may be the root of this advice, but the source does not say so. Treat as unverified. `[web check: TCPA, state mini-TCPA limits, CTIA guidance and Klaviyo policy on flow SMS counts]`
- **Frequency is lower than much common advice.** The source caps at 2 campaigns a week. Other operators often run more around big sales. The source itself says split test and that it varies by category. Keep as this source's view.
- **Best offer up front vs split offer.** The source openly argues against the common "extra 5% for SMS" setup. No test numbers shown.
- **Extra incentive in abandonment flows.** Source recommends it; card warns it can train customers to abandon. Source does not address this.
- **Quiet hours.** 8:00 a.m. to 9:00 p.m. is stated with no jurisdiction or time zone. Rules vary by country and US state. The card says do not rely on one universal time band.
- **Numbering slip.** The presenter lists principles 1 to 3 and then says "for my fifth point". Content is complete; the numbering is off.
- **Header-only claims.** 160 characters, MMS cost and 6 to 10 second pop-up delay are not in the transcript. They may come from the video's on-screen text or be the filer's additions. Weight them lower.
- **Card-only claims.** Step-level opt-in tracking, weekly opt-in vs traffic check, list-growth unsubscribe caveat, and "email carries long content, SMS the short reminder" come from cards, not the transcript.
- **Missing.** 6 of the 7 promised list-growth methods. Abandoned cart and browse abandonment copy, triggers, filters and timing. Post-purchase, winback or other flows. How to build an "engaged SMS segment". Any revenue per recipient benchmark. Attribution method for "SMS revenue". Opt-in confirmation (double opt-in) and required disclosure text. Countries outside the US.
- **Uncited stats.** 98% open rate and 3-minute open time. `[web check]`
- **Brand fit.** Examples are US fashion. For other markets (for example India), SMS rules, costs and platforms differ. `[needs brand data: send countries and SMS platform]` `[web check: SMS marketing rules in each send country]`
