## 1. What this source is for

Use it to set up a value-first newsletter: the fixed email skeleton (reward at top, 1 tactic, contextual CTA, P.S.), plain-text formatting for inbox placement, a "reply yes" welcome email, cadence, subject line and preview text tests, send timing, and CTR as the main metric. It is a newsletter playbook for a B2B/info list. It has no ecommerce flows (no abandoned cart, browse, post-purchase or winback), no discount strategy and no revenue-per-recipient metric. An operator must adapt it to a product store and keep its format rules separate from standard ecommerce campaign practice.

## 2. Principles

1. **Email is follow-up on a lead you already paid for.** The acquisition cost is sunk, so the only costs are software and the writer. Source cites an average email ROI of 35 to 45x "depending on the source" (no source named). Condition: only true if you already have a list. [e23b7d7d24b2]
2. **Email has 1 of 2 jobs: raise the chance of a purchase later, or get the purchase now.** Source treats email as content with guaranteed delivery. On social the algorithm decides reach; in email your past performance decides inbox placement. Source's opinion.
3. **Behavior repeats when it was rewarded last time.** Design every step (open, read, click) to pay the reader back. Look at what happens after an action, not before it, to explain why people repeat it. Source's opinion, framed as "proven", no study named. [69a0425774f8] [e8a55c972c52]
4. **Quality over volume. One bad email can break the habit.** Source: 4 good emails and 1 useless one can make a reader stop opening. He would rather go quiet longer than send something weak. [69a0425774f8] [c53d577bf872]
5. **Low opens push you to the Promotions tab, which the source calls "email death".** Condition: builds up over time. [69a0425774f8]
6. **Align with the inbox provider's goal instead of hunting hacks.** The provider wants the best inbox experience. Its worst failure is hiding an important email (a false positive), which could make a user switch providers. Letting spam through is the lesser risk. So consistent engagement (opens, reads, clicks, replies) is "100 golden BBs", not 1 silver bullet. [84e6a958f0b2] [348db341f122] [c0cbdce17a9d]
7. **Make unsubscribing easy.** Uninterested people who stay on the list go dormant and lower domain deliverability for everyone. Dormant is worse than gone. Someone who unsubscribes still knows your brand; source says many of his workshop attendees had unsubscribed. Source hates brands that make unsubscribing hard. [c1faaf881fc4]
8. **Who gets the email matters more than how good it is.** Mismatch (beginner content to advanced readers, or the reverse) reads as "not for me". Segment by qualifiers collected at opt-in. Cited: HubSpot study, 791% higher email ROI with segmented lists. [d107017a4007]
9. **Same format every time.** "McDonald's principle": readers should know what they will get. Experiment early, then repeat what gets more replies and clicks. [e8a55c972c52]
10. **Make every email valuable; do not run separate promo emails.** Put the offer link in every email like a "link in bio". Then no give/ask ratio is needed. Source's personal model. [c53d577bf872]
11. **Train the audience to click.** Do not avoid links out of deliverability fear. Put a nugget in the email and the full asset behind the link, so clicks get rewarded. Condition: the linked asset must be good, or clicking falls off. [d94ac386d714] [f9275e23494a]
12. **CTAs must be contextual.** Bridge from the email's specific content into the CTA. No copy-pasted stock CTA. [e8a55c972c52] [d94ac386d714]
13. **Sell ongoing membership, not only the lead magnet.** People who opt in only for the freebie will not read anything after. [348db341f122] [dd72241f1f75]
14. **The worst outcome is a reward that was not worth the effort.** Source says this "keeps me up at night". Always give a positive return on the reader's time. [dd72241f1f75] [69a0425774f8]
15. **Judge performance over a period, not per email.** Some sends are deposits (pure value), some are withdrawals (a real ask). [f9275e23494a] [c5f48c89de68]
16. **A quality threshold beats a fixed cadence (source's own preference).** If he has 2 good emails in him, 2 go out; 0 means 0. This sits next to the "about 3 per week" advice from other marketers (see section 9). [c53d577bf872] [e23b7d7d24b2]

## 3. Procedures

### 3.1 Start (or restart) an email program
1. If you have never mailed, or mail about once every 6 months, start at once a month. [e23b7d7d24b2] [c53d577bf872]
2. Commit to a finite batch: 12 emails (monthly for a year) or 24 (twice a month). Source wrote 24 in about half a day. [e23b7d7d24b2]
3. Write them all in 1 sitting. Anything beyond the batch is bonus.
4. Expect a higher unsubscribe rate on the first send ("shaking the tree", pulling forward churn). Source's first send: just over 1%. Later: about 0.42% per email. Program is fine while list growth between sends is higher than unsubscribes. [c1faaf881fc4] [needs brand data: current list size, last send date, unsubscribe rate per send, list growth between sends]
5. Raise frequency only while quality holds. Cut frequency, not quality. [c53d577bf872]

Caveat: batching fits evergreen value content. Card notes it fits badly with drops, launches and time-bound promos [e23b7d7d24b2]. The source did not discuss this.

### 3.2 Build one email (the "Money Minute" skeleton)
1. **Subject line.** Either a fixed branded prefix plus a hook (source: "Mozi Money Minute" + e.g. "391% increase in sales from one change"), like a podcast episode title, or a "proof over promise" subject. Source chose the prefix to build a newsletter brand that people open out of habit, and accepts some short-term open rate loss for it. [4516dab6a1af] [e8a55c972c52]
2. **Preview text.** Write it by hand. Do not let it default to the first ~150 characters ("Hey John..."). Pull the most valuable, curiosity-driving nugget forward. Takes about 10 seconds. [4516dab6a1af]
3. **Top of body: one-glance reward.** A quote or tweet the reader gets value from before reading further. Source uses his most-shared tweets (already tested). [e8a55c972c52] [69a0425774f8]
4. **Body: 1 tactic, under ~200 words (~1 minute read).** Cut until only 1 clear idea is left. Structure: name a problem or condition that makes the solution needed, give the solution, then the concrete tactic. The reader should be able to use it right away. [e8a55c972c52]
5. **Sign-off, then CTA.** 1 or 2 links, each for a different audience or goal (source: "scaling your business, go here; starting a business, go here"). Make it contextual to the tactic. [d94ac386d714]
6. **P.S.** Always include one. Source: after the headline it is the most-read part of the email. It can be a meme, a reward for finishing, a second CTA, or the CTA itself. No fixed placement. [e8a55c972c52]
7. **Format check.** Mostly text, no images, 1 to 2 links max, remove money language (see 3.4). [84e6a958f0b2]
8. **Render check.** Preview on desktop and mobile before sending. [d1d5d6fe035a] [verify UI: preview tool in the brand's ESP]

Source says this format is his choice, not a law. His colleague Lila sends longer, story-based emails to the highest-revenue segment and they also do well. [e8a55c972c52]

### 3.3 Welcome email after opt-in ("reply yes")
1. Open with anticipation: a big announcement is coming and subscribers hear first. [dd72241f1f75]
2. Deliver on the opt-in promise (reinforces opting in).
3. Sell regular communication before the extra gift: say why they should stay on the list. Source did this "in 4 lines". [348db341f122]
4. Offer a bonus they did not know about (source: a finished book chapter that did not fit the book), near the end, to "surprise and delight". [dd72241f1f75]
5. Gate the bonus: they get it only by replying "yes". The reply is an engagement signal to the provider and helps keep you out of Promotions. [348db341f122]
6. Sell the gated asset with specifics, including page numbers (source's list: how to make advertising 9.5x as profitable, "on page 5"; the most powerful offer of all time; 5 ways to add hoops that raise lead quality; 4 ways to display discounts). [348db341f122]
7. On reply, send the asset automatically. [verify UI: reply-triggered automation in the brand's ESP; the source does not say how it is built]

Source is thin on the mechanics: no tool, trigger or fallback for people who never reply. [needs brand data: current welcome flow, what the opt-in promises (for a store usually a discount code)]

### 3.4 Plain-text deliverability setup
1. Make the email look like one you would send a friend: text, a handful of sentences. [84e6a958f0b2]
2. No images (source sends none). Avoid the "Vegas Strip" template look.
3. 1 link, 2 at most.
4. Remove money words from the body. Source observed more money language means a higher share in Promotions. Header also says the subject. Source's own observation, no data shown.
5. Ask for replies (see 3.3).
6. Optional, untested today: link to "Google-native" assets (YouTube video, sales letter or video sales letter hosted in Google Drive). Source says providers recognize the file type, so deliverability and CTR may rise, and rough "duct-taped" assets sometimes convert better. "Worth a shot." [c0cbdce17a9d] [f9275e23494a] [web check: whether Google Drive or YouTube links still help or hurt Gmail placement]

### 3.5 Subject line A/B test
1. Create 2 subject variants. [4516dab6a1af]
2. Let the platform send each to a small share of the list.
3. When a winner is picked, send it to the rest.
Source gives no test share, wait time, winner metric or sample size. [verify UI: A/B test settings in the brand's ESP] [needs brand data: list size, to judge whether a test slice is large enough]

### 3.6 Segment at opt-in
1. Add qualifier questions to the opt-in form. Source's: revenue band, has a business or not. [d107017a4007]
2. Route content by segment (source: send some emails only to 500k+, 5 million+, or 0 to 100k revenue).
3. Tell people to answer honestly: those who pick a higher tier get content that does not fit them.
For a store the qualifiers must be translated (for example skill level, use case). The source gives none for ecommerce. [needs brand data: which qualifiers matter for the brand's products]

### 3.7 Schedule the send
1. B2C: Monday or Tuesday. B2B: Wednesday. [5c6c3e8e029f]
2. Time: 10:00 to noon or 13:00 to 15:00. Avoid first thing in the morning, when real work mail competes.
3. Use one reference time zone (source uses Pacific). If the list is spread across time zones, do not spend much effort on timing.
Source calls these "golden BBs": small lifts. [needs brand data: the brand's own open and click by day and hour]

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Not emailing at all, or waiting years to start ("saying no to money"). [e23b7d7d24b2]
2. Avoiding email for fear of unsubscribes. Logical extreme: then why have a list. [c1faaf881fc4]
3. Making unsubscribe hard, which keeps dormant people who drag down deliverability. [c1faaf881fc4]
4. Sending one weak email that breaks the reading habit. [69a0425774f8]
5. Sending just to hit a cadence; writing gets worse when forced. [c53d577bf872]
6. "Vegas Strip" image-heavy templates that read as pure promotion. [84e6a958f0b2]
7. Money language in the email body. [84e6a958f0b2]
8. Pasting the whole blog into the email instead of a nugget plus a link. [d94ac386d714]
9. Copy-paste CTAs unrelated to the content (including his own testimonial email). [d94ac386d714]
10. Too many links (5 is aggressive). [d94ac386d714]
11. Leaving preview text on default. [4516dab6a1af]
12. Not checking mobile rendering, which hurts CTR, worst for image-heavy ecommerce emails. [d1d5d6fe035a]
13. Sending advanced content to beginners or the reverse. [d107017a4007]
14. Lying on opt-in qualifiers (subscriber side). [d107017a4007]
15. Sending the lead magnet with nothing else, so people take it and never read again. [348db341f122] [dd72241f1f75]
16. A lead magnet that is not worth the effort of getting it. [dd72241f1f75]
17. Judging open rate as accurate after Apple privacy changes. [c5f48c89de68]
18. Asking hard for the click in every email. [f9275e23494a]
19. Asking testimonial givers "how was life before?", which yields long stories with no hook. [38bfd47e311d]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

**Conflicts with common ecommerce email practice (keep as conflicts, do not resolve here):**
- Plain text, 0 images, 1 to 2 links vs ecommerce campaigns built on product photos and multi-product grids. The source even says ecommerce uses more images. Its own rule is for an info newsletter. For a store, treat it as a test for some sends (founder letters, education), not a rule for product launches. [84e6a958f0b2] [needs brand data: plain vs designed test results]
- "No promotional emails, every email is value" vs the usual store mix of sales, launches and restocks. [c53d577bf872]
- Reply-gated lead magnet vs the common instant discount code in a store welcome flow. Gating a discount code would add friction at the first purchase. The source did not test this for stores. [348db341f122]
