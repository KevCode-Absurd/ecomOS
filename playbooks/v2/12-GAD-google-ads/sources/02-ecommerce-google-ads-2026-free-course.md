## 1. What this source is for

It gives an operator a full build order for an ecommerce Google Ads account: tracking, Merchant Center, Standard Shopping, branded and non-branded splits, Search, DSA, PMax, Demand Gen remarketing and YouTube Shorts. Each lesson has click-by-click setup, the settings the agency changes from Google's defaults, bid strategy stages with conversion thresholds, and optimisation loops. It is strongest on setup and structure. It is weak on proof: numbers are the agency's heuristics, and several thresholds disagree between lessons.

## 2. Principles

### 2.1 Channel role and build order

1. **Google Ads is the stability layer under Meta.** Meta can break through CPM spikes, conversion drops, bans and stalls. Google high-intent Search and Shopping give a second channel you can trust. This is the presenter's thesis, not a measured claim. [1059247fff4e]
2. **Build Search and Shopping first, and mostly Shopping.** Source says: "if you don't nail your shopping and your search campaigns, you won't be successful on Google Ads." Everything else is "a bonus". Do not spread budget thin across all 6 campaign types. Add others once you see success. The gate is success, not a date. [b7bf73d92363]
3. **Shopping is the main earner for ecommerce.** Source calls Shopping "the bread winner" and "the most important campaigns by a country mile", and the most profitable campaign type. [911db04602d6] [4995c5440e92]
4. **PMax is not for most beginners.** It is quick to set up but blends all placements, so it is hard to see what works. [b7bf73d92363]
5. **YouTube goes on top of a working Shopping and Search base.** Brand search, Shopping and prospecting should be "dialled in" first. [a5cf27b657fa]

### 2.2 Tracking

6. **Nothing else matters if conversion tracking is wrong.** Without it both the advertiser and Google's automated bidding "fly blind". Smart bidding needs positive and negative feedback. [2b368492270c]
7. **One primary purchase action, and only purchases as primary.** Primary actions drive bidding and reporting. Secondary actions are for observation only. Two primary purchase actions double revenue and ROAS on paper. [30fdf39203d6] [a3831e8b2c64]
8. **Do not use the GA4 purchase import as primary.** Source says GA4 under-counts Google Ads conversions by about 20% versus GTM or the Shopify action, so the algorithm gets less data. Use it as a secondary backup. [30fdf39203d6] [a3831e8b2c64]
9. **Micro-conversions as primary only in rare high-ticket cases.** Condition: product around $10k to $15k and only 8 to 20 conversions a month. Then optimise to add to cart and remarket hard. Source says this is not the case for 99% of advertisers. [30fdf39203d6]

### 2.3 Brand versus non-brand

10. **Always separate brand from non-brand.** Mixing them in PMax, Shopping or Search hides what non-brand really delivers. Brand ROAS is structurally high and inflates non-brand results if mixed. [c4b8343d0510] [f52ecc6c8839] [fe8bd2cdf490]
11. **Branded campaigns defend and feed the algorithm.** They stop competitors and resellers taking your brand traffic. In a new account they give the cheapest conversions to feed machine learning. [c4b8343d0510] [fe8bd2cdf490]
12. **Run branded Search only when there is a reason.** Run it if competitors bid on your brand or the account is new. Source would "almost always" skip it if there is zero competition and your organic listing always shows first. This is the presenter's opinion. [fe8bd2cdf490]

### 2.4 Shopping and the feed

13. **Shopping has no keywords; the feed is the targeting.** Google matches the search to your product data. Better product data gives better queries, CTR and conversion. [21229935ad81]
14. **Price, title and image drive about 95% of Shopping performance.** Source's heuristic: spend 95% of feed time on these 3. Price is the most important because Shopping is a comparison surface. [1f2977dd9a2c] [21229935ad81]
15. **Negatives are a plaster; the feed is the cure.** If a product shows for poor queries, add negatives, then fix the title and description that caused the match. [25c967c6e637]
16. **Store-level trust signals are free wins.** Product ratings and the Top Quality Store badge cost nothing and, per the source, only help CTR and sales. [69bd2ba35622] [c9b580a81edf]

### 2.5 Relevance and bidding

17. **Fix ad rank through quality before paying more.** Ad rank has 2 inputs: bid and ad quality (Quality Score). Relevance is rewarded with lower CPCs. Raise bids only when quality is maxed out. [be07dfd74db8] [4d0a3e281799] [ffbb287b9903]
18. **Keep keyword, ad and landing page on one theme.** Single theme ad groups (STAGs) lift expected CTR, ad relevance and landing page experience together. The presenter says low relevance is "the biggest issue stopping ad accounts from scaling". [1b793cd8bbb3] [76be15912789]
19. **Manual first, automated later.** New accounts have no data, so automated bidding is erratic and inflates CPCs. Use manual CPC or Maximize Clicks, gather conversions, then move to Maximize Conversion Value and later Target ROAS. [805871e8952b] [5d9dd44f8d9a]
20. **Set targets from actuals.** A target ROAS far above what the campaign has achieved does not create efficiency. It stops spend. Set it at or slightly below actual ROAS. [5da4b51eabe6] [9edd4e9d7860]
21. **Consolidate at typical DTC scale.** Source says brands doing about $50k to $500k a month (Shopping lesson) or spending about $100k to $500k a month on Google (PMax lesson) rarely have the traffic for heavy segmentation. Above that there is more merit in splitting. [4995c5440e92] [4692423b2426]
22. **Nothing is set and forget.** Search copy, PMax assets and remarketing creative all need regular testing because the auction is relative and competitors change. [533ef4069e40] [2bf868bfb28d] [74a36fd11057]

### 2.6 Audiences and YouTube

23. **Exclude existing customers from prospecting.** Upload customer lists and exclude them from non-brand campaigns, and often all campaigns. On YouTube, if you do not exclude buyers, Google will retarget them, take credit, and add no new customers. [ce4012235cdc] [5d0be265e08e]
24. **On YouTube, targeting is targeting.** Source: on Meta "your creative is your targeting", but he has not seen YouTube behave that way. Start narrow on the ICP and expand sideways. [5d0be265e08e]
25. **Test creative on Meta, scale it on YouTube.** YouTube is where you test audiences, not creative. Do not validate an offer on YouTube. [1abd0e7f1e7c] [a5cf27b657fa]
26. **Remarketing is an objection test.** Cart abandoners left for 1 or more of 5 reasons. Test ads per objection, then push the winning answer up-funnel. [1010d231578b] [74a36fd11057]

## 3. Procedures

All UI paths below are as shown in the lessons (mid-2025). `[verify UI]` applies to every step that names a menu, button or field.

### 3.1 Choose campaign types and launch order [b7bf73d92363]

1. Know the 6 types Google offers at campaign creation: Performance Max, Search, Demand Gen, Video, Display, Shopping. `[verify UI]`
2. Split Search and Shopping each into branded and non-branded.
3. Launch Search and Shopping first. Put most early effort into Shopping.
4. Add remarketing (Demand Gen) and PMax once Search and Shopping work.
5. Add YouTube prospecting (Demand Gen, Shorts) last, only if the fit checks in 3.28 are met.

### 3.2 Connect Shopify to Google Ads (beginner tracking route) [93f8e8d57251] [52efc5146531] [2b368492270c]

1. Use 1 master Google account that owns or manages Google Ads, Merchant Center and Analytics. Source says one account makes linking "10 times easier".
2. In Shopify, install the Google & YouTube app (Sales channels). `[verify UI]`
3. Sign in with the master Google account. In app settings, pick your Google Ads account from the dropdown (shows as name or number). `[verify UI]`
4. This imports Shopify API conversion actions: purchase (labelled "Google Shopping App Purchase"), add to cart, begin checkout, page view, view item, search. Purchase is set to primary. `[verify UI]`
5. Check enhanced conversions are on and the Google tag is firing. `[verify UI]`
6. Wait. The first purchase can take a couple of hours to fire; actions can take a day or 2 to show. No sales means nothing fires.
7. If you already have a purchase action from GTM or GA4, go to 3.4 at once and leave only 1 primary.
8. Source says this route gets you "80 to 90% of the way". For more robust tracking, set everything up in Google Tag Manager, or use the Feed Army pixel (what the agency uses, for enhanced conversions and remarketing). The source gives no GTM steps. [web check: Feed Army pixel current offer]

### 3.3 Link GA4 to Google Ads [a3831e8b2c64]

1. In GA4: Admin > Property settings > Product links > Google Ads links. `[verify UI]`
2. Search for your Google Ads account ID (numbers only, shown in the Google Ads top toolbar). Click Link. `[verify UI]`
3. On the link screen: enable personalised advertising, auto-tagging, allow access. Confirm account number and name. Submit. `[verify UI]`
4. In Google Ads > Conversion actions, find the imported GA4 purchase action. By default it is primary. `[verify UI]`
5. Set the GA4 purchase to secondary (see 3.4). It still records for observation and is a backup if the main action breaks.
6. Alternative link routes exist in Google Ads and in Shopify. The source shows only the GA4 route.

### 3.4 Set primary and secondary conversion actions [30fdf39203d6]

1. Go to conversion actions > Purchase category. List every purchase action (Shopify, GTM, GA4). `[verify UI]`
2. Click into each action. Tabs: Details, Settings, Diagnostics, Web pages. Open Settings. `[verify UI]`
3. Under Settings you see name, value, source, attribution windows and "Action optimization". Set Action optimization to Primary for 1 purchase action only. Set all other purchase actions to Secondary. `[verify UI]`
4. Set add to cart, begin checkout, page views, view item and search to Secondary.
5. Do not set non-purchase actions as account default goals.
6. Use secondary actions to read the funnel (views, carts, checkouts) when purchase volume is low.

### 3.5 Pick the 4 GA4 reports to check every couple of weeks [324a945cc6a5]

1. User attributes: country, active users in the last 15 minutes by town and country, gender, Google-tagged interests, age, language. `[verify UI]`
2. Monetisation > Ecommerce purchases: per product views, add to carts, purchases, revenue. `[verify UI]`
3. Landing page report: sessions, active users, new users, average engagement time per session, bounce rate. Source calls this "one of the more powerful reports", especially for pages that get paid traffic. `[verify UI]`
4. Channels: first-user primary channel (direct, organic search, cross-network, paid search, organic shopping), then source and medium. Needs UTM parameters on all paid traffic. `[verify UI]`
5. GA4 is optional. Many owners use other platforms and the source says that is fine.

### 3.6 Build remarketing lists [4bba31df42c6] [8573a3074cc2] [7b6b3c03be03] [061764977d03]

Website visitors (in Google Ads):
1. Tools > Audience manager > Data segments > blue plus. Options: website visitors, app users, YouTube users, customer list, import from Google Analytics, custom combination. Choose Website visitors. `[verify UI]`
2. Name it clearly, for example "30-day website visitors, all pages".
3. Segment members: visitors of a page; action "visited a web page" in the past 30 days (default). `[verify UI]`
4. Optional: refine with "URL contains" a product or collection. Source: 6 and 7 figure stores may lack traffic to filter by product, so use all visitors; 8 and 9 figure brands can build product-level lists for more specific, dynamic remarketing. [needs brand data: monthly site traffic]
5. Prefill from the past 30 days. Source says they "normally" lower lists to 3-day up to 7-day visitors and do not generally recommend longer, but it depends on traffic.
6. Add a description. Wait up to a day. In data sources, check list size and eligibility for Search, Display and YouTube. `[verify UI]`

Cart abandoners (in GA4, then import):
1. GA4 Admin > Data display > Audiences > New audience. `[verify UI]`
2. Include users when add_to_cart (a built-in GA4 event).
3. Exclude users when purchase. Lesson 1 example: 30-day window, 4.2K add-to-cart users (15% of all users), 603 purchasers excluded, about 3.6K left. Lesson 2 example: a low-traffic brand used a 60-day window and excluded anyone who "ever" purchased: 2.8K included, 1.5K excluded, 1.4K left.
4. Pick the window from traffic. Source says you need "about 1,000 to 1,500 people at least" to be eligible to remarket (the transcript reads "1,00 to,500"). [web check: current Google Ads list-size minimums per network]
5. Save. Mark it as a key audience (source's wording) so it imports to Google Ads. GA4 and Google Ads must be linked (3.3). `[verify UI]`
6. Use the specificity in copy, for example "Saw you weren't ready to make a purchase. Here's a discount code or free shipping."
7. Post-purchase upsell to people who did buy is a separate audience idea; the source mentions it but gives no setup.

YouTube users:
1. Audience manager > plus > YouTube users. Name, customer type, select your brand channel. `[verify UI]`
2. Options: viewed any video, viewed certain videos, viewed certain videos as ads, subscribed, visited channel page, liked any video. `[verify UI]`
3. Source prefers "viewed certain videos as ads" on top-of-funnel education videos, then remarkets those viewers with direct-response creative.

### 3.7 Upload a customer list [ce4012235cdc]

1. Audience manager > Data segments > plus > Customer list. `[verify UI]`
2. Name it with the definition and dates, for example "all customers up to [date], uploaded [date]", if uploading by hand.
3. Customer type: all customers, or most valuable customers. Source's examples of valuable: 3 or more purchases, or top 5% by revenue. [needs brand data: customer purchase counts and revenue]
4. Upload emails, phones and/or postal addresses as CSV. Include email, phone, first name, last name, zip and country when possible. Source: Klaviyo or Shopify export. `[verify UI]`
5. Allow about a day to populate.
6. Use it 3 ways: audience signal (for example in PMax), lookalike seed in Demand Gen, and exclusion from non-brand campaigns and often all campaigns. The agency does not usually remarket to the list directly.

### 3.8 Build custom segments [f2925b7ac7a4]

1. Audience manager > Custom segments > New. Name it at the end if you like. `[verify UI]`
2. Choose one: people with these interests or purchase intentions; people who searched for these terms on Google; people who browse websites similar to (own site or competitor sites); apps.
3. Search-term example for a collagen brand: "why is my hair thinning", "perimenopausal joint problems", "best collagens", "should I take liquid collagen or powder collagen". Source warns this spans problem-aware to solution-aware and is too broad for 1 segment.
4. Source's most-used segment: searched terms only, no websites, no apps ("what's working best for us in 2025"). [needs brand data: customer problem and solution language]

### 3.9 Set up Google Merchant Center and link it [911db04602d6]

1. In the Shopify Google & YouTube app settings, connect Google Ads first, then Merchant Center. Pick an existing Merchant Center from the dropdown, or create one. `[verify UI]`
2. New account prompts: business information; claim and verify website; shipping and returns; tax info; add products. `[verify UI]`
3. Product upload options: manual (Google Sheets or file upload), third-party app (transcript names "Simprosys", spelled "Simproies" and "Srosi"; recommended if you use a third party), or the Google & YouTube app sync. For most brands spending $10k to $30k a month and new to Google, sync from Shopify.
4. Watch product status: approved, pending, not approved. Most start pending. Use Diagnostics to see why items are disapproved. `[verify UI]`
5. Link to Google Ads from Merchant Center: settings cog > Apps and services > Add a service > Google Ads > pick the ad account > Confirm. `[verify UI]`
6. Source says setup "takes 20 minutes". Approval adds time. Merchant Center also enables free listings and local inventory ads (for physical stores).

### 3.10 Turn on product ratings [69bd2ba35622]

1. Submit Google's product ratings interest form. Answer the questions; tick yes if you use a third-party review aggregator. `[verify UI]`
2. Choose a route: Google Customer Reviews (Google collects reviews; most of the agency's clients do not use it); an official aggregator (source says about 33, including Trustpilot, the most common, and Stamped); or a non-official one.
3. For Judge.me (used by 70% to 80% of the agency's brands; no direct Merchant Center integration): set up Judge.me, connect it to Shopify, make sure Shopify is linked to Merchant Center, then follow Judge.me's own guide to sync ratings through Shopify. The source does not give those steps.
4. Check eligibility: 50 reviews in total to join; at least 3 reviews on a product for its rating to show. [web check: current product ratings thresholds]
5. Expect 2 to 4 weeks from the interest form to ratings serving.

### 3.11 Work toward the Top Quality Store badge [c9b580a81edf]

Google benchmarks 5 categories against other stores in your country. The source's actions per category:
1. Browsing: high-resolution images only; a high-resolution main image plus 8 to 10 more (angles, lifestyle, use); landing page load under 2 seconds on desktop and mobile. [needs brand data: image counts per product, load times]
2. Purchase: Merchant Center promotions must match the site exactly (details, dates, coupon codes) or they get rejected and hurt the store; offer several e-wallets (Apple Pay, Google Wallet, PayPal, Amazon Pay).
3. Shipping: keep delivery time and shipping cost as low as you can.
4. Returns: a window of at least 30 days "if not more"; low return cost to the customer.
5. Store rating: collect post-purchase reviews at product and store level. Store ratings can also show on Search text ads.
6. Check status in Merchant Center under store quality (the example store showed "exceptional"). `[verify UI]` [web check: current Top Quality Store criteria]

### 3.12 Optimise the product feed [21229935ad81] [b5af5e150e67] [6126c42680a9] [d633238d5060] [24a69e143936] [e58f1b803e0f]

1. Focus on 5 of the 40 to 80 attributes per product: price, title, images, description, GTIN.
2. Price: keep it accurate, fair and competitive. If you cannot cut price: lead with the single pack (lowest shown price) and upsell on the landing page; or lead with the biggest pack and sell on unit price; or justify a premium with quality or luxury. A higher price can qualify clicks, and you pay only for clicks. [needs brand data: pack sizes, competitor prices]
3. Title: up to 150 characters; the first 70 show before the ellipsis. [web check: current title limits]
4. Title order: core product type, then key features or benefits, then specifics (quantity, size, material, use case, dosage), then brand at the end if space allows.
5. Brand first only if you are a market leader (Nike). Otherwise brand at the end or not at all. Do not let organic titles that start with the brand flow into the feed unchanged.
6. Images: high resolution; product is the main subject; no text overlays (sale or Black Friday stickers cause a policy violation and the item cannot serve). [web check: current Merchant Center image policy]
7. Test lifestyle or elevated backgrounds against plain white. "Lifestyle" includes a simple or AI-made background. Apparel: model-worn and ironed. Premium items need an elevated look; a many-item value kit can stay on white. Keep props from stealing focus.
8. Use Google Product Studio in Merchant Center (free) to make backgrounds from plain shots by describing the scene. `[verify UI]`
9. Description: up to 5,000 characters ("I believe"). Readable, scannable, features and benefits, natural keywords, no stuffing, answer the questions and objections buyers have before purchase. Keep it brief. Source thinks Google weights descriptions less than it used to. [web check: current description limit]
10. GTIN: every variant needs its own unique GTIN. Shopify sync should handle it.

### 3.13 Launch a Standard Shopping campaign [43c52017c399]

Preconditions: conversion tracking works; Merchant Center is linked.
1. ads.google.com > Campaigns > blue plus > New campaign. `[verify UI]`
2. Objective: Sales. Conversion goal: purchases. Campaign type: Shopping. `[verify UI]`
3. Google will push Performance Max. Decline; choose Standard Shopping. `[verify UI]`
4. Select the linked Merchant Center account. Name the campaign with channel, product scope and country. `[verify UI]`
5. Set average daily budget (example £50 a day). [needs brand data: budget]
6. Bid strategy: manual CPC for a new account; Target ROAS for a seasoned account with lots of conversion data (see 3.18).
7. Bid equally for new and existing customers. Campaign priority defaults to Low (change it if running the brand split in 3.15). `[verify UI]`
8. Locations: pick the country. Open Location options and choose "Presence: people in or regularly in your included locations", not the Google-recommended "Presence or interest". `[verify UI]`
9. Turn Search Partners off. Source: "We never find that performance is particularly strong in this network." `[verify UI]`
10. Ad group: name it; enter default max CPC (example $1). `[verify UI]`
11. Products: all products by default, or narrow by product name, category, product type (example: a skincare brand's balms, about 12 products), custom labels built in Merchant Center (seasonality, bestsellers), or item ID (Shopify SKU). `[verify UI]`
12. Save and publish. Ads usually serve within a couple of hours.

### 3.14 Optimise Shopping (the agency's 6-step framework) [4995c5440e92] [5d9dd44f8d9a] [25c967c6e637] [c6256b30ff46]

1. Structure: new accounts start on best sellers only. Consolidate. In existing accounts, check top performers get enough daily and weekly spend; break an underfunded best seller into its own campaign; only then give budget to new or underfunded products. [needs brand data: product-level sales and spend]
2. Bidding: new account on manual CPC (preferred, with an upper limit) or Maximize Clicks. Move to Target ROAS at 30 to 50 monthly conversions in the Shopping campaign. Set the target at or slightly below the actual ROAS of the first 4 to 6 weeks.
3. Negatives: review search terms very often, up to daily in a new account. Terms irrelevant to the whole business go at account level; terms irrelevant only to some products go on a list, campaign or ad group.
4. Monitor: CTR, CPC, conversion rate, ROAS or CPA; then search impression share, search lost IS (budget), search lost IS (rank) and click share.
5. Poor products: read their search terms. Add negatives, then fix title and description (feed) because that is the root cause.
6. Good products: if lost IS (budget) is high and you hit KPI, raise budget and keep bids. If lost IS (rank) is high, decide whether you can accept lower efficiency for more scale. If yes, raise CPC (manual) or lower Target ROAS (automated). [needs brand data: margin and ROAS floor]

### 3.15 Split branded and non-branded Shopping with priority [f52ecc6c8839]

1. Build a non-branded Shopping campaign: all products; campaign priority High; a moderate Target ROAS (source example 200% to 300%, set by your KPIs); attach a branded negative keyword list. `[verify UI]`
2. Build 1 branded Shopping campaign: the same products; priority Low; a higher Target ROAS. The transcript's figure is garbled ("15, 16, 17, 100%"), so no reliable number is given.
3. How routing works: a query goes first to the High priority campaign. A brand query ("Nike shoes") is blocked there by the brand negative and falls to the Low priority branded campaign. A non-brand query ("running shoes") serves from non-branded.
4. Keep the 2 campaigns identical in products, audiences and locations. Only priority and bidding may differ.
5. Guard 2 failure modes: (a) non-branded is limited by budget, so non-brand queries spill into branded; (b) a product missing from non-branded, so its non-brand queries serve from branded.
6. Source calls this an advanced technique. It is the basis for reading true non-brand performance.

### 3.16 Launch a Search campaign [535fcd04bd8b] [b14c89dd124a] [96b95d8a8ec0]

1. ads.google.com > Campaigns > plus > New campaign. Objective: Sales (Leads for B2B). `[verify UI]`
2. Conversion goals: purchases only. Remove page views and add to carts from the goal list. Source: in 99% of cases purchase is the only goal. `[verify UI]`
3. Type: Search. Enter website URL. Choose website visits (not phone calls or shop visits). `[verify UI]`
4. Name: channel, country, brand or non-brand (example "Search, USA, non-branded").
5. Bidding: focus on conversion value (not conversions) unless you sell 1 SKU or a subscription where order values are equal. Leave Target ROAS off for now. Leave the customer acquisition setting at default. You can change bidding later. `[verify UI]`
6. Networks: turn off Google Search Partners and Google Display Network. Source: "I've yet to see performance exceed that of Google Search." `[verify UI]`
7. Location: country; exclude states if needed. Advanced: switch to "Presence" only. Source's example: in a US election season, people worldwide show "interest" in the US; if you do not ship to the UK, Europe or Canada you lose money. `[verify UI]`
8. Language: the target market's language, unless you also want, for example, Spanish.
9. Audience segments: leave blank or add for observation only.
10. AI Max: skip it to keep control. The source promises a separate AI Max lesson that is not in this text. `[verify UI]`
11. Keywords: enter your researched keywords by hand rather than taking Google's URL suggestions. Match type by punctuation: none = broad, "quotes" = phrase, [brackets] = exact.
12. Ad: final URL (homepage, collection, product page or listicle); display path (for example /running-shoes), recommended. `[verify UI]`
13. Headlines: up to 15, 30 characters each; Google says start with at least 5; include keywords; each unique; mix features, benefits, offers, urgency and CTAs; title case. See 3.17 for how many to use.
14. Descriptions: at least 2, up to 4, 90 characters each; 2 serve at once; sentence case.
15. Images: no text overlays; product-centric or lifestyle. Add business name (advertiser transparency) and logo (favicon).
16. Sitelinks and callouts (see 3.17). Other assets exist: structured snippets, phone, address.
17. Budget: average daily budget, not a lifetime total. Example: $73 entered; Google suggested 87 or 58. Ignore Google's forecasts ("vastly inaccurate"). Start only with a budget you are comfortable losing. Scale gradually, for example $50 to $60 to $70 to $100 a day, although you can double or triple at any time. [needs brand data: budget]
18. Publish. First clicks can be very expensive; expect volatility. Serving can take up to a day. If nothing serves after 1 to 2 days, check bidding, keyword search volume and keyword choice.

### 3.17 Write and maintain Search ads and assets [089beecb4466] [195e55ecd55d] [bfa79d563469] [533ef4069e40] [850683161175]

1. Headlines (30 characters) do 3 jobs: grab attention (emotional hook or benefit), match intent (use the target keyword), guide action (a clear CTA in at least 1 headline).
2. Headline tactics: title case; imperative verbs (Shop, Buy, Explore, Learn); numbers and symbols (Vuori: 5", 7.5", 9" inseams; AG1: "#1"); "you" and "your" ("Your New Favorite Shorts", "Formulated for your body"); positive outcomes ("Burn fat and glow"); show the offer (Casper: free 100-night trial; limited-time 25% off bundles, in 2 headlines).
3. Descriptions (90 characters, 2 serve) close the click: handle objections, reinforce value, add urgency, add trust. Formats: problem-solution (a review quote about a dog's bad breath); offer-deadline (BarkBox: "Hurry before they're gone"); benefit plus trust signal (Billie: gentle close shave, dermatologist approved for sensitive skin).
4. Use 7 to 9 headlines, not 15, so each gets enough traffic to read. Add more only if you truly have more viable angles. Ad strength is not ad rank; do not fill slots to please it.
5. Pin only with a reason: a promotion that must always show, or an angle proven to have high CTR.
6. Review regularly. Keep winners, remove losers, add a couple of new angles. Source: Google copy testing is neglected compared with Meta.
7. Sitelinks: collection pages, best sellers ("always works really, really well"), reviews page, promotions (evergreen or clearance), new arrivals, product categories.
8. Callouts: 25 characters each, up to 10 per ad (Whoop: "14+ day battery life", "daily training guidance"; others: free shipping over $X, 25,000+ happy customers, 5-star reviews, 30-day returns).
9. Promotion asset: event plus details (example "summer sale, up to 49% off everyday items"). You can also put the offer in a headline.
10. Price assets: to show value on low-cost ranges ("from $X") or to put off unqualified clickers on high-priced items. Source uses them rarely.
11. Structured snippets: styles or types (Nike: jerseys, gloves, sleeves, pants) or brands if you are a reseller.

### 3.18 Bid strategy progression [805871e8952b] [5da4b51eabe6] [5d9dd44f8d9a] [9edd4e9d7860]

1. Is the account new, or without recent conversions? (An account paused from 2023 for 2 years counts as new.) If yes, use manual CPC (the source's choice 70% to 80% of the time; example cap $2 per keyword) or Maximize Clicks (daily budget, example $50, optional max CPC cap, fast data but weaker click quality).
2. Trim what does not work by hand until conversions build.
3. At about 30 conversions a month, ask: is order value fixed or variable? Fixed (lead gen, single SKU): Maximize Conversions, then Target CPA (example $30). Variable (most ecommerce): Maximize Conversion Value.
4. Start new campaigns in an existing account on Maximize Conversion Value. Daily budget about 3 to 4 times AOV. [needs brand data: AOV]
5. At 30 to 50 conversions a month (the mind map says above 50), review actual ROAS and CPA over the last 30 days. Move to Target ROAS at that actual or slightly below (actual 250%: set 250%, or 230% to 220%). Hold it at least 2 to 3 weeks.
6. If stuck below 50 conversions a month, expand budget or targeting.
7. Later, raise the target gently as efficiency improves, or hold it and scale budget.

### 3.19 Keyword research: seed, expand, segment [8c0b85636322] [6bfe7d97b858] [4691ad952431] [c37ad85535b2]

1. Classify intent: informational (top of funnel; send to advertorial or blog, not PDP; cheap clicks), commercial (comparing; "best collagen powder for women over 50", "[competitor] vs [brand]"), transactional (buy, for sale), navigational (mostly branded; "[brand] collagen reviews" to a reviews page; promo codes). Commercial plus transactional = "performance search", where most competition sits.
2. Seeds: find 20 to 30 two-to-three-word terms that name the core offer (collagen, women's collagen, marine collagen, collagen for skin, collagen capsules, flavorless collagen, collagen for hair). The presenter says a human picks better seeds than ChatGPT.
3. Expand each seed into long-tail terms. Source says queries are getting longer since ChatGPT. Example for "women's collagen": best collagen for women; women's collagen for joint support; best collagen for women over 50; tasty collagen for women.
4. Validate in Keyword Planner: average monthly searches, 3-month change, year-on-year change, competition, top-of-page bid low and high range. Add Semrush (paid, optional). Use ChatGPT to extend long-tail lists from your seeds when other ideas run out. `[verify UI]`
5. Filter by economics: expected CPC versus expected conversion rate (from past paid traffic and store data) versus goal CAC or Target ROAS. Drop keywords where the numbers fail. Cut hundreds to about 20 to 25 to start. [needs brand data: conversion rate, target CAC or ROAS]
6. Segment by theme into ad groups (women over 50, joint support, good flavour versus flavorless as separate groups, peptides, hair growth, collagen with vitamin C, best collagen, tasty collagen). Break out rather than bundle.
7. Choose match types: new account or efficiency focus, exact match on keywords known to convert (from Amazon or past tests). Growth focus with lots of data, broad match with Maximize Conversion Value or Target ROAS. Middle ground: about 80% exact plus core keywords in broad to find new terms, then add converting terms back as exact. The agency rarely uses phrase match now because it has become broader.

### 3.20 Build single theme ad groups (STAGs) [1b793cd8bbb3] [de648eb98d87] [76be15912789]

1. Break a mixed ad group ("buy running shoes", "cheap gym trainers", "shoes for plantar fasciitis", "running shoes for women") into themes: general running shoes; bargain hunters (cheap, low cost, discount code, affordable; send to a sale or clearance page); plantar fasciitis (orthopedic, supportive; name the problem and arch support in ad and page); ladies trainers (send to the women's collection).
2. Use 2 to 3 keywords per ad group, up to about 5 to 8. Source struggles to justify more than 5 to 7. A keyword that does not fit starts a new ad group.
3. Split further by intent where needed (source says his own example groups still mix intent).
4. Match ad copy and landing page headline to the theme and query. Use dynamic keyword insertion to echo the query (example "collagen for women in their 50s") in a headline or description. `[verify UI]`
5. Use the searcher's language: no jargon for problem-aware queries.
6. Landing page: check speed with PageSpeed Insights; message match; clear CTA and path; low friction; mobile optimised.

### 3.21 Negative keywords [2cd86d3bc065] [423e6a50781c]

1. Syntax mirrors keywords: broad (plain), phrase ("quotes"), exact ([brackets]).
2. Broad negative "running shoes" blocks searches with both words in any order ("blue running shoes", "shoes running", "running shoes"). It does not block "blue tennis shoes" or "running shoe" (singular).
3. Phrase negative blocks the words in the same order with extra words allowed ("blue running shoes" blocked; "shoes running" not blocked).
4. Exact negative blocks only the exact query with no extra words.
5. Add negatives one by one from search terms, or as lists. Apply at ad group, campaign (Search, Shopping, PMax) or account level. Account-level negatives hit all existing and future campaigns. `[verify UI]`
6. Cadence: daily for new campaigns and new products; at least a couple of times a week once stable.

### 3.23 Branded Search [fe8bd2cdf490] [c769e50b230c]

### 3.27 Answer the 5 cart-abandonment objections [1010d231578b] [74a36fd11057] [421c83b436b6]

1. Complacency or distraction ("I'll come back later"): an enticing offer plus urgency.
2. Price or value ("too expensive"): justify the price, compare to competitors, use price per unit (CPG); Harry's compared a $16 refill with Gillette at $40; frame the product as an investment.
3. Trust ("is this company legit?"): PR callouts, a third-party review platform such as Trustpilot, social proof.
4. Efficacy ("will this even work?", "one of the biggest"): real customer photo with a review inset, someone like the prospect.
5. Logistics friction (delivery, shipping cost, returns): state terms clearly; example sunglasses brand with free worldwide shipping, free returns and a 24-month warranty. Offer the best terms within your means. [needs brand data: shipping, returns and warranty terms]
6. Once the list is big enough, test ads per objection. If trust wins, add more trust and social proof to higher-funnel creative so fewer people abandon.
7. Share learnings with the Meta team for a joined-up remarketing plan across Google and Meta.

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. 2 primary purchase actions (double counting). The worst of the "3 cardinal sins". [30fdf39203d6]
2. GA4 import as the primary purchase action. [30fdf39203d6]
3. Non-purchase actions as primary or as account default goals. [30fdf39203d6] [535fcd04bd8b]
4. Treating a sudden ROAS doubling after linking GA4 as a win. [a3831e8b2c64]
5. Starting with PMax as a beginner, or spreading a small budget across all campaign types. [b7bf73d92363]
6. Starting PMax with under $100 a day. [c178b83767e8]
7. Mixing brand and non-brand in the same campaign, which hides non-brand results and overpays for brand traffic. [c4b8343d0510] [c769e50b230c]
8. Letting non-branded Shopping hit its budget cap, or leaving products out of it, so non-brand queries flow into branded. [f52ecc6c8839]
9. Leaving location on "Presence or interest". [535fcd04bd8b] [43c52017c399]
10. Leaving Search Partners or Display Network on. [535fcd04bd8b]
11. Setting Target ROAS far above actual (example 700% target on a 200% campaign; 400% target on 250% actual). The campaign stops spending. [5da4b51eabe6] [5d9dd44f8d9a]
12. Using automated bidding in a new account; it inflates CPCs. [805871e8952b]
13. Trusting Google's budget forecasts. [96b95d8a8ec0]
14. Spreading a new Shopping budget across the whole catalogue. [4995c5440e92]
15. Only adding negatives without fixing the feed. [25c967c6e637]
16. Brand name first in titles when you are not a market leader; repeating the brand in the title. [b5af5e150e67] [35ccb3d32bf5]
17. Text overlays on product images; blurry images; crinkled apparel on white. [d633238d5060]
18. Keyword stuffing titles or descriptions. [e58f1b803e0f]

20. Sending informational searches to a product page. [4691ad952431]
21. Maxing out 15 headlines to please ad strength; pinning without a reason; set-and-forget ad copy. [bfa79d563469] [533ef4069e40]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Header-only claims.** The document header describes a "feed-only Performance Max" (PMax with all text, image and video assets removed, used for product remarketing) and a switch from Maximize Clicks at "15 to 30 conversions per month". Neither appears in the lesson text. Card [eb5d63ad4917] (feed-only PMax) carries an editorial correction: PMax audience signals do not restrict targeting, removing assets does not isolate placements, and Merchant Center data can auto-generate assets. Card [c0c56417dfbd] (15 to 30) repeats the header. Treat both as unsupported by this transcript. [web check: current PMax asset and placement behaviour]
- **Thresholds disagree across lessons.** Shopping: Target ROAS at 30 to 50 conversions a month. Bid lesson: value bidding at 30, Target ROAS above 50 (mind map) or at 30 to 50 (text). DSA: automated at 30+. PMax: Target ROAS at 50 in one campaign. Header: 15 to 30. Keep each figure tied to its campaign type; do not average.
- **PMax first-stage bidding.** The setup lesson says "focus on conversion value" and then "run it on maximize conversions" for 2 to 4 weeks. The optimisation lesson says start on Maximize Conversion Value. Card [8e83ab723ce9] copies "maximize conversions". Likely a slip; confirm with the account's own goal.
- **Demand Gen remarketing and product feed.** The source says it performs better without the product feed. Card [bff4fce7c8df] says "serve dynamic remarketing creative", which conflicts with the setup lesson. Follow the lesson text. The earlier Display lesson says Display can show exact products viewed [4bb6cbc0d838]; the later lesson says Demand Gen "far outperforms display" for remarketing.
- **Demand Gen channels differ by use.** Remarketing: YouTube (in-stream, in-feed, Shorts), Discover, Gmail, no Display. Prospecting: Shorts only. Both from the same source, for different jobs.
- **Bidding objective for Demand Gen remarketing** is "optimize for conversions" with purchases, not conversion value. The source does not explain why.
- **Branded Shopping target ROAS** is garbled in the transcript ("15, 16, 17, 100%"). No usable number.
- **Remarketing window.** The lesson demonstrates a 30-day list but says the agency normally uses 3 to 7 days. The GA4 cart list used 60 days for low traffic.
- **List minimum.** "About 1,000 to 1,500" is the presenter's figure; card [8573a3074cc2] adds that it is not a universal minimum. [web check: Google's current list-size rules per network]
- **Customer lists.** The source says it uses lists for signals, lookalikes and exclusion, not direct remarketing. It does not mention Customer Match eligibility rules or consent. [web check: Customer Match policy and eligibility]
- **Card claims not in the text.** Card [911db04602d6] names Feedonomics; the text names only Simprosys. Card [35ccb3d32bf5] suggests an A/B title test and catalogue roll-out; the text gives no test method. Card [421c83b436b6] assigns roles to Meta vs Google retargeting; the text only says share learnings with the Meta team. Card [43c52017c399] gives the name "Shopping | All Products | UK"; the text only says to name it with product scope and jurisdiction.
- **Missing how-to.** No GTM or server-side tracking steps. No enhanced conversions setup. No Feed Army or Judge.me steps. No custom column formulas (said to be "in the description"). No YouTube placement exclusion list (also "in the description"). No AI Max or customer acquisition lessons, though both are promised. No Merchant Center disapproval fixes ("talk for a complete new video"). No negative keyword starter lists. No budget split between campaign types. No attribution discussion ("we'll skip attribution"). No measurement of incrementality beyond the brand split.
- **Evidence.** Every lift figure (20% Top Quality Store, 90% lifestyle image wins, 20% GA4 under-count, $5.84 to $2.38 CPC) is the agency's own observation with no sample size.
- **Regional mix.** Examples mix US ($, US locations) and UK (£50 budgets, UK location, "the tube"). Settings are the same; currency examples are not.
- **Opinions stated as rules.** "Never" automated bidding on brand; "never" in-market or affinity on YouTube; "always" Search Partners off; "always" Presence only. These are the presenter's positions.
- **Date risk.** Video Action campaigns deprecation (March 2025), PMax negatives, PMax channel reporting, full PMax search terms, AI Max and Demand Gen channel controls were all new or rolling out when recorded. [web check: current state of each]
- **Brand data needed before acting:** AOV, margin and ROAS floor, non-brand CPA, conversion volume per campaign, product-level sales and spend, site traffic for list sizes, review counts, shipping and returns terms, competitor bidding on the brand, customer avatar and pre-purchase behaviour, proven Meta creatives.
