## 2. Principles

1. **Google captures demand; it does not fix a broken offer.** Run Google to scale only after product-market fit, a proven offer, angle and message, and a converting page. If those are missing, source says "go back to Meta", test the offer there, and only set up basic Google. Evidence: opinion plus the Eugene Schwartz line "advertising cannot create demand". [447589198c09]
2. **Google is 4 ad platforms: Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display.** Search covers product-level, branded and problem-aware queries. Shopping is "60 to 80% of all ecommerce Google ads" and the highest converting because image, title, price, offer and reviews show together. YouTube (in-stream and Shorts) generates demand and sales. Display is for remarketing, and for cold reach only at "tens of thousands per day". For "90%" of brands: only Search, Shopping and YouTube. [c38dea49205d]
3. **Google is a comparative shopping platform.** Buyers see your product next to competitors. You win on price and promotions, offer (discounts, free shipping, deals), reviews, high-quality images, copy that matches the search, and fast, simple landing pages with few steps to buy. The ad only gets the click; the page sells. Biggest mistake: treating Google like Meta or running "2018-2019" keyword tricks. [c38dea49205d] [f4ea91fcade1]
4. **Run Google alongside Meta or you pay for demand a competitor captures.** People see a Meta ad, search later, and buy from a competitor, Amazon or a reseller. Source says Google should be about 20% to 30% of ecommerce revenue when done right; below that there is room. At scale it should be "20 to 30% at most"; more than that means other channels underperform. [447589198c09] [e97aeff823a5] `[needs brand data: Google share of total revenue from Shopify or attribution tool]`
5. **Growth = marketing message x volume.** A bad message will not be saved by more spend. A great message with low volume caps growth. [c38dea49205d]
6. **MER is "the only metric that matters" at the top level.** Total revenue / total marketing spend. If blended MER is strong, campaign ROAS, CPC and CPM matter less. [e97aeff823a5] [7f0f4a47add4]
7. **Scale to break-even on the first order, then profit on the back end.** Goal is new-customer acquisition at break-even or slightly profitable on first purchase, even for high AOV products. Raise AOV and LTV rather than only cutting CAC. "The person that can spend the most to acquire a customer wins." If backend makes LTV 3x to 5x, you can spend 2x to 3x more per customer. Condition: you must know break-even and have a real back end (email, SMS, remarketing, new products). [e97aeff823a5] [3335ab579d2a] [e2980a3a4e74] `[needs brand data: contribution margin, AOV, repeat rate, cohort LTV]`
8. **Structure first, scale later.** Before adding spend, have: branded search (brand protection), Shopping (biggest placement), and a controlled PMax. Only then add YouTube and cold search. "Poorly structured accounts will not scale no matter how much money is spent." [f6230028c4ce] [188ae31b3990]
9. **Segment branded from non-branded, and prospecting from retargeting.** Google converts the easiest customer first. Brand terms inside a prospecting campaign make ROAS look high while budget goes to people who would buy anyway. Keep 80% to 90% of budget on cold traffic. [f6230028c4ce] [a56ce106bb55]
10. **Trust automation, but set guardrails.** Let Google go broad (broad keywords, broad audiences, "no audience signals as much as you can", creative does the targeting) and use tROAS or tCPA where it fits (not standard shopping for the most part, never branded search). Keep control of: negative keyword scripts, daily search term pruning, landing pages, and budget decisions. Turn off Google's automated recommendations. "Default to letting Google take the wheel, but keep your foot near the brake." [b8508808a53a]
11. **Scaling is a process with daily, weekly and monthly actions.** Fix leaks before adding budget. [36a8c46cb661]
12. **Efficiency comes from focus.** 80/20 the catalog: push the hero product, cut poor SKUs, keep brand spend to 3% to 8% of monthly spend, 10% at most. [e248a09cecef]
13. **Search and Shopping are capped by search volume; YouTube breaks the cap.** YouTube, Display and Demand Gen create search volume and feed PMax and Shopping with intent. Most audited brands only run Search and run it badly. [5f52247ede2b] [6be29457f355]
14. **Always test to beat a control.** A/B test search ads and product feeds weekly (headlines, descriptions, CTAs) and test landing pages. Keep several ads per ad group. [e5912712faf6]
15. **Order of work: offer, landing page, unit economics, goals and KPIs, then conversion tracking, structure, targeting, creative, and bids and budget last.** Most brand owners start with bids and budget; source says that is the last thing. [f4ea91fcade1] [4c549b970082] [3335ab579d2a] [fa9689ea4d16]
16. **Let converting data pick keywords, not intuition.** Run Shopping and PMax broad, read search term reports, and move proven converting queries into search, titles, PMax assets, YouTube angles and landing pages. Buyer intent beats search volume. [a223f5b65ca8] [287eff9e6167]
17. **PMax is an engine, not the whole car.** It was built to give Google revenue by forcing spend into YouTube, Display, Discover and Gmail, but it is useful as a mid-funnel catch-all when fed good signals. It is not set-and-forget. [49aaa2e22390] [5beebcb72872]
18. **Signal density: fewer, fuller campaigns.** Split PMax only when products serve different audiences (wallets vs luggage) or countries differ. Otherwise 1 campaign with asset groups. Source claims Google does not optimize across campaigns at account level. [329599511dc8] [96fdc15f1d9a] [d5f436322917]
19. **Know break-even CPA and ROAS or you cannot scale on any platform.** "Everything is a CPM arbitrage." If your unit economics cannot hold the platform's natural CPA even with good setup, fix the business, not the campaign. [7f0f4a47add4] [5beebcb72872]
20. **For Meta-heavy direct response brands, YouTube first.** Put about 80% of Google budget into Demand Gen. Run Search and Shopping only if the product has real category search demand. Creative plus conversion mechanism is "80% of the outcome". [78000b7f5684] [e0e3cef53d9f]
21. **Cold traffic is not branded conversions.** Do not let an agency claim branded or organic demand as paid growth. [a56ce106bb55]
22. **Be in the account daily.** Demand spikes (for example after a podcast) can only be funded if someone sees them within hours. [d40cdd66a2bc]
23. **Ads are only the traffic half; the conversion mechanism is the other half.** Source's agency builds the whole chain: ad, presale page, product page, order, after-sell (to raise AOV and lower CAC), then email, SMS and upsells. Match the page to market sophistication: an unaware market needs education first; a known market needs differentiation. Condition: cold traffic. Evidence: methylene blue case, where the rebuilt product page "basically doubled" cold conversion rate. [2f74141822ca] [7e3c5f19e16e] [58e05cd75459]
24. **When a category gets attention, competitors follow; win on proof and differentiation, not on being first.** In the methylene blue case competitors entered in late 2024. Their pages had no education, founder story or differentiation. The source added evidence, clarity and differentiation at every step. [83d48a362b05]
25. **Decide what you can pay per customer from long-window numbers.** Source says to "run the numbers" at 30, 60, 90, 180 and 365 days and buy customers at or just under break-even, as the methylene blue brand did without ever going below break-even. Condition: a repeat-purchase product and real retention data. [4478e71195a6] `[needs brand data: cohort revenue and margin by 30/60/90/180/365 days]`

### 3.1 Three growth phases for a new account [4aee86b1351f]

1. Phase 1, foundation and testing, about 30 to 60 days: launch branded search, branded shopping and 1 prospecting shopping campaign. Set up conversion tracking and analytics. Use manual bidding to control spend. Wait for at least 1 conversion per day across 30 days before smart bidding, PMax or YouTube.
2. Phase 2, about months 2 to 6: switch to Maximize Conversions or tROAS set from margins and break-even ROAS. Remove low performing keywords. Start retargeting and audience segmentation. Launch YouTube and PMax.
3. Phase 3, after about 6 months: raise budget on winners, add search and shopping keywords from data, scale with PMax under control. With spare budget, add cold search and Display.
4. Source says a full build covering "90% of the inventory" takes about 1 to 4 months.

A shorter version in the "9 things" video: start at the bottom of the funnel, get conversions, then add 1 mid-funnel campaign (usually Shopping or PMax), and only after smart bidding is possible (about 1 conversion per day for 30 days). Then take Meta winners into YouTube Shorts and statics at about $50 a day, fully broad. [a2427f18e5fe]

### 3.2 Pre-ads checklist ("9 things", in order) [f4ea91fcade1] [4c549b970082] [3335ab579d2a] [b27fb4f22fa1] [a2427f18e5fe] [52816ff36204] [fa9689ea4d16]

1. Offer. Shipping threshold framed as "shipping's on us" (credited to Maxwell Copy). Buy 1 get 1, buy 2 get 1, percent off. AOV above about $100: dollar off (for example "$50 off"). Bundles. Put the offer everywhere in the ads. `[needs brand data: current offers, margins that allow them]`
2. Landing page. Must be congruent with the ad. Check average session duration: good CTR, CPC and impression share with short sessions means the page, not the ad, is the problem.
3. Unit economics. Enough margin to put into ads. Raise AOV (offer, bundles) before trying to push COGS down. With strong LTV you may go break-even or below (source: "at like 0.8") and earn it back over 12 months in cohort data.
4. Goals and KPIs. Set break-even new-customer ROAS and CPA. Decide: LTV play (go a bit below break-even) or first-order profitable. Optimize to purchase "99% of the time", not add to cart or clicks.
5. Conversion tracking: enhanced conversions, done first (section 3.3).
6. Campaign structure: by funnel stage (section 3.4).
7. Targeting: country; then deep audience understanding: their language, psychographics, problems, pains, goals, desires, why they buy and why from you. Ask: what person searches this, what pain, what problem, what desire, what goal.
8. Creative: Search = headlines, images, descriptions (main), plus callouts, sitelinks, structured snippets (bigger ad, more clicks). Shopping = image, title (targeting), promotion, ratings and social proof, description. YouTube = Shorts creative, heavy direct response, obvious CTA ("click here", "type this in", "search this", "tap the screen"), big offer.
9. Bids and budget: last. Bid and budget as high as KPIs allow. Branded shopping on Manual CPC; branded search on Target Impression Share (absolute top); smart campaigns on Maximize Conversion Value (main for ecommerce) or Maximize Conversions, then tROAS or tCPA. Use Maximize Conversions or tCPA only when you know break-even CPA and want volume, not value.

### 3.3 Conversion tracking for Shopify (enhanced, via GTM) [fff2f5c0c47c]

Result: purchase, add to cart, begin checkout and product view tags, plus dynamic remarketing and GA4. The source's GTM container and Shopify custom pixel code come from its free guide; the text does not include the code. `[verify UI]`

1. Shopify admin > Google & YouTube app. Connect it. Make sure Google Analytics 4 is installed and connected (pop-up or Settings).
2. Google Ads > Goals > Settings. Turn on Enhanced conversions. Method: Google tag.
3. Goals > Conversions > Summary > Create > Website. Enter your domain > Scan. At the bottom choose "Add a conversion action manually".
4. Purchase action: Category = Purchase. Add to account default goals. Primary. Name "<brand> purchase". Value = "Use different values for each conversion". Count = Every. Click-through window = 90 days. Engaged-view window = 30 days. View-through window = 30 days. Attribution = Data-driven. Managed through the Google tag.
5. Add to cart action: same settings, but remove it from account default goals.
6. Begin checkout action: same as add to cart.
7. Product view action: category Page view, same settings.
8. Save and continue. Copy the conversion ID and the 4 conversion labels into a doc.
9. tagmanager.google.com > Admin > Import container. Upload the downloaded container. Choose Merge. Add to existing workspace.
10. Variables: edit the constants "conversion ID", "purchase label", "add to cart label", "begin checkout label", "product view label". Paste each value. Save each.
11. Submit > Publish > Continue. Copy the GTM container ID into your doc.
12. Shopify > Settings > Customer events > Custom pixels > Add custom pixel. Name "Google conversion pixel". Delete the default code. Paste the GTM customer-event code. Set the country code (examples: US, Canada, UK, Australia, Germany). Paste the GTM ID. Save, then Connect.
13. Back in Google Ads conversion actions: edit add to cart, begin checkout and page view to "Secondary (observe only)". Purchase stays the account default goal for every ecommerce campaign.
14. Keep only 1 primary conversion action per campaign goal. In the cold search case the Shopify app conversion sat as secondary and the Google tag purchase as primary. [68c39167796f] [634ed94f1328]

Check after: conversions in Google Ads should match Shopify orders within reason. The source gives no numeric match threshold. `[needs brand data: Shopify orders vs Google Ads purchases for the same days]`

### 3.5 Campaign structure: the "cold customer magnet" [0cc26b9c7f70] [71d14cc19eab] [188ae31b3990] [2242de590259]

- Top of funnel (create demand): Demand Gen on YouTube Shorts and in-stream (statics also named), non-brand search, Display (optional, "if you have the balls"). Landing pages: advertorials, listicles, VSLs, quiz funnels, or a well-built product page.
- Middle of funnel (capture demand, capped by search volume): PMax (search, shopping, Discover, display), prospecting standard shopping, cold search, competitive search "if it works well", Dynamic Search Ads. Lands on the product page.
- Bottom of funnel (convert): branded search, branded shopping, dynamic remarketing via Merchant Center or a remarketing campaign. Drop-offs at homepage, product page and add to cart are remarketed until they buy; cart abandoners go back to the product page.
- Role of each: PMax is the "big spender", then Shopping; together they cover the Shopping placement. Non-brand search takes high-intent and problem queries. Brand search and shopping capture aware people cheaply and block competitors. YouTube creates search volume and runs its own funnel. Dynamic remarketing ties it together.
- Exclusions for cold campaigns: exclude all customer lists; exclude brand from PMax. [a2427f18e5fe]
- Source claims the full system returns "3 to 5x" per dollar. Example claim only.

### 3.6 Keyword research [a223f5b65ca8] [287eff9e6167]

Method 1, let Google find keywords (for speed and scale):
1. Launch Shopping and PMax, broad.
2. Daily, read search term reports. Rank by impressions, CTR, conversions and CPC. In Shopping this also shows what your product title actually matches.
3. Split queries into converting vs wasted spend. Exclude low performers.
4. Move converting queries into search campaigns as exact match first; phrase match for close variations. Avoid broad match in search campaigns unless testing.
5. Add the same phrases to product titles and attributes, PMax assets, YouTube angles, offers and landing pages. Example: running shoes; Shopping shows "best marathon shoes for flat feet" converting; build copy, a landing page or even a listicle or advertorial for it.
6. Repeat. It is a flywheel and also an angle-discovery tool.

Method 2, traditional (for control, and for the very first keywords):
1. Google Keyword Planner: enter your main product category; pick product-level terms with high volume and acceptable CPC.
2. Ahrefs or Semrush: reverse engineer competitor keywords. SpyFu: see what competitors bid on.
3. Limit: no purchase data. "Assume your competition is full of it" and bid on things that do not work.

Source's order: method 1 first, then layer method 2 for extra keywords and the "first rung". The transcript is unclear on whether to use method 1 for the very first search keywords (see section 9).

Title examples from converting queries: "men's Nike Air Zoom running shoes, lightweight, cushioned, for marathon runners"; "Adidas Ultraboost 21 breathable mesh running shoes for men". [a223f5b65ca8]

### 3.7 Merchant Center setup SOP [5fa36e157a58] [bb3574f9e67a] [7255257382d0] [5157f5f5a687] [8a99ccae1285] [b9ecdf07948a]

Phase 1, business setup:
1. Settings > Add-ons > Discover. Enroll in the 4 core programs: dynamic remarketing, Google customer reviews, product ratings, promotions. Check each is active, not just available. `[verify UI]`
2. Business info: enter accurate details. Verify and claim the online store. Add checkout link and settings, accepted payment methods. Anything that does not match the website risks suspension ("Google are ruthless with this").
3. Business info > Edit logo and colors: square logo, rectangular logo, main color, accent color.
4. US only: Business info > Edit business details > business identity attributes (woman-owned, veteran-owned, Black-owned, Latino-owned, small business). Source recommends "small business" even if you are not small, citing a Bureau of Labor Statistics "$40 million" line. Do not follow without checking; see section 9. `[web check: Google's definition of the small business attribute and current eligibility]`
5. Add social profiles (Instagram, Facebook, others).

Phase 2, shipping, returns and tax (source: the first thing that gets brands suspended):
1. Shopify > Google & YouTube channel > product feed settings > shipping settings = Manual (set up in GMC), so Shopify changes do not silently create mismatches. `[verify UI]`
2. GMC > Shipping and returns: a shipping policy for every country you ship to, by speed and method (example brand: US and Canada). Accurate ship-from location.
3. Returns: a return policy is effectively required. Add it in GMC, match the website, and link directly to the specific returns policy page.
4. Tax is in the phase name but the source gives no tax steps. `[web check: current Merchant Center tax settings for your country]`

Phase 3, feed health:
1. Settings > Data sources: find the primary feed (Content API via the Shopify Google & YouTube app), any supplemental feeds and promotion sources. Confirm the primary is updating.
2. Products > Needs attention: fix every issue Google lists, red errors and yellow warnings (example: approved products missing age group).

Phase 4, trust and visibility:
1. Add promotions (example: buy 2 get 1 free; percent offers are easier to set up). Most brands do not.
2. Reviews > Product reviews: confirm the review feed is importing; check disapprovals.
3. Shopping experience scorecard (store quality): fix what is missing across shipping, returns, purchase, browsing and promotion. Aim for "exceptional" to earn the trusted badge. The score is compared against competitors.

Ongoing: weekly, fix new red and yellow items in Products and diagnostics; monthly, check that website shipping and returns match GMC; when a sale starts add the promotion with a correct end date. [b9ecdf07948a]

### 3.8 Product feed optimization [3cce923d11c9] [f74643a53f4d] [eeaaed11a13b] [61f93049590a] [c32fb2d0de63] [ac32e7e0613d]

1. Title (most important attribute): 130 to 150 characters. Small brand pushing cold traffic: highest-volume keyword + variant details (color, size, capacity) + second-highest-volume keyword + angles and USPs + brand name last. Big brand: brand first, then the same order. Edit in GMC > Products > open SKU > Edit product, or in a feed tool such as DataFeedWatch. Source suggests pasting the formula and product into ChatGPT for a draft, then choosing the 2 keywords with most impressions yourself. `[verify UI]` `[web check: current title character limit]`
2. Description: about 1,000 characters. Front-load high-volume keywords like an SEO article. Key features, USPs, product info and a call to action. No promotional text (policy). Do not just pull the Shopify page copy.
3. Image: highest quality, most standout. Test product alone vs product in context (sofa in a lit living room, golf club mid-swing, watch band on a watch) and people using it. For the example brand, the product image was the main one that worked. Test your own.
4. Brand field: your brand, or the real brand if you resell.
5. Product type and Google product category: most accurate one per SKU (source uses GPT to suggest). Condition: new unless used. Price accurate. Availability in stock.
6. Never change the product ID or SKU. Let it import from Shopify or the feed tool.
7. GTIN, UPC, EAN, JAN or ISBN: get from the supplier and enter the barcode. Custom-made products may have none.
8. Filter attributes (apparel): color, size, size type, sizing system, gender, material, pattern, age group. Other: bundle, adult, energy class.
9. Shipping and returns pull from Shopify or GMC policies.
10. Additional attributes: cost of goods, sell quantity on Google, currency. Minor for performance, helpful for trust.
11. Custom labels: group SKUs for targeting and reporting (example: small bands = label 1, large bands = label 2).
12. Never edit price, availability or sale price in GMC. Change them in Shopify so the feed matches the site.

### 3.9 Anti-suspension checklist (source says 34 items) [95550c2e178c] [eea0c9ae49af] [a9d854c2d98d] [173c252bafd5] [9de75b008814] [f7f63ab97480] [8ff6407379e3]

The source says each item has a frequency (daily, weekly, monthly, once) but only gives some. Items as spoken:

Payment and checkout:
1. SSL on checkout: URL starts with https.
2. Test every listed payment method (PayPal, cards, crypto, Afterpay, others). A listed method that fails is an error that can suspend the account.
3. Final price transparency: sales tax (including state tax) and shipping by destination shown before payment and matching Shopify and GMC. Run test orders.
4. Test discount codes. A GMC promotion will not go live if the code fails at checkout; an approved promotion that later breaks is "a massive flag".

Product listings:
5. Remove exaggerated or unverifiable claims. Scientific claims need credible sources on the listing, description and landing page. Source calls misrepresentation "the biggest thing that hits brands", especially in health. `[web check: Google misrepresentation and healthcare policies for the category]`
6. Real, recent reviews only. Use a Klaviyo flow to collect reviews and send the review feed to GMC (via Judge.me) for star ratings.
7. Out of stock must show as sold out, synced to real stock. "In stock" on the page while out of stock is a violation.
8. Check every link on the page, especially from product page to the listing. Broken links can be flagged as a scam.
9. Price on page = price at checkout = price in GMC; discounts apply correctly; keep them auto-synced.

Branding:
10. Logo quality and placement.
11. All brand info in GMC.
12. Verify URL and business info (once, at setup).
13. Monthly: refresh homepage branding, message and visuals. A default Shopify theme looks low trust.
14. Navigation and hamburger menu: no broken links; direct links to contact, home, products, about, return policy, shipping policy, subscription management.

Shipping:
15. Website shipping settings match GMC.
16. When shipping cost or delivery time changes, update GMC. Case: a client changed courier, did not update GMC, and was suspended for about a week.
17. Name couriers (UPS, FedEx, DHL) in the site policy and GMC policy.
18. Order tracking works.

Returns:
19. Step-by-step return instructions in GMC and on the site; GMC links to the site returns page. No returns policy, shipping policy or terms of service on the site = suspension "guaranteed".
20. Refund processing times updated monthly.
21. Return period current.
22. Test return links.
23. Monthly: verify return address and timing.

Legal:
24. Review terms and conditions.
25. Privacy policy matches current data practice (Shopify defaults).
26. Footer holds all policies: returns, about, terms, contact, shipping, subscription management.

Contact:
27. Monthly: physical address is current.
28. Test the customer service phone number. A phone line is a strong trust signal; at minimum an easy contact route.
29. Branded email (support@yourdomain), not Gmail. Set up once.
30. Same contact details across GMC, Google Ads and all Google accounts.
31. SSL across the whole domain.

Store design and performance:
32. Test load speed and navigation. Source claims slow sites pay higher CPCs and can lose the trusted badge. `[web check: Google statements on landing page speed and store badges]`
33. Proper brand design: no default theme, high-quality images, "no AI gen stuff", clear fonts; product images and descriptions congruent with GMC.
34. Test all apps and plugins; remove dead apps.

If suspended, Google usually will not say why; assume it is one of these items.

### 3.10 AI copy workflow [a9aae4ff1aef] [f3a2d35f0aea] [3f95898cc997] [3b4c43576e04]

Prompt structure the source uses every time: role, context, task, examples, constraints, output format. The actual prompts sit in the agency's free pack and are not in the text.

1. ChatGPT, prompt 1: role "world-class direct response copywriter" for ecommerce cold traffic. Attach a PDF of a product page (browser print, save as PDF). Ask for an audit: value proposition, emotional and logical persuasion, CTAs, voice of customer, proof, offer positioning, copy structure and flow, conversion blockers, missed psychological levers, fixes. Example brand: Ridge wallets.
2. Same chat, prompt 2: role "elite advertising strategist and deep researcher" to build a research framework (product intelligence, psychographics, competitors, keyword and intent mapping, positioning, guarantees and offer).
3. Same chat, prompt 3: role "prompt engineer" to write 1 deep-research prompt about your own brand.
4. Gemini > Tools > Deep Research: paste that prompt, attach the product page PDF and the website URL. Approve the plan. Runs about 15 to 20 minutes. Output was 31+ pages. Save to Google Drive.
5. Claude (Sonnet 4 in the video): role "AI prompt engineer and brand strategist". Paste the research. ### 3.16 Scaling PMax and other smart campaigns [7f0f4a47add4] [4175eb319be4]

1. Compute break-even ROAS and CPA (section 4 formulas). Treat them as non-negotiable.
2. ROAS consistently above break-even: raise budget. CPA trending below break-even CPA: raise bids (loosen target) for volume. Below break-even: pause, adjust or optimize.
3. Weekly: scale winners 10% to 20% ("you can scale more, but you didn't hear that from me").
4. Spending 100% of budget and at or above KPI: raise budget 10% to 15%.
5. Spending under 80% of budget and underperforming: lower tROAS (or loosen tCPA) to widen reach.
6. "Cut fast, scale slow." Google is sensitive to big changes.
7. After a bid strategy change, wait 2 to 4 weeks before big tweaks.
8. Check MER across Meta, TikTok, organic, Klaviyo and Google. A profitable PMax can add no incremental lift or hurt other channels. `[needs brand data: total revenue and total marketing spend by week]`

Advanced options [798fd2575bf2]:
- Mike Rhodes' PMax script (free) for placement, cost and search detail. `[web check: script still maintained]`
- Standard shopping at a low tROAS plus PMax as catch-all at a higher tROAS. Sometimes works; test.
- Use PMax search term data to launch dedicated search campaigns (PMax takes a lot of search spend). Start search on product-aware terms.
- Multi-country: a separate Google Ads account per country, or duplicate the PMax per country.

### 3.17 YouTube via Demand Gen [87c6c1776dbe] [a1e9a7459f53] [0a48fe8e9e6d] [6803e3b2bebe]

Placements:
- In-stream (skippable): most scale; clients at $8,000, $12,000 and $16,000 a day.
- Shorts: cheap CPMs, 1 to 2 cent clicks, fast creative tests; Shorts winners usually win in-stream after tweaks.
- In-feed: needs a thumbnail and higher production; suits long, organic-style videos (example: an 8-minute competitor product test posted as a sponsored video with a CTA at the end). Do later.
- Do not run Discover, Gmail or Display in this setup.

Setup: `[verify UI]`
1. + New campaign > Sales > Purchases only (remove other goals; 1 primary action) > Demand Gen. Name it clearly.
2. Product feed: off ("never worth running").
3. Bidding: Maximize Conversions, goal purchase, no target CPA yet.
4. Budget: at least $100 a day.
5. Brand guidelines optional. No EU political ads.
6. Location: your country. Language: set English explicitly (in the US, avoids Spanish-language traffic).
7. Devices: tablets always off. New to YouTube with only Meta creative: mobile only. To test, turn on computers or TV and watch by device. As you scale: computers and TV on, with horizontal ads.
8. Ad schedule: all day. Third-party measurement: none. URL options: Triple Whale handles it.
9. Ad group > channels "Let me choose": only YouTube in-stream and YouTube Shorts. Off: in-feed, Discover, Gmail, Display.
10. Audience: none. Fully broad. Creative, copy and landing page do the targeting. Case: $8,000 a day broad and profitable.
11. Optimized targeting: on when scaling; off when testing creative, testing policy approval, or at low budget.
12. Ad: Video ad. Final URL: never the homepage; use a product page, VSL, listicle, advertorial or quiz funnel.
13. Upload videos unlisted to YouTube; paste URLs; up to 5 per ad group. Test 5 concepts; move the winner to its own ad group; hook-test against it.
14. Length rules: in-feed off. Under about 60 seconds: Shorts on (speed up slightly and shift pitch if just over). Over 60 seconds: Shorts off, in-stream only. 9:16 works in in-stream.
15. Logos. Asset optimization: off (no auto shorter versions, no vertical or square auto versions).
16. Text: 5 headlines up to 40 characters (search allows 30), 5 long headlines up to 90, descriptions up to 90. Use the full length. CTA: "Shop now". Business name. Sitelinks: off ("look really bad on YouTube").
17. Publish. Structure: 1 campaign like a CBO with a winners ad group and a testing ad group; graduate winners based on dashboard or Triple Whale.

Creative (in-stream): 5 forced seconds, about 30 seconds to count as a view. Formula: 0 to 5 seconds strongest audio and visual "movie trailer" hook; next about 5 seconds intro product or problem; product demo; social proof near the end; strong direct response CTA. Types that work: high-production talking head (4K, great audio, editing), podcast style (2 people, podcast hook), founder ads, story ads (customer or founder), product demos. Target above 1% CTR on in-stream.

Problem-aware hooks [d2116e31fe30]: call out the situation, not the demographic. Arthritis example: do not say "50 year olds"; talk about bending over or playing with grandkids. Aim for "uh-huh, that's me". Edutainment. Helps compliance because it makes no condition claim. CTA matches the mechanism: "learn more" (article, listicle, advertorial), offer or product page, VSL, "take this 1-minute quiz". No lazy AI voice ads (ElevenLabs v3 named); make it look organic, intentional and native to the feed.

Conversion mechanism order [7d3bf600db06]: (1) a "decked-out" sales page (enhanced product page with bundles and presale content, not just a buy button); (2) advertorial (info, value or story; source prefers story); (3) quiz funnel; (4) listicle (has not "panned out too well"); (5) VSL only at scale after the others saturate.

### 3.18 YouTube scaling [9bfa186f1531] [b934c312198e]

Traffic lights per ad:
- Poor (poor view rate, poor CTR, no conversions, new-customer CPA out of KPI): pause now.
- Soft (OK view rate, good CTR, some conversions, new-customer CPA near KPI): keep a while; keep looking for a stronger ad.
- Strong (great view rate, great CTR, new-customer CPA in KPI): raise budget fast. Source: "morally obligated to spend more".

Scale order ("more, better, new", credited to Hormozi):
1. More: raise budget (still on Maximize Conversions) until the natural ceiling. Add more creatives to beat the control.
2. Better: hook-test winners (first 5 seconds drive view rate). Make 16:9 versions of winners (duplicate in sequence in Premiere or CapCut with a blurred background, or re-edit from 16:9 footage). Case: Shorts CPM about $17 vs about $8 for the horizontal version. Test new offers and CTAs (CTR). Test new conversion mechanisms (conversion rate).
3. New: in-feed with an 8 to 12 minute long-form sales video and a proper thumbnail.

### 3.19 Plan for Meta-heavy direct response brands [78000b7f5684] [e0e3cef53d9f] [d8674659134f] [a56ce106bb55]

Condition: brand spends mostly on Meta, often 1 product and 1 offer, cold traffic, sometimes Funnelish funnels, $500,000 to $1M a month in sales.

2. Decide on search. Run search only with real category demand (blood pressure, methylene blue). Unique low-volume products (tanning pill, hyaluronic acid skin product): skip.
3. Budget: about 80% Demand Gen (in-stream and Shorts, mobile only until about $1,000 a day); about 10% to 15% mid-funnel (shopping, PMax without brand, cold search), up to 20% to 30% for big categories; bottom funnel (brand search, brand shopping, dynamic remarketing) about 1% to 5%, "maybe even 0".

6. Brand campaign: never overspend. If running it, use absolute top impression share bidding; an account with 30 cent clicks at 14% or under 10% impression share gets none of the benefit. Source says to experiment with turning brand off.
7. Creatives: your best, compliant, well-produced ads. No AI "spam volume" creative.
8. Do not copy stock setups. DSA only with a large content site across many categories (not a 1-page skincare funnel).
9. Tracking: new-customer tag (section 3.4). Site: mobile-optimized, trustworthy.
10. Playbook order: problem-aware creative; choose mechanism; CRO; iterate creative across awareness levels; scale budget once the mechanism holds.

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

Conflicts inside the source:
1. **Competitor campaigns.** Philosophy video: running competitor campaigns is "a waste of time" because brand protection neutralizes them. 9-things video: "competitive search if it works well" sits in the mid funnel. AI workflow outputs "competitor campaign" test variations. [f6230028c4ce] [a2427f18e5fe] [3b4c43576e04]
2. **Brand spend.** "Every single ecom brand needs a branded search campaign" and branded shopping, with brand at 3% to 8% (max 10%). For Meta-heavy brands: 1% to 5%, "maybe even 0", "you don't actually need it", "just turn your brand off". The Meta-heavy drawing also says "5 to 10%". One garbled line says "don't ever spend on brand... make sure you're at like 78% search impression". Resolve by brand type and test; do not average. [6319ccc026d9] [e248a09cecef] [a56ce106bb55] [78000b7f5684]
3. **PMax budget share.** "PMax will be your big spender" vs "20 to 30%" of budget vs 10% to 15% mid-funnel for Meta-heavy brands. The methylene blue case ran mostly PMax. [188ae31b3990] [49aaa2e22390] [78000b7f5684] [d5f436322917]
4. **Audience signals.** Philosophy: "no audience signals as much as you can". PMax video: "use strong audience signals" (customer list, in-market, custom intent). Demand Gen: no audiences at all. Case: customer list "as observation" plus custom audiences from cold search terms. [b8508808a53a] [25eaf30834e2] [87c6c1776dbe]
5. **Phrase match.** Keyword research: exact for proven winners, phrase for variations. Cold search case: "never phrase match", exact plus broad. Brand search: add phrase later. [a223f5b65ca8] [68c39167796f] [b62174aad487]
6. **Broad match in search.** Keyword video: avoid broad in search unless testing. Cold search case: broad match with AI Max is the core method. The case is itself a test phase, so both can hold. [a223f5b65ca8] [68c39167796f]
7. **Scaling speed.** "Cut fast, scale slow", 10% to 20% a week, wait 2 to 4 weeks. But also "scale winners at the same speed if not faster", YouTube "morally obligated... break it as fast as possible", and a 10x budget jump in the podcast spike. Condition matters: steady state vs a clear demand spike or a clear winner. [4175eb319be4] [9bfa186f1531] [d40cdd66a2bc]
8. **Impression share.** 50% to 60% "for all your campaigns" vs 95% for brand search. [36a8c46cb661] [b62174aad487]
9. **Display.** Only at tens of thousands a day; but TOF includes display "if you have the balls"; Meta-heavy video says start with "YouTube and display", then says display is hard, go YouTube. [c38dea49205d] [2242de590259] [e0e3cef53d9f]
10. **VSL.** Listed as a normal TOF landing page early; later "only at scale once other mechanisms are saturated". [0cc26b9c7f70] [7d3bf600db06]
11. **DSA.** Listed in the mid funnel; later called a stock agency mistake unless there is a large content site. [71d14cc19eab] [e0e3cef53d9f]
12. **First keywords.** "To get your very first keywords for any basic search campaigns... I don't recommend it" (seemingly about broad), then "the best approach is method 1 first, then layer method 2". Unclear transcript. [287eff9e6167]
13. **YouTube start budget.** $50 a day with Meta winners (9-things video) vs at least $100 a day (setup video) vs calling a $50 a day, $500 test "not a real test". [a2427f18e5fe] [87c6c1776dbe] [e0e3cef53d9f]
14. **Feed description.** Include a call to action, but avoid promotional text. [f74643a53f4d]
15. **Location option slip.** PMax video once says "presence or interest"; everywhere else the rule is Presence only.
16. **Shopping share.** Shopping is "60 to 80% of all ecommerce Google ads" but also Search is "one quarter". Treat both as rough.

Conflicts with common ecommerce advice:
1. **Break-even formulas use gross margin.** Source: break-even ROAS = 100 / gross margin %, break-even CPA = AOV x gross margin %, with a muddled "minus any fixed cost of COGS". Common practice uses contribution margin after COGS, shipping, payment fees, fulfillment and returns. Card 7f0f4a47add4 already rewrites it that way. Use contribution margin. `[needs brand data: full variable cost per order]`
2. **Brand search incrementality.** Source admits most brand conversions "would happen anyway" but still says every brand needs it. Many incrementality tests find brand search mostly non-incremental. Treat brand campaigns as protection and data, and test pausing. `[needs brand data: brand search holdout or pause test]`
3. **"Small business" attribute for any company under $40M.** Google's identity attributes are self-declared; claiming "small business" when it is not true risks misrepresentation. Do not apply without checking Google's definition. `[web check: Merchant Center small business attribute definition]`
4. **Hiring someone to act as the founder** (founder ads "cheat code", "even if you're drop shipping"). This is deceptive and against ecomOS rules on invented claims. Do not use. Use the real founder or a clearly labeled spokesperson. [6803e3b2bebe]
5. **Tablets always off.** Source's preference, not tested evidence. Check your own device data. [4184750845e0]
6. **Max conversion windows (90 days click).** Longer windows raise reported conversions. Pick windows that match the purchase cycle and compare with Shopify. `[needs brand data: time from first click to purchase]`
7. **Speed claims** (slow site means higher CPC and badge loss) and **TV share** (50% to 60% of US YouTube views) and **2 billion users** and **incrementality studies** are stated without sources. `[web check: YouTube TV-screen share of US watch time; YouTube incrementality studies]`
8. **YouTube case ROAS 1.29 called first-order profitable.** Only true if contribution margin is very high (break-even ROAS under 1.29 means margin above about 78%). Check against your own margin before copying any target. `[needs brand data: break-even ROAS]`
