## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator plan a Google Ads program for an ecommerce store at the strategy level. It covers which campaign types to start with, which products to push to cold traffic, how unit economics set the cost you can afford per customer, how to design front-end offers and discounts, where to find ad copy angles (reviews, founder story) and when to scale. It is thin on platform how-to. It gives almost no settings, bid strategies, feed rules or keyword steps.

## 2. Principles

1. **Start with Search and Shopping. Add Performance Max later.** Condition: new Google advertisers, or accounts with little purchase history. Search (text ads) and Shopping (carousel with product image, price and click-through) are the most intuitive types because they match how people think of Google: someone searches, relevant ads appear. Performance Max uses Google's automatic targeting, bidding and placement across Search, Shopping, YouTube, Display, Discover and Gmail, so it needs more conversion data. Source says to start thinking about it at "more than say 100 purchases, ideally as many as possible". Performance Max is an addition, not a replacement. Evidence: agency practice. [575bdc0721ef]
2. **On cold traffic, focus spend on 1 or a few winning products.** Condition: cold audiences only. Budgets spread thin across many SKUs hurt ROAS and scalability. Source says 1 product or a handful "perform way better" for cold acquisition, and moving budget to them is one of the agency's usual early wins when taking over an ecommerce account. Evidence: agency practice. [f08cac856543]
3. **On warm traffic, open up the full catalog.** Condition: site visitors, cart adders, past buyers. Use product catalogs in remarketing so a person sees the product they viewed or added to cart, not the storewide best seller. Sell the rest of the range through remarketing, email and SMS once people are "in your ecosystem". Evidence: agency practice. [422b680d3617]
4. **Know your numbers: AOV, gross margin and LTV set what you can pay per customer.** Condition: any paid advertising. Source says an AOV over $100 makes profitable ads "much easier". A $120 AOV at 10% gross margin leaves only $12 gross profit per order, which acquisition can easily exceed. Know which products have better margins and put more budget there. Evidence: illustrative numbers. [efe781ef33f1]
5. **Improve your numbers, and LTV most of all.** Condition: businesses with repeat purchase potential. A higher LTV raises the cost you can afford per customer. Source says the biggest companies rarely have the lowest CAC; they pay more per customer at scale and win by raising LTV, not by chasing a 15% to 20% CPA cut. Evidence: Starbucks and Apple illustrations, plus a worked example ($30 to $120 allowable CPA). [ef1555c15a96]
6. **Scale has value beyond first-order profit.** Condition: when cash flow allows. More customers give a bigger remarketing pool, email list and SMS list, more people for seasonal sales, and possibly better supplier prices. Source says it is worth making "a little bit less" per customer to gain scale "in many cases". Evidence: opinion. [ef1555c15a96]
7. **Law of reciprocity: give more up front to get more back later.** Condition: the entry product naturally leads to more purchases, and cash flow can fund the gap. Example format: free plus shipping. Best for bringing people into a new type of product or hobby. Evidence: example only (free football). [5632d9d930a2]
8. **Discount with a plan, not by default.** Condition: most brands, which are not "the cheapest in the market". Heavy discounting risks a race to the bottom where volume moves but gross profit is gone. The source is "a big fan" of sales; the rule is to be tactical. Exceptions: brands whose whole model is lowest price or bulk should lean in; premium or luxury brands should give free extras instead of price cuts. Evidence: opinion and analogy. [38b728817a44]
9. **Accurate cross-channel tracking comes first.** Condition: especially when running ads on more than 1 channel or getting organic customers too. "Without accurate data, you are flying blind." Source recommends Hyros, inside a paid promotion. Evidence: the presenter's own use. [d7752a494148]
10. **Use reviews as ad copy research.** Condition: any ecommerce brand. Ecommerce gets more reviews than service businesses, so the sample is large. Five-star themes often differ from what the owner thinks people buy for. Negative reviews point to product fixes. With no reviews yet, use competitor reviews. Evidence: opinion with examples. [082b2980e92f]
11. **Scale from strength. ROAS falls as spend rises.** Condition: always. Google shows ads to the best prospects first. More spend reaches less interested people, so ROAS drops. The drop "doesn't have to be super dramatic", but it will happen. A campaign that fails at a low budget fails because the campaign or offer is not good enough, not because spend is too low. Evidence: explanation of how the auction works, plus agency experience. [af051bd7984a]
12. **You may scale below your usual ROAS target if LTV has risen.** Condition: LTV work has been done and measured. Example: ROAS of 1.5 was not profitable, LTV doubled, so 1.5 or even 1.2 becomes acceptable. [af051bd7984a] [ef1555c15a96]
13. **Build a personal brand, or at least tell your story.** Condition: owners willing to invest time and resources. Source says his ecommerce clients whose results "absolutely fly" typically have a celebrity or a heavily involved, well-followed person in the company. Personal brand is "trust at scale" and matters most when a buyer compares you with a competitor. A founder's own following is "more powerful" than hired influencers, though he also uses influencers. Minimum version: tell why you made the product. Evidence: anecdote (2x vs 6x ROAS). [3fe898d29aec]

## 3. Procedures

The source gives order and logic, not screen-level steps. Where it does not give the how, this says so.

### 3.1 Campaign sequencing for a new ecommerce account [575bdc0721ef]
1. Launch Search campaigns (text ads) and Shopping campaigns (product carousel). `[verify UI]` Setup steps are not given in the source.
2. Run them to learn the platform and build purchase conversions in the account.
3. Count purchases recorded in the ad account. `[needs brand data: purchase conversions in Google Ads account]`
4. At more than about 100 purchases (more is better), add a Performance Max campaign as an addition. Keep Search and Shopping running. `[verify UI]`
5. Source does not say how to split budget between Performance Max and the existing campaigns, or how to stop them competing. Gap.

### 3.2 Hero-product focus for cold traffic [f08cac856543]
1. Pull cost per purchase and ROAS by product for the account. `[needs brand data: product-level Google Ads cost, purchases, conversion value]`
2. Find the 1 product, or handful, with much lower cost per purchase and much higher ROAS than the rest.
3. Move cold-traffic budget to those products and away from the long tail.
4. In hobby niches, check whether the beginner or entry version is the winner. Source says it "typically" is.
5. Plan stock and supply for higher volume on the hero product. Source warns this may mean re-engineering the supply chain. `[needs brand data: inventory and supplier lead times]`
6. Source does not say how to structure campaigns, product groups or listing groups to isolate the hero. `[verify UI]` Gap.

### 3.3 Warm-audience catalog remarketing [422b680d3617]
1. Build remarketing that uses a product catalog, so people see the product they viewed, added to cart, or something similar to what they bought. `[verify UI]` The source names no campaign type, feed setting or audience list.
2. Let Google choose which product to show each person based on their past behavior.
3. Sell the wider range to warm audiences through Google remarketing, email and SMS.

### 3.4 Setting the maximum cost per purchase [efe781ef33f1] [ef1555c15a96]
1. Get AOV. `[needs brand data: AOV from store orders, not ad platform]`
2. Get gross margin, overall and per product. `[needs brand data: product-level gross margin]`
3. Compute gross profit per first order = AOV x gross margin. Example from source: $120 x 10% = $12.
4. Get LTV (average total spend per customer over their lifetime). `[needs brand data: cohort repeat purchase and LTV]` Source gives no method to calculate LTV.
5. Set the acceptable cost per purchase. Source's example: $100 AOV supports a $30 cost per purchase; LTV rises to $400 (4x), so the acceptable cost per purchase rises 4x to $120. Note: the source scales CPA in line with revenue value (AOV to LTV). It does not say whether margin on repeat orders was considered. Do this arithmetic in code with the brand's own margin.
6. If AOV is well below $100 (source example: $40 main product), raise it through bundles, buy-one-for-you and one-as-a-gift, complementary products, or spend thresholds.
7. Put more ad budget on products with better margins.

### 3.5 Front-end reciprocity offer (free plus shipping) [5632d9d930a2]
1. Pick an entry product that starts a need for your other products instead of ending it. Source example: a free football leads to boots, apparel and garden goals.
2. Offer it free; the customer pays shipping.
3. Check cash flow before launch. `[needs brand data: cash position, product cost, shipping cost, expected follow-on purchase rate]`
4. Follow up to sell the related products. Source implies the same remarketing, email and SMS ecosystem.
5. Source gives no numbers for how many free buyers convert to paid, or payback time. Gap.

### 3.6 Planning a discount or sale [38b728817a44]
1. Decide the brand's price position: cheapest or bulk, mid-market, or premium or luxury.
2. If cheapest or bulk: lean into discounts; it is the competitive advantage.
3. If premium or luxury: skip straight price cuts. Give a free extra alongside the main product (source analogy: a high-end restaurant gives free wine advice and valet parking, not menu discounts).
4. For everyone else, pick 1 or more tactical options:
   - Discount only high-margin products.
   - Give an inexpensive free item with a high-margin purchase.
   - Gate the discount behind a spend threshold (source example: "spend over X, get 20% off"), in the same way as a free-shipping threshold.
5. Check gross profit after the discount, not just unit volume. `[needs brand data: margin by product after discount]`

### 3.7 Review mining for ad copy and creative [082b2980e92f]
1. Collect the brand's reviews. `[needs brand data: review export]`
2. List themes that repeat in 5-star reviews.
3. Compare them with what the team thinks people buy for. Flag themes the team missed.
4. Use the repeated themes in ad copy and creative.
5. List themes that repeat in negative reviews (source example: packaging complaints). Send them to product or operations.
6. If the brand has no reviews: read competitor reviews for similar products. Copy the benefits buyers praise, if they are true for your product. Note what buyers complain about. If your product fixes it, put that up front in ads (source example: competitor has long install or delivery time; you guarantee 24 or 48 hours). `[web check: competitor reviews]`
7. Only use claims that are true for your product.

### 3.8 Scaling decision [af051bd7984a]
1. Check whether the campaign hits the target ROAS at the current budget. `[needs brand data: target ROAS from unit economics in 3.4]`
2. If not: do not raise budget. Fix the offer, ad copy, creative and other campaign parts first.
3. If yes and "nicely profitable": scale, expecting ROAS to drop.
4. Judge the result on total profit and customer count, not on ROAS alone. Source example: 4x at $100,000 per month beats 6x at $10,000 per month.
5. If LTV has risen and been measured, you may scale at a lower ROAS (source example: 1.5, or down to 1.2 after doubling LTV).
6. Source says scaling done wrong "will wreck your results" and points to a separate video for the method. The method is not in this source. Gap.

### 3.9 Founder brand or origin story [3fe898d29aec]
1. Decide if a founder or owner can build a public following. Weigh the time and cost and the harder exit.
2. If yes: build the personal brand and use it in ads.
3. If no: at minimum write the origin story: why the product was made. Use it in ad copy and creative. Source example: a parent whose child had eczema made a special moisturizer and started a business. `[needs brand data: founder story, verified]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

1. Starting with Performance Max, Demand Gen or Display before understanding the platform or having conversion data. [575bdc0721ef]
2. Spreading budget thinly across many SKUs on cold traffic. [f08cac856543]
3. Applying the cold-traffic focus rule to remarketing, and so showing warm visitors the wrong product. [422b680d3617]
4. Running ads without knowing AOV, margin and LTV. Ignoring margin even when AOV looks healthy. [efe781ef33f1]
5. Fighting for a small CPA cut instead of raising LTV. [ef1555c15a96]
6. Running a free or generous front-end offer without watching cash flow, or with a product that does not lead to more purchases. [5632d9d930a2]
7. Discounting too heavily or without a plan, which starts a race to the bottom with no gross profit. Using straight discounts as a premium or luxury brand. [38b728817a44]
8. Running multi-channel ads without accurate tracking ("flying blind"). [d7752a494148]
9. Writing ads from the owner's assumptions instead of what reviews say. [082b2980e92f]
10. Believing Google Ads needs a large budget to work. Raising the budget on an unprofitable campaign; source says this "nearly always" makes ROAS worse. [af051bd7984a]
11. Scaling the wrong way, which source says "will wreck your results". Method not given here. [af051bd7984a]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Single practitioner, sales context.** Every number is an illustration or anecdote from 1 agency owner who sells management services and has a Hyros affiliate link. Treat Hyros as his stated preference, not a tested pick. [d7752a494148]
- **100-purchase threshold is his rule of thumb**, not a Google figure. Common practice differs: some advertisers start with Performance Max or run it alongside Shopping from launch, and Google's own guidance changes. `[web check: current Google guidance on Performance Max conversion volume]` [575bdc0721ef]
- **CPA scaling in the LTV example uses revenue, not profit.** The $30 to $120 example multiplies allowable CPA by the LTV revenue ratio. It does not say how margin on repeat orders or the time to earn back the cost is handled. Use the brand's gross profit LTV and cash flow in code before applying. [ef1555c15a96]
- **Card wording error.** Card [af051bd7984a] says the acceptable ROAS floor shifts "upward" as LTV work matures. The source says the opposite: after LTV rises you can accept a lower ROAS (1.5 or 1.2). Follow the source.
- **Card wording error.** Card [3fe898d29aec] says a "friend" of the presenter bought the eczema product. In the source it was his wife. Minor.
- **Hero-product rule vs Shopping practice.** Many ecommerce Shopping and Performance Max setups feed the whole catalog. The source says focus cold spend but does not say how to do that in Shopping or Performance Max (product groups, listing groups, separate campaigns). Gap. `[verify UI]`
- **"Algorithm needs clean conversion data" reasoning.** Card [422b680d3617] says focus gives the algorithm clean signals. The source's stated reason for focus is ROAS and scalability, not algorithm data. Treat the algorithm reason as the card's addition.
- **Exit risk.** Source admits a personal brand makes the business harder to sell, "but not impossible if you reach big scale". [3fe898d29aec]

- **Header expected 15 to 20 cards; only 11 exist.** Promotional segments were rightly skipped.
- **Missing how-to.** No keyword strategy, match types, bid strategies, ROAS targets in the platform, feed setup, negative keywords, budget split, conversion setup, or test design. No method to calculate LTV. No payback period for free-plus-shipping. No scaling method (deferred to another video).
- **Not Google-specific.** Principles 4 to 8, 10 and 13 are general ecommerce offer and positioning advice. They apply to any paid channel. Pass 2 may want to cross-link them to the offer, pricing and brand folders.
- **Measurement link.** The source does not say what to reconcile against. A store backend sales record is the obvious cross-check. `[needs brand data: store sales vs Google Ads reported conversions]`
