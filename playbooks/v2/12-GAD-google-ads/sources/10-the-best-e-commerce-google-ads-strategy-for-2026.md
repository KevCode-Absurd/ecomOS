## 1. What this source is for

It helps an operator of a Google Ads account that already runs Performance Max (PMax) decide whether and how to rebuild PMax's parts as separate campaigns: Standard Shopping, Search split by brand and non-brand, and Demand Gen for image and video retargeting. It then shows how to keep PMax on as a low-priority catch-all by giving it a much higher ROAS target. It is for advanced advertisers with time to manage more structure. It is strong on structure logic and weak on exact settings: it gives almost no bid strategy names, budget numbers, audience windows or feed rules.

## 2. Principles

1. **PMax is a fine start for beginners and time-poor operators. It is not the ceiling for advanced advertisers.** Condition: the operator has the time and skill to run several campaign types. Source calls PMax "a jack of all trades" that "does everything okay, but nothing exceptionally well" and says his 3-part build "can outperform Pmax by a significant degree when you run it right". Evidence: agency experience only. [10c2368513b5] [be7ffdbb4c17]
2. **Know where PMax spends before you replace it.** Condition: any account running PMax. Source's typical split: about 60% to 70% Standard Shopping, about 20% to 30% Search text ads, about 5% to 10% images and videos. He says the image and video share is "likely to be 100% retargeting", not cold traffic. Evidence: his observation across accounts, not a guaranteed split. [be7ffdbb4c17]
3. **High PMax ROAS can hide low growth.** Condition: PMax, or any blended campaign, that captures retargeting and branded traffic. Source says PMax can over-rely on retargeting and brand searches, which makes ROAS "look fantastic on paper" while those sales are "often less incremental" than new customers. So "the ROAS looks great, but your actual business growth is then limited." Evidence: opinion based on agency experience. [be7ffdbb4c17] [72c8c5c5e72f]
4. **Rebuild each PMax component yourself and optimize it better.** Condition: advanced advertiser, PMax already running. Build 3 dedicated campaign types: Standard Shopping, Search text ads, Demand Gen (image and video retargeting). Each gets its own custom structure. Keep PMax on, but not as the main traffic driver. [10c2368513b5]
5. **Shopping is the core placement for ecommerce on Google. Give it the largest budget share.** Condition: ecommerce accounts. Source calls Shopping "the best placement for e-commerce" and "your bread and butter" and says to give it "the lion's share of your overall Google Ads budget". No percentage given. Evidence: opinion. [cc19e5a055f2]
6. **Pick the Shopping structure by margin spread.** Condition: if product-level profit margins are all "within 10% of each other", segment by past ROAS and spend (4 tiers). If margins vary a lot (example: 30% on some products, 70% on others), segment by gross margin tier (3 to 5 tiers). [a06b74f587c9] [3fec1d2be46d]
7. **High-margin products get a lower ROAS target. Low-margin products get a higher one.** Condition: margin-tier Shopping structure. A lower target lets you bid harder and get more volume, and you stay profitable because the margin is high. A higher target on low-margin items keeps you conservative where there is "less room for error". Source says PMax "doesn't know your margins" and "treats every product the same". [3fec1d2be46d]
8. **Split brand and non-brand Search into separate campaigns. This is "the first and most important thing" in Search.** Condition: any Search build. Brand searchers already know you; "some other marketing channel was likely the first touch point", so a brand sale is "less likely to be a truly incremental sale". Source says PMax does not separate them by default and "tends to overrely on branded traffic". [51226e3b8539]
9. **Brand gets a much higher ROAS target than non-brand.** Condition: brand and non-brand in separate campaigns. Brand converts more easily and is less incremental. Non-brand sales are more incremental, "a higher proportion of them will be sales that you would not have gotten anyway", so it gets a lower target. Source still sees "value to be had in bidding on your brand", but "ROAS expectations need to be a lot higher". No target numbers given. [51226e3b8539] [72c8c5c5e72f]
10. **Non-brand growth is where real business growth comes from.** Condition: separated brand and non-brand reporting. If you are "overindexed on brand", shift budget to non-brand, and build a plan to grow non-brand keyword presence. [51226e3b8539] [72c8c5c5e72f]
11. **Fewer RSA assets give cleaner tests.** Condition: responsive search ads where you want to learn which copy works. Source uses 8 headlines and 2 descriptions, not the maximum 15 and 4, so fewer combinations exist and each gets enough impressions to test. Add multiple ads per ad group to rotate and test. Evidence: presenter's preference. [966d6939f047]
12. **Relevance drives quality score, and quality score drives cost.** Condition: manual Search. Quality score "directly impacts your cost per click and your ad position" and "improving it can dramatically reduce your costs". Single-themed ad groups with theme-matched copy raise relevance. No numbers given. [11dd9a205267] [4a2c02a70a66]
13. **Put images and videos in the same Demand Gen retargeting campaign.** Condition: Demand Gen retargeting. Source says 1 campaign gives "consolidated data" and lets the algorithm "track an individual user and seamlessly switch" between image and video ads. [cd21a20937c4]
14. **Demand Gen beats PMax at retargeting because of reporting, not reach.** Condition: segmented audiences are set up. You can read ROAS, spend and clicks by audience, and results by network or placement, which PMax hides. "Better data means better optimization of your creative and targeting." [146aa74746f4] [ffb75a5b8076]
15. **Keep PMax as a safety net with a much higher ROAS target.** Condition: after the 3 manual campaign types are live. Lower ROAS targets on manual campaigns mean higher CPC bids, so they win more auctions; PMax at a much higher target picks up "stragglers" (queries Search did not cover, audiences Demand Gen missed). Do not eliminate PMax. Evidence: source's own "hack", not a documented platform rule. [56795ebc9766]

## 3. Procedures

The source gives order and logic. It gives few screen-level steps. Gaps are marked.

### 3.1 Decide whether to rebuild PMax [10c2368513b5] [be7ffdbb4c17]
1. Decide who runs the account. If the operator is a beginner or short on time, stay on PMax. Source says it is "a solid starting point and is an efficient use of your limited time".
2. If the goal is to scale past PMax and someone has time for keyword research, feed work, copy tests, audience setup and bid decisions, plan the 3-part build.
3. Pull PMax spend by channel: Shopping, Search text, image and video. `[needs brand data: PMax spend and conversion value by channel or asset type]` `[verify UI]` The source does not say where in Google Ads to find this split.
4. Compare with the source's typical split (60% to 70% Shopping, 20% to 30% Search, 5% to 10% image and video).
5. Start the rebuild with Standard Shopping, since that is where PMax usually spends most. Then Search, then Demand Gen. This is the source's order of presentation; he says Shopping comes first "because this is typically where Pmax spends most of your budget anyway".
6. Keep PMax on throughout (see 3.7).

### 3.2 Standard Shopping, structure A: ROAS performance tiers [a06b74f587c9]
1. Check the margin spread. Use this structure only if product-level profit margins are within 10% of each other. `[needs brand data: product-level gross margin]`
2. Pull historical ROAS and spend per product. `[needs brand data: product-level Google Ads spend, conversion value, ROAS]` Source gives no lookback window and no cutoffs for "high" or "low" ROAS or "low spend".
3. Put each product in 1 of 4 tiers:
   - Tier 1, high ROAS: best performers. Give them more budget to scale.
   - Tier 2, low ROAS: getting spend but converting poorly. Reduce bids.
   - Tier 3, low spend: not enough data to judge. Give them more opportunity.
   - Tier 4, zombies: zero spend, never tested. Isolate in their own campaign with a dedicated budget.
4. Build campaigns so each tier has its own budget and bids. Source states the dedicated campaign for Tier 4 explicitly; separate campaigns for every tier is the card's reading. `[verify UI]` The source does not say whether to split by campaign, by product group, or by custom label.
5. Revisit tier assignment as new data arrives (card guidance; source does not give a cadence). [a06b74f587c9]

### 3.3 Standard Shopping, structure B: gross profit margin tiers [3fec1d2be46d]
1. Use this when margins vary a lot across products. Source example range: 30% to 70%. `[needs brand data: product-level gross margin]`
2. Choose 3 to 5 margin tiers, depending on how many products and conversions you have. Simplest version: high, mid, low margin.
3. Build 1 campaign per margin tier. `[verify UI]`
4. Set a ROAS target per tier: lowest target on high margin, highest target on low margin. Source gives no target numbers and no formula to turn margin into a target. `[needs brand data: margin per tier and break-even economics]` Do the arithmetic in code.
5. Review tiers when costs or prices change (card guidance, not in source). [3fec1d2be46d]

### 3.4 Shopping optimizations beyond structure [cc19e5a055f2]
1. Rewrite product titles to be keyword-rich. Put the most important search terms people use to find the product in the title, to show on more searches and get more clicks. `[needs brand data: search terms report and product feed]` Source gives no title formula or order.
2. Upgrade product images. High-quality images "really stand out in the shopping ads carousel" and "directly impact your clickthrough rate". Source gives no image specs.
3. Set Shopping's budget as the largest share of the Google Ads budget. No share given.

### 3.5 Search campaigns [51226e3b8539] [4a2c02a70a66] [966d6939f047] [11dd9a205267]
1. Create 2 Search campaigns: brand (searches for your company name) and non-brand. `[verify UI]`
2. Set a much higher ROAS target on brand than on non-brand. Source gives no numbers. `[needs brand data: current brand vs non-brand ROAS and margin]`
3. Research non-brand keywords with Keyword Planner. `[verify UI]`
4. Open the PMax search terms report and pull the best-performing terms (the queries already driving sales in PMax). `[verify UI]` `[web check: current depth of PMax search terms reporting]`
5. Add those terms to the dedicated Search campaigns alongside the Keyword Planner keywords.
6. Group keywords into single-themed ad groups (STAGs): 1 theme per ad group.
7. Write ad copy for each ad group's theme.
8. In each RSA, use about 8 headlines and 2 descriptions rather than the maximum 15 and 4. `[verify UI]` `[web check: current RSA asset limits]`
9. Create multiple ads per ad group for rotation and testing.
10. Read which combinations get better CTR and conversions, and use the learnings to improve quality score. Source gives no test length, impression floor or winner rule.
11. Check quality score for each keyword. `[verify UI]`
12. Read brand ROAS and non-brand ROAS as separate numbers. If brand takes too much of the budget, move budget to non-brand.
13. Build a plan to grow non-brand keyword presence. Source gives no steps for this.

The source mentions split-testing ad copy and running campaign experiments as things manual Search allows and PMax does not. It gives no experiment setup. [11dd9a205267]

### 3.6 Demand Gen retargeting [cd21a20937c4] [146aa74746f4] [ffb75a5b8076]
1. Create a Demand Gen retargeting campaign. `[verify UI]`
2. Take the videos and images that performed in PMax and put them in this campaign. `[needs brand data: PMax asset performance]`
3. Put images and videos in the same campaign.
4. Build these retargeting audiences as separate segments `[verify UI]`:
   - product page viewers
   - add-to-cart users who did not purchase
   - abandoned cart users who started checkout but did not finish
   - category page viewers
   - all site visitors
   - past purchasers, for repeat purchases and upsells
   Source gives no lookback windows, no exclusions between segments and no audience size minimums.
5. Read ROAS, spend and clicks by audience. Keep what works, cut what does not.
6. Click "segment by network" to see results at ad or ad group level by placement: YouTube in-stream, YouTube Shorts, Google Discover, Gmail, Display network. `[verify UI]`
7. Act on placement data. Source's illustration: if Shorts is "crushing it" and Display underperforms, make more portrait videos for Shorts and "potentially turn off display network entirely". `[verify UI]` `[web check: whether Demand Gen currently allows turning off individual placements]`
8. Test creative variations. Double down on winners, cut losers. No test rules given.

### 3.7 Keep PMax as a de-prioritized safety net [56795ebc9766]
1. Only after Shopping, Search and Demand Gen are running.
2. Keep PMax on. Do not pause or delete it.
3. Set PMax's ROAS target well above the manual campaigns. Source example: manual campaigns at about 300% to 400% average ROAS target, PMax at 600% or even 700%. `[verify UI]`
4. Expected result, per source: manual campaigns bid higher, win most auctions and get "the lion's share of impressions and clicks"; PMax catches missed queries and audiences.
5. Watch whether PMax still takes a large share of spend and widen the target gap if so. This check is the card's addition; the source gives no check. `[needs brand data: spend share by campaign type before and after]` `[web check: current PMax vs Standard Shopping and Search prioritization rules]`

## 8. Mistakes the source warns about

- Relying on PMax alone when you want to scale; it does everything "okay" and nothing "exceptionally well". [10c2368513b5]
- Reading high PMax ROAS as growth when it comes from retargeting and brand traffic that was already in the funnel. [be7ffdbb4c17] [72c8c5c5e72f]
- Mixing brand and non-brand in 1 campaign or target; brand ROAS "looks amazing, but it's deceptive". [51226e3b8539]
- Treating every product the same regardless of margin, as PMax does. [3fec1d2be46d]
- Letting the algorithm "throw everything together" instead of allocating budget by performance tier. [a06b74f587c9]
- Filling RSAs to the maximum 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, so no combination gets enough impressions to test. [966d6939f047]
- Removing PMax entirely. Keep it as a catch-all. [56795ebc9766]
- Leaving a weak placement (example: Display) running when data shows another (example: Shorts) is winning. [ffb75a5b8076]

## 9. Caveats, conflicts and what is missing

- **Commercial interest.** The presenter sells the managed service that builds this structure. The claim that the manual build beats PMax "by a significant degree" rests on agency experience and 2 case claims, not a controlled test. [10c2368513b5]
- **Platform mechanics may have changed.** The safety-net hack assumes a lower ROAS target makes a campaign bid higher and win the auction over PMax. The source does not address Google's own rules for which campaign serves when PMax and Standard Shopping or Search can both enter the same auction, and these rules have changed over time. `[web check: current Google rules on PMax vs Standard Shopping and Search priority]` [56795ebc9766]
- **Brand exclusions not mentioned.** The source says PMax over-relies on brand traffic but does not mention excluding brand terms from PMax. `[web check: PMax brand exclusion options]`
- **Retargeting and incrementality.** The source says retargeting is less incremental, then builds a dedicated Demand Gen retargeting campaign and gives it no ROAS target guidance. The card [72c8c5c5e72f] groups branded and retargeting traffic together as high intent; the source only gives a target rule for brand vs non-brand. No cold prospecting plan for Demand Gen is given.
- **No numbers where they matter most.** No brand or non-brand ROAS targets, no margin-to-target formula, no Shopping budget share, no ROAS cutoffs for tiers, no lookback windows, no audience windows, no test length or significance rule.
- **"Within 10%" is ambiguous.** It is unclear if this means 10 percentage points or 10% relative. Treat as the presenter's heuristic. [a06b74f587c9]
- **Demand Gen placement control.** Turning off Display "entirely" may not be possible in every setup. `[web check: Demand Gen placement controls]` [ffb75a5b8076]
- **RSA advice goes against Google's default guidance.** Google's interface usually pushes more headlines and descriptions. The source chooses fewer on purpose for testable data. This is his preference. `[web check: current Google RSA ad strength guidance]` [966d6939f047]
- **Conflict with source 08 (Google Ads For Ecommerce: 9 Keys To Success).** Source 08 says new accounts should start with Search and Shopping and add PMax after about 100 purchases. Keep both views; the agent should choose by account maturity and the operator's time.
- **Card additions.** Some card steps are not in the source text: reviewing margin tiers when costs change [3fec1d2be46d], landing page mismatch and pausing weak keywords [11dd9a205267], monitoring and adjusting the PMax target gap [56795ebc9766], audience size thresholds [146aa74746f4]. - **Library note.** The source text begins with an 11-ADC folder scope note and a library header; these are not part of the presenter's content.
- **Measurement.** The source does not discuss conversion tracking, attribution windows or how to measure incrementality. Incrementality is not shown in default ROAS reporting. [72c8c5c5e72f]
