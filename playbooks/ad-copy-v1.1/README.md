## Editorial principles

### Message
Write a short internal message brief: customer situation, one main promise, available support, tone and next action. Keep this planning out of customer copy.

**Conditions:** Preserve clarity and point-of-need relevance; do not adopt the source list’s claim that every visitor is persuadable.

Source IDs: `20fa6900506ffc06314306b2`

### Lead
Choose a benefit-led or feature-led opening according to the customer’s knowledge and buying question. Connect features to outcomes and outcomes to factual support.

**Conditions:** Resolve benefit-first versus feature-first advice conditionally. Feature-first guidance is adapted from page headlines; it is a static-ad hypothesis, not a proven lift.

Source IDs: `40638b840ce1e28ea89b4b19`, `d5db0dddac4ab42c3d5640c1`

### Specificity
Replace vague adjectives with a supported action, object or product detail the shopper can picture. Never invent a number, time promise or comparison to make copy sound concrete.

**Conditions:** Research supports specificity. Retain plain product identification when a vivid line would obscure the category.

Source IDs: `8e6c32baa35ff2f2aa1ef544`

### Readability
Prefer clear language and readable sentence structure. Remove repetition and empty claims; keep the qualifications needed to avoid changing the promise.

**Conditions:** No compulsory grade-level score or universal word count. Clarity is not a claim of measured conversion lift.

Source IDs: `17183d94160ce2f95770a9d7`

### Voice
Use approved brand examples to choose vocabulary, tone and the size of the promise. With website-only input, infer a provisional voice and keep it separate from approved owner preferences.

**Conditions:** Adapt the source’s email critique to ads. Do not turn another brand’s phrasing or promises into this brand’s facts.

Source IDs: `3f95809c1b82aca30df46509`

### Awareness
Make the visual, opening and destination coherent with what the intended shopper plausibly knows. If awareness is unknown, label it as a planning hypothesis.

**Conditions:** Do not equate awareness with targeting buckets or treat a product photograph as proof of audience knowledge. Reject categorical claims that a mismatch can never convert.

Source IDs: `28025475600243ca3a03ad4c`, `5ca4fa6b39ad6ab0102ceae0`

### Proof
For a considered purchase, select relevant available evidence that actually supports the promise. If testimonials are unavailable, use supported product details or demonstrations without disguising them as independent endorsements.

**Conditions:** Brand assertions are not independent verification. The prohibition on fabricated evidence is an operating rule; no universal emotion-versus-reason formula is inferred.

Source IDs: `a9db8de6d903f43da80e956e`

### Objection
Use real reviews, support or interview evidence to select an objection. When only a website is available, treat the objection as a hypothesis and avoid invented customer quotations.

**Conditions:** Transfer only the research method from the VSL card. Do not import a seven-part video script or medical example into a static ad.

Source IDs: `4fb56bc1019fff1c2b939750`

## Write ecommerce static-ad copy
1. Build a fact sheet separating approved facts, website assertions, customer evidence, hypotheses and unknowns. Lock the offer and other user constraints.
2. Choose a customer situation and awareness hypothesis; ask only for missing facts that materially block the requested claim or deliverable.
3. Set one promise and its support. Choose a feature or benefit lead based on the actual buying question.
4. Match copy to the supplied image/concept and destination. Use approved voice examples; make any website-only voice inference explicit internally.
5. Produce the requested number of finished primary texts, headlines and CTAs. Make variants meaningfully distinct without changing fixed facts.
6. Check claim support, offer fidelity, clarity, essential qualifications and visual coherence. Revise at most twice; separate unresolved notes from copy.

## Revise copy using owner feedback
1. Build a fact sheet separating approved facts, website assertions, customer evidence, hypotheses and unknowns. Lock the offer and other user constraints.
2. Classify owner feedback as fact, brand voice, concept, structure or applicability. Preserve approved copy that the feedback does not require changing.
3. Set one promise and its support. Choose a feature or benefit lead based on the actual buying question.
4. Match copy to the supplied image/concept and destination. Use approved voice examples; make any website-only voice inference explicit internally.
5. Return revised finished copy and a short change note. Do not add new variants unless requested.
6. Check claim support, offer fidelity, clarity, essential qualifications and visual coherence. Revise at most twice; separate unresolved notes from copy.

## Write copy addressing a buying objection
1. Build a fact sheet separating approved facts, website assertions, customer evidence, hypotheses and unknowns. Lock the offer and other user constraints.
2. Select one evidenced objection, or label a provisional objection as a hypothesis. Map it to a supported answer; do not invent reassurance the product cannot provide.
3. Set one promise and its support. Choose a feature or benefit lead based on the actual buying question.
4. Match copy to the supplied image/concept and destination. Use approved voice examples; make any website-only voice inference explicit internally.
5. Produce the requested number of finished primary texts, headlines and CTAs. Make variants meaningfully distinct without changing fixed facts.
6. Check claim support, offer fidelity, clarity, essential qualifications and visual coherence. Revise at most twice; separate unresolved notes from copy.

## Resolved conflicts and removals
- Removed the sales-page template and long-copy formatting material from static-ad context.
- Benefit-first advice and feature-first advice are alternatives selected by the buying question, not simultaneous rules.
- Replaced blanket awareness-to-targeting claims with a coherence check and explicit uncertainty.
- Kept the VSL customer-research method; excluded video structure and medical examples.
- Did not adopt blanket benefit bombardment, third-grade readership claims, or compulsory testimonial use.
- Original card excerpts remain unchanged in the audit file, including claims this playbook deliberately does not adopt.

## Review boundary
This is a scoped synthesis of ten cards, not a claim to have reconciled all 756 ad-copy cards. Human outcome testing remains necessary. Feedback must be routed to brand facts, brand voice, applicability or shared workflow before a version is changed.
