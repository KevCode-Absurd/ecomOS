#### Align Ad Concept to the Five Stages of Awareness
Card: `1a178c0c2400662c164760b6` · Source: How to Make Static Image Ads That Scale in 2026.docx · Section: 0

Use Eugene Schwartz's five stages of awareness (unaware, problem aware, solution aware, product aware, most aware) to match static ad concepts to the correct audience temperature.

Applies when: Mapping your store's static ad concepts to cold, warm, or retargeting audiences and diagnosing why a static ad underperforms.

- Classify each ad concept by the awareness stage it assumes: does it presume the viewer knows the problem, the solution category, your product, or just your brand?
- Check the audience you're targeting against that assumption — a plain product shot presumes product/most-aware audiences.
- For cold, unaware traffic, use curiosity-driven native statics that name a problem or provoke interest, not product showcases.
- Build a funnel map showing at least one concept per awareness stage so the account covers the full path, not just bottom-funnel retargeting.
- Diagnose underperformance by asking whether the ad was served to an audience below its assumed awareness stage.
- **Condition:** The source identifies a mismatch — deploying most-aware/product-aware assets to cold unaware audiences — as the primary reason static ads fail to scale; treat this as its stated diagnosis, not a controlled finding.
- **Condition:** Stage labels are qualitative heuristics, not measurable audience segments in the ad platform.

#### Match Static Ad Format to Stage of Awareness
Card: `28025475600243ca3a03ad4c` · Source: How To Make Better Meta Ads_ 8 Static Creatives Analysed.docx · Section: 0

Most brands fail top-of-funnel by running product-aware or most-aware statics to cold, unaware audiences — instead lead cold traffic with education or problem agitation.

Applies when: Choosing which static ad concepts to produce for a cold prospecting campaign versus a retargeting campaign.

- Classify each concept by Eugene Schwartz awareness stage before producing it: most-aware/product-aware statics (plain product shots, 'guaranteed energy in 5 minutes') belong in retargeting, while unaware/problem-aware audiences need statics that lead with education or a named problem.
- For cold audiences, open with a problem the viewer recognises rather than the product: the GLP-1 hair loss static leads with 'GLP1 hair loss hits hard' and only then introduces the supplement.
- Use advertorial-style statics (native statics that look like content) to educate before the hard sell, since they let you agitate the problem without immediately demanding a purchase.
- Check the mismatch symptom: a clean product shot with a benefit headline running against cold traffic is the format-audience mismatch the source names as the primary reason top-of-funnel statics fail.
- Route each stage to the right destination — education/curiosity concepts need an educational landing page, while product-aware concepts can go to a standard PDP.
- **Condition:** Awareness stages are used as a strategist's organising framework here; the source offers no measurement data linking stage to ROAS, so treat the alignment as a planning discipline rather than a proven lift.

#### Five Questions Before Choosing an Ad Format
Card: `bc432cb2ccf73b3ac6040022` · Source: The Creative Strategy Behind $100M_yr of Meta Spend.docx · Section: 2

Decide format last, after concept, education needs, single-frame communicability, demo needs and awareness stage.

Applies when: When selecting whether a concept becomes a static, carousel, UGC video or long-form asset.

- Define the concept first (persona, angle, offer) and only then pick the format; the source states format is the last step, never the starting point.
- Ask whether the concept requires education: if yes, lean to a longer-form asset that can teach.
- Ask whether the value proposition can be communicated in a single frame: if yes, prefer statics, carousels or other cheap-to-produce assets.
- Ask whether the product needs a demo: if yes, that pushes toward video unless you have a strong designer.
- Match format to awareness stage: higher awareness often needs a longer teaching format; lower awareness can use DPAs and statics with bottom-of-funnel ads.
- Rebuttal the belief that statics are only bottom-of-funnel; the source states top-of-funnel statics are possible and that the belief is a skill issue.
- **Condition:** The source's claims about statics doing top-of-funnel are its own position, not a platform guarantee.

## Create variations of a brand winner
Workflow ID: `paid-social-creative.iterate-winner`

**Deliverable:** Variation matrix with parent creative links

### Proposed decision sequence
1. Identify what is observed about performance and what is only a hypothesis
2. Preserve the likely message backbone while changing a defined element
3. Specify comparable tests and avoid claiming the variation will win

#### Founder face is a sameness signal: diversify concept and format
Card: `73f8f845b93b16f5f7a931db` · Source: How To Create Founder Ads That Scale (Masterclass).docx · Section: 1

Repeated talking-head founder ads fatigue because the founder's face creates sameness; fix with true concept diversification plus format and scene variation.

Applies when: A founder ad has started to fatigue and you are deciding what to shoot next.

- Diagnose whether the problem is format fatigue or concept fatigue: the same face, scene and talking-head delivery serving the same audience decays quickly.
- Attack format first because it is easy: reshoot the identical script in a new location. The source reports taking a fatigued in-gym ad and running another $40,000 of spend through it by only changing the scene.
- Stretch into new formats as volume grows: tier-list videos, founder walking a store aisle interacting with competitor products, or other organic-style formats instead of another talking head.
- Attack concept next by changing one of three levers: the persona being addressed, the angle/unique mechanism, or the offer.
- Note the source's difficulty ordering: offering a new or different product is easiest, changing the angle is hard because there may be only one true mechanism, and changing persona is hardest for founder ads because the founder must credibly represent that persona.
- **Condition:** The $40,000 incremental spend figure is one source example, not a repeatable benchmark.
- **Condition:** Reusing the same script is acceptable when editing or scenes vary substantially; the source frames this as true under current Meta conditions.

#### Don't Abandon Hook Testing Because of Andromeda
Card: `2d5a90d21b0dfb6e27fbf792` · Source: Why Hook Testing Still Works With Andromeda.docx · Section: 0

Andromeda may group near-identical ads but rotating substantially different hooks still unlocks net-new reach for fatigued winners, so hook testing remains high-value.

Applies when: Deciding whether to keep testing hooks on a fatigued winning ad or to kill it and start entirely new creative concepts.

- Recognize the widespread claim that Andromeda groups minor variations into single Entity IDs and therefore punishes hook testing; the source argues this leads advertisers to a mistake.
- Understand that Andromeda groups visually and contextually identical ads, not every variation of a hook; the source maintains hook testing still works on live accounts.
- Agree that diverse creative has always been valuable, but don't let that reasoning cancel the hook's outsized importance in paid social video.
- Because the hook sells the ad, the source places roughly 90% of a creative's impact in the first 3-5 seconds, citing Ogilvy's headline logic.
- Continue running hook variations on proven bodies as your default low-effort, high-ROI play rather than only producing entirely new assets.
- Verify current Meta/Andromeda behavior on your own account rather than assuming the algorithm has made hook testing obsolete, because tool behavior can change.
- **Condition:** This is the source's opinion and account experience, not a documented platform rule; confirm with your own tests.
- **Condition:** Andromeda grouping behavior may change over time, so re-validate the assumption on live campaigns.

#### Hook Testing Still Works Post-Andromeda
Card: `2f44cbccd498fdfe8d9f9a82` · Source: The Creative Strategy Behind $100M_yr of Meta Spend.docx · Section: 2

Test new openings as low-cost refinements of existing product-ad concepts and measure their purchase impact.

Applies when: When deciding whether to invest in hook variation under Meta's Andromeda-era diversity expectations.

- Create hook variants while keeping the body stable. A changed opening does not by itself prove that delivery will reach a new audience.
- Accept overlap risk: because most of the body is identical, the concept is the same, so treat hook swaps as refinements of one concept, not new concepts.
- Do it because the effort is nearly free: recording hooks six times takes minutes.
- Revive fatigued winners: the source reports ads that spent $50,000-$100,000 then fatigued, and rotating 50 new hooks yielded another $100,000 of spend at the same efficiency from roughly one hour of shooting.
- Revive losers with a bad hook rate by fixing only the hook, keeping the rest of the asset.
- Treat hooks as the highest-leverage point across video, organic and YouTube, per the source.
- **Condition:** The $50k-$100k figures are the source's own case examples, not guaranteed results.
- **Condition:** Andromeda behavior may change; verify current platform behavior.

#### Test a broader creative portfolio when one format dominates spend
Card: `80dbff5fec17a080e104e74d` · Source: The Best Facebook Ad Creative Strategy for 2027.docx · Section: 0

The source recommends distinct formats and angles to reduce dependence on a few winners; validate with store economics.

Applies when: Deciding whether to iterate on a single winning ad or invest in new formats, or diagnosing sudden ROAS decay after scaling.

- Recognize the old pattern (source labels it '2026 strategy'): find one winning ad, then produce minor variants (new background, same format) and scale. Result per the source: nearly all spend concentrates in 1-2 creatives and, when that format fatigues, the account crashes because nothing else can absorb the budget.
- Adopt the newer approach: maintain 10-15 distinct formats and angles running simultaneously — e.g., three-reasons-why, before/after, try-on videos, comparison ads, ingredient breakdowns, educational breakdowns.
- Inspect spend concentration as a portfolio diagnostic, then judge purchases and contribution margin; evenly distributed spend alone is not success.
- Do not immediately kill a low-ROAS creative that occupies a valid funnel stage — per the source, it still has a role in moving people down the funnel.
- Recheck after scale increases. In the 2026 pattern the source describes ROAS dropping as spend rose and only holding temporarily until fatigue hit.
- **Condition:** This is one advertiser's strategic opinion (backed by claimed 2x-10x cases, not benchmarks) — test against your own account dynamics.
- **Condition:** The 10-15 format count and the 'spend spread' success signal are source-specific heuristics, not calculation-based thresholds.
- **Condition:** Maintaining 10-15 distinct formats is production-intensive; do not attempt without the 33% sourcing pipeline in place.
- **Condition:** Year labels are the source framing, not a verified platform transition or a requirement to stop hook iteration.

#### STORMING: Diversify Idea Sources So You Never Run Out
Card: `36945e14679a7c05c0b0ffb3` · Source: creative strategy is easy actually.docx · Section: 0

Generate ad ideas from seven distinct sources — Swipes, Templates, Organic research, Matrix, Internal vectors, New styles, Gambit — to raise your hit rate.

Applies when: Filling a creative pipeline for frequent ad testing so ideas are not all rip-offs of the same competitor or your own brain.

- Swipes: pull winning ad ideas from competitor accounts.
- Templates: reuse proven formats and structures.
- Organic research: mine comments, Amazon reviews, Facebook comments, forums and new papers for language and angles.
- Matrix: cross segments with formats to reveal missing combinations.
- Internal vectors: identify patterns inside your own account data.
- New styles: adopt trending visual styles such as Pixar or claymation looks.
- Gambit: allow shower-thought wild cards — low hit rate but occasionally bangers when executed well.
- Track which source each test came from so you can see whether your diversification is real.
- **Condition:** Gambits are explicitly low-probability; don't let them crowd out higher-likelihood work.
- **Condition:** Sourcing organic content as idea input doesn't license copying it directly.
