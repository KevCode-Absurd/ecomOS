## Build an evidence-led customer avatar
Workflow ID: `avatars.build-evidence-avatar`

**Deliverable:** Avatar brief with evidence and unknowns

### Proposed decision sequence
1. Extract situations, desired progress and objections from supplied evidence
2. Separate observed segments from inferred personas
3. Select a useful situation and attach evidence to each important claim

### Supporting knowledge candidates
These cards were ranked locally for this task. They are evidence to assess, not proof that every proposed step is supported. Full steps and conditions are retained below.

#### Give every avatar quoted evidence
Card: `294308de8309bca847b78784` · Source: Competing Against Luck_ The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice (1).docx · Section: 0

A non-negotiable baseline for avatar records: direct quotes from research that support the job, the trigger and the objections.

Applies when: Writing or reviewing avatar records, especially when tempted to summarize without evidence.

- Attach at least one verbatim quote to each key claim: the job, the circumstance, the trigger and the opposing forces.
- Keep quotes specific and contextual; a quote about a feeling or a workaround is more useful than a quote about liking the brand.
- Note the source of each quote, such as an interview or an observation, so the record can be traced.
- Remove or flag claims with no supporting quote rather than smoothing them into confident prose.
- Use the quotes in downstream messaging as raw language the market actually uses.
- **Condition:** Quotes can be edited for clarity but not for meaning; preserving the customer's phrasing is much of the value.
- **Condition:** A single quote illustrates a job; it does not prove prevalence.

#### Set the Universal Avatar Baseline: Quotes, Trigger, Job
Card: `c5efc108c9c95161a0da15a4` · Source: How I find Avatars that Scale Facebook Creatives to 100k_Day.docx · Section: 0

A minimum evidence standard for every avatar record so personas don't drift back into demographic fields with no job.

Applies when: Creating or auditing avatar documents, and onboarding writers or media buyers to them.

- Require quoted evidence for each avatar: verbatim language drawn from reviews, forums, surveys, calls, or DMs.
- Require a trigger moment: the situation in which the person enters the market (the source's example is post-knee-surgery, when PT failed).
- Require a job statement: what progress the person is trying to make in their day-to-day life, including restored identity markers such as a sport or hobby.
- Reject persona templates that contain only demographics and no job, and reject broad segmentation without situational context or failed past attempts.
- Follow the source's stated baseline: quoted evidence, a trigger moment, and a job statement for every avatar — this part is universal, while the number of avatars is not.
- Keep the record usable by writers: store the phrasing, the restricted activities, and the specific terminology so copy can be built directly from it.
- **Condition:** The source presents this baseline as its own standard, not a measured benchmark; treat it as a discipline for research quality.
- **Condition:** Avatar-count guidance is explicitly not universal in the source — don't force one uniform number across markets.

#### Avatar Record Requirements: Quotes, Trigger, Job
Card: `ef0ecef3dfbbbc8a8ff41853` · Source: How I Find Avatars That Scale Facebook Ads to $10k_day.docx · Section: 0

The minimum contents any avatar must have in your library before it is usable for creative work.

Applies when: You are cataloguing research into avatar records or auditing whether an existing avatar is complete.

- Include quoted evidence from real sources so the avatar is traceable to what people actually wrote, not to your paraphrase.
- Record a trigger moment: the specific situation that starts the search or purchase, such as a friend's comment about hair loss in front of others, or a cat refusing water.
- Write a job statement capturing what progress the avatar wants, not just the demographic fields.
- Capture specific situational context rather than broad life stages, e.g. the source's example of a 55-year-old empty nester with her third cat rather than 'women 45 and up'.
- Capture procedural and category vocabulary exactly: 'Neograft rebuilding the front and crown' and 'minoxidil' rather than 'transplants and drugs', or 'fountains and water bowls' rather than 'hydration products'.
- Note vernacular differences between avatars, since the source's method hinges on exact phrasing rather than a generic persona template.
- **Condition:** The source warns against persona templates with demographic fields and no job, such as '30+ year old woman with knee pain'; those should not count as avatars.
- **Condition:** A quoted line from one forum user may not represent the whole demographic; consolidate multiple sources into the record.

## Mine customer language and objections
Workflow ID: `avatars.mine-objections`

**Deliverable:** Objection and customer-language map

### Proposed decision sequence
1. Group verbatim customer language by buying barrier
2. Distinguish frequency from severity and buying relevance
3. Translate supported themes into messaging inputs without inventing quotes

### Supporting knowledge candidates
These cards were ranked locally for this task. They are evidence to assess, not proof that every proposed step is supported. Full steps and conditions are retained below.

#### Mine Competitor 1-Star Reviews for Failure and Trust Markers
Card: `2fd2d4d2c90b6cf9830c7f4f` · Source: How I find Avatars that Scale Facebook Creatives to 100k_Day.docx · Section: 0

Extracting explicit mechanical flaws, trust breaks, and repeated claims from 1-star competitor reviews to feed ad hooks and objections.

Applies when: Sourcing granular complaints, objections, and physical product friction before writing creatives or landing pages.

- Search Amazon (and wherever else your category is reviewed) for 1-star reviews on competitor listings.
- Catalog explicit failure points and mechanical friction verbatim, e.g. 'I was in serious pain and couldn't get the brace off my knee fast enough' — the source treats this kind of complaint as ad-ready material.
- Log broken trust markers: things customers say they no longer believe, and repeated product claims that have become exhausted through overuse.
- Log recurring venting topics and objections; the source argues these are typically skipped and then reinvented from scratch by marketers.
- Look for language patterns that reveal an angle most competitors aren't targeting, e.g. reviews mentioning post-knee-surgery use as an underexploited hook.
- Feed the collected phrases into the pre-lander, landing page, and creative so the messaging mirrors what buyers already said rather than what you brainstormed.
- **Condition:** Reviews reveal prior-product failures and objections, not necessarily your own product's performance; don't present competitor flaws as proven claims about you.
- **Condition:** The source gives one illustration ('couldn't get the brace off fast enough'), not a guaranteed winning hook — treat it as evidence of a pattern to verify in your own category.

#### Mine 1- and 5-Star Reviews for the Failed-Solution Buyer
Card: `b08301158fb6bfe1530f48ed` · Source: EXACTLY how I do market research for new products.docx · Section: 0

Identify buyers who already spent money on solutions that did not work — the source's definition of a best prospect.

Applies when: You are hunting for high-intent, self-aware prospects and want pain-point language they will recognize instantly.

- Scan 1-star and 5-star reviews for variations of 'I spent thousands of dollars and it didn't work' — e.g. the source's example of a reviewer who hired many personal trainers across a variety of exercises and was never satisfied.
- Treat these buyers as your strongest prospects for two reasons the source gives: they already recognize the problem enough to have spent time and money, and they have not gotten results, so they are still hunting for a better solution.
- Do not waste messaging convincing these people the problem exists; lead instead with a better solution and the reasons previous attempts failed.
- Apply the same signal in customer interviews: when someone says 'I already tried X and it didn't do Y', lean in and ask more. The source flags this as your potential best customer.
- Capture their exact vocabulary for later use in copy, since they describe the failed-solution cycle in their own words.
- Separate this from beginner prospects who have never tried anything — they need education about the problem first.
- **Condition:** A 100% never-tried-anything list is not necessarily worse; the source's claim is that failed-solution buyers are among the best, not the only good segment.
- **Condition:** You still have to solve their actual problem; the source does not suggest exploiting past frustration.

#### Social listening for pain-point topics, not brand mentions
Card: `bf1e76633e2e5db03b1ac490` · Source: _Adrienne Barnes on Best Buyer Personas, Marketing & Motherhood.docx · Section: 0

Track what people say about problems and frustrations rather than your brand so personas reflect real language and concerns.

Applies when: Adding a social listening layer to persona research or messaging development.

- Set up a listening tool (source used Brand24; verify current alternatives and interfaces).
- Monitor the topics and pain points relevant to your category, not brand handles or mentions, because the goal is audience context rather than community management.
- Capture how people describe frustrations and objections in their own words for use in copy and messaging.
- Use listening data to fact-check interview themes and to gauge whether interviewees' descriptions match broader public conversation.
- **Condition:** Listening provides background and validation, not the why behind individual behavior; that still comes from conversations.
- **Condition:** Tool capabilities and pricing change; confirm before committing.

3. Specify how answers will change positioning or product decisions

#### Jobs Interviews: Track the Timeline of Triggers, Not Product Attributes
Card: `ab9f2f70e75c76d6233d11d4` · Source: Competing Against Luck_ The Story of Innovation and Customer Choice (1).docx · Section: 1

The interview method for surfacing a job: reconstruct the causal timeline of what happened before a purchase and avoid questions about the product's features.

Applies when: Building avatars, running win/loss or purchase interviews, or briefing an agency or researcher before qualitative fieldwork.

- Reconstruct the timeline of events and thoughts that led to the decision, in order, rather than asking for opinions about the product.
- Do not focus on product attributes; the goal is the causal story of the choice, not feature preferences.
- If the customer struggles to answer directly, ask what else they considered or sometimes hired instead of this product — substitutes expose the real competition and the real job.
- Capture the language in the customer's own words, since the job statement should reflect their framing ('I'm full all morning', 'it fits right here in my cup holder') rather than the company's category vocabulary.
- Treat the interview output as evidence: each avatar should carry quoted evidence plus the trigger moment and a job statement.
- **Condition:** Attribute-focused interviews and direct feature requests are the failure mode this method is designed to replace — they tell you what people say they want, not why they chose.
- **Condition:** A trigger moment and a job statement are presented as the minimum baseline for any avatar; how many avatars you end up with is not fixed by the method.

#### Run 30-minute discovery calls, not sales calls
Card: `2534ec17bc505b60c5981c6a` · Source: _Adrienne Barnes on Best Buyer Personas, Marketing & Motherhood.docx · Section: 0

Frame customer interviews explicitly as exploratory conversations about the buyer's experience to fill your calendar and avoid the demo/upsell fear.

Applies when: Recruiting and conducting customer interviews to extract buyer insights for personas, messaging or product decisions.

- State clearly up front (in the scheduling email and at the start of the call) that you are not a product expert, cannot solve their problem, and are not there to demo or upsell.
- Tell them exactly what the call is about and how much time you need (source uses 30 minutes) so there is no confusion or concern about a hidden sales motive.
- Ask about their full customer journey: how they found the product, why they use it, their experience and emotions around it.
- Aim for about 20 conversations; the source cites Nielsen/company research (via Deploy Empathy by Michele Hansen) that 20 conversations yield about 97% sentiment coverage of the audience.
- Expect customers who love or hate the product to want to talk; if few people will book, that itself signals weak feeling toward the product.
- **Condition:** The 97% figure is a cited research claim, not a guarantee for every audience.
- **Condition:** Fear of bothering customers or triggering cancellations is largely unfounded per the source's experience, but interviews should still be handled with care.

#### Source data externally and pull CRM firmographics
Card: `ff3969ab7ccbc2dba810219e` · Source: How to Define Your Ideal Customer Profile (ICP).docx · Section: 0

Combine external research (surveys, interviews, social mining) with internal CRM fields to assemble the ICP data picture.

Applies when: You are assembling ICP data and trying to work out where the evidence should come from for a specific company size.

- Gather external data: customer surveys, direct customer conversations and social platforms such as LinkedIn, Reddit and Instagram where prospects discuss pain points and solution needs.
- Improve your collection prompt for communities: ask what pain points people have, or what they would want if you were designing a solution for their industry.
- Pull internal data: industry, company size, location, seniority and job profile already in your CRM or database.
- Mine sales and support calls for recurring pain points, company types and who on the buying side seeks information.
- If you lack a CRM, use LinkedIn to research target accounts in your market, and use sales/call transcripts if you have them; the source jokes you could simply get a CRM, so treat tooling as a sourcing decision, not a blocker.
- Package everything together into the data layer you will use to define ICP or persona.
- **Condition:** Which data you can access depends on company size and existing tooling.
- **Condition:** No CRM and no transcripts means leaning more heavily on community mining and direct conversations.
- **Condition:** The source notes only 48% of over 300 surveyed marketers had a clear, well-documented ICP, so starting from thin data is common.
