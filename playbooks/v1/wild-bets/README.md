## Design a bounded customer delight experiment
Workflow ID: `wild-bets.design-delight`

**Deliverable:** Delight experiment brief

### Proposed decision sequence
1. Confirm the basic service experience is functioning
2. Choose a relevant voluntary customer signal and respectful intervention
3. Cap cost and define learning without assuming every gift drives retention

### Supporting knowledge candidates
These cards were ranked locally for this task. They are evidence to assess, not proof that every proposed step is supported. Full steps and conditions are retained below.

#### Use pet portraits as an unfair content and referral engine
Card: `ca8e2e37165b5babbd84dd01` · Source: Unconventional E-Commerce Offer Strategies.docx · Section: 0

Chewy's hand-painted surprise pet portraits show that a costly, unscalable gift can generate high-converting user-generated content and referrals.

Applies when: You are considering a signature high-cost surprise gift as a brand differentiation and acquisition flywheel.

- Identify a gift that is personalized to the customer's emotional identity (for pet owners, a portrait of their pet).
- Send it unannounced to selected customers so the emotional bonding lands absolutely.
- Anticipate second-order effects: the source's claim is that recipients inevitably share the artifacts on social media, generating authentic UGC that acts as organic ambassadors and drives referral acquisition.
- Accept that the direct ROI on the gift itself will look negative and judge it on the aggregate of UGC, referrals and advocacy instead.
- Be realistic about the operational scale Chewy operates at (hundreds of independent artists, roughly 1,000 portraits per week).
- **Condition:** The Chewy example is a case study at enterprise scale, not a promise that portrait-like tactics will pay back for a smaller store.
- **Condition:** UGC and referral outcomes are described as typical consequences in the case, not guaranteed metrics.
- **Condition:** Ensure you have the artist supply chain before announcing this.

#### Five Low-Cost 'Doesn't Scale' Ecommerce Experiments
Card: `2d99a5dbeda9f6c12661a039` · Source: Wild bets.docx · Section: 1

Concrete, cheap founder-led tests — from the 20-customers exercise to a no-ads week — designed to surface customer insight and referrals.

Applies when: You want to test unscalable, human-touch tactics in acquisition, retention, or product development without a big budget.

- The '20 customers' exercise: imagine you could only have 20 customers. Write down every touchpoint from discovery to post-purchase, then identify where you can add a manual, human touch.
- The 'founder's inbox' test: for one month forward every customer-service email to the founder and reply personally. The source claims you'll learn more in 30 days than in a year of surveys.
- The 'unscalable gift' experiment: pick your top 10 customers from the last quarter, send a handwritten note and a small unexpected gift, and track whether they reorder and refer.
- Adapt the source's no-ads-week idea as a bounded acquisition experiment; set a maximum acceptable revenue loss and account for campaign disruption before changing an established paid channel.
- The 'community first' approach: before launching your next product, spend six months building an audience and gathering feedback (as Gully Labs did), letting the product emerge from community conversation.
- **Condition:** Timelines and counts (20 customers, 30 days, 10 gifts, 6 months) are the source's suggestions, not validated thresholds.
- **Condition:** Founder-inbox replies only work while volume is low; it is an early-stage diagnostic, not a permanent support model.

#### Add a low-cost surprise insert to VIP and first orders
Card: `9a0245f0095aff02a10477f5` · Source: Wild bets folder_ unconventional offer, retention and CX plays for e-commerce owners.docx · Section: 0

Include a small unexpected gift, sample, or sticker in orders to trigger reciprocity and repeat buying.

Applies when: You're designing unboxing and packaging inserts for first orders or VIP orders and want a low-cost retention lift.

- Add a low-cost surprise to first orders or VIP orders: a sticker, sample, or small gift. Cost runs cents to a few dollars.
- Pair the gift with a handwritten thank-you note. Grilla Grills adds free gifts and a handwritten thank-you note to every package.
- Make it feel personal. Generic filler adds cost without delight.
- Add a welcome note inviting buyers into the community (the boAt pattern) if you have a named tribe or VIP program.
- Use the insert to drive a measurable action: a referral code, VIP invite, or reorder offer with a tracking code.
- **Condition:** Generic filler adds cost without delight; make it feel personal.
- **Condition:** Printed notes pretending to be handwritten can feel fake.

## Prioritise recovery before delight
Workflow ID: `wild-bets.recover-before-delight`

**Deliverable:** Recovery-first action plan

### Proposed decision sequence
1. Identify the unresolved customer failure
2. Specify a straightforward remedy and accountable owner
3. Consider extra delight only after the core issue is resolved

### Supporting knowledge candidates
These cards were ranked locally for this task. They are evidence to assess, not proof that every proposed step is supported. Full steps and conditions are retained below.

#### Resolve failures effortlessly before attempting to delight
Card: `9f47b7d4326c95d77b1e8ea0` · Source: Unconventional E-Commerce Offer Strategies.docx · Section: 0

Dixon's Effortless Experience research (97,000+ interactions) shows delight does not create loyalty during service failures — eliminating effort does.

Applies when: You are deciding how customer support should handle shipping delays, broken items, billing errors and returns, and whether to attach freebies or discounts to those interactions.

- Classify the customer's state: failure scenarios are negative-to-neutral and the objective is least resistance to a resolution, not delight.
- Do not attempt to exceed expectations with free gifts or discounts after making customers wait — the data indicate loyalty plateaus once expectations are met quickly and effortlessly.
- Streamline: eliminate channel switching, avoid customers explaining their issue multiple times to different transferred agents, and target first-contact resolution.
- Implement 'next issue avoidance' to preempt future contacts (e.g., proactively answer the follow-up question that typically triggers a second ticket).
- Measure CES, first-contact resolution rate and resolution time for these interactions — not NPS.
- **Condition:** The finding is from a large research study and is presented as a strong rule; even so, the source does not say delight in failure is forbidden, only that it yields negligible loyalty gain.
- **Condition:** Forcing high effort is described as roughly four times more likely to create active disloyalty than a low-effort experience is to create loyalty — treat as a study finding at that study's scale.
- **Condition:** Perceived effort differs from actual effort: fast resolution via repeated transfers still reads as high effort.

#### Automate Rule-Based Operations Before Chasing Wild Bets
Card: `7ccc9fc1a3f252a66f8e9c08` · Source: Wild bets.docx · Section: 1

Use AI to absorb repetitive customer service, outreach and content tasks so solo operators can spend judgment on the bets that matter.

Applies when: You are a small or solo ecommerce team deciding what to automate versus what to keep manual.

- Audit the business for tasks that are repetitive and rule-based — the source names personalized emails, customer-service responses, and content creation.
- Automate those with AI tools to reach a volume a solo founder could not match manually, per the 'AI Gothpreneur' model.
- Reserve founder time for strategy and creative work, and for the wild bets that require human judgment.
- Do not automate the high-touch 'doesn't scale' moments the source treats as differentiation (personal follow-ups, referral asks, over-delivery).
- As with any AI tooling, verify the current capabilities, pricing and data-handling terms of each provider before committing.
- **Condition:** This is a LinkedIn-post model, not a measured case study; no performance data is presented.
- **Condition:** The source pairs this with human-touch differentiation elsewhere, so automation is not a blanket prescription.

#### Write an outcome guarantee to move risk from buyer to seller
Card: `3a74574fff82037c39f3df6a` · Source: Wild bets folder_ unconventional offer, retention and CX plays for e-commerce owners.docx · Section: 0

Promise a specific result and a clear remedy if it fails, which can lift sales while barely raising refunds according to Hormozi.

Applies when: You sell a product with a measurable outcome (supplements, tools, courses, services) and want a stronger offer than a standard refund.

- Identify a specific, measurable outcome your product produces.
- Offer a product-appropriate remedy for a substantiated, measurable claim. The source's keep-working-until-success example comes from services and should not become an unsupported physical-product guarantee.
- Make terms specific. Vague promises invite disputes and abuse.
- Best for products with a measurable result; a generic money-back guarantee is weaker than a stated-outcome commitment.
- Track refund rate after launch to verify the guarantee doesn't erode margin before scaling the promise.
- **Condition:** The ~30% sales lift is a source's figure, not a universal benchmark.
- **Condition:** Vague promises invite disputes; terms must be specific.
- **Condition:** Only suitable for products with a measurable result.
- **Condition:** The cited sales lift is not evidence of an ecommerce effect. Validate the product outcome and remedy before testing the offer.

## Evaluate a customer experience experiment

2. Separate anecdotes from measurable incremental outcomes
3. Decide stop, refine or extend with explicit uncertainty

#### Track earned attention separately from incremental sales and campaign cost
Card: `89bcc55e9cc42ec768b88d27` · Source: Unconventional E-Commerce Offer Strategies.docx · Section: 0

Wild bets are not direct-response spend — their expected return shows up as earned media, organic referrals and UGC rather than attributable revenue in the campaign window.

Applies when: You are building the business case to leadership for an unscalable retention or brand spend that fails direct-attribution tests.

- Do not judge a wild bet on immediate revenue attribution — the source repeatedly describes ROI-defying spends (Chewy's ~1,000 portraits per week; CAH's Bullshit Box) whose returns came as headlines, shares and referrals.
- Forecast the second-order effects: social sharing and UGC acting as organic ambassadors and referral acquisition.
- Track a proxy for earned media value and referral volume separately from the campaign cost.
- Describe the program as a relationship-building experiment in planning; this does not determine accounting treatment or turn media-value estimates into revenue.
- Combine with the metric-matching card so the right KPIs are reported for delight plays.
- **Condition:** The source's examples (Chewy, CAH) are at enterprise scale with well-documented viral outcomes; small stores may not reproduce them.
- **Condition:** Earned media value is an estimate with wide methodology variance.
- **Condition:** Do not use earned-media framing to avoid ever measuring whether the tactic produces repurchase or referral.

#### Send handwritten notes and direct mail to repeat buyers
Card: `42d04b3223baf4a108fb4cd9` · Source: Wild bets folder_ unconventional offer, retention and CX plays for e-commerce owners.docx · Section: 0

Use physical mail to cut through a full inbox and signal real effort, target proven repeat customers to control cost.

Applies when: You're designing a retention touch for repeat buyers or VIPs and want a measurable, non-discount incentive to reorder.

- Start with your top 50-100 customers by lifetime spend, not the whole list. Cost is roughly $1-$3 per note plus time.
- Target repeat buyers and high-value orders; do not waste the effort on first-time buyers with no demonstrated affinity.
- Include a private offer code or referral tear-off insert so you can measure response against a control group.
- Send annual or anniversary letters to repeat buyers with a VIP offer or club invite. Cost runs $1-$5 per piece depending on format.
- Use handwriting services (e.g., LettrLabs and similar) once volume grows, but keep it genuine. Printed notes pretending to be handwritten can feel fake.
- **Condition:** Cost scales with list size; reserve for proven repeat buyers.
- **Condition:** Partial response measurement requires a control group to isolate the effect.

#### Reactivate lapsed buyers with a personalized physical letter
Card: `70a71f09d8c29cc7981ab857` · Source: Wild bets.docx · Section: 0

Test a personalized letter campaign against an email-only control for customers who haven't purchased in 6-12 months.

Applies when: Win-back and replenishment campaigns for lapsed customer segments where email open and click rates have decayed.

- Define the lapsed segment - the source's example, Fressnapf, targeted a second-purchase campaign to 15,000 customers; the adaptation guidance suggests a 6-12 month no-purchase window.
- Personalize the letter with prior purchase data and include a specific return incentive rather than a generic greeting.
- Always run a control group receiving only your existing email treatment, because the entire value case rests on incremental lift, not raw response.
- In the source case, the campaign reported an ROI of 6 and doubled conversion versus control; use that as an illustration of the test design, not as a forecast for your list.
- Iterate on timing, incentive depth, and creative across successive sends, and keep the physical channel in your lifecycle map rather than as a one-off.
- **Condition:** The ROI and doubled-conversion figures come from one retailer's campaign and one vendor's write-up.
- **Condition:** Direct mail is expensive per contact; it only works if the segment value justifies the unit cost.
- **Condition:** Customers who moved or changed addresses won't receive it.
