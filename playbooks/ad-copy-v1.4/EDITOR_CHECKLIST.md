# Copy editor's checklist

Use these questions on the finished ad, not on an explanation of its strategy.

1. **Who is this for?** Can this particular shopper recognise their situation? A scenario supplied in a brief is a hypothesis unless customer evidence supports it.
2. **What do they want to change?** Name a small, recognisable frustration or buying hesitation. Do not invent fear to create a problem.
3. **What becomes better?** Show a useful, realistic gain. An aspiration is appropriate only when the product can credibly help with it.
4. **How does the product help?** Link the gain to a supported feature. A before-and-after connection can be implicit; do not force those labels into the ad.
5. **Would a person say this?** Prefer familiar words, direct sentences and a warm tone. Aim below grade 10. A readability formula is a rough check, not a substitute for reading the copy aloud.
6. **Can the reader picture it?** Use a relevant object or action. Concrete detail should clarify the message rather than decorate it.
7. **Does every sentence help?** Remove jargon, repetition and empty praise. Keep necessary qualifications concise.
8. **Are the variants different?** Different nouns, headlines or opening questions do not necessarily create different reasons to buy.
9. **Are the claims supported?** A strong score never compensates for invented proof, fit, ease, savings or outcomes.

For example, an installation-hesitant shopper's bridge might be: “unsure where to start” → “1-to-1 installation video support” → “guidance instead of working it out alone.” That is supported by the saved product facts. “A completely safe home in minutes” is not.

## How to improve the playbook

Record the exact sentence that failed, the owner's preferred alternative, the reason and the affected brief. Distinguish a reusable writing rule from a brand-specific preference or a missing product fact. Put approved voice and facts in the brand context; put reusable writing rules in a new playbook version. Keep original cards and old outputs intact.

Test a revised version against the previous version on the same facts and model, including at least one fresh situation. Judge finished copy blindly when possible. Preserve losses and reviewer disagreements. Do not keep generating until a favourite version wins. Human preference and later campaign results are separate evidence from AI editorial ratings.

This version adds checks to writing, revision and objection workflows. The current generation experiment covers static-ad writing only; it does not validate the other workflows, other brands or campaign performance.

First check: does every standalone ad answer the actual buying question? A vivid scene or simple wording cannot compensate for missing that answer.
